Reliance is the first Indian company run through this checklist, and it's a useful stress test of a framework built mostly around single-business US/Europe names. It's genuinely three or four businesses under one roof: the original Jamnagar refining and petrochemicals engine (O2C, still ≈63% of revenue), India's largest retailer (Reliance Retail, 20,000+ stores), and Jio, India's #1 telecom and broadband carrier with roughly 48.9% wireless-broadband share — now on the verge of what would be India's largest-ever IPO after Jio Platforms received SEBI approval for a ₹37,700 crore listing. Revenue and EBIT have both compounded at roughly 11% a year over FY22-26 — real growth, just short of this framework's 15% bar — and the consolidated operating margin of 11.47% looks thin next to the software and pharma names on this site, though that single number can't show that it's blending a low-margin refining business with much higher-margin retail and telecom units. What holds up well: the balance sheet is genuinely conservative for a company spending this much on capex (net debt/EBITDA under 1x), the share count is flat (the October 2024 1:1 bonus issue was a split, not dilution), and the Ambani family/promoter group's roughly 50.5% stake is about as concentrated an insider holding as anywhere on this site. The stock itself is a genuine curiosity: split-adjusted, it's essentially flat since the FY2022 fiscal year-end close even though trailing EPS is up roughly 31% over the same stretch — a straightforward story of P/E compression, not earnings decline. Net: 7 of 14 criteria met, Martinero Index 57/100, a mid-pack score driven mostly by the growth and profitability criteria this framework weighs most heavily. This isn't a call to buy or avoid Reliance — just what the checklist says today.

Gross Margin Trend (FY2022-FY2026)
Gross margin has held in a narrow 32-35% band and improved modestly overall, from 32.04% (FY2022) to 34.86% (FY2026) — a +1.90pp gain versus FY2023 specifically (the window used for criterion #14), driven mainly by the growing weight of higher-margin retail and digital services in the mix.
Market position, honestly stated: the three segments sit at very different points on the concentration spectrum, which is why criterion #6 (market concentration) scores a middling 3/5 rather than a clean "Met". Jio is genuinely dominant in a near-duopoly telecom market (≈48.9% share vs Airtel's ≈34.9%). Jamnagar gives Reliance real refining scale, but the global refining/petrochemicals market itself is competitive, not concentrated, against Aramco, ExxonMobil and Sinopec. Retail is the fragmented outlier: Reliance is India's largest organized retailer, but organized retail overall is still only ≈18-20% of India's total retail spend, so even the #1 organized player controls a small slice of the whole market.

Forward P/E (19.67x currently) has generally run below trailing P/E — see explainer below.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY22-26 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and generally the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are headed. For Reliance specifically: trailing P/E (23.45x) sits just below its own FY22-26 average (24.89x), and forward P/E (19.67x) is noticeably cheaper still, consistent with analyst consensus expecting continued double-digit EPS growth (3-year forecast ≈34.5% cumulative, per stockanalysis.com). One more lens worth a glance: the PEG ratio, forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether a "cheap" multiple is actually cheap once growth is accounted for. Reliance's PEG currently runs around 1.5 — above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, suggesting the stock isn't dramatically cheap relative to its own growth outlook despite the modest discount to its own trading history.
Balance sheet: net debt of ₹1,39,853 crore against an estimated FY2026 EBITDA of roughly ₹1,70,732 crore gives a Net Debt/EBITDA of ≈0.82x — comfortably under this framework's <2x bar (note: Reliance's own reported EBITDA figure, ₹2,07,911 crore, differs from this standardized EBIT+D&A calculation due to differing treatment of other income and JV/associate income; either way, leverage stays well under 1x). ROIC of 8.07% clears neither the assumed 11.5% WACC nor this framework's 15% absolute bar — a genuine miss, not a borderline one. What's Working

Genuine #1 positions across all three core segments (telecom, organized retail, refining scale), a conservative balance sheet for a company spending this heavily on capex (net debt/EBITDA under 1x), a flat share count, gross margin that's actually improved over three years, and a promoter/family stake (≈50.5%) that's among the most concentrated insider ownership on this site — recently increased, not reduced. The current entry multiple also sits modestly below Reliance's own five-year average.

What to Watch

Growth (revenue and EBIT both ≈11% CAGR) and profitability (11.47% operating margin, 8.07% ROIC) are the real misses, and they sit in the two categories this framework weighs most heavily. Capex remains very heavy across all three segments simultaneously, keeping FCF conversion under the bar despite an improving trend. The pending Jio Platforms IPO (SEBI-approved, ₹37,700 Cr) could unlock value or introduce new minority-shareholder dynamics depending on structure and pricing — worth watching closely once it prices.
