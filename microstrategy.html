Strategy Inc. is, honestly, the hardest company this checklist has been run against. It is not a growth story, not a margin story, not a capital-efficiency story — it is a leveraged bet on bitcoin's price, wrapped in the corporate shell of a once-mid-size enterprise software company. The legacy business (Strategy One / MicroStrategy BI) still exists and is real: ≈$477M revenue, roughly flat-to-declining over five years (-1.68% CAGR), 68.7% gross margin, a niche also-ran against Microsoft, Salesforce/Tableau, Qlik, SAP and Google in a crowded BI market. But it stopped being the reason to own the stock years ago. Since 2020, under Executive Chairman Michael Saylor, the company has raised roughly $8.2B in staggered 0%-coupon convertible notes (maturities 2028-2032) plus a growing stack of preferred instruments (STRK/STRF/STRC/STRD) and diluted the common share count by over 240% since FY2021 (112.85M → 384.23M shares) — all to fund the purchase of roughly 845,000 bitcoin, about 4.0% of the total supply that will ever exist, making Strategy the largest corporate bitcoin holder on earth. That means the stock's value lives or dies with the "mNAV" — market cap divided by the value of the bitcoin held — and that number has compressed hard: from a historical range that included a brief 6x spike during the 2021 bull run, down toward 1.0x-1.1x today, and briefly below 1.0x as recently as June 2026 (the stock trading for less than its own bitcoin). Every traditional operating metric on this checklist reads poorly here almost by construction: GAAP operating margin is -1,140.8% (FY2025), driven overwhelmingly by non-cash mark-to-market swings on the bitcoin holdings under new fair-value accounting rules (ASU 2023-08), not by the software business deteriorating. ROIC/WACC, net debt/EBITDA and FCF conversion are all effectively meaningless for a company whose "earnings" are really unrealized bitcoin price swings — scored honestly at zero rather than smoothed into something that looks more normal than it is. The two criteria that do score positively say something real: the entry-multiple criterion (substituting mNAV for P/E, since P/E is undefined) shows the stock trading at a genuine discount to its own historical premium, and Michael Saylor's ~5.3% remaining economic stake (down from majority ownership before the dilution wave, but still worth roughly $3.3B and paired with 35.8% of voting power via Class B shares) represents real, if shrinking, founder skin in the game. Net: 2 of 14 criteria met, Martinero Index 13/100 — not a verdict on whether bitcoin itself is a good long-term holding, just an honest statement that Strategy, evaluated as an operating business the way every other company on this site is evaluated, does not look like one anymore.
The one number that actually matters here: mNAV (market cap ÷ value of bitcoin held). At ≈0.9x-1.1x today, Strategy trades roughly in line with — or even at a discount to — the raw value of its own bitcoin stack, down from historical premiums that ran 1.5x-2x+ and briefly spiked to 6x in the 2021 bull run. A discount here is the opposite of what made the "Saylor trade" work for years: the whole flywheel (issue overpriced stock → buy more bitcoin → premium justifies more issuance) breaks down once the market stops paying a premium for MSTR shares over the bitcoin backing them.

Where things stand (Sept 2026)

Strategy holds approximately 845,000+ bitcoin (≈4.0% of the 21 million that will ever exist), making it the largest corporate bitcoin holder in the world by a wide margin — ahead of every other public "bitcoin treasury company" combined. The company resumed buying in mid-September 2026 after a brief pause, purchasing 4,600 BTC on Aug 31 and a further 950 BTC in the days before this dashboard was compiled. It funds these purchases almost entirely through capital markets activity: common-stock ATM issuance, convertible notes, and a growing stack of preferred securities (STRK, STRF, STRC, STRD) designed to appeal to yield-seeking rather than growth-seeking investors.

Leverage: the "true" picture vs. the traditional ratio. On a traditional Net Debt/EBITDA basis, this metric simply doesn't compute honestly — net debt is real (≈$4.3B-$5.9B depending on the period) but EBITDA is deeply negative and dominated by non-cash bitcoin mark-to-market swings, not recurring cash generation. Scored at the worst band here (0/5) precisely because the ratio is undefined against a genuine debt load, not smoothed over. The more honest way to read leverage at Strategy is debt against the bitcoin collateral itself: roughly $8.2B of convertible notes (staggered maturities 2028-2032, mostly 0%-coupon) against ≈$71.8B of bitcoin held is an implied loan-to-value of only ≈11-12% — genuinely low, and a key reason the company has survived prior bitcoin drawdowns of 70-80% without a forced liquidation. The real risk isn't insolvency at current bitcoin prices; it's a scenario where a much deeper, multi-year bitcoin bear market combines with mNAV staying at or below 1.0x for an extended period, which would strand the company's entire capital-raising flywheel (it can no longer profitably issue stock to buy more bitcoin once the market stops paying a premium for the shares).

What's Working

Strategy remains the largest corporate bitcoin holder on earth by a wide margin, and its convertible-debt structure has proven durable through multiple severe bitcoin drawdowns (70-80%+ peak-to-trough) without a forced liquidation — the roughly 11-12% loan-to-value against the bitcoin stack is genuinely conservative. The legacy software business, while not the point anymore, is still cash-generative at the gross-margin line and subscription revenue is growing at a healthy clip (+54% YoY). And the stock's current mNAV of roughly 0.9x-1.1x represents a real discount to Strategy's own historical premium, which some would read as the closest thing to a value signal available here.

What to Watch

The entire capital-raising flywheel depends on the market paying a premium for MSTR shares over the bitcoin they represent — that premium has now compressed toward parity, and briefly went negative in June 2026. If it stays there, the company loses its main tool for accretive bitcoin accumulation. Roughly $8.2B of convertible notes come due in a concentrated 2028-2029 window; refinancing terms will depend heavily on where bitcoin and MSTR's own share price sit at that time. Share dilution (+240% since FY2021) continues to erode per-share economics even as headline bitcoin-per-share metrics the company promotes can still rise. And virtually every traditional profitability and leverage metric on this dashboard is either negative or not meaningfully computable — this is, by design, not an operating business anymore.
