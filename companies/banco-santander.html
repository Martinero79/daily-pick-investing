Banco Santander is Spain's largest bank by assets and one of Europe's largest by market value, with retail and commercial banking in Spain, the UK (Santander UK, plus TSB, acquired in 2026), Brazil, Mexico and the US (where the Webster acquisition closed on Aug 20, 2026), plus Corporate & Investment Banking, wealth and payments businesses. FY2025 total income was €62.4bn and attributable profit €14.1bn (+12%), with RoTE of 16.3% and an efficiency ratio of 41.2% — strong on both counts. H1 2026 attributable profit rose 31% to €9.0bn but includes a €1.9bn net gain on selling Santander Bank Polska; underlying profit was €7.3bn (+15%). The scorecard case is profitability, efficiency and shrinking share count (about 17.3bn shares at FY2021 → about 15.0bn at FY2025, −13.5%), not top-line growth: S&P-standardised revenue compounded at only ~4.7% a year over FY2021–FY2025 and fell 2.0% in FY2025 (largely currency translation and perimeter effects). The stock more than doubled in 2025 (€4.46 → €10.07) and is up ~3.9x since end-2021, so its trailing P/E of ~10.8x (flattered by the Polska gain) and P/B of ~1.4x sit well above the FY21–25 averages of ~6.0x and ~0.7x.
Framing note — why this scorecard looks different for a bank: Banco Santander, S.A. is a bank, so the standard revenue/EBIT/FCF/net-debt framework does not map cleanly. The same substitutions used on the JPMorgan Chase page are applied here, all flagged inline: Total net revenue stands in for revenue (criterion 1); net income attributable to ordinary shareholders substitutes for both EBIT (criterion 2) and free cash flow (criterion 4) — a bank's cash flow statement is dominated by deposit and trading-book swings, so criteria 2 and 4 are necessarily correlated, a genuine limitation of the adaptation and not a workaround; RoTE vs. an assumed ~10–11% cost of equity substitutes for ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7; 10.5% midpoint used for the points); CET1 ratio vs. the regulatory requirement substitutes for net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8; points banded by the CET1 cushion: 4pp+ = 5, 3–4pp = 4, 2–3pp = 3, 1–2pp = 2, 0–1pp = 1, below requirement = 0); (dividends + buybacks) ÷ net income substitutes for FCF conversion (criterion 9); criterion 10 averages the P/E and P/B ratios versus the bank's own FY21–FY25 year-end history; and criterion 14 uses the efficiency (cost/income) ratio because banks report no gross margin. Annual revenue and net income on this page are S&P-standardised (EUR) so five years sit on one basis; Santander's own reported figures (total income €62.4bn, attributable profit €14.1bn in FY25) are quoted where they differ.

P/E ratioP/B ratio
Reading the valuation: Santander is the most expensive of the three banks against its own history: its year-end P/E ranged from 5.1x to 6.6x in FY21–FY24 and then jumped to 11.0x at end-2025 after the share price more than doubled; P/B went from 0.5–0.6x to 1.3x. Today's P/E of ~10.8x is about 1.8x the FY21–25 average (~6.0x) and P/B of ~1.4x is about 2.0x its average (~0.7x), a mean ratio near 1.9, so criterion 10 scores 0/5 and is Not Met. Higher returns (ROE 10.3% → 12.7%) explain part of the re-rating, and trailing earnings include the Polska gain, which makes the P/E look lower than it would on underlying profit. Own FY21–FY25 year-end averages: P/E 6.8x, P/B 0.70x; current P/E 10.8x (1.58x of average) and P/B 1.43x (2.03x of average). Criterion 10 averages those two ratios (1.81).

What's Working

Profitability and efficiency: RoTE 16.3% against a ~10.5% assumed cost of equity, an efficiency ratio of 41.2% (down ~3pp since FY23), net income compounding at ~15.6% a year with EPS growing from €0.44 to €0.90, a share count down ~13.5% and a CET1 cushion of ~3pp over the requirement even after the Webster acquisition. Capital return is substantial (≥€10bn committed for 2025–26, new €5bn buyback).

What to Watch

Revenue (S&P basis) is flat-to-down in FY25 (−2.0%) and has grown only ~4.7% a year; H1 2026 profit growth of +31% includes a €1.9bn Polska sale gain (underlying +15%). The valuation is far above the bank's own history (P/E ~10.8x vs. ~6.0x average, P/B ~1.4x vs. ~0.7x) after the stock rose 3.9x since end-2021. CET1 is guided down to 12.8–13.0% after Webster. Currency exposure (Brazil, Mexico, UK, US), integration of Webster and TSB, and thin insider ownership are further watch-items.
