GSK is a well-run, profitable diversified pharma with real category leadership in a couple of important franchises — HIV treatment (through majority-owned ViiV Healthcare) and the Shingrix shingles vaccine — plus a strong balance sheet and a 28.5% ROIC that clears its cost of capital by a wide margin. What it doesn't offer is exciting growth: revenue has compounded at a modest 7.2% a year since FY2021, operating income growth (13.8%) falls just short of this framework's bar, and free cash flow has actually declined slightly over the period as capex and business-development spending have crept up. FY2022's results are a genuine outlier worth understanding on their own terms: GSK demerged its Consumer Healthcare business (Haleon) in July 2022, and the accounting gain on that separation temporarily more than doubled net income to £15.0B against only £7.3B of operating income — a one-off that has nothing to do with GSK's ongoing earnings power and is excluded from this dashboard's valuation-average calculation for that reason. Ownership is also unremarkable by this framework's standards: GSK is a widely-held FTSE 100 company with no founder or controlling shareholder.

Pipeline & patent-cliff watch: like all large pharma companies, GSK faces an ongoing treadmill of patent expiries balanced against new drug approvals. None of this is unique to GSK — it's the standard structural risk of the pharma industry — but it's the main reason growth here is measured in single digits rather than double digits.

What's Working

Genuine category leadership in two important, durable franchises (HIV via ViiV, and Shingrix in shingles vaccines), a strong 28.5% ROIC well clear of its cost of capital, moderate and manageable leverage, a roughly flat share count, and low capex intensity typical of an R&D-driven rather than capital-intensive business. The current valuation (16.4x TTM P/E) sits almost exactly at GSK's own FY21-25 average, once the Haleon demerger distortion is stripped out — a fair, unremarkable entry point rather than either a bargain or an overpay.

What to Watch

Growth is genuinely modest here — revenue, EBIT and free cash flow have all grown well below this framework's 15% bar, and FCF has actually declined slightly since FY2021 as capex and business-development spending rose. The global pharma market is fragmented and intensely competitive, ownership carries no founder or anchor shareholder to align incentives, and GSK faces the same structural patent-cliff and pipeline risk every large pharma company does — most notably early competitive threats emerging around Shingrix (Moderna's mRNA candidate) and HIV. None of this makes GSK a bad business, but it is a mature, defensive compounder rather than a growth story.
