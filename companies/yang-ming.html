Yang Ming is one of the smaller listed pure-plays on the same global container-shipping trade that Maersk competes in — Taiwan's #2 carrier by size (behind Evergreen), ranked #7 worldwide with roughly 2.1-2.3% of global TEU capacity, and a member of the Premier Alliance alongside Japan's ONE and Korea's HMM. Its recent history is a textbook boom-bust cycle: pandemic-era freight rates pushed operating margin to 60.75% in FY2021 and revenue to a peak of NT$375.9B in FY2022, generating windfall profits that built a genuinely strong net-cash balance sheet (NT$110.8B net cash today). That windfall has since fully unwound — FY2025 revenue fell to NT$163.6B (-26.6% YoY), operating margin compressed to 8.99%, and free cash flow turned negative (-NT$10.9B) as a large fleet-renewal capex program (new vessels ordered through 2029) collided with a weaker freight-rate environment. On this framework's terms, that shows up as a weak scorecard: revenue and EBIT both show deeply negative 5-year CAGRs (-16.3% and -48.1% respectively) purely because the window's starting point was itself an extraordinary boom year, ROIC (3.86%) sits well below this framework's 8.5% WACC assumption, and capex intensity (23% of TTM revenue) is the opposite of asset-light. The balance sheet strength and roughly 14% Taiwan-government-linked ownership stake are the clearest positives; everything else reflects a capital-intensive, highly cyclical carrier currently sitting well below its own recent peak, not a hidden bargain.

Combined government-linked ownership of roughly 14% reflects Taiwan's long-standing policy interest in maintaining domestically-controlled container shipping capacity for a trade-dependent island economy — conceptually similar to the state-linked structures seen at Saudi Aramco or China's COSCO elsewhere in global shipping, though at a much smaller stake than those examples. What's Working

The net-cash balance sheet (NT$110.8B) built during the 2021-22 windfall is the clearest structural strength here, giving Yang Ming real financial flexibility to fund its current fleet-renewal program without relying on debt markets. Share count is essentially flat, government-linked strategic ownership (≈14%) is meaningful and policy-durable, and Q2 2026 showed a genuine sequential recovery (+18.78% YoY revenue, the best operating margin in a year) off the FY2025 trough.

What to Watch

This is a fundamentally cyclical, sub-scale (#7 globally, ≈2% share) container carrier whose FY2021-22 profits were a pandemic-driven windfall, not sustainable earnings power. ROIC sits well below even this framework's WACC assumption, FCF has turned negative twice in the FY21-25 window (most recently on rising capex, not just weak rates), and the current P/E already sits at a premium to a genuinely volatile own-history average. Freight rates remain volatile and geopolitically exposed (Red Sea rerouting, US-China tariff policy, industry-wide new-vessel oversupply), and Yang Ming's position within the Premier Alliance depends on continued cooperation with larger partners ONE and HMM.
