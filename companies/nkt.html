NKT is a 135-year-old Danish cable manufacturer that has re-rated from a low-margin, break-even industrial into the centre of Europe's offshore-wind and grid-interconnection buildout. Its Solutions segment designs, manufactures and installs extra-high-voltage (up to 525kV HVDC) submarine and land cables for offshore wind farms, interconnectors and grid reinforcement — a market with only a handful of qualified global suppliers (Prysmian, NKT, Nexans, LS Cable & System, Sumitomo Electric) because of the specialised extrusion towers, cable-laying vessels and multi-year qualification track records required. NKT is riding a structural, policy-backed demand wave: European and UK offshore wind targets, HVDC interconnectors, and grid reinforcement are driving a record order backlog (€10.2bn at end-2025, jumping to €13.5bn after Q1 2026's award of Eastern Green Link 3 and two Scottish transmission projects worth over €4.2bn combined). To meet this demand NKT is mid-way through a roughly €1bn capacity expansion — a new extrusion tower and factory at Karlskrona, Sweden (targeted to become the world's largest high-voltage offshore cable site) and a new, record-capacity cable-laying vessel entering service in 2027. This is a genuine capital-intensive industrial cycle, not a software growth story: FY2025 capex reached €695m (19.5% of revenue) and free cash flow turned negative (–€196m) for the first time in the current cycle. The balance sheet can absorb this (net cash of ~€965m from two prior equity raises, debt/EBITDA under 1x), and returns on the capital already deployed are strong (ROIC well above cost of capital), but the next 2-3 years hinge on execution — ramping the new factory and vessel on time and on budget while converting the backlog into cash. This is a cyclical, oligopoly-quality industrial compounding into a multi-decade infrastructure theme, not a hyper-growth tech name — score it accordingly.
Capex supercycle risk: NKT is roughly halfway through a ~€1bn investment programme (Karlskrona SE factory expansion + third 200m extrusion tower; new market-leading cable-laying vessel, operational 2027) that pushed FY25 capex to €695m (19.5% of revenue, up from 7.5% in FY22) and drove free cash flow negative for the first time since 2021. Execution risk (cost overruns, ramp timing, vessel delivery) and project-phasing lumpiness in quarterly revenue are the key swing factors over the next 2–3 years — the €10–13.5bn order backlog is the best forward read on demand, not any single quarter's revenue print.
H1 2026 update (released Aug 13, 2026): Revenue €1,811m (+1.6% reported; organic -7% at standard metal prices as the Champlain Hudson Power Express project rolled off a high prior-year base). Operational EBITDA margin expanded to 15.8% (H1 2025: 13.8%) on better project mix. FY2026 guidance was raised on profitability: operational EBITDA now guided to €400–430m (from €360–410m), revenue guidance narrowed to €2.65–2.75bn at standard metal prices. FCF stayed deeply negative (–€341m) as the capex cycle continues, but net cash still stood at €591m at June 30, 2026 (net debt/EBITDA –1.5x LTM). Full detail on page 3.

Quarterly Performance & Order Backlog
Last 9 reported quarters (Q2 2024 – Q2 2026), EUR millions. The high-voltage order backlog — not any single quarter's revenue — is NKT's key forward demand indicator.
Order backlog: the real story

High-voltage backlog stood at €10.6bn at end-2024, €10.7bn after Q1 2025, and €10.2bn at end-2025 (€8.9bn in standard metal prices) plus >€3.5bn of additional booking commitments. Q1 2026 delivered NKT's highest-ever quarterly order intake — awards of Eastern Green Link 3 (UK) and two Scottish transmission projects worth a combined >€4.2bn — pushing the Transmission backlog to €13.5bn (€12.0bn std. metal prices). At end-Q2 2026 the backlog stood at €13.0bn (€11.6bn std. metal prices) — the dip vs. Q1 reflects project execution/revenue recognition (including the Champlain Hudson Power Express line reaching commercial operation), not a slowdown in awards. A further >€2.5bn across five TenneT framework-agreement projects is not yet reflected in that figure. This backlog underwrites revenue visibility deep into the 2030s, even as quarterly revenue recognition remains lumpy.

"Solutions" = high-voltage/offshore project cables; "Applications & Accessories" = standard low/medium-voltage cables plus service & accessories. Revenue Growth YoY %
Growth decelerated through 2025 off tough prior-year comparisons and project phasing; Q4'25 dipped negative, Q1'26 re-accelerated modestly, Q2'26 was essentially flat (+0.2% reported) as the Champlain Hudson Power Express project rolled off a high prior-year base — organic growth at standard metal prices was -9% for the quarter.
Operating Margin Trend (Quarterly)
Margin bounces with project mix quarter to quarter — Q3'25's prior 9.3% peak was broken in Q2'26 at ~10.5% (EBIT margin on standard-metal-price revenue) as project mix improved further.
Read quarters, not weeks: Q4 2025 revenue fell -4.8% YoY purely on project-delivery phasing on large offshore contracts, and the Solutions share of revenue swung from ~55% of the group in FY2025 to ~44% in Q1 2026 under the new segment definitions. Q2 2026 organic revenue then fell -9% (std. metal prices) as the Champlain Hudson Power Express (CHPE) transmission line — a large NYC-bound project — reached commercial operation and rolled off an unusually high Q2 2025 comparison base; CEO Claes Westerlind called CHPE "a historic milestone... for the transition to renewable energy in New York City." None of these swings reflect weaker demand — order intake and backlog both hit records in the same window, and profitability actually improved (Q2'26 operational EBITDA margin 15.7%, up from 14.5% a year earlier). Treat single-quarter revenue and mix swings as noisy; the multi-billion-euro backlog is the more reliable signal.

Forward P/E (~29.3x, consensus NTM estimate) is shown only for today — NKT is a thinly-covered Nordic mid-cap and reliable historical forward-P/E consensus for earlier fiscal year-ends wasn't available from sourced data, so no multi-year forward series is plotted.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E (current only)
Trailing vs. forward P/E: current P/E divides today's price by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings; the FY22-25 bars are that same backward-looking ratio at each fiscal year-end, showing NKT's own re-rating over time. Forward P/E instead divides today's price by consensus estimates of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and better at capturing expected growth that trailing P/E can't see. For NKT specifically, forward P/E (~29.3x) sits above trailing (~26x), an unusual pattern versus most growth names on this site — it implies consensus expects near-term earnings to dip slightly before the Karlskrona/high-voltage cable capacity ramp (and its backlog) fully convert to profit, not that the market sees imminent earnings growth. The PEG ratio (forward P/E divided by expected EPS growth) sits close to 1.0 for NKT, roughly the textbook "fair value" line — a useful counterweight to the P/E-alone read, since it suggests the current multiple is broadly in line with consensus growth expectations rather than pricing in either a bargain or excess optimism.

Share Count Trend

Shares outstanding rose from ~43m (2021) to 53.45m (2026), up ~24%, driven by the November 2020 rights issue and June 2023 follow-on offering used to fund the capex programme. Dilution has since stabilised — FY2025 buyback/dilution yield was roughly flat (+0.3%). No dividend has been paid; all capital is being reinvested into capacity.

Governance note: NKT has no controlling family, founder or state shareholder — a legacy of its 2017 demerger from the former NKT Holding conglomerate (which also spun off Nilfisk and Photocure). The only disclosed holder above Denmark's 5% notification threshold is BlackRock, Inc. (~5.3%, largely index/passive money). Ownership is otherwise dispersed across Nordic pension funds and long-horizon institutional asset managers with energy-transition mandates — a stable but not "skin-in-the-game" anchor-owner register.

What's Working
  • Record order backlog (€10.2bn end-FY25 → €13.5bn post-Q1'26 → €13.0bn at end-Q2'26, the dip being execution not demand) gives multi-year revenue visibility; a further >€2.5bn of TenneT framework awards isn't even booked yet
  • FY2026 profitability guidance raised with H1 results — operational EBITDA now guided to €400–430m (from €360–410m) — while revenue guidance merely narrowed, signalling margin, not volume, is the upside surprise
  • Operational EBITDA margin expanding: 15.8% H1 2026 vs. 13.8% H1 2025; Q2'26 EBIT margin (~10.5%, std. metal price basis) is a new quarterly high
  • Champlain Hudson Power Express reached commercial operation in Q2 2026 — a flagship project delivered, now generating revenue on the grid instead of construction risk on the books
  • Still net cash (~€591m at end-Q2'26) despite continued heavy capex — balance sheet remains a strength, not a risk
  • ROIC (22-30%) far exceeds cost of capital — capacity is being deployed into pre-contracted, value-accretive projects
  • Structural, policy-backed demand: European/UK offshore wind, HVDC interconnectors, grid reinforcement — multi-decade addressable market
  • High barriers to entry (extrusion tower height, cable-laying vessel fleet, qualification track record) keep the competitive set to ~5 credible global players
What to Watch
  • FCF is deeply negative and getting worse in absolute terms: –€196m FY2025, already –€341m for H1 2026 alone, as capex continues; net cash fell from €965m (FY25-end) to €591m (Q2'26-end) as a result
  • EBIT margin (~7-7.4% TTM) remains structurally thin — a project/manufacturing margin profile, not a platform/software one, despite the EBITDA-margin improvement
  • Quarter-to-quarter lumpiness continues: Q2'26 organic revenue fell -9% (std. metal prices) as CHPE rolled off a high base; Solutions-equivalent mix has swung between ~44% and higher across recent quarters under the new segment definitions
  • Execution risk on the Karlskrona factory and second cable-laying vessel (NKT Eleonora), both still targeted for 2027; Q2'26 also saw a "slightly negative" cost impact from Strait of Hormuz-related material disruption
  • Meaningful past share dilution (+24% share count since 2021) to fund growth; no dividend
  • No anchor/founder shareholder — ownership is diffuse institutional/index money
  • Valuation not undemanding at ~26x trailing earnings against a still-volatile EPS base (analyst consensus: Hold)
