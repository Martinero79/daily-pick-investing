Half-Year Development — Latest Results
L'Oréal publishes quarterly sales updates but reports full P&L semi-annually. Latest reported period: H1 2026 (six months to June 30, 2026, reported July 29-30, 2026).
H1 2026 Headlines

Sales reached €23.7B, up 5.8% as reported and a strong 6.5% on an adjusted like-for-like basis — more than double the 3.0% pace of H1 2025, a genuine acceleration. Growth was broad-based across all four divisions, with Dermatological Beauty, hair care, and e-commerce cited as particular strength areas, alongside emerging-market momentum (double-digit growth) and a mainland China market that returned to modest expansion. Operating margin reached a first-half record of 21.3%, up 20 basis points year-over-year despite a 70-basis-point increase in brand-building investment ("brand fuel"). Net profit excluding non-recurring items rose 4.7% to €3.96B. Q1 2026 alone posted an especially strong +7.6% like-for-like print, with Q2 moderating slightly to a still-solid pace as reported growth normalized.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E (current only)
Current, trailing & forward P/E: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. The trailing P/E series above is that same backward-looking ratio at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated L'Oréal relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's price divided by consensus estimates of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and it captures expected growth that trailing P/E can't see. L'Oréal's forward P/E of 27.33x is meaningfully below its 32.82x trailing P/E, implying the market expects solid earnings growth to continue, consistent with the modest discount the trailing multiple already shows versus its own history. Dividing that forward P/E by consensus EPS growth, though, puts L'Oréal's PEG up around 3.5-3.8 — well above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, since expected growth has slowed to the mid-to-high single digits. In other words, the multiple looks cheaper than L'Oréal's own history, but not cheap relative to how fast earnings are actually expected to grow from here.
Balance sheet: L'Oréal held roughly €11.9B in total debt against €9.9B of cash & investments as of FY2025-end (Dec 31, 2025) — a small net debt position of about €2.05B, or just 0.19x EBITDA. This is very low, comfortable leverage, among the lowest in this GRANOLAS batch.

What's Working

L'Oréal combines genuine, rare founder-family control (≈34.8% Bettencourt Meyers) with clean financials virtually free of one-off distortions — the only GRANOLAS name in this batch with no flagged anomaly year. Leverage is minimal, FCF conversion clears 80%, the share count has declined modestly, and capex intensity is genuinely low. Unusually for this batch, the current valuation sits below its own five-year average, and H1 2026 showed real, broad-based acceleration (like-for-like growth more than doubling year-over-year) with a first-half record operating margin.

What to Watch

Growth, while steady, has never approached this framework's 15% CAGR bar on revenue, EBIT, or FCF, and ROIC at 13.33% falls just short of the 15% threshold despite clearing an estimated cost of capital comfortably. Global beauty remains a genuinely fragmented, well-competed market with several large, well-resourced rivals. The Nestlé cross-holding (≈20.1%) is a structural feature worth understanding rather than a risk per se, but it does mean a meaningful block of shares sits with a strategic rather than purely financial holder. The five-year P/E range (28.6x-50.8x) has been genuinely volatile, so today's below-average multiple is not a guarantee against further re-rating in either direction.
