Novo Nordisk is the company behind Ozempic and Wegovy — the drugs that created the GLP-1 obesity and diabetes-care category and, for a couple of years, made Novo briefly Europe's most valuable listed company. The underlying business is still genuinely exceptional: 41% operating margins, ROIC north of 39%, and a controlling shareholder (the Novo Nordisk Foundation, via Novo Holdings) that has run the company for a century with a multi-decade horizon rather than a quarterly one. But the last two years have been a real test of the thesis. Eli Lilly's Zepbound/Mounjaro franchise and its newly approved oral pill (orforglipron, branded Foundayo) have overtaken Novo in combined GLP-1 market share (roughly 60% vs. 39% as of Q1 2026), and two separate disappointments from Novo's next-generation drug candidate CagriSema — first in December 2024, then again in February 2026 — repeatedly punctured the growth narrative. The stock fell from an intraday peak of $148.23 (June 25, 2024) to a 52-week low near $35 in March 2026, a roughly 75% drawdown, even as revenue and earnings kept growing throughout. What's left is a business still compounding, now priced at about a third of its own 2022-2023 valuation multiple — the scorecard below is an attempt to separate the real deterioration (a genuine competitive setback, a heavier capex load) from what looks more like a sentiment reset.
This is a "was this a bubble or a reset" case: almost every fundamental keeps growing — revenue, EBIT, dividends — while the stock is down ~75% from its 2024 peak and trades at 11.4x trailing earnings versus a 2021-2025 average closer to 30x. The open question isn't whether Novo is still a good business (the numbers say yes); it's whether Lilly's lead in oral GLP-1s marks a permanent shift in the category's #1 spot, or a gap Novo's pipeline can close.

Quarterly Trend & the Competitive Setback
Last 6 reported quarters, DKK millions. Two GLP-1 pipeline disappointments (Dec 2024, Feb 2026) — not the quarterly numbers themselves — are what actually broke the stock.
What actually moved the stock

Revenue and margins have stayed strong throughout 2025-2026 (see table below) — the drawdown was driven by pipeline and competitive news, not by the reported numbers. Dec 20, 2024: Novo's next-gen drug CagriSema missed weight-loss expectations in the REDEFINE 1 trial (~-20% headline result was reported as "disappointing" as the market had priced in more); the stock gapped down ~19% in a day. Feb 23, 2026: a second CagriSema trial (REDEFINE 4) again failed to beat Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (20.2% vs. 23.6% weight loss at 84 weeks) — a second, harder confirmation that Novo's next-generation pipeline hadn't closed the gap. Combined with Lilly's oral pill (orforglipron/Foundayo) reaching the market and taking share, the stock made a fresh multi-year low of ~$35 in March 2026.

Capital Structure Note

Novo Holdings A/S — wholly owned by the Novo Nordisk Foundation, a Danish charitable foundation — holds A- and B-shares equal to ~28.1% of share capital but ~77.3% of votes, since A-shares carry 10x the voting power of B-shares and are legally non-transferable while the Foundation exists. This is one of the most entrenched, long-horizon control structures of any large-cap globally — for better (patient capital, no activist-driven short-termism) and worse (no external check on capital-allocation decisions like the Catalent deal).

Dividend note: DPS has grown every year, DKK 5.20 (FY2021) → DKK 11.70 (FY2025), roughly 2.25x over five years. Current yield sits around 2.8-3.8% depending on the exact date/FX used — the yield rose mechanically as the share price fell, not because the dividend was cut.

What's Working
  • Revenue, EBIT and dividends have all kept growing every year through the entire drawdown — this was a valuation reset and a competitive setback, not a business breaking down
  • Still #2 globally in a two-player category with enormous, still-growing total demand (obesity care is nowhere near saturated)
  • ROIC (39.2%) far exceeds any reasonable cost of capital; margins remain elite even after compression
  • Trailing P/E (11.4x) is roughly a third of the 2022-2023 peak multiple — a quality compounder now priced closer to an average industrial than a growth pharma
  • Controlling Foundation ownership gives genuine multi-decade patience — no activist pressure for short-term fixes
What to Watch
  • Two consecutive CagriSema trial disappointments (Dec 2024, Feb 2026) suggest the next-gen pipeline hasn't yet closed the gap to Lilly's tirzepatide/orforglipron
  • Eli Lilly's oral pill (Foundayo) has an easier dosing profile than Novo's own oral Wegovy pill — a real product-level disadvantage in the fastest-growing part of the category
  • FCF conversion and capex intensity both fail the checklist right now — the capacity buildout needs to actually convert into volume/share, not just cost
  • Net debt swung from cash-positive to ~DKK 104bn in two years; leverage is still low in absolute terms but the direction of travel is worth tracking
  • Full-year 2026 guidance, even after being raised, still points to a year-over-year decline in adjusted sales/profit growth at constant currency
