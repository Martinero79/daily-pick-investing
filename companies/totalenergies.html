TotalEnergies is one of Europe's largest integrated energy companies: upstream oil and gas, a large integrated LNG business, refining and chemicals, fuel marketing and a growing integrated-power (renewables and electricity) segment. Over FY2021-FY2025 the numbers are flat to down because FY2021 was a recovery year and FY2022 was a price spike: revenue fell 0.3% a year, EBIT 5.0% a year and free cash flow 10.5% a year, while the share count fell about 15% through buybacks and the dividend per share rose from $3.00 to $3.99. Balance-sheet leverage is modest (≈1x net debt/EBITDA by the S&P definition used here). Recent quarters show the cycle turning: Q2 2026 revenue rose 27.8% YoY to $57.1B and EBIT 72%, and the company flagged on Oct 14 that Q3 2026 will include a sharp rise in downstream cash flow and production of 2.45-2.50 Mboe/d. With Brent near $114, these are cycle-high conditions, so the FY21-25 scorecard is shown as measured rather than adjusted. What's Working

Leverage is modest (≈1x net debt/EBITDA) and the share count is down roughly 15% since FY2021 through buybacks, while the dividend per share rose from $3.00 to $3.99 over the period. Gross margin has been stable at 35–36% for five years. The company is a top-3 global LNG player and is expanding integrated power. Recent quarters (Q1–Q2 2026) show a sharp earnings recovery, and Q3 2026 is flagged by the company as strong.

What to Watch

Multi-year growth is negative: revenue, EBIT and FCF all sit below FY2021's recovery-year level at the FY2025 end-point, and FCF conversion is below 60%. Current earnings depend on elevated oil, gas and refining margins (Brent around $114) tied to geopolitical conflict, which can reverse quickly. ROIC (8.05%) barely clears an estimated WACC, capex is about 8.6% of revenue, the P/E is above its own FY21-25 average and the dividend plus buybacks absorb much of the free cash flow. Reported figures here are not the adjusted / CCS numbers the company headlines.
