Wells Fargo is one of the four largest U.S. banks (~$2.15tn of assets at FY25), built around consumer and small-business banking, commercial banking, corporate and investment banking and wealth management. Its story over FY2021–FY2025 is dominated by the Federal Reserve's asset cap: imposed in February 2018 after sales-practice and compliance failures, it held total assets near $1.95tn until it was lifted in June 2025. During those years the bank could not grow its balance sheet, so growth was muted — revenue compounded at just ~1.6% from FY21 to FY25 and net income to common was essentially flat (about 0% on the company-reported FY21 base) — and capital went largely into buybacks: period-end shares fell ~20% and FY25 buybacks ($17.5bn) plus common dividends ($5.4bn) were ~113% of net income to common. Since the cap came off, total assets have grown (to ~$2.28tn mid-2026) and average loans were up 11% YoY in Q3 2026; Q3'26 revenue rose 2.0% YoY and EPS $1.87 (+13%), with company-reported ROTCE of 16.3%. CET1 has fallen to a preliminary 10.0% (from 11.0% a year earlier), still ~1.5pp above the author's estimated ~8.5% requirement, and the shares trade at ~1.85x tangible book against a FY21–FY25 average of ~1.50x.
Framing note — why this scorecard looks different for a bank: the standard revenue/EBIT/FCF/net-debt framework does not map cleanly onto a bank, so this page uses the same substitutions as the JPMorgan Chase dashboard, all flagged inline: Total Net Revenue stands in for revenue (criterion 1); Net Income to common substitutes for both EBIT (criterion 2) and Free Cash Flow (criterion 4) — a bank's cash flow statement is dominated by deposit and trading-book swings, so criteria 2 and 4 are necessarily correlated here, a genuine limitation of adapting the framework; ROTCE vs. an assumed ~10.5% cost of equity (mid-point of 10–11%) substitutes for ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7); CET1 ratio vs. the regulatory requirement substitutes for net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8) — points are banded on the CET1 buffer over an estimated requirement (≥3.0pp = 5, 2.0–3.0 = 4, 1.0–2.0 = 3, 0.5–1.0 = 2, 0–0.5 = 1, below = 0; Met = buffer ≥1.0pp), and the requirement itself (4.5% + stress capital buffer + G-SIB surcharge) is the author's estimate, not a quoted figure; (common dividends + buybacks) ÷ net income to common substitutes for FCF conversion (criterion 9); and entry multiple (criterion 10) is the average of two ratios — current P/E and current P/TBV, each divided by its own FY21–FY25 average of year-end values. Criterion 14 uses the change in the efficiency ratio FY22→FY25 (a falling ratio counts as a rising margin) because banks report no gross margin. Wells-Fargo-specific: (1) FY21 and FY22 net income to common ($20.3bn / $12.1bn) and EPS ($4.95 / $3.14) use the company-reported figures, which were not re-fetched in this build; S&P Global shows $20.8bn / $12.6bn and $5.08 / $3.27 for those years. The net-income CAGR is base-sensitive: ~+0.0% on the company-reported base (1/5 points) versus about −0.6% on the S&P base (0/5). (2) The efficiency ratio is derived and FY22 was inflated by operating losses (legal and customer-remediation costs), which exaggerates the FY22→FY25 improvement. Capex intensity (criterion 13) is N/A: no capex line is available.

What's Working

Capital return is large and visible: shares are down ~20% since FY21 while tangible book value per share rose from $36.75 to ~$44.5, and FY25 buybacks plus dividends were ~113% of net income to common. With the asset cap lifted in June 2025 the balance sheet is growing again (average loans +11% YoY in Q3'26), earnings momentum is strong (Q3'26 EPS +13%, company ROTCE 16.3%), headcount and noninterest expense are falling, and CET1 remains above the estimated requirement.

What to Watch

Four-year growth is minimal (revenue +1.6% a year, net income to common about flat) and the net-income CAGR call is sensitive to the FY21 base used. CET1 has fallen to a preliminary 10.0% from 11.0% a year earlier as buybacks continue, and the true regulatory requirement may be higher than the ~8.5% estimate used here. The shares trade at ~1.85x tangible book vs. a ~1.50x five-year average. Wells Fargo is a #3–4 U.S. bank rather than a leader, noninterest income was down 1% YoY in Q3'26, and insiders own under 1%.
