Ferrari N.V. designs and builds luxury sports cars in Maranello and Modena, Italy, and combines them with Formula 1 racing, sponsorship and a growing lifestyle/brand business. Revenue in FY2025 was €7.15B: €6.0B from cars and spare parts, €0.8B from sponsorship, commercial and brand, and €0.3B other. The model is the opposite of mass-market autos: Ferrari deliberately keeps volumes near 13-14k cars, runs an order book that stretches to the end of 2027, and extracts value through price, a richer model mix (F80, Special Series, Icona) and heavy personalisation. That produced an EBIT margin of 29.5% (versus single digits at most volume carmakers), industrial free cash flow of €1.54B and almost no net industrial debt. Q2 2026 showed the pattern again: revenue €1,938M (+8.4% YoY, +11% at constant currency), EBIT €605M (31.2% margin), diluted EPS €2.62 (+10%) even though deliveries fell to 3,366 cars from 3,494. Guidance was raised to ~€7.60B revenue, ≥€2.26B EBIT and ≥€9.68 EPS. The stock nevertheless trades about 21% below its 52-week high of €429.90 and about 17% below a year ago, because the market has trimmed what it will pay for a company growing revenue at mid-to-high single digits: the trailing P/E is ~37x versus ~50x at the end of 2021 and ~49x at the end of 2024.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Capex is not light — Ferrari spends ~13% of revenue (about €950M a year) because it develops its own engines, new models and racing programme, and capitalises ~€420M of development costs; reported “capex” on some data sites (~€485M) leaves that out. (2) Electrification and tariffs — the first all-electric Ferrari (Luce) launched in 2026; reception, EU-US tariffs and a China shipment decline (941 units in 2025 vs 1,162) are live risks. (3) Racing — Scuderia Ferrari's results and a debated team-principal situation affect brand sentiment and F1 prize money, though the financial effect is modest.

Quarterly Trend (Q3 2024 – Q2 2026)
Eight quarters. Ferrari reports mix and personalisation rather than volume growth, so deliveries and revenue can diverge in any one quarter. Q3 2026 is due Nov 3, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was €1,938M (+8.4% YoY; +11% at constant currency) and EBIT €605M (+10%, 31.2% margin, up 30bp), with diluted EPS of €2.62 (+10%). Cars and spare parts revenue grew 8% on mix and personalisation even though shipments slipped 4% to 3,366 (Americas and China down, EMEA up). Part of the margin gain came from temporarily lower D&A during the model change-over, which management says reverses as new models start production. Industrial FCF was €276M (+39%); net industrial debt moved to €131M after more than €800M of dividends and buybacks. The stock's reaction was muted: it was ~€326 on Jul 22 and ~€344 a week after results.

Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership
Valuation uses trailing P/E (no adjustments needed: Ferrari's adjusted and reported EPS were equal in 2025 and H1 2026). Year-end prices are approximate, derived from S&P Global P/E data.
Trailing P/E — FY-End vs. Current
Current P/E (~37.0x = €341.20 / TTM EPS €9.23) is ~85% of Ferrari's FY2021-25 year-end average (43.5x). The stock has always carried a luxury-brand multiple; the multiple has fallen as EPS rose 99% while the price rose ~51% since end-2021.

Forward P/E & PEG: forward P/E (next-12-month consensus) is ~32x versus a FY21-25 year-end average of ~41x (about 78% of its own average); S&P Global shows a PEG of ~3.5. The scorecard uses the trailing figure (ratio 0.85 = 3/5, at the very edge of the 4/5 band). Average analyst price target €389.60 (19 analysts) implies ~14% upside; a broker survey shows targets being trimmed this week (UBS to $450, Morgan Stanley to $456). Consensus EPS growth over three years is ~10% a year per S&P, so the premium multiple still needs sustained double-digit earnings growth.

Share Count & Buybacks

Diluted weighted shares fell from ~184.6M (FY2021) to ~176.8M (Q2 2026), -4.2%, as Ferrari bought back stock every year; the new ~€3.5B programme to 2030 implies continued reduction. Dividends are growing ~20% a year (€1.36 → €3.62), a ~38% payout. A loyalty scheme gives long-term holders special voting shares (double votes).

Founder & control caveats: Exor, Piero Ferrari and his Trust renewed their shareholders' agreement from Jan 4, 2026 for three years (to 2029, auto-renewing); together they hold ~32% of capital and ~48% of votes, per the 13D/A filing and Ferrari's shareholder page. Exor and Ferrari share directors with overlapping interests, which the company itself flags as a risk factor. The percentages are of outstanding common shares; I did not verify any later change.
Balance sheet: Ferrari has almost no net industrial debt (€32M at Dec 2025, €131M at Jun 2026) after returning more than €1.3B to shareholders in 2025 and €800M+ in Q2 2026 alone. Consolidated net debt of €1.7B (S&P Global: debt €3.17B vs cash €1.43B at Jun 2026) is dominated by €1.5B of securitised customer financing in the US, which is why net industrial debt is the relevant figure here.

What's Working

A rare combination: a 29.5% EBIT margin, 31% ROIC, industrial FCF of €1.5B, virtually no net industrial debt and a ~32% founder/Exor/family anchor with a shareholder pact to 2029. Pricing power shows in mix: revenue +8% in Q2 2026 with fewer cars delivered, an order book covering 2027, and guidance raised in July. EBIT has compounded at ~18% a year, gross margin has recovered to 51.7%, buybacks are shrinking the share count and the multiple is well below its own five-year average (ratio ~0.85 trailing, ~0.78 forward).

What to Watch

Revenue growth is slowing (FY2025 +7.0%, Q1 2026 +3.2%) and shipments are flat or falling while the model range is rebuilt; some of the 2026 margin lift comes from temporarily lower D&A. Capex at ~13% of revenue and FCF conversion at 73% limit how fast cash can be returned. The Luce (first EV) and new models need to land; China shipments fell 19% in 2025 and tariffs and currency (USD, JPY) are headwinds. At ~37x trailing earnings the shares still price in durable double-digit EPS growth, and the stock is already 21% off its high.
