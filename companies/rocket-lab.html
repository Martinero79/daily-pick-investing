Rocket Lab is the second-most active US launch provider (Electron and the sub-orbital HASTE vehicle; the 100th Electron launch came in Nov 2026) and, increasingly, a spacecraft and components manufacturer, which is now the larger part of revenue. Q3 2026 (reported Nov 9, 2026) was a record: revenue $267.8M, +72.7% YoY, GAAP gross margin 33.6%, adjusted EBITDA -$18.0M, GAAP net loss $79.7M (including a $27.3M loss on debt extinguishment and $21.3M of deal transaction costs), and positive GAAP operating cash flow of $7.2M for the quarter, although the nine-month figure is still -$127.3M. Backlog reached about $3.57B, up 52% in the quarter. Q4 guidance is revenue of $270-290M and an adjusted EBITDA loss of $14-20M. The weak spots are the same as before: every year of the FY21-FY25 window carries a GAAP operating loss, capex ran at 26% of FY25 revenue, and the share count is up about 42% since FY21, including roughly 17.6% in the first nine months of 2026 as the company raised about $3.9B of equity through its at-the-market program. The first launch of the medium-lift Neutron rocket, the project the business case leans on most, has slipped to no earlier than 2027, and a pending cash-and-stock acquisition of Iridium Communications (about $8.0B enterprise value, expected to close mid-2027) will add scale, debt and shares. The stock closed at $45.42 on Nov 20, about 70% below its 52-week high of $151.00.

What moved the stock: Q3 2026 (reported Nov 9, 2026)

1. Small starting cap (under ~$5–10B) — FAIL. Market cap is about $29.0B ($45.42 × 639.2M shares), roughly three to six times the checklist ceiling. A further tenfold from here would imply a market cap near $290B (simple arithmetic, not a forecast).
2. Revenue acceleration toward 40%+ — PASS. Year-on-year growth by quarter: Q4'25 +35.7%, Q1'26 +63.5%, Q2'26 +62.0%, Q3'26 +72.7% (FY24 +78.3%, FY25 +38.0%). Company guidance for Q4 2026 of $270-290M implies about +50% to +61% YoY, so growth is guided to stay well above 40% but to ease from Q3's +72.7%. Caveat: part of the growth is acquired (Geost, Mynaric, Motiv and others); organic growth is not separated in the figures used here, and the Neutron delay pushes the next big step to 2027+.
3. Profitability flip (operating margin moving from negative toward positive, and when) — PARTIAL. Direction is right: GAAP operating margin was -164.0% (FY21), -64.1% (FY22), -72.7% (FY23), -43.5% (FY24), -38.0% (FY25), -27.4% for 9M 2026 and -29.6% in Q3 2026. A 2027 profit date is NOT confirmed: the company has not guided to GAAP operating profit, adjusted EBITDA break-even or cash-flow positive for any date, and management said on the Q3 call that moving to positive cash flow “hinges on Neutron”, whose first launch is now no earlier than 2027. No clear analyst consensus date for a GAAP operating profit was found; aggregator forward P/E figures (~680x-920x) imply near-zero positive EPS for the next fiscal year, which is an estimate and not a profit-date confirmation.
4. ROIC trending toward ~20% — FAIL. ROIC is -3.95% (aggregator, trailing twelve months; ROE -6.4%, ROCE -4.1%). With a GAAP operating loss of $243.7M over the last twelve months on top of a capital base swollen by $4.7B of fresh cash, ROIC is unlikely to turn positive before operating profit does, and a 20% ROIC is a long way from here. No company target for returns on capital exists.
5. Warning: P/S above 50x — NOT TRIGGERED (pass). P/S is about 32.9x on trailing-twelve-month revenue of $881.9M (about 23x on aggregator forward sales).
