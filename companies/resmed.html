ResMed, founded in Sydney in 1989 and led by Chairman and CEO Mick Farrell, is the world’s largest maker of CPAP (continuous positive airway pressure) machines, the standard treatment for obstructive sleep apnea, in which the airway repeatedly collapses during sleep. Revenue grew from $3.58B in FY2022 to $5.65B in FY2026 (+12% a year), GAAP operating margin rose from 28.0% to 33.4%, and free cash flow reached $1.65B in FY2026. The model is a razor-and-blades one: a patient buys a device once, then re-buys masks and accessories regularly, which is why masks & other grew faster than devices in FY2026 (+13% vs +9%). Its main rivals are Philips Respironics (hit by a 2021 foam recall and, since 2024, restricted from selling new sleep devices in the US under a consent decree, per public reporting; see the Philips dashboard), Fisher & Paykel Healthcare (strong in masks) and smaller makers such as React Health, BMC and Löwenstein. Shareholder returns are a pillar: ~$1.05B was paid out in FY2026 (about $700M of buybacks plus $350M of dividends), the quarterly dividend was raised 10% to $0.66, and management plans to return more than $1.85B in FY2027. The open debate is GLP-1 weight-loss drugs: Eli Lilly’s Zepbound won US approval for moderate-to-severe sleep apnea in obese adults in December 2024. Bears see a shrinking long-term patient pool; ResMed says the drugs are widening the funnel by getting more people diagnosed and into therapy. So far the reported numbers show no volume damage, but the share price has been range-bound: $246.52 at the end of June 2021 and $245.50 now.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) The GLP-1 question is unresolved — evidence so far (ResMed’s own claims-data analyses, which are company-sponsored, and its commentary) points to GLP-1 users entering CPAP therapy at least as often as before, but multi-year effects of weight loss on sleep-apnea severity are not yet visible in the results. (2) FY2027 growth is guided lower — core revenue growth of 5-7%, with Reuters reporting a below-consensus outlook that sent shares down ~6% on Aug 7, 2026, linked to suspended Astral ventilator sales and costs. (3) Competitor setbacks flatter today’s share — Philips’ US restrictions are a large part of ResMed’s clear #1 position; if Philips returns at scale, pricing and share could tighten.

Share Count & Capital Returns

Diluted shares were ~147.0M in FY2022 and ~146.1M in FY2026 (Q4 FY26 basic: 144.7M), roughly flat: buybacks ($700M in FY2026, up from $300M in FY2025) are now offsetting stock compensation, and the pace is rising. Dividends of $350M in FY2026 give a payout of ~25% of earnings and a ~1.1% yield on a $2.64 annual rate, raised 10% in August; StockAnalysis counts 13 straight years of increases. FY2027 plans: more than $1.85B returned.

GLP-1 and sleep-apnea demand — the two sides: Bear case: obesity is a main risk factor for obstructive sleep apnea, and Zepbound (tirzepatide) is FDA-approved for moderate-to-severe OSA in adults with obesity; in Lilly’s trials a large share of patients saw major falls in apnea severity, which could shrink the treated population over many years. Bull case: management says GLP-1s and consumer wearables are bringing more people into the diagnosis funnel, and ResMed’s company-sponsored data analyses suggest GLP-1 users start and stay on CPAP at least as often as others; many patients still need a device, and masks (recurring) grew 13% in FY2026. Reading the numbers: devices +9% and masks +13% in FY2026 show no volume damage yet, but the FY2027 growth guide of 5-7% and a P/E that fell to 18.7x show investors remain cautious. This is a debate, not a verdict.
Balance sheet & competition: cash $1,469M, borrowings $659M (a $260M short-term portion is due within a year), net cash ~$810M, current ratio ~3.1. $457M of assets and $41M of liabilities were held for sale at June 30 (MatrixCare), which is now closed. Direct competitors: Philips Respironics (restricted in the US for new sleep devices since 2024, per public reporting), Fisher & Paykel Healthcare (masks and humidifiers) and smaller device makers (React Health, BMC, Löwenstein). ResMed does not publish market share; I treat it as a clear #1 in CPAP/flow generators on sell-side and press estimates (flagged as an estimate).

What's Working

A clear leader in a recurring-revenue medical niche: a 33% GAAP operating margin, gross margin up 5.3pp in three years, $1.65B of free cash flow, net cash, and low capex. Masks and accessories (+13%) give a recurring base behind the devices. Competitor setbacks have supported share, awareness of sleep health keeps widening the patient funnel, and the capital return programme is growing fast: a 10% dividend raise and more than $1.85B planned for FY2027. The shares trade at a lower multiple than their own five-year average.

What to Watch

The GLP-1 debate, the sustainability of share gains if Philips returns, and slowing growth: FY2027 is guided at 5-7% core revenue growth, with Astral ventilator sales suspended. Q4 gross margin showed how field-safety charges can hit results. Insider ownership is almost nil. Several inputs are third-party or estimated: ROIC/WACC, ownership, consensus, market share and the sleep-apnea share of revenue (~75-85% is my estimate). The Sept-2026 quarter (Oct 29) will be the first read on FY2027.
