Reckitt Benckiser is a genuinely profitable, cash-generative consumer-staples business dressed in a growth-framework checklist that isn't built for mature FMCG. Operating margin has been stable-to-improving for five years (24.11% in FY2025), gross margin actually widened (+2.93pp versus three years prior), ROIC of 19.70% comfortably clears an assumed 7.5% WACC and this framework's 15% bar, leverage has come down to a genuinely low 1.75x net debt/EBITDA, and the entry multiple looks cheap against Reckitt's own history — a trailing P/E of 11.53x versus a FY2022-25 average of roughly 19.3x. But the top-line growth criteria simply don't fit a company growing low-single-digits: 5-year revenue CAGR is just +1.79% (FY2021-2025), EBIT CAGR +5.16%, FCF CAGR +7.39%, all well short of the framework's 15% bars, and free cash flow conversion (51% of EBIT in FY2025) lags the 80% bar too, partly on a genuine step-up in capex. There's also no meaningful insider or founder ownership — management holds under 1% of shares, and the largest shareholder is BlackRock's index funds at roughly 8.8% — so this fails the alignment bar cleanly. And it would be dishonest to leave out the overhang: Reckitt's Mead Johnson/Enfamil infant-formula unit is a defendant in the US NEC ("necrotizing enterocolitis") baby-formula multidistrict litigation, with mixed bellwether verdicts through 2026 (Mead Johnson won a federal bellwether in July 2026, then lost an Illinois state case with a $60M compensatory award the same month) and no global settlement in sight. Net: a solid, cash-generative operator trading at a reasonable multiple, with real balance-sheet health, but low structural growth and a live legal risk this checklist is right to flag — 8 of 14 criteria met, Martinero Index 67/100. This isn't a call to avoid or buy Reckitt — just what the checklist says today.

Trailing & Forward P/E — FY21-25 & Current
Current trailing P/E of 11.53x sits well below the FY22-25 average of roughly 19.30x (FY2021 trailing P/E is not meaningful — net income was slightly negative that year). Forward P/E (14.52x-16.46x FY23-25, now 14.78x) has stayed in a tighter band than trailing P/E — see explainer below.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed. For Reckitt specifically: trailing P/E (11.53x) looks cheap against its own recent history, but the more useful lens here is the PEG ratio — forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether a "cheap" multiple is actually cheap once growth is priced in. Reckitt's current PEG is elevated at roughly 10.8x, well above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, because consensus has next-twelve-months EPS growth running low single digits (analysts currently model FY2026 revenue actually declining versus FY2025, largely a portfolio-divestment effect, not a demand problem in the underlying like-for-like business). The honest read: Reckitt looks statistically cheap on trailing earnings, but not obviously cheap once you weigh in how little near-term earnings growth the market is pricing in.
Balance sheet: net debt of £6,478M against EBITDA gives Net Debt/EBITDA of 1.75x (FY2025) — comfortably under this framework's <2x bar, and down from 2.72x (FY2021), 2.19x (FY2022), 2.01x (FY2023) and 2.12x (FY2024). The FY2025 improvement was helped by roughly £1.8B of divestment proceeds (including the Russian hygiene-business exit). Note the trailing-twelve-month ratio has since drifted back up to 2.61x as revenue softened and buybacks/dividends consumed cash — a trend worth watching, even though the last full fiscal year clears the bar. ROIC of 19.70% (FY2025, 16.93% current) clears both the assumed 7.5% WACC and the framework's 15% absolute bar. Share count has been roughly flat-to-declining on balance: minor dilution in FY2022-23 (buyback yield -0.52% and -0.11%) gave way to real buybacks in FY2024 (+2.30%) and FY2025 (+2.94%), funded partly by divestment cash.

What's Working

Operating margin is stable and above 20% every year (24.11% FY2025), gross margin has expanded +2.93pp over three years, ROIC of 19.70% comfortably clears both WACC and the framework's bar, leverage is genuinely low (1.75x net debt/EBITDA FY2025), the entry multiple is meaningfully below Reckitt's own recent history, and share count has turned to real buybacks in the last two fiscal years, funded in part by divestment proceeds.

What to Watch

Top-line growth is genuinely low (five-year revenue CAGR of just 1.79%), FCF conversion slipped to 51% of EBIT on a capex step-up, there is no meaningful insider or founder ownership to align management with shareholders, and the Mead Johnson/Enfamil NEC baby-formula litigation remains unresolved with mixed bellwether verdicts through 2026 and no global settlement — a real, unquantified tail risk sitting alongside an otherwise solid balance sheet.
