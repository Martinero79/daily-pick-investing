Tesla's scorecard reads very differently depending on which lens you use. Revenue has actually compounded at a healthy 15.2% a year since FY2021, and the company still commands a dominant ≈50-59% share of the US EV market. But nearly everything downstream of the top line has weakened: operating margin has fallen every year since its FY2022 peak, EBIT has shrunk on net over the period, ROIC now sits well below Tesla's cost of capital, and share count has crept up roughly 11% — largely tied to 2025's CEO pay-package option exercises rather than open-market fundraising. Meanwhile the stock trades at close to twice its own 5-year average P/E, pricing in a great deal of optimism about robotaxi, Optimus, and AI that hasn't yet shown up in the reported numbers.

Q2 2026 Headlines

Revenue $28.2B, a quarterly record, but operating income fell to just $0.4B (a 1.4% margin) as operating expenses jumped 47% year-over-year to $4.35B. Management attributed the jump to AI/Optimus/robotaxi investment plus stock-based compensation tied to Elon Musk's 2025 CEO pay package. Deliveries and revenue both grew, but profitability fell sharply — a genuine growth-versus-profitability tension playing out in real time.

Quarterly Revenue ($B)
Revenue has been choppy quarter to quarter, with Q3 and Q4 typically stronger on delivery push, but has grown modestly on a year-over-year basis through Q2 2026.
Quarterly Operating Income ($B) & Margin
The Q2 2026 drop is the standout data point here — margin fell by more than half sequentially even as revenue hit a new high, underscoring how much Tesla is currently spending ahead of any AI/robotaxi payoff.
The growth-vs-profit tension: Tesla is simultaneously defending its core auto margins against Chinese price competition, ramping FSD/robotaxi and Optimus spending, and guiding FY2026 capex above $20B — more than double FY2025's level. None of this is hidden or undisclosed; it's a deliberate strategic bet on the next phase of the business. But it means today's profitability numbers understate the spend, not the payoff, and the payoff itself remains unproven at scale.

What's Working

Revenue has genuinely compounded at over 15% a year for four years, and Tesla remains the dominant EV brand in its home US market by a wide margin, even as the broader EV category cooled. The balance sheet is a clean net-cash position, Musk's founder stake remains large and aligned, and capex — for now — is still modest as a share of revenue. FCF has also held up reasonably well through the margin decline.

What to Watch

The core story here is margin: operating margin has fallen every year since its FY2022 peak, EBIT has shrunk over the period, and ROIC now sits meaningfully below Tesla's cost of capital. Globally, Tesla is in a genuine, fragmented fight with BYD and a wave of fast-growing Chinese EV makers, not a monopoly. And the stock trades at roughly double its own 5-year average multiple, pricing in robotaxi, Optimus and AI outcomes that remain unproven at commercial scale — a real gap between today's numbers and today's valuation.
