Novartis is, by a clear margin, the strongest scorecard in this GRANOLAS batch. Since completing the Sandoz generics spin-off in 2023, the remaining innovative-medicines business has re-accelerated hard: revenue is up 9.57% in FY2025 alone, operating margin has expanded to 33.5% (versus 21-25% pre-spinoff), and ROIC at 24.47% clears an estimated cost of capital by a wide margin. Leverage is low, free cash flow conversion is strong, and the share count has declined a genuine ≈27% since FY2021 on sustained buybacks funded partly by two large one-off cash events: the 2021 sale of Novartis's long-held Roche stake back to Roche (≈$20B pretax gain, which inflated that year's net income to $24.0B and briefly pushed the P/E down to an artificially low 8.2x), and the 2023 Sandoz spin-off, which added a further discontinued-operations gain to that year's net income. Both are real, disclosed, one-off transactions rather than accounting artifacts, and both are flagged wherever they distort a chart or average. The main offsetting risk: Entresto, still one of the three largest products, lost patent exclusivity and saw sales fall roughly 50% year-over-year in Q2 2026 — a real headwind the newer growth drivers (Kisqali, Cosentyx, Pluvicto, Kesimpta, Leqvio) are so far offsetting, but not without a genuine transition risk.

Q2 2026 Headlines

Reported net sales were $14.95B (GAAP), with core (non-GAAP) net sales of $14.4B, +1% at constant currencies — a genuine return to growth after a soft Q1. Growth drivers (ex-Entresto) collectively grew 36% at constant currencies: Kisqali (CDK4/6 inhibitor for breast cancer) was the single largest contributor, up 43% CC to $1.7B; Cosentyx grew 12%, beating expectations; Pluvicto (radioligand therapy) and Kesimpta also contributed solidly. The offsetting headwind is real and significant: Entresto sales fell roughly 50% year-over-year to $1.18B as US patent protection lapsed and generic competition arrived. Core operating income was roughly flat at $5.9B (41.2% core margin). Full-year guidance was reaffirmed: net sales to grow low-single-digits, core operating income to decline low-single-digits, reflecting the Entresto transition being actively managed rather than a surprise.

Quarterly Revenue (Q2'25–Q2'26)
Revenue dipped through the back half of FY2025 and Q1 2026 as Entresto's patent cliff hit, before returning to growth in Q2 2026 as the newer growth drivers scaled — a real, live transition playing out in the numbers.
Quarterly Operating Income & Margin
Margin bottomed at 27.8% in Q4 2025 alongside the revenue dip, then recovered to a fresh high of 34.6% in Q2 2026 — profitability has held up notably well through the Entresto transition.

What's Working

Since the Sandoz spin-off, Novartis has re-accelerated on nearly every axis this framework tracks: operating margin above 30% and rising, ROIC nearly 25% against a modest cost of capital, low leverage, strong FCF conversion, and a real ≈27% reduction in share count. Growth drivers (Kisqali, Cosentyx, Pluvicto, Kesimpta, Leqvio) collectively grew 36% at constant currencies in Q2 2026, more than offsetting the real Entresto patent-cliff headwind. Capex intensity is genuinely low, as expected for an R&D-driven pharma business, and this is the only GRANOLAS scorecard so far to clear the operating margin bar in every year shown.

What to Watch

Two real, disclosed one-off gains (the 2021 Roche-stake sale and the 2023 Sandoz spin-off) complicate a clean read of net income and P/E across this window — both are flagged and the more extreme one excluded from the valuation average, but they're worth remembering when comparing Novartis's headline numbers to other companies on this site. Entresto's roughly 50% sales decline in Q2 2026 is a real, ongoing patent-cliff transition, not yet fully resolved. The current 23.52x P/E sits about 22% above Novartis's own FY22-25 average, a real premium for the post-spinoff quality re-rating rather than a value entry point. Global branded pharma remains a genuinely fragmented, well-competed market, and Novartis lacks a single dominant founder or controlling shareholder to provide long-term alignment.
