ICBC is the largest bank in China and the world by total assets (RMB 53.5tn at FY25, RMB 57.1tn at June 2026) and the most profitable Chinese bank (RMB 368.6bn net profit attributable to shareholders in FY25). It is majority state-owned (Central Huijin and the Ministry of Finance together hold ~66%) and sits at the center of China's policy-directed lending, which explains both its stability and its limits. The honest case for including it on a growth-oriented scorecard rests on scale, capital strength and income stability, not growth: operating income fell from RMB 860.9bn (FY21) to RMB 801.4bn (FY25) as net interest margin compressed from 2.11% to 1.28%, net profit compounded at just ~1.4%/yr, ROE slid from 12.15% to 9.45% and the cost-to-income ratio crept up from 26.4% to 29.5%. The bank is, however, very well capitalized (CET1 13.21% in June 2026), has a low NPL ratio (1.29%), and H1 2026 showed the first clear income rebound in years (operating income +9.1%, net profit +3.3%, NIM +1bp vs FY25). The catch: the H-share has re-rated from ~2.7x to ~6.4x earnings and from ~0.3x to ~0.6x book in five years, so today's price embeds much of the recovery, and the payout ratio is only ~31%.
Framing note — why this scorecard looks different for a bank: ICBC is the first Chinese bank on this site and follows JPMorgan and Citigroup as a large bank, so the same bank substitutions apply, all flagged inline: Operating income (total net operating income, IFRS, RMB) stands in for revenue (criterion 1); Net profit attributable to equity holders of the parent substitutes for both EBIT (criterion 2) and Free Cash Flow (criterion 4) — bank cash flow is dominated by deposit and interbank swings, so the two are correlated by construction; ROE vs. an assumed ~9.5% cost of equity (midpoint of 9–10%, justified by a low-beta, state-backed franchise; tangible equity is not reported so ROE stands in for ROTCE) substitutes for ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7); CET1 ratio vs. regulatory requirement substitutes for net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8); (dividends + buybacks) ÷ net profit substitutes for FCF conversion (criterion 9); the cost-to-income ratio replaces gross margin (criterion 14, lower = better, bands inverted). Reporting currency is RMB; the share price is the H-share in HKD, and valuation ratios convert EPS and book value per share to HKD at year-end FX (approximate). Nothing else is converted. State ownership (criterion 12) and market structure (criterion 6) are treated as described on page 5.

ROE Trend — bank analog to ROIC
Return on weighted-average equity as reported, vs. an assumed ~9.5% cost of equity (midpoint of 9–10%). ROE has fallen every year and FY2025 (9.45%) is now fractionally below that hurdle; this is the single biggest weakness in the profile.
Market position, honestly stated: ICBC is the largest bank in China and in the world by total assets (RMB 53.5tn at FY25) and the largest by profit among Chinese banks, a genuine #1, so criterion #5 scores Met. But the structure behind that rank matters: the six biggest Chinese banks are all state-controlled, prices (loan prime rate, deposit-rate guidance, mortgage-rate and fee concessions) are heavily shaped by policy, and ICBC is expected to support national priorities (e.g. inclusive and green lending). That makes China's banking market an oligopoly at the top but with thousands of smaller institutions, joint-stock and city commercial banks at the margins, so concentration is scored 3/5 (Not Met) — not a monopoly and not purely commercial competition. G-SIB status brings higher loss-absorbency requirements, partly offsetting the capital strength shown below.

Reading the valuation: At ~6.4x trailing earnings and ~0.60x book value, the ICBC H-share trades well above its own FY2021–FY2025 year-end averages (P/E ~3.7x; P/TBV ~0.37x). The ratio to own history is ~1.75 on P/E and ~1.61 on P/TBV (average ~1.68), far beyond the 1.30 line, so the criterion scores 0. This is a genuine re-rating of a stock that sat at roughly 0.3x book and 2.7–3.0x earnings for three years: the H-share is up ~140% since the 2023 year-end close and pays a ~4.7% dividend yield, but earnings have barely moved (EPS RMB 0.95 to 1.00), so almost all the return has come from multiple expansion, not growth. On a cross-market view the H-share is still cheap versus global banks (and trades below the A-share), but the “vs own history” test used here is not met. Analyst consensus (target ~HK$8.40, third-party, last revised May 2026) implies ~12% upside; that is third-party opinion, not a forecast made here.

Governance & Capital Notes

ICBC is controlled by the Chinese state: Central Huijin Investment (a sovereign-wealth-fund-style holding company) owns ~34.8% and the Ministry of Finance ~31.1% (per the Sep 30, 2025 shareholding disclosure), roughly 66% combined, with the H-share public float held mainly through HKSCC Nominees (~24%). That is a controlling stake in the framework's >30% band, but it is state, not founder or management, ownership — alignment with minority holders is not guaranteed and the bank may be asked to prioritise policy goals (rate and fee concessions, lending to priority sectors) over return on equity. Share capital has not changed since FY21 (356.4bn shares), so there has been no dilution from state recapitalisation at ICBC through FY25 (other big banks have received injections). Management ownership is negligible. Ownership percentages are from the disclosure cited in the sources and may differ at year-end 2025.

What's Working

Unmatched scale (the world's largest bank by assets) and exceptionally strong capital (CET1 13.21%, total CAR 18.57%) with improving asset quality (NPL ratio 1.42% → 1.29% over five years, provision coverage 218%). Net profit has grown every year of the window, even as net interest margin fell from 2.11% to 1.28%, and H1 2026 shows the first clear income rebound (operating income +9.1%, NIM +1bp vs. FY25). The cost base is the leanest of the large banks (cost-to-income 29.5%) and the dividend has risen every year, with a ~4.7% H-share yield.

What to Watch

Returns have been shrinking: ROE 12.15% → 9.45% is now at or below a ~9.5% cost-of-equity assumption, margin compression is only just stabilising, operating income is still below FY21, and the payout (~31%) is modest for a bank with this much capital. After the H-share re-rating (~2.7x → ~6.4x earnings), the valuation test against its own history is failed, and state control cuts both ways — policy support but also policy-directed lending, rate concessions and a controlling holder whose goals are not those of minority holders. Asset-quality risk from property and local-government debt is the main long-run swing factor. Q1 2026 operating income reconciliation is uncertain (see page 3 note).
