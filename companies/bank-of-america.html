Bank of America is the second-largest U.S. bank by assets (~$3.4tn at Q3'26) and a leader in U.S. consumer deposits, wealth management (Merrill and Bank of America Private Bank) and corporate/investment banking. In FY2025 it earned $29.1bn of net income to common on $113.1bn of net revenue, a ~14.2% return on tangible common equity. Revenue compounded at ~6.1% over FY2021–FY2025 (on a mixed basis — see note) while the net-income-to-common proxy for EBIT and FCF slipped ~1.2% per year, as FY21 earnings were lifted by pandemic-era reserve releases and rate-driven net interest income has only recently recovered. The efficiency ratio has improved every year, from 67.0% to 61.7%, and capital return has been large: FY25 buybacks of $21.4bn plus an estimated ~$8.1bn of common dividends were ~102% of net income to common, taking period-end shares down ~11% since FY21. The latest quarter (Q3 2026, reported Oct 14) showed revenue up 3.2% YoY and net income to common of $6.5bn, held down by a ~$1.2bn regulatory settlement charge. CET1 stands at 10.9%, only ~0.9pp above the author's estimated ~10.0% requirement, and the shares trade at roughly their own five-year average on both P/E and P/TBV.
Framing note — why this scorecard looks different for a bank: the standard revenue/EBIT/FCF/net-debt framework does not map cleanly onto a bank, so this page uses the same substitutions as the JPMorgan Chase dashboard, all flagged inline: Total Net Revenue stands in for revenue (criterion 1); Net Income to common substitutes for both EBIT (criterion 2) and Free Cash Flow (criterion 4) — a bank's cash flow statement is dominated by deposit and trading-book swings, so criteria 2 and 4 are necessarily correlated here, a genuine limitation of adapting the framework; ROTCE vs. an assumed ~10.5% cost of equity (mid-point of 10–11%) substitutes for ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7); CET1 ratio vs. the regulatory requirement substitutes for net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8) — points are banded on the CET1 buffer over an estimated requirement (≥3.0pp = 5, 2.0–3.0 = 4, 1.0–2.0 = 3, 0.5–1.0 = 2, 0–0.5 = 1, below = 0; Met = buffer ≥1.0pp), and the requirement itself (4.5% + stress capital buffer + G-SIB surcharge) is the author's estimate, not a quoted figure; (common dividends + buybacks) ÷ net income to common substitutes for FCF conversion (criterion 9); and entry multiple (criterion 10) is the average of two ratios — current P/E and current P/TBV, each divided by its own FY21–FY25 average of year-end values. Criterion 14 uses the change in the efficiency ratio FY22→FY25 (a falling ratio counts as a rising margin) because banks report no gross margin. Bank of America-specific: in Q4 2025 BAC adopted an accounting change for certain tax-credit investments, applied retrospectively, which restated FY2023–FY2025 revenue and expense (originally reported FY24 revenue was ~$101.9bn vs. $105.9bn restated). FY2021–FY2022 are as originally reported, so the FY21→FY25 revenue CAGR and the FY22→FY25 efficiency comparison mix two bases. Capex intensity (criterion 13) is N/A: no capex line is available.

What's Working

The efficiency ratio improved every year (67.0% to 61.7%), the bank is the clear #2 in U.S. banking with leading consumer-deposit and wealth franchises, and capital return is large (FY25 ~102% of net income to common; period-end shares down ~11% since FY21). Tangible book value per share rose from $21.83 (FY21) to $29.14 (Q3'26). Q3'26 saw net interest income +8%, average loans +6% and Global Wealth & Investment Management revenue +13%, and the valuation sits near the bank's own five-year average.

What to Watch

Net income to common is below its FY21 level (−1.2% CAGR) and revenue growth is modest and flattered by a mixed restated/original basis. Q3'26 absorbed a ~$1.2bn regulatory settlement (AML/sanctions) and EPS fell to $0.91 (company-adjusted $1.08, non-GAAP); law-firm securities-class-action solicitations followed with no known outcome. CET1 (10.9%) has fallen from 11.6% a year earlier and the buffer over the estimated ~10.0% requirement is thin, which constrains how much more can be returned. Global Markets revenue fell 6%, the ROTCE spread over a ~10.5% cost of equity is moderate (3.7pp) and insiders own under 1%.
