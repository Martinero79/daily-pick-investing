PayPal is the company that made online wallets mainstream. It was spun out of eBay in 2015 and now runs a two-sided network: roughly 439 million consumer and merchant accounts that move about $1.9 trillion a year through PayPal and Venmo checkout, plus Braintree, a lower-margin unbranded processing business for large merchants. Revenue is mostly transaction fees (90%), with the rest from interest on customer balances, credit and other value-added services. The story of the last five years is a growth stall: revenue grew 18% in 2021 but only 4% in 2025, branded checkout lost share to Apple Pay and others, and the share price fell from $188 at the end of 2021 to $52.53. Management has since pivoted to profitable growth — cost cuts, a focus on transaction margin dollars rather than raw volume, a first-ever dividend, and heavy buybacks (about $6B a year, which cut the share count by 27% since 2021). Q2 2026 showed the early shape of that plan: revenue +4.8%, payment volume +9.7%, transaction margin dollars +1.5% and operating income -5% as costs and investments rose. The stock sits at about 10x trailing and 9.5x forward earnings, a multiple that reflects both the stalled growth and a messy summer: in July a report of a $60.50-per-share bid from Stripe and Advent lifted the shares 17% in a day, and by August 28 the consortium had reportedly walked away, dropping them 12.7%.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Takeover noise — the price since mid-July reflects press reports of bids and rumours (none confirmed by PayPal as agreed deals), so the current $52.53 is partly a bid-speculation price, not a clean read on the business. (2) Definitions — revenue includes pass-through processing costs, so operating margin and transaction margin dollars are the better profit lens than revenue growth alone; EBIT here is GAAP, and FY2023 GAAP operating income (~$5.0B) includes roughly $0.36B of one-off gains on asset sales. (3) FY2021 base — FY2021 included a pandemic e-commerce boom, so 5-year growth rates start from a high point.

Latest Quarter (Q2 2026) & the Takeover Saga
The operating story is steady-but-slow; the share price story since July is about bid reports. Q2 2026 was reported on Jul 28, 2026; Q3 2026 is due Oct 27, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was $8,682m (+4.8% YoY; +3% currency-neutral) on payment volume of $486.4B (+9.7%). Transaction margin dollars, PayPal's preferred profit lens, rose only 1.5% to $3,900m (+2.6% excluding interest on customer balances), because the transaction expense rate edged up and interest income fell. GAAP operating income was $1,427m (-5.1%) and non-GAAP operating margin contracted 248bp to 17.4% as customer support, technology and G&A costs grew faster than revenue. Net income fell 12% to $1,104m, but EPS fell only 3% to $1.25 because the share count was 10% lower. Cash was the bright spot: free cash flow was $1,775m versus $692m a year ago. Active accounts were flat at 439M (+0.3%), while transactions per account rose 3%. Management raised full-year non-GAAP EPS guidance to about $5.38 from a prior range of low-single-digit decline to slightly positive.

Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership
Valuation uses trailing P/E on GAAP diluted EPS against PayPal's own FY2021-25 year-end average. The multiple has compressed from a growth-stock 53x to about 10x.
Trailing P/E on GAAP Diluted EPS — FY-End vs. Current
Current P/E (~10.2x) is only ~38% of the FY2021-25 average of ~26.7x (S&P Global data, fiscal year-end prices). The average is inflated by FY2021's 53x and FY2022's 34x, when earnings were depressed; against the more recent FY2023-25 average of ~15.6x, the current multiple is still ~35% lower. A low multiple is a sign of low growth expectations, not automatically a bargain.

Share Count & Buybacks

PayPal has retired a lot of stock: ~1,174M basic weighted shares in FY2021 to 855.5M now (-27%), with buybacks of $4.4B (FY21), $4.5B, $5.3B, $6.4B and $6.4B (FY25), and a $6.0B trailing-12-month pace in mid-2026. A quarterly dividend of $0.14 began in 2025 (~1.1% yield). Share-based compensation (~$1.0B a year) offsets only part of this.

Balance sheet: at Jun 30, 2026 PayPal held $15.3B of cash and investments against $13.4B of debt (company definition), a small net cash position; on S&P's narrower definition (cash and short-term investments of $11.3B vs $14.2B of debt including leases) net debt is ~$3.0B, or ~0.5x EBITDA. Separately the balance sheet holds ~$39.7B of customer funds receivable offset by $41.7B owed to customers, which is not corporate cash. Debt has risen by about $2B in 2026 to date. Altman Z-score is 1.95 (S&P), which for a financial-flavoured balance sheet like this is not a reliable distress signal.

What's Working

Returns on capital are high (ROIC 22% vs WACC 9%), cash generation is strong (TTM FCF $6.6B, about 19% of revenue, a 14.7% FCF yield) and the balance sheet is close to net cash. Management has retired 27% of the share count since 2021 and still returns about $6B a year, with a dividend added on top. Operating margin is up from 13.9% in FY2022 to 18.3% and gross margin has improved for two straight years. Payment volume is growing about 10% a year, transactions per account are rising, Venmo and Braintree show momentum, and the raised FY2026 EPS guide suggests execution is on plan. At roughly 10x earnings, expectations are low, and a series of takeover reports shows that large strategic and financial buyers see value at a materially higher price ($60.50 per share was the reported bid).

What to Watch

Growth is the problem: revenue +4.3% in FY2025 and +4.8% in Q2 2026, transaction margin dollars +1.5%, and GAAP operating income has been flat for seven quarters (Q2 2026 -5%). Active accounts are flat at 439M, so growth relies on monetising existing users rather than adding new ones, and branded checkout is under pressure from Apple Pay and others. A low multiple is only a bargain if earnings stop shrinking per dollar of volume; Q3 EPS is guided down. There is no founder or insider block to align management with shareholders (0.1% insider ownership), debt has grown in 2026, and the share price now carries takeover speculation in both directions: the Aug 28 drop shows how much of the July gain was bid premium.
