Komatsu, founded in 1921 and headquartered in Tokyo, is the world's second-largest builder of construction and mining equipment, behind only Caterpillar. About 92% of revenue comes from construction, mining and utility equipment; the rest is a small captive retail-finance arm (about 2%) and industrial machinery such as large presses and excimer lasers for chipmaking (about 6%). The last five fiscal years were a strong upcycle: revenue rose from ¥2.80T (FY2022) to ¥4.13T (FY2026), and operating income from ¥324B to ¥571B, with the operating margin peaking at 16.2% in FY2025. But the momentum has stalled: FY2026 revenue grew just 0.7% and operating income fell 14% as tariffs, higher input and shipping costs and lower volumes bit, and management's own FY2027 plan calls for slightly higher sales but a further ~2% decline in operating income. The most recent quarter (Apr–Jun 2026) was a record first quarter for sales (+14.7% YoY), which lifted full-year guidance, though the operating margin still slipped to 14.5%. Two things stand out on the scorecard. First, the growth CAGRs look better than they are: a much weaker yen inflates every yen-reported number, because most Komatsu sales are made overseas. Second, the share price has re-rated sharply — up roughly 49% in a year while earnings were flat — so the trailing P/E of about 16.6x sits roughly 49% above Komatsu's own fiscal-year-end average of about 11.2x. What you get is a high-quality, low-leverage global #2 with active buybacks and clean governance (no cross-shareholdings), but no insider ownership and a valuation that has already priced in a good part of the cycle.
JPY note — read every yen figure with this in mind: all amounts on this dashboard are in Japanese yen (¥) as reported, and the fiscal year ends March 31. Q1 FY2027 headlines

Even after the upgrade, guided profit is below FY2026's, implying a margin near 12.9%.

Gross Margin Trend (FY2022–FY2026)
Gross margin expanded from 27.8% to a 32.2% peak in FY2025 on pricing and mix, then gave back 1.7pp in FY2026. Versus FY2023's 29.3% (the window used for criterion #14) it is still +1.16pp higher.
Market position, honestly stated: Komatsu is a clear #2 to Caterpillar in both construction and mining equipment; industry reports put the two together at over 30% of the global mining-equipment market. I could not verify exact global share percentages from a primary source, so criterion #5 rests on the widely reported ranking rather than a hard figure. In mining, Komatsu's large haul trucks and autonomous haulage systems are a genuine strength; in mid-size construction equipment it is contested by Volvo CE, Hitachi, Liebherr, JCB and increasingly cost-competitive Chinese makers. Customer and geographic concentration figures were not pulled for this dashboard and are flagged as unverified.
What management says about the outlook: demand is expected to soften in regions affected by Middle East instability and for mining equipment, and rising costs (tariffs, fixed costs) should weigh on profit even as selling prices improve. Q1's beat was largely a North America and pricing story; margin, not sales, is where the debate sits. Note that revenue growth in the latest quarter (+14.7%) is well above the full-year guide (+4.1%), so growth is guided to fade again through the year.

Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership
Trailing P/E used as the entry-multiple signal, measured at each fiscal year-end (March 31) versus today's price. WACC of 8.1% is S&P's estimate; beta is about 0.98.
Trailing & Forward P/E — FY22-26 & Current
Current trailing P/E of 16.61x (TTM EPS ¥422.04) sits well above the FY22-26 fiscal-year-end average of 11.15x; forward P/E of about 15.6x compares with a 11.41x average. The multiple has re-rated ~50% in a year while earnings were flat — Komatsu is no longer “cheap for a cyclical”.
Trailing P/EForward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: the current trailing P/E is today's price divided by the last twelve months of reported earnings; the fiscal-year-end series is the same ratio at each March 31. Forward P/E divides the price by analysts' expected next-twelve-month earnings and is generally the more useful lens — here it is about 15.6x (I scaled the ~16.5x reported by S&P on Sep 2 to today's lower price, so treat it as approximate). The PEG ratio is around 2.7x–3.3x, which says the market is paying a lot for modest expected earnings growth. Because the earnings base (FY2026 net income ¥376B) is actually below its FY2025 peak, part of the higher multiple is simply depressed near-term earnings rather than a pure re-rating, but even on a forward basis the premium to Komatsu's own history is about 37%. Cyclical machinery stocks are usually cheapest on peak earnings, so a rising multiple on falling earnings is a mild warning sign.
Balance sheet & approach to leverage: Consolidated net debt was ¥987.2B at FY2026 year-end (¥1,159.6B after the June 2026 quarter) against EBITDA of about ¥729B (operating income ¥570.5B plus depreciation and amortization of ¥158.6B), giving 1.35x (1.55x on trailing figures). I use the consolidated figure, which is conservative: Komatsu's captive retail-finance arm borrows to fund customer loans, so the industrial business alone would show noticeably lower leverage. Komatsu reports its own net debt-to-equity ratio of 0.26x, an equity ratio of 54.7% and ROE of 11.3% (above its 10% target); the separate industrial-only ratio was not independently verified. Interest coverage is about 10.6x.

What's Working

A firm #2 position in construction and mining equipment, with a real edge in mining trucks and autonomous haulage. Operating income has grown about 15% a year since FY2022 (even if yen-inflated), the balance sheet is sturdy (net debt/EBITDA 1.35x, equity ratio 54.7%), and the share count is falling on a ¥100B buyback alongside a ¥190 dividend. There are no cross-shareholdings, gross margin is still above its FY2023 level, and the latest quarter beat expectations (sales +14.7%, guidance raised), showing demand is holding up better than feared.

What to Watch

Margins are off their peak (16.2% to 13.8%, guided to about 12.9%) because of tariffs and cost inflation, and management's own plan shows falling operating income in FY2027. ROIC of 9.4% barely clears an 8.1% WACC, free cash flow converts only about 40% of EBIT, and the stock trades roughly 49% above its own five-year fiscal-year-end P/E average after a ~49% one-year run. Yen weakness flatters every reported growth number, and a yen rebound would reverse that tailwind. Mining demand is guided softer, and there is zero insider ownership to align management with holders.

Maarten's note

I keep Komatsu next to Caterpillar because they are the two names I would use to sanity-check anything in heavy machinery. Where Komatsu genuinely competes is mining: its haul trucks and long-running autonomous haulage systems are a real differentiator, and I like that it is not just a me-too construction-equipment maker. Caterpillar, as far as I can tell, earns higher margins and has a bigger services and aftermarket business, so on quality of earnings I still lean toward Caterpillar; Komatsu is the more exposed to the yen and to Asian and mining-cycle swings.

The interesting angle is Japan itself. Komatsu is a good example of the governance story I find appealing: it has unwound its cross-shareholdings, it buys back stock and it pays a decent dividend. The usual “Japan discount” argument would say it should trade cheaper than its US peer, and historically it did, but at about 16.6x trailing earnings it is now well above its own five-year average, so I do not see an obvious bargain. I would rather watch how margins hold up through FY2027 than chase the re-rating. Not a recommendation, just how I think about it.
