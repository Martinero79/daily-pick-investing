IBM is a hybrid-cloud and AI company founded in 1911, with about 264,000 employees and four reporting segments. Software (44% of FY2025 revenue and ~61% of segment profit) holds Red Hat (Hybrid Cloud), Automation (including HashiCorp, acquired Feb 2025), Data (including Confluent, acquired Mar 2026) and Transaction Processing (mainframe software). Consulting (31%) is a low-margin (≈12% segment margin) services business. Infrastructure (23%) is the IBM Z mainframe, distributed systems and support, and it moves in a product cycle: IBM Z jumped 67% in Q4 2025 on the new z17 generation and fell 20% in Q3 2026 against that comparison. Financing (1%) is a captive finance arm that funds client purchases, carried on its own $12.7B of debt. The investment case is a slow-growth, high-cash-generation franchise whose margin has been rebuilt by shifting mix toward software, while management spends the cash on dividends and on acquisitions (about $8.3B in 2025 and $11.4B in the first nine months of 2026) rather than buybacks. Q3 2026 (reported Oct 21) beat expectations, with Software +8%, Infrastructure +4% and Consulting flat, and free cash flow guidance for FY2026 was raised to growth of more than $1B. The shares nonetheless remain about 26% below their 52-week high because Q2 2026 growth was only +1.1% and analysts questioned how much of Q3's software strength was deals pulled forward from the prior quarter.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Kyndryl — the Nov 2021 spin-off removed a low-margin outsourcing business; FY2021 is the post-spin continuing base, so five-year growth rates start from a low-margin year. (2) M&A — HashiCorp (2025) and Confluent (2026) add revenue and goodwill; IBM does not give a clean organic bridge in the sources used here, so growth is shown as reported and in constant currency. (3) GAAP vs operating earnings — GAAP EPS includes pension charges (FY2022, FY2024) and a FY2025 tax benefit of about $1.2B; operating (non-GAAP) EPS of $11.59 in FY2025 vs GAAP $11.14 (continuing ops) shows how large the adjustments are.

What the quarter actually showed

Revenue was $17.1B, +4.7% reported and +6% in constant currency, an acceleration from Q2 2026's +1.1% (the July miss). Software revenue was $7.8B (+8.2%; Red Hat +11%, Data +19%, Transaction Processing +9%, Automation -3%) with a segment profit margin of 35.0% (32.9% a year ago). Operating pre-tax margin (non-GAAP) was 19.3% (+0.7pt), GAAP diluted EPS from continuing operations $2.31 (+26%, helped by an 11.9% tax rate vs 28.2%) and operating EPS $2.95 (+11%). Free cash flow (IBM definition) was $3.0B, +$0.7B. The shares fell as much as ~6% pre-market on Oct 22 as analysts (per Reuters) said part of the software strength came from deals delayed out of Q2, but closed that day +0.3%; they have since drifted from $257.81 (Oct 30) to $244.53.

Dividend, Buybacks & Share Count

Dividends have been paid every year since 1916 (IBM release); the quarterly dividend is $1.69 ($6.76 a year, 2.8% yield at $244.53), raised by about one cent a year (~0.6%), a 30-year run of tiny annual increases per S&P. Cash dividends were $6.3B in FY2025 and $4.8B in the first nine months of 2026, about 42% of IBM-defined FCF. There is no open-market buyback in the 2026 cash flow, only $0.8B of repurchases for tax withholding on stock awards, so the share count has crept up (~905M at end-2021 vs ~945M, +0.7% in the last year).

Net debt basis (ex-IBM Financing): Sep 30, 2026 total debt $63.8B less IBM Financing debt $12.7B (backed by ~$13.3B of financing receivables) = $51.1B; less $11.1B cash, restricted cash and marketable securities = $40.0B net debt excluding Financing. TTM adjusted EBITDA (IBM definition, adds back stock compensation and workforce rebalancing) = FY2025 $19.2B − 9M 2025 $12.7B + 9M 2026 $13.7B = ~$20.2B (rounded billions), so net debt/EBITDA is ~1.98x; at Dec 2025 it was ($61.3B − $15.1B − $14.5B) / $19.2B = 1.65x. The jump is the ~$11B Confluent purchase, whose EBITDA is only partly in the trailing figure. Excluded: operating leases (~$3.3B), pension, and the S&P definition of EBITDA ($16.6B), which gives ~2.4x. Consolidated net debt (S&P) was $56.1B.
Acquisitions, Kyndryl and pension: Confluent (data streaming) closed Mar 17, 2026 at $31 per share in cash, roughly $11B enterprise value (IBM release); HashiCorp (infrastructure automation, Terraform/Vault) closed in Feb 2025 at about $6.4B per IBM's announcement (not re-verified here); Red Hat (2019, ~$34B) remains the base of the goodwill. IBM spent $8.3B on acquisitions in 2025 and $11.4B in 9M 2026. Kyndryl (spun Nov 2021) is reported as discontinued operations; IBM initially kept a 19.9% stake, and I did not verify the timing of its later sale. Pension: prepaid pension assets were $7.7B against $8.5B of retirement-related obligations at Sep 2026 (a small net liability, ~$0.7B, vs ~$1.5B at Dec 2025); IBM recorded pension settlement charges of $3.1B in 2024, which it excludes from operating earnings.

What's Working

Mix shift is visible in the numbers: gross margin rose from 54.0% to 58.2% in three years and operating margin from 9.7% to 18.7% since FY2021, with Software at ~83% gross margin and 35% segment margin in Q3 2026. Growth has re-accelerated (+1.4% in FY2024, +7.6% in FY2025, +4.7% in Q3 2026) and IBM's own free cash flow reached $14.7B, with FY2026 guided up by more than $1B. Returns clear the cost of capital comfortably (ROIC ~14% vs WACC ~7%), capex is ~2% of revenue, and the dividend has been paid every year since 1916 at ~42% of FCF. The generative-AI book of business (>$12.5B cumulative at Q4 2025) and Red Hat's double-digit growth show demand for hybrid cloud and AI tooling, and the new mainframe generation lifted Infrastructure 12% in FY2025.

What to Watch

Top-line growth is mid-single-digit (4-5% guided) and Consulting, nearly a third of revenue, has been flat for eight quarters at a ~12% margin. Mainframe is cyclical: IBM Z fell 20% in Q3 2026 after +67% in Q4 2025. Analysts questioned whether Q3's software strength was pulled forward from Q2, and Q2's +1.1% shows how uneven quarters can be. Leverage rose to ~2.0x ex-Financing after Confluent, with $88.5B of goodwill and intangibles against $36.2B of equity, so further large deals would stretch the balance sheet and add impairment risk; there is no buyback, and shares are up ~4.5% since 2021. GAAP and non-GAAP earnings diverge sharply (acquisition amortisation ~$2.8B a year, pension and tax items), the GAAP P/E history is distorted, and the forward P/E is slightly above its own 5-year average. IBM Z comparisons, AI-driven pressure on Consulting and a consulting-visa policy change reported on Nov 10 are further uncertainties; I could not verify their financial effect.
