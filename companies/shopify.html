Q2 2026 Headlines

Revenue reached $3.58B, up 34% YoY and ahead of the $3.40B consensus estimate — an acceleration from FY2025's already-strong 30.14% full-year growth. Gross Merchandise Volume (GMV) grew 32% YoY to $115.6B, operating income reached $488M (up 67.7% YoY, a ~13.6% quarterly operating margin), and free cash flow hit $654M (up 55% YoY, an 18% FCF margin) as operating expenses fell to roughly a third of revenue from 37.7% a year earlier — genuine, broad-based operating leverage, not just top-line strength. The stock jumped roughly 17% the day results were reported.

Gross Margin Trend (FY2021-FY2025)
Gross margin has stayed in a tight 48-54% band, easing from 53.80% (FY2021) to 48.07% (FY2025) as merchant-solutions revenue (payments, capital, shipping) — a lower-margin, higher-growth mix than pure subscription software — has grown to roughly 76% of total revenue. The FY2025-vs-FY2022 delta used for criterion #14 is a modest -1.11pp, comfortably inside this framework's -2.5pp threshold.
Market position, honestly stated: Shopify is the clear #1 commercial e-commerce platform by revenue and paid-merchant count — various measurement methodologies put it anywhere from roughly 23% to 46% of the "top-tier" hosted commerce-platform market, and around 30% of the US e-commerce-platform market specifically, several multiples ahead of the next commercial competitors (BigCommerce, Wix Stores, Squarespace Commerce). WooCommerce, the free WordPress plugin, actually powers more raw storefronts by some counts (20-38% of all e-commerce sites depending on the source), but it isn't a comparable business: it has no unified subscription or take-rate revenue model, and most of those sites are small, unmonetized, or run through third-party hosts. At the large-enterprise tier, Adobe Commerce (Magento) and Salesforce Commerce Cloud remain real competitors as Shopify Plus pushes upmarket. This is why criterion #5 (market share) scores Met on Shopify's dominant position among commercial platforms, while criterion #6 (market concentration) scores Not met — the broader field, including free/open-source alternatives and adjacent website builders, is genuinely fragmented, not a tight oligopoly.

Why P/FCF instead of P/E, and reading the numbers: most dashboards on this site use trailing P/E as the entry-multiple signal, computed at each fiscal year-end close. Shopify's trailing P/E series is unusually noisy — it ranges from 59x (FY2021) to 759x (FY2023, when the Deliverr impairment crushed net income) to a currently-reported 170x (FY2025, itself pulled down by smaller investment losses) — which makes year-over-year comparisons close to meaningless. Free cash flow strips out those non-cash, non-operating swings, so P/FCF is the more honest read on how the market is pricing Shopify's actual cash generation over time. On that basis: current P/FCF (70.45x) sits meaningfully below the FY2023-25 average (≈100.4x), a real discount versus Shopify's own recent history, even though 70x is still a rich absolute multiple by most standards. Forward P/E (60.98x, based on analyst consensus next-12-month earnings) sits lower still and continues a multi-year trend of forward estimates running below trailing multiples — the market expects continued earnings and FCF growth, consistent with a PEG ratio of roughly 2.0, above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line but not unusual for a business still compounding revenue in the mid-20s to mid-30s percent.
Balance sheet: Shopify ended FY2025 with $6.82B in cash & investments against just $188M in total debt, a net cash position of $6.63B — comfortably inside this framework's net-debt/EBITDA bar (any net-cash position auto-qualifies), and net cash every year back through FY2021 ($6.62B then). ROIC (TTM) of 26.24% clears both the assumed 12.0% WACC and the framework's 15% absolute bar with real headroom. Diluted share count grew modestly from FY2021 (1,273.6M) through roughly FY2025 (≈1,309.6M, implied from reported EPS), reflecting years of stock-based-compensation-driven dilution typical of a high-growth tech company — but shares outstanding are now down 0.74% year-over-year on a trailing basis as a newly authorized $2B buyback program (announced alongside FY2025 results) has begun offsetting that dilution.

Governance note — the Founder Share, precisely stated: Tobi Lütke co-founded Shopify in 2006 and holds roughly 6.1-6.2% of shares outstanding today — above this framework's ~5% "meaningful" bar on economic ownership alone. But the more consequential structure is the non-transferable "Founder Share," approved by shareholders in 2022 alongside a 10-for-1 stock split: combined with his Class B shares, it guarantees Lütke exactly 40% of Shopify's aggregate voting power at all times, regardless of how much equity he actually owns or sells down. That's a genuine decoupling of voting control from economic stake — roughly 6-7x his ownership percentage — that governance watchdogs have criticized as an entrenchment device. This framework scores the criterion on economic ownership (which is what "skin in the game" actually measures), but the voting mechanism is a distinct and important governance fact worth understanding in its own right, not folded into the score.

What's Working

Revenue growth is both large and still accelerating (+25.8%/yr since FY2021, +34% YoY in the latest quarter), free cash flow has scaled even faster, the balance sheet has run net cash every year since the IPO, capex is negligible, and ROIC comfortably clears both WACC and this framework's 15% bar. The entry multiple (P/FCF 70.45x vs a ≈100.4x own-history average) is a real discount, share count has turned to a net decline as a new buyback program kicks in, and founder Tobi Lütke's ~6.2% economic stake clears the ownership bar.

What to Watch

Operating margin (16.42%) is still under this framework's 20% bar, even after nearly doubling in two years — whether that trend keeps closing the gap is the clearest open question. The competitive field is genuinely fragmented once free/open-source alternatives (WooCommerce) and enterprise players (Adobe Commerce, Salesforce Commerce Cloud) are counted, not the tight oligopoly a "#1 platform" label might suggest. GAAP net income remains noisy (the FY2023 Deliverr impairment, swinging investment gains/losses), which is why this dashboard leans on FCF and P/FCF rather than trailing earnings. And the Founder Share voting structure — 40% control on a ~6% economic stake — is worth watching as a governance precedent even though it isn't part of the score.
