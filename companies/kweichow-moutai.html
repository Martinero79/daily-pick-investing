Kweichow Moutai, founded in 1951 and listed in Shanghai in 2001, makes Feitian Moutai and the rest of China’s most prestigious premium baijiu (a distilled sorghum liquor). Production is slow (a roughly five-year cycle) and tied to one place, which is the scarcity story: like Hermès or Ferrari, the company sells fewer bottles than the market would take at the official price, and the gap between the ex-factory price and the street price has been its pricing power. Revenue grew from ¥106.2B in FY2021 to ¥168.8B in FY2025 (+12.3% a year) with an EBIT margin of 65-67% and a net margin near 50%. Then the cycle turned: FY2025 revenue fell 1.2% and net profit 4.5% to ¥82.3B, the first declines since the listing, as official anti-extravagance rules and weaker business and gift demand hit high-end baijiu. In 2026 management began moving sales away from wholesalers towards its own channels (the i-Moutai app and company-run stores), raised the ex-factory price of Feitian from ¥1,169 to ¥1,269 and then ¥1,369 (reported), and delivered H1 2026 revenue of ¥90.7B (+1.5%) with net profit of ¥44.5B (-2.0%); the second quarter alone was -5.1% on revenue. The shares are down more than 40% from their 2021 peak, and the price-to-earnings ratio has compressed from ~49x (end-2021) to ~19x, which is why this page scores the entry multiple well while also flagging that earnings are no longer growing. The state (Guizhou SASAC) is the controlling shareholder via Moutai Group (~54%).
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Policy and consumption are the swing factors — baijiu is taxed with a consumption tax, official banquet and gifting restrictions have repeatedly hit demand, and the 2026 channel reform is explicitly aimed at curbing speculation, not at growth. (2) The controlling holder is the state — Guizhou SASAC, via China Kweichow Moutai Distillery (Group), holds ~54%; that aligns the company with a stable dividend (payout ~79% for FY2025) but its objectives can differ from minority holders’. (3) Wholesale vs ex-factory price — Feitian has traded at roughly ¥1,600-1,720 on third-party wholesale quotes in 2026 against a ¥1,269-1,369 ex-factory price; if that premium narrows further, the logic of the scarcity model weakens (a comparable model to Hermès and Ferrari on this site, but with far less evidence of demand growth right now).

Quarterly Trend (Q3 2024 – Q2 2026)
Eight quarters. Revenue growth fell from +15% YoY to roughly zero by Q3 2025, dropped 19% in Q4 2025, recovered to +6.5% in Q1 2026 and turned negative again (-5.1%) in Q2 2026. Moutai’s sales are seasonal (Q1 and Q4 are the strongest), so compare YoY, not quarter to quarter.
What the half-year actually showed

H1 2026 revenue was about ¥90.7B (+1.5% YoY) and net profit attributable to shareholders ¥44.5B (-2.0%); basic EPS ¥35.57 (-1.7%). The weakness was in Q2: revenue ¥36.8B (-5.1%) and net profit ¥17.3B (about -6.9% on the data provider’s figures) missed expectations according to broker commentary. Gross margin was 89.3% in Q2 versus 90.4% a year earlier. Chairman Chen Hua described the half as stable and said the company began market-based reforms in 2026 to counter weak demand; he also acknowledged that the average unit price and profit per tonne of wine declined year on year. Operating cash flow jumped to ¥70.7B in H1, but the company attributes this mainly to higher deposits from group members and lower interbank deposits at its finance arm, so it is not a sign of stronger earnings. Direct sales (including i-Moutai) were reported by broker commentary to be roughly half of Q2 revenue. Management gave no formal full-year earnings guidance.

Pricing & channel (2026): the company moved non-standard distribution into self-operated channels and opened regular Feitian sales to consumers on the i-Moutai app at ¥1,499 (reported). Trailing P/E — FY-End vs. Current
Current P/E (~19.3x) is ~62% of Moutai’s FY2021-25 year-end average (31.2x). The multiple has fallen every year while earnings rose until FY2024: EPS is up ~57% since end-2021 but the share price is down ~39% (from ¥2,050 to ¥1,256), so the entire de-rating is the market pricing in lower future growth. A low multiple on falling earnings is not necessarily cheap.

What's Working

A 65%+ EBIT margin, ~49% net margin, ROIC far above any plausible cost of capital, almost no debt and capex of under 2% of revenue: few consumer companies earn this much on so little capital. Moutai is the clear leader in premium baijiu, still raised its dividend (¥51.95, ~4.1% yield, ~79% payout), has started buying back stock, and the multiple has fallen to ~19x trailing from ~49x at end-2021, below its own average. Ex-factory price increases in 2026 and a larger direct-sales share give it more control over pricing than in the wholesaler era.

What to Watch

Growth has stopped: FY2025 revenue and profit fell for the first time since the listing, H1 2026 profit fell again and Q2 revenue was -5.1%. Policy risk is structural (anti-extravagance and official-banquet rules, consumption tax) and the consumer backdrop is weak. The channel reform lowers the wholesaler’s role and the street-price premium may narrow, which is the core of the scarcity model. The state is the controlling holder, and state-backed funds were reported to be reducing exposure. FCF and cash conversion are noisy because of the in-house finance company. ROIC/WACC, the P/E history and the analyst target are third-party estimates, and Q3 results are due around Oct 31.
