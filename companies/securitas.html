Securitas AB is a Stockholm-headquartered, Nasdaq Stockholm-listed security services group founded in 1934, and one of the two or three genuinely global players in manned guarding — on-site officers, mobile patrols, aviation security, remote video monitoring, and increasingly "intelligence-led" risk services, delivered through three reporting segments (Securitas North America, Securitas Europe, Securitas Ibero-America) plus a growing Technology and Corporate Risk Management layer on top. This is a fundamentally different economic model from Verisure, today's daily pick: Securitas sells labor by the hour, on contracts that get re-bid and re-priced constantly, with roughly 322,000 employees on payroll; Verisure sells a recurring subscription to monitor hardware it has already installed in a customer's home. The two companies share real history — Verisure was literally Securitas's own alarm-monitoring division until it was spun off as "Securitas Direct" in 2006 and later sold on to private equity (EQT, then Bain Capital and Hellman & Friedman by 2011) — but they have diverged completely since. What Securitas has been doing well: in 2022 it acquired STANLEY Security (Convergent Security Solutions) from Stanley Black & Decker for roughly $3.2bn, its largest deal ever, deliberately building out a North American electronic-security and technology installed base to shift the revenue mix toward higher-margin, more defensible services. That's a real, structural margin lever — operating margin has climbed from 4.68% (FY2021) to 6.91% (FY2025), and EBIT has compounded at +20.7% a year over the same stretch, comfortably clearing this framework's growth bar even as reported revenue actually shrank in FY2025 (down 4.2%, mostly from shedding low-margin contracts on purpose). The acquisition was funded partly by a heavily dilutive rights issue completed in 2023, which is the main reason the share-count criterion fails outright on this scorecard. Net debt/EBITDA has come down from a roughly 4.5x peak in FY2022 to 2.1x today (S&P upgraded Securitas to BBB+ in August 2026 on the back of it), just above this framework's 2x line. The core tension for an investor: this is a genuinely well-run #2 player in a durable, if fragmented and low-margin, global industry — but no amount of "Technology-led" repositioning is likely to get manned guarding anywhere near a 20% EBIT margin, because the cost base is fundamentally labor, not software or installed hardware.
Maarten's note

Built this one as a direct pair with today's Verisure pick, since the historical link is too good to skip: Verisure literally used to be Securitas's own alarm-monitoring division, spun out as "Securitas Direct" in 2006 and eventually sold on to private equity (EQT, then Bain Capital and Hellman & Friedman) by 2011. Same DNA at the root, completely different business today. Verisure kept the parts of the security industry I actually find attractive as an investor — recurring subscriber revenue, high gross margins, and capex that mostly happens once per customer at install rather than every year. Securitas kept the parts that don't fit that mold: hundreds of thousands of guards on payroll, contracts that get re-bid constantly, and margins that top out in the high single digits no matter how much "Technology" and "intelligence-led" language gets layered on top. The EBIT CAGR here (+20.7%) genuinely clears my growth bar, and the capex story is real and clean (1.4-1.7% of revenue every year) — this isn't a story I'm inventing, Securitas has actually gotten better at converting revenue into profit. But the ceiling is just structurally lower than Verisure's: a 20%+ EBIT margin isn't realistically in reach for a guarding-led business, because the cost base is people, not installed hardware or software. Worth keeping both dashboards side by side as a reminder that "security industry" isn't one business model, it's at least two very different ones, and who owns the customer relationship and pricing power matters more than the industry label on the tin.

The Stanley Security acquisition and the Technology mix shift

In 2022 Securitas acquired STANLEY Security (Convergent Security Solutions) from Stanley Black & Decker for roughly $3.2bn, its largest deal ever, to build a genuine North American electronic-security and technology installed base rather than relying purely on manned guarding. The deal was funded partly by a heavily dilutive rights issue completed in early 2023 — shares outstanding jumped from roughly 468 million to around 573 million in the space of a year, the single biggest driver of this dashboard's failed share-count criterion. In exchange, Securitas got a real structural margin lever: EBIT margin has climbed from 4.68% (FY2021) to 6.91% (FY2025) as Technology and Corporate Risk Management revenue, priced more like electronic-security/software than guarding labor, has grown as a share of the total mix.

Q2 2026 and the new Investor Day targets: adjusted organic sales growth was 3% in Q2 2026 (headline organic growth flat at 0%, masked by the ongoing pruning of low-margin contracts), operating margin improved to 7.6%, and EPS grew 7% — but aviation and Pinkerton (investigations/executive-protection unit) contract losses weighed on both Europe and North America margins in the quarter. At its June 2026 Investor Day, management set new medium-term targets: roughly 10% average annual EPS growth, higher cash-flow conversion, and continued "disciplined" bolt-on M&A rather than another transformational deal on the scale of Stanley Security.

Share Count Trend

Shares outstanding rose from an EPS-implied ~365m (FY2021) to 572.92m currently, up roughly +57% — almost entirely from the dilutive rights issue completed in 2023 that helped fund the $3.2bn Stanley Security acquisition. The count has been essentially flat since the raise (573.4m per the March 2025 shareholder disclosure vs 572.92m now).

Ownership note: Securitas trades a dual-class share structure (Series A, 10 votes; Series B, 1 vote). Investment AB Latour, the Gustaf Douglas family's holding company, is the largest shareholder at 10.9% of capital but 29.6% of votes — a genuine long-standing cornerstone stake, though a family/institutional holding-company position rather than a founder-operator stake (Securitas was founded in 1934 and has been professionally managed for decades). Melker Schörling AB, another long-tenured Swedish holding company, adds a further 4.5% capital / 10.5% votes. Together with pension funds AMF and Alecta, this is a genuinely concentrated, stable ownership base by the standards of a large-cap industrial services company.

What's Working

A genuine #2 global market position in a fragmented industry, EBIT growth that clears this framework's bar (+20.7% CAGR) on real margin-mix improvement rather than one-off items, structurally low and stable capex (1.4%-1.7% of revenue, the clearest contrast with Verisure's heavier per-subscriber hardware model), gross margin that's actually widened over the trailing three years, a positive ROIC/WACC spread, and a long-standing concentrated dual-class ownership base (Investment AB Latour, Melker Schörling AB) with real voting-control skin in the game. S&P's August 2026 upgrade to BBB+ on improving leverage is a genuine credit-quality signal, not marketing.

What to Watch

The EBIT-margin ceiling here is real and structural — even after years of Technology-led mix shift, Securitas is nowhere near this framework's 20% bar, and probably never will be, because manned guarding is inherently priced on labor. Net debt/EBITDA (2.1x) sits just above the 2x line, FCF conversion (67%-71%) is still short of 80%, and the 2022-23 rights issue diluted shareholders by more than half over the trailing five years — a real cost of the Stanley Security bet that per-share earnings growth hasn't fully offset yet. Aviation and Pinkerton contract losses flagged in Q2 2026 are a reminder that guarding and investigations contracts can be lost on a re-bid as easily as they're won, a structurally different risk profile than Verisure's stickier subscriber base.
