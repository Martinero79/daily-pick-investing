BP is a cyclical, low-margin integrated major in the middle of a strategy reset. After the 2020-2024 push toward renewables, the February 2025 reset pivoted spending back to oil and gas, cut low-carbon capex and launched a disposal programme (including the sale of a 65% stake in Castrol to Stonepeak for $10B and a planned exit from the UK North Sea). In February 2026 the company suspended its share buyback entirely to prioritise balance-sheet repair, withdrew its earlier 30-40%-of-operating-cash-flow shareholder-return guidance, and kept the dividend policy of roughly 4% per-share growth a year; Meg O’Neill took over as CEO in April 2026. The five-year record is uneven: reported revenue has grown only 4.7% a year, operating income 3.4% a year, free cash flow has slipped, and statutory net income was near zero in FY2024 and FY2025 after large impairments. The share count, however, has fallen about 24% since FY2021 through buybacks. Brent near $114 in late October 2026 (Iran-related tensions) has lifted the latest quarters sharply — Q2 2026 operating income was $9.1B — but that is a commodity-cycle tailwind, not a new baseline, and these results come from a low-margin base.

Forward P/E rose from a 4.8x low in FY2022 (peak earnings, so the average is flattered low) to 11.8x at end-FY2025 as earnings estimates fell, and sits at ≈7.3x now — roughly in line with the FY21-25 average of 7.51x. Cyclical-earnings caveat: a low P/E on peak earnings is not the same as cheap.
Reading the P/E numbers: trailing P/E divides price by the last 12 months of reported earnings (≈23.6x now, distorted by impairments). Forward P/E divides by consensus next-12-month estimates (≈7.3x now) and moves a lot for an oil major because analysts re-mark oil prices. What's Working

Leverage is moderate (net debt/EBITDA about 0.9x on the latest TTM) and falling, the share count is down about 24% in five years, forward P/E near 7x is in line with its own history, and the dividend has been protected and still grows about 4% a year. Q1-Q2 2026 operating income of $8-9B a quarter shows how much earnings power the portfolio has at current prices, and the reset (disposals, simpler focus on oil and gas, new CEO) is aimed at improving returns.

What to Watch

Growth and returns are weak: revenue, EBIT and FCF CAGRs are low or negative, operating margin was 7.7% in FY2025, ROIC is roughly equal to the cost of capital, and gross margin has slipped 1.7pp in three years. The latest quarters ride a Brent price near $114 (Iran-related tensions) that can reverse quickly. Buybacks are suspended, the strategy has been reset twice in two years, statutory profit has been near zero, and legacy liabilities (Deepwater Horizon) remain. Q3 2026 results on Oct 30 will update all of this. Market share ranks last among the Western supermajors.
