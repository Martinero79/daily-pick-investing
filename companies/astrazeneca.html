AstraZeneca posts the strongest scorecard of any GRANOLAS name in this batch (10/14 criteria met, Martinero Index 78/100), but getting there required untangling a real distortion: the 2021 Alexion Pharmaceuticals acquisition ($39bn, closed July 2021) loaded that year's income statement with one-off inventory fair-value step-up costs, intangible amortization, and integration charges, crushing operating margin to 7.74% and net income to just $112M despite revenue growing 40.6%. Because this makes both the FY2021 EBIT/FCF base and the FY2021 P/E (a nonsensical 1625x) statistically meaningless, this dashboard computes EBIT and FCF CAGR from the cleaner FY2022 base year (explicitly flagged below) and excludes FY2021 from the P/E average, mirroring the same honest-disclosure approach used for Novartis's 2021 Roche-stake gain earlier in this batch. On that basis: operating income has compounded at roughly +17.5% annually since 2022, operating margin has expanded to 24.3%, ROIC sits at a strong 17.0% clearing an estimated cost of capital comfortably, leverage has come down to a moderate 1.24x net debt/EBITDA, and the stock trades at 24.44x earnings versus a 39.0x FY22-25 average — a meaningful discount. The clearest weak spot: insiders own less than 1% with no founder or controlling family, and global pharma remains a genuinely crowded, competitive field.

H1 & Q2 2026 Headlines

Total Revenue for H1 2026 was $30.67B, up 9% as reported (+6% at constant exchange rates), with double-digit growth in Oncology and Rare Disease offsetting headwinds from Farxiga's US loss of exclusivity and China's volume-based procurement pricing pressure. Core EPS rose 12% in H1 to $5.21. In Q2 2026 specifically, Total Revenue was $15.38B (+6% reported, +5% CER), Core Operating Margin expanded 2 percentage points to 34%, and Core EPS grew 21% to $2.63 — even as Reported EPS ($1.61) grew just 2% due to higher intangible amortization and one-off items excluded from the Core measure. Management reconfirmed FY2026 guidance for mid-to-high-single-digit Total Revenue growth and low-double-digit Core EPS growth, and remains on track for its stated ambition of $80B in Total Revenue by 2030. The period also saw a disappointing Phase III miss (CARDIO-TTRansform, for Wainua in ATTR-CM) alongside six positive Phase III readouts and eight new approvals in major markets.

What's Working

The strongest all-around fundamentals in this GRANOLAS batch: operating margin has expanded from a distorted 7.74% in FY2021 to a clean 24.33% in FY2025, ROIC of 17.0% comfortably clears an estimated cost of capital, leverage has come down to a moderate 1.24x net debt/EBITDA from 4.02x post-Alexion, and FCF conversion clears 80%. At 24.44x earnings versus a 39.0x recent average, this is also the biggest valuation discount found anywhere in this batch, with revenue growing every single quarter shown and management reiterating its $80bn-by-2030 ambition.

What to Watch

FY2021's numbers are genuinely distorted by Alexion-related one-off charges, requiring a base-year adjustment to compute honest CAGRs — worth remembering when comparing this scorecard to others in the batch. Revenue and FCF growth, while solid, fall short of this framework's 15% bar. There is no founder or controlling shareholder, and insider ownership is negligible. China revenue declined 13% in H1 2026 on generic competition and volume-based procurement pricing pressure, and the July 2026 CARDIO-TTRansform Phase III miss (Wainua in ATTR-CM) is a reminder that pipeline setbacks are a structural risk in this industry, even for a company executing as well as AstraZeneca currently is.
