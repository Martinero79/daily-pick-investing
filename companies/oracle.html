Forward P/E (25.91x–29.99x FY25-26, now 18.09x) has consistently run below trailing P/E, and the current gap is the widest of the window — see explainer below.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY22-26 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed, since it accounts for expected growth (or decline) that trailing P/E can't see. For Oracle specifically: trailing P/E (25.0x) looks roughly in line with its own recent history, but forward P/E (18.1x) is meaningfully cheaper — the market already expects the AI-capex-driven earnings growth that's baked into consensus estimates, which is exactly the kind of gap forward P/E is designed to surface. One more lens worth a glance: the PEG ratio, forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether that "cheap" multiple is actually cheap once growth is accounted for. Oracle's PEG currently runs around 0.6 — comfortably under the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, suggesting the stock isn't just cheaper than its own history, it's cheap relative to how fast earnings are expected to grow.

ROIC has fallen from 20.84% (FY2022) to 13.16% (FY2026, 11.48% current) as the capital base has grown faster than the returns it generates — it still clears the assumed 9.5% WACC, but no longer clears this framework's 15% absolute bar. What's Working

Revenue growth is real and accelerating (+17.35% FY2026, OCI +93% YoY in the latest quarter alone), operating margin is genuinely improving (33.23%, best in four years), the entry multiple is meaningfully below Oracle's own recent history after the stock's pullback, buybacks have reduced share count in 4 of the last 5 years, and Larry Ellison's ~40% ownership is about as aligned as insider ownership gets on this site.

What to Watch

The AI-cloud buildout has consumed all of free cash flow and then some: capex more than doubled in FY2026 to 82.6% of revenue, turning FCF sharply negative and pushing net debt/EBITDA to 4.45x. ROIC is trending down as the capital base outgrows returns, and OCI — the growth engine absorbing all that spending — remains a distant #4-5 in cloud infrastructure at just ~3% share. Whether the OCI bet pays off before the balance sheet strain becomes a real problem is the central question for Oracle from here.
