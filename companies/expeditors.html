Expeditors International of Washington is a Seattle-area global logistics company founded in 1979. It sells airfreight forwarding, ocean freight and ocean services, and customs brokerage plus a set of other services (warehousing and distribution, order management, cross-border ground transportation) through 171 district offices worldwide and about 19,800 employees. The model is asset-light: it owns no ships or aircraft, buys space from carriers and resells it, and is unusually decentralised, with district managers paid largely on local profit. That gives very low capex (about 0.5% of revenue), high cash conversion and a long record of dividend growth (31 consecutive years) and buybacks, which have shrunk the share count by roughly 24% since FY2021. The trade-off is that the top line follows freight rates: revenue went from $16.5B (FY2021) to $17.1B (FY2022), collapsed to $9.3B in FY2023 and has only recovered to $11.1B. Q2 2026 was the strongest quarter in years: revenue $3.5B (+32% YoY), operating income $350M (+41%), EPS $2.03 (+51%), helped by Middle East-driven air capacity shortages, AI-hyperscaler demand and double-digit customs growth. The stock has risen about 50% in a year and now trades at about 27x trailing earnings, above its own five-year average, so the market has already priced in much of the recovery.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) COVID boom — FY2021-22 revenue and profit ($1.9B operating income) were inflated by extraordinary air and ocean rates, which is why 4-year CAGRs from FY2021 are negative even though FY2025 profit sits near the pre-COVID trend. (2) Pass-through revenue — revenue swings with freight rates (Q2 2026 revenue +32% is largely rate-driven; tonnage was +14% and ocean containers flat), so net revenue and operating income are the cleaner lens. (3) Cash timing — FY2022 FCF of $2.0B was a working-capital release as rates fell; the FY2021-25 FCF CAGR (+3.5%) is therefore measured from a working-capital-depressed FY2021 base and into a normalised FY2025.
Maarten's note

Expeditors is the cleanest example on this site of the asset-light forwarder done without acquisitions. DSV grows by buying competitors; Expeditors grows organically, pays its district managers on local profit, keeps capex near zero and sends most of its free cash flow back to shareholders. Net cash, a 57% ROIC and an FCF/EBIT ratio near 90% are exactly what I look for in the quality criteria.

What stops it scoring higher is the same thing that holds the whole sector back: freight is cyclical and the growth criteria are measured against the COVID peak. Operating income is 45% lower than FY2021 and still only about 10% of billings. On top of that, the stock is up about 50% in a year, trades at 27x trailing earnings versus a 19x five-year average, and analysts rate it Hold with an average target below the current price. Q2 2026 was excellent, but a good part of it came from temporary factors (Middle East disruption squeezing air capacity, a surge of tariff-refund filings), so I would not extrapolate it.

Against the peers already on this site, Expeditors at 46 sits just below DSV (48) and above DFDS (42) and Maersk (37). It beats all three on balance sheet, returns and capital intensity; it loses to DSV on market position and entry price. I would want to see whether margins hold once air rates normalise before treating the Q2 jump as a new baseline. My own reasoning only, not advice.

Latest Quarter (Q2 2026) & Recent Trend
Reported Aug 3, 2026. The first quarter of strong YoY growth after eight quarters of flat-to-down revenue; Q3 2026 is due Nov 3, 2026, with an Investor Day on Nov 18.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was $3,502M (+32.1% YoY), operating income $350M (+41%), net earnings $266M (+45%) and diluted EPS $2.03 (+51%). Drivers: airfreight tonnage +14% (+16% vs Q1) with sharply higher buy and sell rates as the conflict in the Middle East cut passenger belly capacity and AI-hyperscaler server shipments needed freighter space; ocean containers were flat YoY but up 7% sequentially, the first sequential increase since Q3 2025; and customs, Transcon, distribution and order management all grew double digits for a second straight quarter, helped by tariff complexity and a temporary surge in IEEPA-related filings. Operating efficiency improved to 32.2% even after a $25M technology restructuring charge (offset by a $16M property gain), which management says cuts costs by about $50M a year. The soft spots: free cash flow was only $167M (+2% YoY, 4.8% of revenue), net cash fell to $464M as $461M went to buybacks and dividends, and much of the revenue jump is rate-driven rather than volume.

What's Working

The model compounds cash without leverage: net cash, capex under 0.5% of revenue, FCF/EBIT of 91% in FY2025 and a 57% ROIC mean nearly all profit can be returned, and it has been, with the share count down 24% since FY2021 and 31 years of dividend increases. Q2 2026 showed real operating leverage (operating income +41% on revenue +32%, EPS +51%) with double-digit growth in customs, Transcon, distribution and order management, and a $25M restructuring should lower overhead by about $50M a year. Management says it is gaining share, airfreight tonnage grew 14% against a tight market, and ocean volume turned up sequentially for the first time since Q3 2025. Margins have held up through a revenue halving: gross margin moved only 0.7pp and operating margin 0.6pp in three years.

What to Watch

Valuation is the main issue: 26.9x trailing earnings against a 19.3x five-year average, a stock up about 50% in a year, and a Hold consensus with an average target below the price. The growth criteria are poor (revenue -9.5% and EBIT -13.8% a year since FY2021) and a good part of Q2 2026 was temporary: Middle East-driven air rate spikes, a tariff-refund filing surge and rate-inflated revenue with ocean containers flat. If air capacity normalises, the comparison base turns harsher from Q3 2026. Free cash flow has not grown much (CAGR +3.5%) and buybacks of $0.9B a year are above FCF, so net cash is draining. Expeditors is not a top-two forwarder, faces larger, better-integrated rivals, and has almost no insider ownership.
