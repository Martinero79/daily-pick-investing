IDEXX Laboratories was founded in 1983 and is based in Westbrook, Maine. Its core business is a razor-and-blade model in veterinary diagnostics: clinics buy or lease IDEXX analyzers (the Catalyst chemistry analyzer, ProCyte hematology, SediVue urinalysis and the newer inVue Dx cytology platform, now over 9,000 installed) and then buy IDEXX consumables on every test. Together with reference laboratory services (IDEXX runs its own labs and courier networks) these recurring revenue lines made up roughly 65-70% of FY2025 revenue (VetLab consumables $1.50B and reference labs $1.42B of $4.30B), supplemented by rapid assay tests, veterinary practice software ($346M) and the smaller Water ($201M) and LPD ($132M) segments. Revenue grew 7.6% a year over FY2021-25, operating margin rose from 29.0% to 31.6% and the share count fell about 9% via buybacks. Q3 2026 was solid (revenue +9.9%, CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue +10.8%, VetLab consumables +14.3%, operating margin 33.1%, GAAP EPS $4.01, +18%) and 2026 guidance was raised again on Nov 2, but the last verified share price (Oct 30) was roughly 28% below its 52-week high because US veterinary clinical visits keep declining (an estimated -1.3% in Q2 2026) and the premium multiple (about 37x trailing on TTM EPS to Sep 2026, ~35x forward) leaves less room for disappointment. I could not find a post-results closing price, so the price on this page pre-dates the Q3 release.
Three things to read carefully: (1) Volume vs price — IDEXX's growth is volume-led (more tests per visit, new premium analyzers) while overall US visit counts are falling; if utilisation gains stall the growth rate is exposed. (2) Share-based-compensation tax benefits lift reported EPS ($0.14 in Q2 2026; ~$0.24 in the 2026 outlook; ~$0.35 in FY2025), so GAAP EPS growth runs slightly above the 'comparable' 15% growth the company cites. (3) Capex is stepping up to ~$180M guided for 2026 (about 3.8% of revenue vs 2.9% in FY2025), which scores on a lower band going forward.

What's Working

Category leadership with a recurring-revenue razor-and-blade model (consumables and reference labs ~two-thirds of sales), margins that keep expanding (gross +3.0pp in four years, operating margin 33.1% in Q3 2026 and 35.0% in Q2), ROIC far above any plausible cost of capital, steady buybacks that cut the share count ~9% and modest leverage (~0.4x). Q3 2026 growth was broad (CAG recurring +10.8%, VetLab consumables +14.3%), the inVue Dx base passed 9,000 instruments in Q2, 2026 guidance was raised twice, free cash flow TTM is about $1.30B, and the stock traded at about 0.68x its own five-year average trailing P/E at the Oct 30 close.

What to Watch

Growth is solid but not fast: 4-year revenue CAGR 7.6% and EBIT CAGR 9.9% miss the 15% bar, and FCF conversion is just under 80%. US clinical visits are falling (an estimated -1.3% in Q2 2026), so growth relies on utilisation and pricing; competition from Zoetis and Mars (Antech) is real. The stock is ~28% off its 52-week high and the PEG is ~2.4, so the multiple still assumes continued double-digit EPS growth. Capex steps up to ~$180M in 2026, buybacks are partly debt-funded, equity is small (ROIC/ROE flattered), insider ownership is ~0.3%, and the valuation sits right at a band edge, so a ~3% share-price rise would cut criterion 10 back to 4/5. Market-share and concentration scores are judgment calls.
