Share classes & brands: VW AG has two listed classes: ordinary shares (VOW, voting) and preferred shares (VOW3, no vote, slightly higher dividend). This page tracks the preferred shares, which are the more liquid line and the one in the Euro Stoxx 50 until its removal in September 2026. Group brands include Volkswagen, Audi, Porsche (VW holds roughly 75% of Porsche AG, listed since Sep 2022), Skoda, SEAT/Cupra, Bentley, Lamborghini, Ducati, Traton (trucks/buses) and Volkswagen Financial Services.

Volkswagen Group is the world's second-largest carmaker by volume (behind Toyota), selling mass-market (Volkswagen, Skoda, SEAT/Cupra), premium (Audi) and luxury (Porsche, Bentley, Lamborghini) cars, vans and, through Traton, trucks and buses, plus a captive finance and leasing arm. It delivered about 9.0M vehicles in FY2025, earned €321.9B revenue and an €8.9B operating result (2.8% margin, down 53% from €19.1B), and generated €6.4B of Automotive net cash flow (+24% YoY, helped by lower investment and a year-end working-capital release). Net profit attributable to shareholders was €6.7B and the preferred dividend was cut to €5.26 from €6.36. 2026 has gone worse: H1 operating result fell 11.6% to €5.9B (3.8% margin) with China deliveries down 32%; on September 18 VW cut guidance to an operating return on sales of up to 1% (from 4-5.5%) and revenue of about €315B, flagging ~€10B of negative one-offs (about €0.9B booked in H1, most of the rest in Q3) including a ~€6B non-cash Porsche goodwill impairment and ~€2B of early-retirement and Osnabrück-sale costs tied to a restructuring that now targets about 100,000 job cuts worldwide. The shares fell as much as 7.5% that day and VW was removed from the Euro Stoxx 50 on Sep 21. The next catalyst is the Q3 report on Oct 29, 2026.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Captive finance — consolidated debt, cash flow and ROIC are dominated by Financial Services, so Automotive-Division net cash flow and net liquidity are used where disclosed. (2) Special items — FY2025 and 2026 operating results include very large non-cash and restructuring items (Porsche impairments, tariffs, job-cut provisions); the group's own “before special items” figure was €6.9B in H1 2026 versus €5.9B reported. Scoring here uses the reported (after special items) figures. (3) A trailing P/E of ~6.5x on TTM EPS of €10.46 is not a measure of cheapness: TTM earnings do not yet include the Q3 2026 one-offs, so trailing EPS will probably fall further, and the S&P forward P/E (~2.6x) reflects stale consensus that pre-dates the warning and should not be relied on.

Restructuring, China & Latest Quarter (Q2 2026)
The near-term case rests on whether job cuts and cost programmes can offset China volume losses, US tariffs and Porsche's decline. Q2 2026 was reported Jul 24, 2026; guidance was cut again on Sep 18; Q3 2026 is due Oct 29, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was €82.4B (+2.0% YoY) and the operating result €3.46B (-9.5% YoY; 4.2% margin). H1 2026 operating result was €5.9B (-11.6%; 3.8% margin; €6.9B before special items), held back by about €0.5B of costs for ending US ID.4 production and by negative mix. Deliveries fell 6.3% to 4.13M in H1, with China -31.6%, partly offset by growth elsewhere. Automotive net cash flow was a better €3.2B in H1 (H1 2025: -€1.4B) and Automotive net liquidity was €32.7B at June 30. The bigger event came afterwards: on Sep 18 VW guided to a 2026 operating return on sales of up to 1% and revenue of about €315B, citing ~€10B of one-offs (a ~€6B non-cash Porsche goodwill impairment, ~€2B for expanded early-retirement schemes and the planned sale of Volkswagen Osnabrück, plus other restructuring), a further deterioration in China and a faster shift to battery-electric demand. The Q3 2026 operating result will therefore likely be very weak or negative.

Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership
Valuation uses trailing P/E on reported preferred-share EPS (S&P) at year-end Xetra closes (FY2021 €177.48 from VW's annual report; FY2022-25 closes derived from S&P's year-end P/E multiplied by EPS and so approximate). For a cyclical automaker with collapsing earnings, a low P/E can signal peak earnings rather than cheapness.
Trailing P/E — FY-End vs. Current
Current P/E (~6.6x = €68.54 / TTM EPS €10.46) is ~1.25x VW's FY2021-25 year-end average (~5.3x), a ~25% premium. Year-end closes (approximate): €177.48, ~134.8, ~115.8, ~90.9, ~104.5; EPS: 29.61, 29.69, 31.81, 21.39, 13.31. The low FY2022-24 multiples reflect peak earnings, not cheapness; the FY2025 multiple jumped as EPS halved.

Forward P/E & PEG: S&P shows a forward P/E of ~2.6x (consensus EPS of about €26), but this cannot be reconciled with VW's own guidance of an operating return on sales of up to 1% (a ~€3B operating result on €315B revenue) and almost certainly reflects stale analyst estimates from before the Sep 18 warning. I do not score or rely on it; its own FY2021-25 year-end average is ~4.5x, so even on that basis the multiple is not stretched, but the earnings it implies are unlikely. PEG (0.12 per S&P) is not meaningful. Average analyst target €104.33 (18 analysts, consensus Buy; Kepler Cheuvreux cut to Hold on Sep 21) implies ~52% upside; targets are third-party and may lag the warning.
Balance sheet, Automotive vs consolidated: VW's Automotive Division had net liquidity of €34.5B at YE2025 (€34.4B restated at YE2024; €32.7B at Jun 30, 2026), after paying the €9.5B Porsche-IPO special dividend in 2023. The consolidated balance sheet (VW definition) shows third-party borrowings of €264.7B against €86.2B of gross liquidity, i.e. net liquidity of −€178.5B, but €247.5B of those borrowings sit in Financial Services, which funds a matched lease and loan book. S&P's consolidated net debt is €214B at Jun 2026 and book equity €202B. I use the Automotive figure for net debt/EBITDA and say so in the scorecard. Capex: Automotive capex excluding capitalised development was €15.0B (5.1% of Automotive revenue) in FY2025, down from €16.9B; capitalised development costs add another €9.0B (€10.2B in FY2024). Including them, total investment is roughly 8% of Automotive revenue.

Share Count

VW has had 295.1M ordinary and 206.2M preferred shares (501.3M in total) outstanding for years; the count was identical at year-end 2021 and today, so it is flat, not declining. There is no regular buyback programme. Treat the capital percentages above as approximate: they come from VW's investor-relations shareholder structure and Porsche SE disclosures and I did not re-verify them as of October 2026.

Control, insiders & honest caveats: Volkswagen is effectively controlled by Porsche SE (the Porsche and Piëch family holding), together with the State of Lower Saxony's special veto rights, so minority holders of the non-voting preferred shares (VOW3) have no vote. I could not verify management's own shareholdings. Separately, VW faces a UK motor-finance redress scheme (reported ~£9B industry-wide cost; VW's share not verified here) and an ongoing dispute with JSW over a reported $1.4B Indian tax liability — both unquantified in the figures above.

What's Working

The industrial business is a net-cash position (Automotive net liquidity €34.5B) despite a very heavy investment programme, Automotive net cash flow improved to €6.4B in FY2025 and €3.2B in H1 2026 on investment discipline and lower working capital, and the group still earned a profit in FY2025 (net income €6.7B). VW is the world's #2 carmaker by volume with a broad brand portfolio (Audi, Skoda, Cupra, Traton and the finance arm all remained profitable), the Porsche SE family holding and Lower Saxony provide a stable anchor, capex intensity is moderate at ~5% of revenue, and the share count has not been diluted. Revenue held at €322B, and management is acting with a restructuring programme of roughly 100,000 job cuts and portfolio moves (Gotion, Osnabrück, Porsche reset).

What to Watch

Earnings, margins and returns have all collapsed: the operating result is 54% below FY2021, gross margin has lost 3.65 points in three years, the 2026 margin is guided to at most 1%, and ROIC (2.4%) is far below any reasonable cost of capital. China deliveries are down about a third and Chinese competition is intensifying in other regions, US tariffs cost billions, Porsche has needed about €4.7B of goodwill and realignment charges in 2025 (€2.7B and €2.0B over nine months) and ~€6B more in 2026, and a restructuring of this scale carries union-conflict and execution risk. The dividend was cut 17% and may be cut again, the cheap-looking trailing P/E is based on earnings that do not yet reflect the Q3 one-offs, and non-voting preferred holders have no say in control.
