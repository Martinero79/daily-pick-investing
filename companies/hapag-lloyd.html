Hapag-Lloyd is the cleanest illustration in this entire shipping batch of just how violent container-freight cycles can be. Revenue went from €22.3B (FY2021) to €34.5B at the freight-rate supercycle peak (FY2022) to €17.9B after the crash (FY2023) and has drifted only modestly since, closing FY2025 at €18.6B — a five-year revenue CAGR of -4.36% despite starting from an already-strong base, not a weak one. EBIT fell even harder, from €9.4B in FY2021 to just €0.95B in FY2025 (-43.6% CAGR), and 2026 has piled on fresh, real disruption: adverse weather and Red Sea rerouting hurt Q1, then the closure of the Strait of Hormuz added roughly $600M of extra cost in Q2 alone. None of this reflects a broken business — Hapag-Lloyd's Gemini Cooperation with Maersk delivered a record 90% schedule reliability in 2025, and the company remains solidly the world's #5 carrier by capacity — but this framework's 5-year growth and margin criteria are built to catch exactly this kind of round trip, and they do: 9 of 13 criteria miss, mostly on growth, margin, ROIC and valuation. What still works is almost entirely balance-sheet and governance: net leverage remains low (1.08x EBITDA) even after swinging from net cash to net debt, the share count hasn't moved in five years, and control sits with one of the most durable ownership pacts on the site — CSAV, the Kühne family and the City of Hamburg's HGV together hold 73.9%, locked in under an agreement renewed through at least 2031. The pending $4.2B acquisition of ZIM, if it clears regulatory review, would meaningfully change Hapag-Lloyd's scale; as things stand, this is a supporting, non-featured dashboard meant to sit alongside Maersk's for direct comparison, not today's pick.

Q2 2026 Headlines

Revenue reached €5.02B, up 8.37% YoY and up 19% sequentially from Q1 2026, as freight rates recovered to $1,475/TEU (+11% QoQ, +9% YoY) and volumes rose to 3.5 million TEU. EBIT swung back to a positive €144.7M (2.88% margin) from a Q1 2026 loss of €-57.5M, and net profit was €68.3M. The recovery came despite the Strait of Hormuz blockage adding roughly $600M in extra bunker, insurance, storage, rerouting and inland-transport costs during the quarter — management raised full-year 2026 guidance even so, to Group EBITDA of €0.9-2.6B and Group EBIT of €-1.3B to €0.4B, a range that still allows for a full-year loss.

Trailing P/E is excluded here as genuinely not meaningful: TTM net income is just €58-248M against a €22.8B market cap (depending on snapshot date), producing a trailing P/E near 400x that swings wildly on small earnings changes — textbook near-zero-earnings noise. Price / Free Cash Flow — Year-End (FY21-25), Plus Current
Current P/FCF of 22.69x sits about 2.5x the FY21-25 average of 9.14x — a real, large premium, though it says more about how depressed trailing FCF is right now (€1.08B, the smallest in this window) than about the market suddenly overpaying.
Balance sheet: €4.85B in cash & investments against €6.28B total debt as of Jun 30, 2026 — a net debt position of €1.42B, still just 1.08x TTM EBITDA and comfortably under this framework's 2x threshold. Current ratio 1.36x and quick ratio 1.19x are both fine but a clear step down from the extraordinarily liquid FY2022 peak (current ratio 3.41x, €12.87B net cash). Notably, dividends have not been cut nearly as fast as earnings: the FY2025 payout ratio was 160% of that year's EPS (versus just 6.8% in FY2021), which is part of why the net cash cushion built during the supercycle has eroded.

Governance note — a three-way anchor pact, not a single founder: Hapag-Lloyd's controlling block isn't one family or founder but a shareholders' agreement between Chilean shipping group CSAV (30.0%), German logistics entrepreneur Klaus-Michael Kühne's holding vehicles — Kühne Holding AG and Kühne Maritime GmbH (30.0%) — and HGV, the City of Hamburg's investment arm (13.9%). The pact was renewed in 2026, with a new four-year agreement taking effect January 1, 2027, meaning control is locked in well past this scorecard's horizon. This criterion is scored on Kühne's 30.0% stake alone as the closest analog to a founder-style anchor holder (already far past this framework's 5% "meaningful" bar); the full 73.9% combined bloc is even more concentrated than that headline number implies.

What's Working

The balance sheet and ownership structure are genuinely strong: net leverage stayed at just 1.08x EBITDA even after swinging from net cash to net debt, the share count hasn't moved in five years, and control sits with one of the most durable, longest-tenured anchor pacts on the site (73.9% combined, locked in through at least 2031). FCF conversion on a clean annual basis clears the 80% bar comfortably, and the Gemini Cooperation with Maersk delivered a record 90% schedule reliability in 2025.

What to Watch

Every growth, margin and returns criterion misses by a wide margin — this is a genuinely weak scorecard, not a borderline one, and the stock trades at roughly 2.5x its own 5-year cash-flow multiple average despite depressed near-term earnings. 2026 has brought fresh, real disruption on top of the post-supercycle normalization: adverse weather hurt Q1, and the Strait of Hormuz closure added roughly $600M of extra cost in Q2 alone, with full-year EBIT guidance (€-1.3B to €0.4B) still allowing for an outright loss. The pending $4.2B ZIM acquisition faces a "major regulatory setback" per Reuters and would materially reshape scale and leverage if it proceeds. Not today's pick — built as a direct, supporting comparison point against Maersk within the same alliance.
