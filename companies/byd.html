BYD's top line tells one of the most explosive growth stories on this site: revenue compounded at roughly 38.9% a year from FY2021 to FY2025 (¥216.1B → ¥804.0B), and BYD overtook Tesla in 2025 to become the world's largest NEV (EV+PHEV) maker by volume — 4.6 million vehicles delivered, including for the first time more than 1 million overseas. But nearly everything downstream of that top line tells a much thinner story. Consolidated operating margin is just 4.20% in FY2025 (down from 6.41% in FY2024), blending a highly competitive, price-war-battered auto business with BYD's lower-margin handset-components/electronics segment; free cash flow swung from +¥36.1B (FY2024) to -¥97.7B (FY2025) as capex surged to fund overseas plants; and the picture has gotten worse in 2026, not better — H1 2026 revenue fell 7.13% YoY and net profit fell 20.54% YoY as Chinese rivals (Geely, Xiaomi, and others) cut prices further. The one clear bright spot inside that slowdown is exports: overseas shipments grew 71% YoY in H1 2026 to over 790,000 vehicles (44% of the total) at a 22% gross margin, versus much thinner margins at home. The balance sheet remains net cash and founder Wang Chuanfu (roughly 17% of shares) is genuinely aligned, but this is a company scaling faster than it's currently converting that scale into profit or cash — 8 of 14 criteria met, Martinero Index 60/100.

Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings; the trailing-P/E series above is that same ratio at each fiscal year-end. Forward P/E divides today's price by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful lens for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where earnings are actually headed. For BYD specifically: trailing P/E (≈26.2x) sits almost exactly on its own FY22-25 average, but forward P/E (≈17.3x for 2026, ≈14.9x for 2027 consensus) sits meaningfully lower — the market is pricing in a real earnings recovery from the 2026 price-war trough. Western sell-side coverage of this dual-listed, RMB-reporting name is thinner than for US mega-caps on this site, so a full historical forward-P/E line isn't available and a single current PEG figure isn't reliably published either; conceptually, dividing ≈17.3x by the expected 2026-2027 earnings recovery would plausibly land the PEG near or modestly above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line — not expensive, but not the screaming discount the low trailing multiple of a typical China EV headline might suggest, given how much of that recovery is still a forecast rather than a fact.

What's Working

Revenue has compounded at nearly 39% a year for four years, and BYD is now the world's largest EV/PHEV maker by volume, having overtaken Tesla in 2025. The balance sheet remains net cash, founder Wang Chuanfu holds a genuinely meaningful (≈17%) and aligned stake, gross margin has held up on a 3-year basis despite 2025-26 pressure, and the current valuation sits right at BYD's own recent trading history rather than at a premium. Overseas expansion is the clearest current bright spot: exports up 71% YoY in H1 2026 at a 22% gross margin, well above domestic profitability.

What to Watch

Consolidated operating margin (4.20%) is nowhere near this framework's 20% bar, free cash flow swung deeply negative in FY2025 on a capex surge, and the trend has gotten worse, not better, in 2026: H1 revenue fell 7.1% YoY and net profit fell 20.5% YoY as China's brutal EV price war compressed per-vehicle economics. ROIC has fallen from a 47.76% FY2024 peak to just 10.47% on the latest TTM basis, and the net-cash cushion has shrunk fast as debt roughly tripled. Share count has crept up (+4.5% since FY2021, mostly from one large 2025 placement) rather than staying flat or declining, and capex intensity remains high as BYD builds out overseas capacity. Whether the H1 2026 export strength and the Q2 profit rebound mark a genuine stabilization, or just a pause before the domestic price war resumes, is the central open question here.
