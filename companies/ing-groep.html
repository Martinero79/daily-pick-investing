ING is one of Europe's largest retail and wholesale banks, with leading positions in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany, a fast-growing direct-banking franchise across Europe and roughly 60,000 employees. FY2025 total income was €23.0bn (+1.9%) and the net result €6.3bn, a 13.6% return on tangible equity, with a 54.6% cost/income ratio and a 13.1% CET1 ratio. Over FY2021–FY2025 income compounded at ~5.6% a year and net result at ~7.3% — well below this framework's 15% bar, flattered at the start by a FY2021 base with a 9.2% ROE and a weak FY2022 (7.2%) before the rate cycle lifted profit in FY2023. Capital return is the standout: FY2025 cash dividends of €3.2bn, an extra €0.5bn cash distribution and €3.1bn of buybacks were about 107% of the net result, and the share count is down ~23% since FY2021. Momentum has improved in 2026: Q2 total income rose 10.2% year on year, net result 16%, and management lifted its 2026 and 2027 ROTE outlook to above 15% and 16%. The caveats are the rich re-rating — ~12.6x trailing earnings and ~1.65x book value against FY21–FY25 averages of ~9.3x and ~0.98x — and dependence on interest rates and European credit conditions.
Framing note — why this scorecard looks different for a bank: ING is a bank, so the standard revenue/EBIT/FCF/net-debt framework does not map cleanly onto it. The same substitutions used on the JPMorgan Chase page are applied here, all flagged inline: Total Income stands in for revenue (criterion 1); Net Income substitutes for both EBIT (criterion 2) and Free Cash Flow (criterion 4) — a bank's cash flow statement is dominated by deposit and trading-book swings that do not map to a “free cash flow” concept, so criteria 2 and 4 are necessarily identical here, a genuine limitation of the adaptation and not a workaround; ROTE vs. an assumed ~10% cost of equity substitutes for ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7; ING reports return on tangible equity (ROTE) from 2026 (restating prior periods) and ROE on IFRS-EU equity before that; ROTE is used for the spread. A ~10% cost of equity is used, the low end of the 10–11% range, given a beta near 0.9 and a European-bank credit profile); CET1 ratio vs. the regulatory requirement substitutes for net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8), scored on the cushion over the requirement; and (dividends + buybacks) ÷ net income substitutes for FCF conversion (criterion 9). Entry multiple (criterion 10) is scored on the average of the P/E and P/B ratios, each current value divided by its own FY2021–FY2025 year-end average (P/TBV history is not available, so P/B is used). Criterion 14 uses the efficiency (cost/income) ratio because banks report no gross profit; a falling ratio counts as a margin rise. Everything is reported in EUR and nothing is converted.

What the latest quarter showed (Q2 2026)

Q2 2026 total income was €6,284m (+10.2% YoY, +7.9% on Q1) and the net result €1,947m (+16% YoY), with profit before tax up 23% to €2,919m. Commercial net interest income rose 10.7% YoY on net core lending growth of €15.2bn (about 8% annualised) and deposit inflows of €15.9bn; fee income was €1,278m (+14% YoY) and the cost/income ratio fell to 49.1% from 55.3% in Q1. Risk costs were 15bp of average lending against a through-the-cycle 20bp. ROTE was 17.0% for the quarter (14.5% over four quarters) and the CET1 ratio 13.1%, helped by €1.0bn of risk-weighted-asset relief from a significant risk-transfer transaction. ING lifted its 2026 total income outlook to above €24.5bn and its ROTE outlook to above 15% (2026) and above 16% (2027), declared a €0.40 interim dividend, and has a €1.0bn buyback running to October. Costs excluding regulatory items rose 4.2% YoY, and Australian regulators set extra liquidity and capital conditions on ING's local unit in September (third-party report).

Market position, honestly stated: ING is one of the two or three largest banks in the Netherlands (alongside Rabobank and ABN AMRO), a top-tier Dutch mortgage and savings provider, and runs one of Europe's largest direct banks in Germany, plus Belgium, Poland, Spain, Italy and Australia — a strong #2/top-three position that supports criterion #5 (scored as a strong #2, a judgment call rather than a published rank). What's Working

Strong commercial momentum (primary customers +1m in 12 months, Q2 2026 income +10%), a 13.6% ROTE with guidance lifted to above 15% for 2026, and a very generous capital return — about 107% of FY25 profit via dividends and buybacks, with the share count down ~23% since FY2021. Credit quality is solid (risk costs 15–20bp), fee income is growing double digits, and the cost/income ratio has improved by ~5.7pp over three years. Capex is light and the 13.1% CET1 ratio sits ~2pp above the requirement.

What to Watch

Growth has been modest: income has been roughly flat since FY2023 and the five-year CAGRs are 5–7%, well below the framework's 15% bar. The shares have re-rated to ~12.6x trailing earnings and ~1.65x book, far above FY21–FY25 averages of ~9.3x and ~0.98x, so much of the improvement may already be priced in. Net interest income is sensitive to rates and deposit competition, the Q4'25–Q1'26 results showed volatility in market-related income, the CET1 buffer is deliberately thin (~2pp), and the planned sale of the Russian business carries an expected ~€0.8bn post-tax loss. Insider ownership is negligible and the Dutch market is concentrated.
