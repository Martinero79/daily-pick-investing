Market concentration detail: 3 / 5 — dominant in custom-ASIC design and enterprise virtualization niches, but a genuinely diversified conglomerate facing real competition in each vertical
Broadcom is two overlapping stories bolted together by the November 2023 VMware acquisition: a semiconductor franchise that is now the leading merchant designer of custom AI accelerators for hyperscalers (Google, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, and reportedly Apple and ByteDance), and an enterprise infrastructure-software business built around VMware's virtualization stack that behaves more like a subscription cash cow than a semiconductor line. The headline five-year growth numbers — a 23%+ revenue CAGR, 30%+ EBIT CAGR — are real cash flows, but they are not purely organic: FY2024 revenue jumped 44% and GAAP operating margin briefly collapsed to 26.1% simply because VMware was consolidated for the first time and its acquisition costs hit the income statement; the recovery to 39.9% in FY2025 mostly reflects those one-time costs rolling off, not a sudden efficiency breakthrough. What is organic and re-accelerating is AI semiconductor revenue, up 143% year-over-year last quarter on a >$73B backlog and multi-year hyperscaler supply deals, while infrastructure-software growth has cooled to single digits and is drawing real customer pushback over pricing. The balance sheet has healed fast — net debt/EBITDA fell from roughly 2.8x right after the deal closed to about 1.1x today — but the stock has re-rated even faster, with P/S near 27x versus a ≈21x trailing eight-quarter average and P/FCF near 60x versus a ≈31x five-year average. Nine of thirteen screening criteria are met; the misses cluster exactly where you'd expect for a story this well covered: a rich entry valuation, thin insider ownership, and a share count that is still net dilutive from the all-stock portion of the VMware deal.

What's Working

Nine of thirteen criteria clear the bar; margins and ROIC have recovered sharply post-VMware integration; AI semiconductor revenue (≈$10.8B in the latest quarter) is compounding at triple-digit growth rates on a >$73B committed backlog and multi-year hyperscaler agreements including a Google TPU deal through 2031; leverage has been cut from ≈2.8x to ≈1.1x net debt/EBITDA in about two years; the capex-light model converts roughly all of EBIT to free cash flow; and the dividend has now grown for 15 consecutive years.

What to Watch

Valuation has re-rated hard — P/S near 27x vs. a ≈21x trailing 8-quarter average and P/FCF near 60x vs. a ≈31x five-year average — leaving little room for disappointment. Infrastructure-software (VMware) growth is decelerating and missed consensus in the latest quarter, even as list-price increases (reportedly 50–200% per-core for some customers) draw enterprise pushback and EU antitrust scrutiny. Insider ownership is thin (1.11%) and share count is still net dilutive year-over-year from the all-stock portion of the VMware deal. FY2024 numbers are heavily distorted by one-time deal accounting, so the multi-year CAGRs partly reflect the VMware step-change rather than organic execution — don't extrapolate them at face value.
