Zeta Global sells a cloud marketing and data platform to large enterprises: the Zeta Marketing Platform (ZMP), built on a proprietary first-party data set and identity graph, plus an AI layer (“Athena by Zeta”) that now leads the company's pitch, which describes Zeta as “intelligent AI infrastructure”. Q3 2026 (reported Nov 9, 2026) was its 21st consecutive “beat and raise” quarter by the company's own count: revenue $492.7M (+46.1% YoY, $22M above the guidance midpoint), GAAP operating income $45.9M (9.3% margin), GAAP net income $38.3M (diluted EPS $0.14), adjusted EBITDA $121.0M (24.6% margin) and free cash flow $81.5M. Full-year 2026 guidance was raised to revenue of $1,853-1,857M (+42%), adjusted EBITDA of $414.1-415.2M, free cash flow of $270.6-271.6M and GAAP EPS of $0.22-$0.24. About 16 points of the 46% growth come from the Marigold Enterprise acquisition and political candidate revenue; excluding both, growth was 30%. The weak spots are the ones the pitch glosses over: the share count has risen about 23% since FY21 and stock-based compensation is still about 12% of revenue, the first GAAP profits are thin (FY2025 operating margin 0.4%), one customer was more than 10% of 2025 revenue, and the stock is not a calm anchor, falling about 24% in the week after its Nov 13 high.
The pitch, tested against the filings.
“Stability anchor” — does NOT hold. The 52-week range is ~$14.36 to ~$38.80 (a 2.7x spread); the stock fell about 29% from the Dec 31, 2025 close to its Mar 2026 low, then rose about 167% to the Nov 13 high, then lost about 24% in a week (Nov 20 close $29.51) before bouncing 9.9% on Nov 23. Yahoo shows a 5-year monthly beta of 1.39; one third-party note cites a one-year beta near 2.9. The business is steady in its beat pattern; the share price is not.
“20+ beat-and-raise quarters” — HOLDS, with a caveat. The company counts 21 consecutive quarters (Q3 2026 was the 21st; Q4 2025 the 18th). The caveat: Zeta sets its own guidance, and it has been beating the midpoint by about 4-5% (Q4'25 +$14M; Q2'26 +$23M; Q3'26 +$22M), so a streak is partly a function of conservative guidance.
“Sticky enterprise AI marketing software” — PARTIAL. Super-scaled customers (at least $1M trailing revenue) grew to 212 (+18% YoY) with ARPU of $2.0M (+22%), and the company reported net revenue retention of 112% for 2022. Against that, the 10-K says many contracts have no automatic renewal or exclusivity, customers use several vendors, the top ten customers were more than one-third of 2025 revenue and one customer was more than 10%.
“Zero dilution” — does NOT hold. Class A + B shares went from 197.8M (Dec 2021) to 244.1M (Feb 2026) and ~252M (Sep 2026): +23% and +27%. SBC was $259M, $299M, $243M, $195M and $178M in FY21-FY25 (56.5% falling to 13.6% of revenue) and $155M in 9M 2026; buybacks were $121M in FY25 and $86M in 9M 2026 but did not offset it. Dilution is falling, not zero: weighted shares used for non-GAAP EPS were still up 5.3% YoY in Q3 2026 (269.9M vs 256.3M).

What's Working

Growth with operating leverage: revenue +46.1% in Q3 2026 (+30% excluding M&A and political, up from 28%), GAAP operating margin up from -54.2% (FY21) to 9.3% in Q3 2026, free cash flow of $237M TTM (+73% YoY in Q3) and SBC down from 56.5% to about 11.6% of revenue. The 21-quarter beat-and-raise streak is real and the 2026 outlook has been raised repeatedly (GAAP EPS guidance from $0.03 to $0.23 at the midpoint). Net cash of about $96M, super-scaled customers up 18% with ARPU of $2.0M (+22%), and a founder-led structure (Steinberg is Chairman/CEO with a 10-vote share class).

Tenbagger check
A six-point checklist for stocks that might multiply tenfold, tested against Zeta Global's numbers. Result: 3 pass (starting size, profitability flip, the P/S warning is not triggered), 1 partial (revenue acceleration), 2 fail (ROIC, dilution). These tests are separate from, and do not change, the 14-criterion scorecard.
1. Small starting cap (under ~$5–10B) — PASS (upper end of the range). Market cap is about $8.2B ($32.43 × ~252M shares; about $8.8B on the diluted count), inside the $5–10B ceiling but above $5B. For context, it was about $1.7B at the end of 2021 ($8.42 × 197.8M), about $1.9B at the end of 2023, about $4.3B at the end of 2024 and about $5.0B at the end of 2025. The stock has risen about 3.9x since the end of 2021 and 3.7x since the end of 2023. A further tenfold from here would imply a market cap near $82B (simple arithmetic, not a forecast).
2. Revenue acceleration toward 40%+ — PARTIAL. Reported year-on-year growth by quarter: Q4'25 +25.4%, Q1'26 +49.9%, Q2'26 +43.5%, Q3'26 +46.1% (FY24 +38.0%, FY25 +29.7%). The headline clears 40% for three straight quarters, but the jump is largely acquired (Marigold's Enterprise business adds about $48M a quarter) plus political candidate revenue. Excluding both, growth was 30% in Q3'26 (28% in Q2 and in Q4'25; 27% for FY25), below 40% but accelerating. Company guidance for Q4 2026 is +32% to +33% (22-24% excluding M&A and political), so the reported rate is guided to ease, and FY2026 guidance is +42% (+27% excluding both).
3. Profitability flip (operating margin moving from negative toward positive, and when) — PASS. GAAP operating margin was -54.2% (FY21), -43.8% (FY22), -23.0% (FY23), -6.8% (FY24) and +0.4% (FY25, $5.4M), the first positive fiscal year; Q3 2026 was +9.3% and TTM is +3.6% ($61.9M). GAAP net income turned positive in Q2 2026 ($8.2M) and Q3 2026 ($38.3M), and the nine months to Sep 2026 total +$33.2M (vs -$38.0M a year earlier). The first full fiscal year of GAAP net profit is guided for FY2026 (EPS $0.22-$0.24). Caveats: Q1 is seasonally loss-making (Q1'26 -$13.2M), tax benefits helped Q4'24 and Q4'25, and the margin is thin; FCF after SBC is only about $38M TTM.
4. ROIC trending toward ~20% — FAIL. My estimate of ROIC is about 5-7% (TTM GAAP EBIT of $61.9M over roughly $902M of invested capital; no audited ROIC figure exists), below an assumed ~11.5% WACC and far below 20%. Goodwill and intangibles of about $694M from acquisitions (LiveIntent, Marigold and others) sit in invested capital. A 20% ROIC would need roughly three to four times the current operating profit on the same capital. No company target for returns on capital exists, though management targets a 25% adjusted EBITDA margin by 2028 (non-GAAP).
5. Warning: P/S above 50x — NOT TRIGGERED (pass). P/S is about 4.7x on TTM revenue of $1,726M (about 4.4x FY2026 guided revenue). It has never been close to 50x at a year-end (range 2.6x to 4.3x over FY21-FY25). Valuation risk here is of a different kind: about 19x enterprise value to FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA, a multiple that roughly doubles if stock-based compensation is treated as a cost (one third-party note puts it near 38x on 2027 EBITDA net of SBC at $29.51).
6. Warning: dilution (share-count change over 3–5 years) — TRIGGERED (fail). Class A+B shares: 197.8M (FY21) → 217.7M (FY23) → 244.1M (FY25) → ~252M (Sep 30, 2026): +23.4% over FY21-FY25, +12.1% FY23-FY25 and about +27% since FY21. SBC was $259M, $299M, $243M, $195M and $178M in FY21-FY25 ($155M in 9M 2026), and buybacks ($121M in FY25, $86M in 9M 2026) have not been enough to hold the count flat. The trend is improving (SBC from 56.5% to ~11.6% of revenue; weighted shares +5.3% YoY in Q3 2026), but the “zero dilution” claim is wrong.
Caveat: Checklists like this are built from the winners we remember (survivorship bias): many stocks that matched them did not multiply, and most that did looked risky and expensive at the time. A pass on starting size or profitability does not make a stock a candidate by itself. This is research, not a recommendation.
