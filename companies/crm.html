Agentforce ARR: $1.5B, up 240% YoY — the clearest evidence yet that Salesforce's AI push is converting into real, growing revenue rather than just roadmap talk
Salesforce is the single clearest "core enterprise OS" complement play in this batch — 20% global CRM market share, roughly 4x its nearest competitor, and 13 consecutive years at #1 by IDC's count. The AI story here has genuinely started showing up in the numbers rather than just marketing copy: Agentforce annualized revenue crossed $1.5B in Q2 FY2027 (up 240% YoY), agentic workflow actions hit 3.2 billion in the quarter (up 97% sequentially), and management raised full-year guidance on the back of that momentum plus two pending acquisitions (Contentful, Fin). Set against that: overall revenue growth has structurally decelerated to the low-teens as the company has scaled past $40B, missing this framework's 15% CAGR bar even though the AI-specific growth rate is far higher. ROIC has improved dramatically from near-zero in FY2022-23 but still hasn't cleared 15%. And in March 2026, Salesforce raised $25B in new debt — its largest-ever bond sale — specifically to fund an accelerated buyback, following a $50B repurchase authorization and a dividend increase, after real pressure from investors to sharpen capital discipline. That's flipped the balance sheet from net cash to a 2.40x net-debt/EBITDA position overnight; not distress, but a real trade-off between shareholder returns and balance-sheet flexibility worth watching.

Q2 FY2027 Headlines

What's Working

Salesforce is the cleanest "core enterprise OS" complement story in this batch — a dominant, 13-years-running #1 CRM market position, a genuinely accelerating and now revenue-material Agentforce AI business ($1.5B ARR, +240% YoY), five straight years of uninterrupted margin expansion, and a valuation that's the cheapest relative to its own history of any name covered here. Free cash flow generation and conversion are both excellent.

What to Watch

Overall revenue growth has structurally decelerated below this framework's 15% bar as the company has matured past $40B, and ROIC, while much improved, still hasn't cleared 15%. The March 2026 $25B debt-funded buyback flipped the balance sheet from net cash to a 2.40x leveraged position overnight — a deliberate capital-allocation choice under investor pressure, not distress, but worth tracking as debt levels normalize (or don't) over the next few years. Founder/insider ownership is thin relative to some AI/SaaS peers.
