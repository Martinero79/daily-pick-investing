Adyen is an Amsterdam-based payments company founded in 2006 that built one technology stack, rather than buying and stitching together local acquirers, so a merchant can take online, in-store and in-app payments in one integration and one set of reports. It earns a small fee on each euro processed (net revenue was about 17 basis points of €1.39 trillion in FY2025, by this dashboard's calculation), and has added risk tools, card issuing and embedded financial products. Growth has settled near 20% a year, EBITDA margins are above 50%, capex is a few percent of revenue and the company carries almost no debt. The shares have not followed the business: from about €2,300 at the end of 2021 and €1,375 at the end of 2025 they closed September 2026 at €881.50. The reasons are visible in the record. February 2026 guidance of +20-22% growth and a flat 2026 margin disappointed, the CFO resigned in May, analysts questioned European enterprise pricing in June, and capex is now guided to about 7% of net revenue. H1 2026 (reported Aug 13) then lifted the revenue outlook to 21-23% and the stock rose 16% in one day, before giving most of that back during September.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Definitions — EBIT, FCF and some half-year splits on this page are derived by subtraction from Adyen's published headline numbers (see the estimates list in the sources footer). (2) Volume vs revenue — FY2025 volume grew only +8% including one large customer that scaled down (+21% excluding it), so volume headlines can differ sharply from net revenue growth. (3) The P/E history is extreme — year-end P/E ratios of 150x in 2021 make the current ~25x look cheap against own history, which says as much about 2021 as about today.

Latest Period — H1 2026 & Half-Year Trend
Adyen reports in half-years, so the last seven halves (H1 2023 to H1 2026) stand in for a quarterly view. H1 2026 was published on Aug 13, 2026; the next update is a Q3 business update expected Oct 28.
What the half showed

H1 2026 net revenue was €1,302.9M, up 19.1% reported and 21% in constant currency (a stronger euro against the dollar and other currencies trimmed the reported figure), on processed volume of €803.8B (+24%). EBITDA was €641.5M, a 49% margin (50% excluding one-time transaction costs) against 50% a year earlier, and free cash flow conversion was 86%. The margin step-down is seasonal and deal-related rather than structural: H2 has been the higher-margin half in each of the last three years. Capex was 5% of net revenue in H1 but management now expects about 7% for the full year because it is pulling data-centre spending forward from 2027 to lock in compute and storage supply. Two acquisitions closed on July 1 (Talon.One, loyalty and promotions; Orb, usage-based billing) and add 1-2pp to 2026 growth. Implied take rate fell to about 16.2 basis points in H1 2026 from about 17.0 in FY2025 (calculated here), consistent with fast growth in large, lower-priced volume.

Valuation uses trailing P/E on S&P Global's reported EPS (Adyen has no adjusted EPS). Cash-rich balance sheet, but most reported cash belongs to merchants.
Trailing P/E — FY-End vs. Current
Current P/E of about 24.8x (€881.50 / TTM EPS of €35.57) against a FY2021-25 year-end average of 72.9x (median 51.8x), a ratio of 0.34. Year-end P/Es are S&P Global figures. Forward P/E is about 20.6x versus a 59.5x average of year-end forward P/Es; PEG about 1.1. The decline reflects both earnings growth (EPS has more than doubled since 2021) and a lower share price.

Against the 2023-2025 year-end range (41x to 52x), today's 24.8x is roughly half; against a mature payments company the multiple is no longer extreme, and consensus 3-year revenue growth of about 21% and EPS growth of about 20% are what it is paying for. Share Count

About 30.7M shares at end-2021 (derived from S&P net cash per share) and 31.57M now, up 3% in four years and +0.5% in the last twelve months, all from employee share plans. Adyen pays no dividend and has no buyback programme that I could verify. The co-founders have sold a minority of their holdings in the past (a 15% sale by four management board members was announced in earlier years) but remain the best-known insiders.

Balance sheet caveat: S&P reports €12.4B of cash and investments against €0.4B of debt (mostly leases), a net cash figure of €12.0B. Most of that cash is owed to merchants awaiting settlement and is not available to shareholders. Adyen's equity is €5.9B (book value €187 per share), so the market value is about 4.7x book. The net-debt criterion is scored on the debt side (0.3x EBITDA), and I make no claim about distributable cash.

Where Adyen compares well

Adyen grows faster than the two card networks (about 18-19% against 11-15%) and sits between them and Shopify. It has a founder-led culture, essentially no debt and cash conversion in the high 80s, and its valuation against its own history is the widest discount of the four. Unlike Visa and Mastercard, Adyen sells a full processing service rather than only the rails, so it earns a fee on more of the payment value chain per euro processed.

Where it lags

The networks have a verified duopoly position that Adyen does not; Adyen pays scheme and interchange fees to them (net revenue is stated after those costs) and competes against acquirers and platforms with similar capabilities. Adyen scores lower than Mastercard and Shopify mainly on market share, concentration and ROIC vs WACC. Its net revenue and volume are also more exposed to individual large customers, and its share price has been far more volatile (beta 1.88).

What's Working

Growth at scale: net revenue compounded about 24% a year over four years and still grew 18% in FY2025 and 19% in H1 2026, with guidance raised to 21-23% in constant currency. Margins have rebuilt from the 2023 trough (EBIT 40.7% to 47.1%, with a stated goal of EBITDA above 55% by 2028), cash conversion is in the high 80s, the balance sheet carries almost no debt and capex is a modest share of revenue. The company has a single global platform, a roster of blue-chip enterprise customers, new products (issuing, embedded finance, agentic payments), and a share price that has already compressed to about 25x trailing earnings from over 150x in 2021.

What to Watch

Adyen is not a verified #1 or #2 in a fragmented market and pays the card schemes as an input cost, ROIC is below a high WACC on S&P's numbers, and the valuation discount is partly a product of the 2021 bubble. The stock has fallen about 50% from its 2025 high on a slowing growth rate, the CFO's exit, European enterprise pricing worries and capex rising to about 7% of net revenue. Single large customers can swing volume (FY2025 volume +8% including one, +21% without), the margin goal slips if investment continues, and the first two acquisitions are new territory for a historically organic company.
