This page covers the HKEX-listed Geely Automobile Holdings (0175), which owns the Geely, Lynk & Co, Zeekr and Galaxy brands. It is not the unlisted parent Geely Holding Group, which also owns the Volvo Cars stake, Polestar and Lotus. Revenue grew from ¥101.6B (FY2021) to ¥345.2B (FY2025), roughly 35.8% a year, and net profit rose from ¥4.7B to ¥16.9B. Part of that jump reflects perimeter changes: FY2024 onward appears to include the Zeekr business after its merger into Geely Auto, while FY2021-23 are as originally reported, so the CAGRs overstate organic growth (our reading of S&P's presentation; not verified against the annual report). The earnings quality is mixed: FY2024 net income was lifted by a one-off gain visible in Q2 2024 (net margin 13.8% that quarter), and operating margin is only 3.3%. Cash generation is strong (FY2025 free cash flow ¥43.1B on ¥4.1B reported capex), though the S&P capex figure likely excludes capitalised development costs and the cash flow benefits from supplier payables. H1 2026 revenue rose 15% to ¥173.6B and core net profit (ex FX and impairments, company-defined) rose 46%, while reported attributable profit slipped 1.8% to ¥9.09B. Exports (474,000 units, +158%) are carrying growth as China's domestic market contracts. The shares trade at about 8.3x trailing earnings, below their five-year average, and have fallen sharply since the summer — 11 of 14 criteria met, Martinero Index 80/100.

Market position, honestly stated: Geely Auto is China's second-largest NEV/EV maker by group volume according to press coverage (behind BYD) and, by sales, one of the larger Chinese automakers; its overseas business is growing from a low base. The industry backdrop is poor: China's passenger-car sales have fallen year-on-year for ten straight months through July 2026, the Chinese market is oversupplied with 100+ brands, and exporters from China are drawing political and tariff scrutiny in Europe, the US (connected-car rules) and Mexico. Partnerships (Renault in Brazil, Ford's Spanish plant, a 30% stake in NIO's battery-swap unit) extend reach but also add capital commitments.

Reading the three P/E numbers: trailing P/E is today's price over the last 12 months of reported earnings (≈8.3x); the series above is the same ratio at each year-end. Forward P/E divides price by consensus next-12-month earnings (≈5.9x) — analysts expect earnings to keep growing, but forward estimates for Chinese autos have been revised frequently as the domestic price war and subsidy changes evolve. The data provider's PEG is about 0.7-0.9, which would normally read as inexpensive, though a low multiple in a cyclical, low-margin industry can reflect the market pricing in margin risk rather than a bargain. The multiple has de-rated sharply since FY2021 as earnings rose faster than the share price: net income grew about 3.6x while the share price fell.
Balance sheet: Geely had ¥64.8B of cash and investments against ¥13.0B of debt at Jun 30, 2026, a net cash position of ¥51.8B (net cash/EBITDA is negative; the S&P ratio was about -2.7x at FY2025), well inside this framework's bar. Working capital is negative (current ratio about 0.9): the business funds itself in part from suppliers, which helps cash flow in growth years and would reverse if volumes fall. ROIC (S&P) was 16.56% in FY2025, up from 2.09% in FY2022; the 24.4% current figure is a trailing S&P calculation and is not used for scoring.

What's Working

Revenue and cash flow have compounded at 35%+ a year (partly via the Zeekr merger), gross margin has recovered 2.5 points in three years, and the company holds ¥51.8B net cash. Exports are surging (+158% in H1 2026), Zeekr volumes have doubled, and the shares trade at about 8x earnings, well below their own five-year average, with a growing dividend (RMB 0.449 per share for FY2025, +45%). A controlling founder-linked holder aligns long-term interests.

What to Watch

Operating margin is only 3.3% and reported attributable profit slipped 1.8% in H1 2026. China's car market is shrinking and heavily oversupplied, so growth depends on exports that face tariffs and political scrutiny. Financial comparisons across the Zeekr merger are not like-for-like, FCF is flattered by supplier credit, and capex looks understated. Dilution (~9-10%) and related-party dealings with the wider Geely Holding group are structural concerns. The share price is near its 52-week low after a JPMorgan downgrade.
