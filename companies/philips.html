Royal Philips is one of the three large global hospital-technology groups (with GE HealthCare and Siemens Healthineers). It has spent a decade turning itself from a conglomerate into a “pure” health-technology company: Lighting was spun out as Signify, and Domestic Appliances was sold in 2021 (the ~€2.7B gain on that sale is why FY2021 net income was €3.3B). What remains is three segments: Diagnosis & Treatment (imaging, ultrasound, image-guided therapy; 48% of FY2025 sales), Connected Care (patient monitoring, informatics and Sleep & Respiratory Care; 28%) and Personal Health (Sonicare, shavers, baby care; 21%), plus IP royalties. In June 2021 Philips Respironics recalled millions of CPAP, BiPAP and ventilator devices over degrading sound-abatement foam. The consequences have run for five years: about €3.3B of net recall charges excluded from Adjusted EBITA (FY2021-25), a €1.36B goodwill impairment in 2022, US$1.1B of personal-injury and monitoring settlements (€1.0B paid in 2025), and a 2024 FDA consent decree that bars US sales of new sleep and respiratory devices until Respironics' quality systems are certified compliant. Underneath, the rest of the company has been repaired: gross margin rose from 40.4% to 45.2% in three years, Adjusted EBITA margin from 7.4% to 12.3%, and Connected Care went from a 2.1% to a 10.7% Adjusted EBITA margin. Reported results are still well below the adjusted ones, sales are flat, and criminal and civil investigations, US securities litigation and a Dutch shareholder inquiry remain open.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) The sleep business is on a long, regulated path back — new CPAP/BiPAP and respiratory device sales in the US stay suspended under the 2024 consent decree (no re-entry date; the CEO said in 2024 it could take 5–7 years), while sales outside the US continue and grew in 2026 (trade press: low single digits in Q1, low double digits in Q2). Competitor ResMed took much of the share. (2) Two sets of earnings — Philips' headline Adjusted EBITA (12.3%) excludes ~€0.8B of items in FY2025 including recall costs; reported EBIT is 8.0%. H1 2026 profit is also flattered by a one-off €186M US tariff refund (about 2.2pp of the H1 margin). (3) Legal outcomes are outside every guide — Philips' 2026 outlook and its 2026-28 targets (mid-teens Adjusted EBITA margin by 2028, €4.5–5.0B cumulative FCF) exclude the DOJ, State Attorneys General, SEC and shareholder proceedings.

The Sleep & Respiratory Care Business: Recall, Consent Decree and Recovery
What happened, what it cost, and what “recovery” means for numbers. Philips does not report Sleep & Respiratory Care (S&RC) as a segment, so the best available proxy is Connected Care, which also holds patient monitoring and informatics.
Timeline in five lines

June 2021: Respironics recalls CPAP, BiPAP and ventilators (foam degradation risk); a global repair/replace programme starts, with DreamStation 2 and silicone foam as the fix. 2021-22: a €719M recall provision in 2021, then a €1.36B goodwill impairment (mostly S&RC) and further provisions in 2022. Apr 2024: consent decree with the US DOJ/FDA entered: Respironics must meet defined quality-system milestones before selling new sleep and respiratory devices in the US again, and pays disgorgement on US sales of “medically necessary” devices. 2024-25: personal-injury and medical-monitoring settlements (US$1.1B in total) become final; €1,025M is paid in Q1 2025. 2026: more than 99% of registered CPAP/BiPAP devices are reported as remediated; ventilator remediation and the DOJ, SEC, 41 State Attorneys General and Australian TGA investigations continue; a Dutch Enterprise Chamber hearing on a shareholder inquiry took place on June 25, 2026 (I did not find the decision).

How the sleep business recovers

Outside the US: Philips may keep selling new devices, masks, consumables and services (subject to export controls). Trade press (HME News) reports S&RC grew low single digits in Q1 2026, with double-digit obstructive-sleep-apnea growth outside the US led by Japan, and low double digits in Q2 2026; Philips itself gives no figure. In the US: servicing of installed devices, masks, accessories and replacement parts are allowed; new CPAP/BiPAP devices return only when independent experts certify the facilities and the FDA agrees. The CEO told Reuters in 2024 this could take 5–7 years; no date has been set. Competition: ResMed gained most of the displaced US demand (an analyst estimate put it at roughly half of Philips' former position), and rivals will not hand it back automatically.

The transition to pure health technology

Philips today has no lighting and no kitchen appliances. Remaining Personal Health (Sonicare, OneBlade) is consumer-facing but sold on a health proposition and delivers the highest margins (FY2025 Adjusted EBITA 18.0%). The 2026-28 plan sets mid-single-digit comparable sales growth, a mid-teens Adjusted EBITA margin in 2028, €4.5–5.0B of cumulative FCF and €1.5B of productivity savings, and explicitly excludes the Respironics proceedings. The 2025 base is 12.3%, so the plan needs ~2–3pp of further margin improvement.

Neither alone is a clean read. Quarterly Trend (Q4 2024 – Q2 2026)
Seven quarters. Comparable sales growth has stepped up from ~-2% in Q1 2025 to +4% in 2026, while nominal sales are still flat to down because of the weaker US dollar. Q2 2026 is the latest reported quarter (H1 2026); Q3 2026 is due Nov 3, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 sales were €4,360M (+0.5% nominal, +4% comparable, growth in every segment; Personal Health +8%). Reported income from operations was €609M (14.0%) and Adjusted EBITA €717M (16.4%), but both include a one-off €186M US tariff refund worth ~4.2pp of margin: excluding it, Adjusted EBITA slightly decreased on cost inflation and tariffs. Comparable order intake fell 1% as some large Connected Care orders slipped into Q3 (Europe grew double digits). Philips reiterated comparable sales growth of 3%–4.5% for 2026, lifted its Adjusted EBITA margin guide to 13.5%–14.0% (about 1pp from the refund) and its FCF guide to €1.5–1.7B. The shares fell 4.8% on the day (intraday low €20.90, about -11%) and have since recovered to about €23.

What's Working

The core company is being repaired: gross margin is up ~4.8pp in three years to 45.2%, Adjusted EBITA margin from 7.4% to 12.3%, comparable sales growth has turned positive (+2% in FY2025, +4% in H1 2026) with strong order growth in 2025 (+6%), and Connected Care's Adjusted EBITA margin has gone from 2.1% to 10.7%. The big recall settlements are paid, the sleep business is growing again outside the US, productivity programmes are delivering (€2.5B in 2023-25), leverage is back around 2x, capex is moderate and Exor is an engaged anchor holder lifting its permitted stake. On adjusted earnings the shares trade below their five-year average multiple.

What to Watch

Growth is thin (4-yr revenue CAGR +1.0%) and reported returns are below the cost of capital (ROIC ~6.4% vs ~7.7%). Reported EBIT margin is 8.0% against 12.3% adjusted, and the 2026 profit benefit includes a non-recurring tariff refund. US sales of new sleep and respiratory devices remain suspended with no restart date, DOJ criminal and civil, SEC, state-level and shareholder proceedings are unresolved and outside company guidance, and ResMed holds the share Philips lost. FCF has been volatile and the share count has risen ~9%.
