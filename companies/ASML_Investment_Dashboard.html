ASML is the sole supplier of EUV lithography systems — 100% of EUV, ~83–94% of lithography overall — making it the irreplaceable chokepoint behind every leading-edge chip, including the ones powering the AI buildout. The model's own numbers say growth, margins, cash generation, ROIC and balance-sheet strength all clear the bar comfortably, and by a wider margin than a year ago. The one real miss, and the one that matters most right now: valuation. The stock is up 140% over the past year and now trades well above its own trading history on both earnings and free cash flow — you'd be paying a materially higher multiple than ASML has commanded for most of the last five years. Insider ownership is also negligible (0.01%), though this is a professionally-governed Dutch public company rather than a founder- or parent-controlled one, so it reads differently than Arm's or Nvidia's version of the same miss.

What's Working

Every growth, profitability, ROIC, balance-sheet, market-position and cash-conversion criterion clears the bar; the closest thing to a genuine monopoly in this whole screen (score 1/5); net cash and share buybacks both healthy; margins have stayed remarkably stable through a volatile revenue cycle.

What to Watch

Valuation is stretched and getting more so — P/E and P/FCF are both roughly 55–70% above their own 5-year averages after a 140% 52-week run; quarterly FCF and net cash are genuinely volatile (prepayment timing), don't over-read a single quarter; Canon nanoimprint and China's SMEE are real but currently distant threats, worth monitoring rather than acting on; concentration risk if you already hold Nvidia or other AI-infrastructure names — same cycle, adjacent layer of the stack.
