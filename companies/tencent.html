Tencent is one of the highest-quality businesses in this framework, and the scorecard mostly says so: 10 of 14 criteria met (down from 12 before Q3 2026), a Martinero Index of 74 (down from 81). WeChat/Weixin's 1.43 billion combined monthly users anchor a platform that is simultaneously the world's largest video-game publisher by revenue, the #2 player in Chinese mobile payments, and a genuine margin story — operating margin has climbed from 19.99% in the FY2022 regulatory-crackdown trough to 32.67% in FY2025, gross margin is up 13.17 points over the same window, and the balance sheet was net cash through FY2025 (it flipped to RMB13.7B net debt at Sep 30, 2026 after AI-compute prepayments), with buybacks running for four straight years (about HK$7.0B repurchased in Q3 2026 alone). ROIC (17.71%, current basis 18.27%) comfortably clears both the 15% absolute bar and an assumed 10.5% WACC. Where the checklist bites is growth: 5-year revenue CAGR is 7.63% and FCF CAGR is 10.25%, both well short of the 15% bar this framework applies everywhere — and because the window starts at FY2021's peak and dips through FY2022's crackdown-driven trough (revenue actually fell 0.99% that year), even the "good" EBIT CAGR of 18.67% is flattered by a depressed base rather than representing clean, linear growth. The valuation looks statistically cheap at roughly 10.9x trailing P/E (price-scaled estimate) versus Tencent's own FY21-25 average of 18.45x, and the Q3 2026 sell-off has widened that discount — but the cheapness comes with a cost: Q3 2026 capex hit RMB81.4B (accrual basis; RMB65.1B on a cash-payment basis), free cash flow was -RMB69.1B (+RMB16.9B excluding compute prepayments), and trailing FCF conversion and capex intensity now fail this framework's bars. A forward P/E near 9.6x against consensus EPS growth in the mid-single-digits still pencils out to a PEG of roughly 1.8 (estimate, consensus not yet refreshed for the capex step-up). Net: a dominant platform business whose cash generation is being temporarily (management says capex is at or near peak) consumed by AI infrastructure, trading at a clear discount to its own history, with regulatory risk (Chinese gaming approvals, minor playtime limits) and ByteDance's relentless competition for user attention as the two things most likely to derail the current margin trajectory. This isn't a call to buy or avoid Tencent — just what the checklist says today.

Q3 2026 Headlines

Revenue reached ¥207.3B, up 8% YoY — a miss versus ~¥210.5B consensus and a slowdown from +11% in Q2. Marketing services rose 19% to ¥43.0B, FinTech & business services 9% to ¥63.7B (cloud +35%), and value-added services only 3% to ¥98.3B (domestic games +11%, international games -9% to ¥18.9B). Gross profit was ¥115.9B (+7%). On Tencent's reported (IFRS) basis, operating profit fell 7% to ¥59.15B and profit attributable to equity holders rose 0.8% to ¥63.6B; the company's adjusted (non-IFRS) basis shows non-IFRS operating profit -6% to ¥68.1B and non-IFRS net profit -6% to ¥66.5B (in line with consensus). Management attributes roughly ¥15B of the quarter's operating-profit drag to new AI products (Hy, Yuanbao, CodeBuddy, WorkBuddy, Xiaowei). The big change is cash: capital expenditure was ¥81.4B (+527% YoY, +54% QoQ; accrual basis — the cash capex-payments line was ¥65.1B, +225%), free cash flow was -¥69.1B (positive ¥16.9B excluding prepayments for compute procurement), and the balance sheet moved from ¥58.2B net cash at Jun 30 to ¥13.7B net debt at Sep 30. Management called cash capex “at peak levels.” About 15.9M shares (~HK$7.0B) were repurchased in the quarter.

Gross Margin Trend (FY2021-FY2025)
Gross margin has expanded every year since the FY2022 trough, from 43.08% to 56.25% — a +13.17pp gain versus FY2022 (the window used for criterion #14), driven by a richer games/social mix, ad-tech improvements, and cost discipline in fintech.
Market position, honestly stated: WeChat/Weixin's 1.43 billion combined MAU (Q1 2026, +2% YoY) is at or near a ceiling — it already exceeds China's entire mobile-internet user base of roughly 1.28 billion, meaning growth here is essentially over and the platform's value now comes from monetization (ads, mini-programs, fintech), not user growth. In gaming, Tencent and NetEase together take roughly 70% of the Chinese market (Tencent alone >50%), a tight duopoly that scores 2/5 on this framework's concentration scale — but Chinese gaming carries real regulatory risk: Beijing froze new game approvals for months in 2021-22 and maintains playtime limits for minors, and further intervention remains a standing risk, not a resolved one. ByteDance's Douyin ecosystem is the other structural threat, competing hard with WeChat for user attention and advertiser budgets even though it doesn't touch Tencent's gaming or fintech businesses directly.

Trailing & Forward P/E — FY21-25 & Current
Current trailing P/E of roughly 10.9x (price-scaled estimate at the $42.50 close; TTM EPS held at the prior basis as Q3 IFRS profit was +0.8% YoY) sits far below the FY21-25 average of 18.45x (dashed) — a deep discount versus Tencent's own recent history. Forward P/E (roughly 9.6x, price-scaled, shown as the single point at right) is lower still — see explainer below for why that isn't automatically a bargain.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and normally the more useful lens for judging whether a "cheap" multiple reflects genuinely cheap growth or just a slowdown priced in. For Tencent specifically: trailing P/E (about 10.9x, estimate) is a deep discount to its own FY21-25 average (18.45x), and forward P/E (about 9.6x, estimate) is cheaper still. But the PEG ratio — forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth — tells a less flattering story: with consensus EPS growth running around 4-7% over the next two years, Tencent's PEG works out to roughly 1.8 (estimate: price-scaled forward P/E; consensus EPS growth not yet refreshed for the Q3 capex step-up), still above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line. In other words, the low absolute multiple mostly reflects genuinely slower expected earnings growth, not an obvious mispricing — this is the one place the "cheap-looking" headline number and the growth-adjusted reality pull in different directions.
Balance sheet: Tencent ended FY2025 with net cash of ¥16.15B (Net Debt/EBITDA -0.06x), but by Sep 30, 2026 it held ¥13.7B of net debt (vs ¥58.2B net cash at Jun 30) after compute prepayments and capex — still a very small fraction of EBITDA, so the <2x bar is cleared easily. The position has swung around this window — net debt of ¥59.7B (FY2021) and ¥68.4B (FY2022), briefly net cash in FY2023 (¥7.9B), back to net debt in FY2024 (¥19.7B), then net cash again in FY2025. Every year in the window comfortably clears this framework's <2x bar regardless of sign. ROIC has climbed steadily from 11.13% (FY2022) to 17.71% (FY2025, 18.27% current), clearing both the 15% absolute bar and the assumed 10.5% WACC. Buybacks were net share-reducing in 4 of the last 5 fiscal years (FY22 +0.01%, FY23 +0.88%, FY24 +2.10%, FY25 +1.74%), with a mild dilution year only in FY2021 (-0.48%).

What's Working

A genuine post-crackdown margin recovery (operating margin 19.99%→32.67%, gross margin 43.08%→56.25% since FY2022; Q3 2026 gross margin still about 56%), a balance sheet that is net cash on an FY2025 basis (and only marginally net debt now), ROIC comfortably above both the 15% bar and assumed WACC, four straight years of net buybacks, and a dominant, largely unchallenged position in Chinese social/messaging and global gaming revenue.

What to Watch

Revenue and FCF growth both miss this framework's 15% bar, and WeChat/Weixin's user base (1.43B MAU) is at or past saturation relative to China's mobile-internet population, so future growth leans on monetization, not new users. Chinese gaming regulation is a standing risk, not a resolved one, after the 2021-22 approval freeze. ByteDance's Douyin continues to pressure ad share and user attention. TCEHY itself trades as an unsponsored OTC ADR (lower liquidity/wider spreads than the primary HKEX 0700.HK listing), and the stock's "cheap" ~9.6x forward P/E (estimate) still carries a PEG near 1.8 once slower expected growth is factored in. New in Q3 2026: capex up sixfold, free cash flow of -¥69.1B, a revenue miss (+8% vs ~¥210.5B expected), IFRS operating profit -7% and a net-cash-to-net-debt swing — the payoff from AI spending is not yet visible in the P&L.
