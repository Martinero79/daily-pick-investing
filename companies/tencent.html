Tencent is one of the highest-quality businesses in this framework, and the scorecard mostly says so: 12 of 14 criteria met, a Martinero Index of 81. WeChat/Weixin's 1.43 billion combined monthly users anchor a platform that is simultaneously the world's largest video-game publisher by revenue, the #2 player in Chinese mobile payments, and a genuine margin story — operating margin has climbed from 19.99% in the FY2022 regulatory-crackdown trough to 32.67% in FY2025, gross margin is up 13.17 points over the same window, and the balance sheet sits in a net-cash position with buybacks running for four straight years. ROIC (17.71%, current basis 18.27%) comfortably clears both the 15% absolute bar and an assumed 10.5% WACC. Where the checklist bites is growth: 5-year revenue CAGR is 7.63% and FCF CAGR is 10.25%, both well short of the 15% bar this framework applies everywhere — and because the window starts at FY2021's peak and dips through FY2022's crackdown-driven trough (revenue actually fell 0.99% that year), even the "good" EBIT CAGR of 18.67% is flattered by a depressed base rather than representing clean, linear growth. The valuation looks statistically cheap at 14.33x trailing P/E versus Tencent's own FY21-25 average of 18.45x, but a forward P/E of 12.64x against consensus EPS growth in the mid-single-digits pencils out to a PEG north of 2 — not the bargain the headline multiple suggests once growth is priced in. Net: a dominant, cash-generative platform business trading at a reasonable-but-not-cheap multiple, with regulatory risk (Chinese gaming approvals, minor playtime limits) and ByteDance's relentless competition for user attention as the two things most likely to derail the current margin trajectory. This isn't a call to buy or avoid Tencent — just what the checklist says today.

Quarterly Development & the Chinese Gaming/Social Landscape
Tencent reports calendar quarters. Latest reported period: Q2 2026 (three months to Jun 30, 2026, reported Aug 12, 2026).
Q2 2026 Headlines

Revenue reached ¥204.8B, up 11% YoY. Gross profit was ¥118.4B (+13% YoY, +6% QoQ), with gross margin improving to 58%; within that, Value-Added Services (games + social) gross margin hit 64%, up 4 points YoY on a richer mix of internally-developed, higher-margin titles. Non-IFRS net profit rose 9% YoY to ¥68.4B. This continues the post-crackdown pattern visible in the annual numbers: revenue growth in the low-double-digits, margin expansion running ahead of revenue growth as mix shifts toward higher-margin games and advertising.

Gross Margin Trend (FY2021-FY2025)
Gross margin has expanded every year since the FY2022 trough, from 43.08% to 56.25% — a +13.17pp gain versus FY2022 (the window used for criterion #14), driven by a richer games/social mix, ad-tech improvements, and cost discipline in fintech.
Market position, honestly stated: WeChat/Weixin's 1.43 billion combined MAU (Q1 2026, +2% YoY) is at or near a ceiling — it already exceeds China's entire mobile-internet user base of roughly 1.28 billion, meaning growth here is essentially over and the platform's value now comes from monetization (ads, mini-programs, fintech), not user growth. In gaming, Tencent and NetEase together take roughly 70% of the Chinese market (Tencent alone >50%), a tight duopoly that scores 2/5 on this framework's concentration scale — but Chinese gaming carries real regulatory risk: Beijing froze new game approvals for months in 2021-22 and maintains playtime limits for minors, and further intervention remains a standing risk, not a resolved one. ByteDance's Douyin ecosystem is the other structural threat, competing hard with WeChat for user attention and advertiser budgets even though it doesn't touch Tencent's gaming or fintech businesses directly.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and normally the more useful lens for judging whether a "cheap" multiple reflects genuinely cheap growth or just a slowdown priced in. For Tencent specifically: trailing P/E (14.33x) is a real discount to its own FY21-25 average (18.45x), and forward P/E (12.64x) is cheaper still. But the PEG ratio — forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth — tells a less flattering story: with consensus EPS growth running around 4-7% over the next two years, Tencent's PEG works out to roughly 2.3, well above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line. In other words, the low absolute multiple mostly reflects genuinely slower expected earnings growth, not an obvious mispricing — this is the one place the "cheap-looking" headline number and the growth-adjusted reality pull in different directions.
Balance sheet: Tencent ended FY2025 with net cash of ¥16.15B (Net Debt/EBITDA -0.06x), a position that has swung around this window — net debt of ¥59.7B (FY2021) and ¥68.4B (FY2022), briefly net cash in FY2023 (¥7.9B), back to net debt in FY2024 (¥19.7B), then net cash again in FY2025. Every year in the window comfortably clears this framework's <2x bar regardless of sign. ROIC has climbed steadily from 11.13% (FY2022) to 17.71% (FY2025, 18.27% current), clearing both the 15% absolute bar and the assumed 10.5% WACC. Buybacks were net share-reducing in 4 of the last 5 fiscal years (FY22 +0.01%, FY23 +0.88%, FY24 +2.10%, FY25 +1.74%), with a mild dilution year only in FY2021 (-0.48%).

What's Working

A genuine post-crackdown margin recovery (operating margin 19.99%→32.67%, gross margin 43.08%→56.25% since FY2022), a net-cash balance sheet, ROIC comfortably above both the 15% bar and assumed WACC, four straight years of net buybacks, and a dominant, largely unchallenged position in Chinese social/messaging and global gaming revenue.

What to Watch

Revenue and FCF growth both miss this framework's 15% bar, and WeChat/Weixin's user base (1.43B MAU) is at or past saturation relative to China's mobile-internet population, so future growth leans on monetization, not new users. Chinese gaming regulation is a standing risk, not a resolved one, after the 2021-22 approval freeze. ByteDance's Douyin continues to pressure ad share and user attention. TCEHY itself trades as an unsponsored OTC ADR (lower liquidity/wider spreads than the primary HKEX 0700.HK listing), and the stock's "cheap" 12.64x forward P/E carries a PEG near 2.3 once slower expected growth is factored in.
