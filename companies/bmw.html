BMW Group is one of the three German premium car makers, with three brands (BMW, MINI, Rolls-Royce), a motorcycle business (BMW Motorrad) and a captive finance arm (Financial Services). It delivered 2.46M vehicles in FY2025 (flat YoY; 17.9% fully electric), earned €133.5B revenue and €10.2B group earnings before tax (7.7% margin), and returned about €4B to shareholders through the dividend and buybacks. Since then the picture has deteriorated quickly: on June 16, 2026 BMW cut guidance after a sharp downturn in China (H1 retail sales -20%, Q2 -30%) and rising Chinese exports that increased competition elsewhere, and the Automotive EBIT margin guidance fell from 4-6% to 1-3%. Q2 2026 group EBIT was €1.6B (-38% YoY) and the Automotive margin was 2.3%, including about 1.25 points of tariff burden and 1.2 points of depreciation from the China JV (BBA) purchase accounting. At its September 30 Capital Market Day, new CEO Milan Nedeljković did not give a 2026-27 recovery date; the company aims to return to its 8-10% Automotive margin corridor by the early 2030s, with a 3-5% interim range in 2028, and Automotive FCF of at least €7B at that point; further decisions are expected by spring 2027. The shares have fallen from €93.14 at the end of 2025 to €54.22.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Captive finance — consolidated debt, FCF and ROIC are distorted by Financial Services, so Automotive-segment FCF and net financial assets are used where disclosed (see the definition note above). (2) One-offs in FY2022 — FY2022 net income (€17.9B) and Automotive FCF (~€11B) include a ~€7.7B remeasurement gain and roughly €5B of cash acquired on consolidating BMW Brilliance Automotive (BBA); these distort FY2022 comparisons rather than the FY2021 and FY2025 end-points used for the CAGRs. (3) Earnings are cyclical and falling — a trailing P/E of ~5x on FY2025-TTM earnings reflects peak-to-trough risk, not a guarantee of cheapness; 2026 group EBT is guided “significantly lower”.

China Downturn & Latest Quarter (Q2 2026)
The near-term case rests on whether cost cuts, the Neue Klasse ramp and Europe/US strength can offset China and tariffs. Reported July 30, 2026; Q3 2026 is due Nov 4, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was €31.26B (-7.9% YoY) and group EBIT €1.61B (-38% YoY; BMW cites ~€1.63B); group earnings before tax were €1.7B (-35%). The Automotive segment earned €629M, a 2.3% margin versus 5.4% a year earlier, including about 1.25 points of tariff burden and 1.2 points of BBA purchase-price depreciation. Volume, mix and pricing took about €1.8B off EBIT (mostly China) and currencies about €0.4B, partly offset by €1.2B of positive other cost items; expenses were cut by ~€0.4B in the quarter. Deliveries fell 4.9% to ~591,000: Europe +7.6%, US +11.9%, China -30.2%. Automotive FCF was only ~€0.5B (Q2 2025: ~€1.9B) after a ~€1.3B inventory build, and H1 FCF was €1.3B (-45%). Financial Services earnings rose 15.7% in the quarter and Motorcycles earned a 15.2% margin.

Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership
Valuation uses trailing P/E on reported EPS (ordinary shares) at unadjusted Xetra year-end closes. For a cyclical automaker with falling earnings, a low P/E can signal peak earnings rather than cheapness; read it with the forward P/E below.
Trailing P/E — FY-End vs. Current
Current P/E (~5.2x = €54.22 / TTM EPS €10.52) is ~92% of BMW's FY2021-25 year-end average (~5.6x). Year-end closes (Xetra): €88.49, 83.38, 100.78, 78.98, 93.14; EPS: 18.77, 27.31, 17.68, 11.62, 11.89. The FY2022 low reflects peak earnings (including the BBA gain), not cheapness.

Forward P/E & PEG: forward P/E (next-12-month consensus, S&P) is ~7.6x, which implies consensus EPS of roughly €7.1, about a third below FY2025's €11.89. S&P's year-end forward P/E series averages ~6.4x for FY2021-25 (it may use dividend-adjusted prices, so treat it as approximate), putting today's forward multiple at ~1.2x its own average. The scorecard scores the trailing ratio (0.92 = 3/5) for consistency with the rest of the site; on the forward basis the ratio would score 1/5. PEG is ~2.3 per S&P but not meaningful with earnings falling. Average analyst target €71.23 (21 analysts, consensus Hold) implies ~31% upside; targets are third-party and may lag the latest guidance cut.
Balance sheet, Automotive vs consolidated: BMW reports Automotive net financial assets of more than €44B at year-end 2025 (almost €46B at year-end 2024), meaning the industrial business is a large net-cash position. The consolidated balance sheet (S&P) shows €110.5B of total debt against €15.6B of cash and investments at FY2025 (€101.1B net debt at June 30, 2026), but most of that funds the Financial Services lease and loan book, which is matched by receivables. I use the Automotive figure for net debt/EBITDA and say so in the scorecard. Capex ratio: 5.4% of revenue in FY2025 (€7.2B, down 20% from the 2024 peak of €9.1B, 6.4%); management's strategic corridor is below 5% by 2027, and R&D at 4-5%.

Share Count & Buybacks

The share count fell from about 657M (end-2021, ordinary + preferred) to about 599M now (S&P), roughly -9%, through three buyback programmes (the third, €2B, runs to April 2027 at the latest but its final tranche is due to finish Nov 30, 2026). The 2026 preferred-to-ordinary conversion does not change the number of shares. I derived the end-2021 figure from S&P's net-debt-per-share data and BMW's reported share capital, so treat it as approximate.

Founders, insiders & honest caveats: BMW is controlled by the Quandt family (~47%, an approximate figure; I did not verify the exact current percentages for each holder). That is a genuine long-term anchor and a reason the company has rarely been a takeover target, but it also means minority shareholders have limited influence over strategy and dividends. CEO Oliver Zipse handed over to Milan Nedeljković in 2026; I could not verify management's own shareholdings.

What's Working

The industrial business is a net-cash position (Automotive net financial assets over €44B), the Quandt family has kept BMW stable and independent for decades, and the group still earns a profit through the cycle (FY2025 net income €7.3B). Volumes held at 2.46M in FY2025, Europe and the US are growing in 2026 (Q2 +7.6% and +11.9%), the Neue Klasse (iX3, i3) ramp is under way with strong early demand, and Financial Services and Motorcycles remain profitable. The share count is down ~9% since 2021, cost reductions of €2.5B were delivered in FY2025, capex is falling toward the below-5% corridor, and the stock trades at about 5x trailing earnings with an ~8% dividend yield.

What to Watch

Earnings, margins and cash flow have all turned down: group EBIT is 26% below FY2021, gross margin has lost 2.4 points in three years, the Automotive margin is guided to just 1-3% in 2026, and Automotive FCF has more than halved since FY2021. China is the swing factor (retail -30% in Q2) and Chinese exports are intensifying competition in Europe and Asia-Pacific, tariffs cost ~1.25 points of margin, and returns on capital are below the cost of capital. The Capital Market Day pushed the 8-10% margin target to the early 2030s with decisions not due until spring 2027. The cheap-looking trailing P/E is based on earnings that are guided significantly lower, the dividend is covered by a falling profit, and the Quandt stake limits minority influence.
