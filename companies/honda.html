Honda is two very different businesses under one roof. The motorcycle division is the best in its industry: world #1 by volume (22.1M units in FY2026), a record ¥731.9B segment profit on ¥4,018.8B of revenue (an 18.2% margin), and a Q1 FY2027 margin of 20.5% on strength in India and Brazil. The automobile division, which is about two-thirds of revenue, is the opposite: it swung to a ¥1,411.1B segment loss in FY2026 after ¥1,453.6B of EV-related charges (Honda booked ¥1,577.8B of EV-related losses in total), and tariffs, China price competition and over-capacity continue to squeeze margins. Financial services added ¥275.5B. The result was a consolidated operating loss of ¥414.3B (−1.90% margin), net loss of ¥423.9B and, on the framework's numbers, a long list of unmet criteria: revenue grew just +10.6% a year over FY2022-26, operating income is negative, ROIC is below any reasonable cost of capital, and trailing earnings are too depressed to give a meaningful P/E. What the framework does credit: Honda is genuinely #1 in motorcycles, the industrial (non-finance) balance sheet holds ¥3.3T of net cash, the share count is down roughly a quarter since FY2022 through large buybacks, and capex runs at a modest 3.4% of revenue. The recovery has started — Q1 FY2027 operating profit hit a record ¥530.7B with no EV charges, and management raised full-year operating-profit guidance from ¥500B to ¥650B (net income ¥400B) — but this checklist scores reported history, not guidance. 4 of 14 criteria met, Martinero Index 33/100.

What actually moved the numbers

Q4 FY2026 (Jan-Mar 2026) carried the EV reset: Honda cut its EV plans and booked ¥1,577.8B of EV-related losses for the full year (¥1,453.6B inside the automobile segment), producing a quarterly operating loss of about ¥958B (S&P basis) and Honda's first annual operating loss since its 1957 listing. Q1 FY2027 then rebounded sharply: revenue ¥6,061.5B (+13.5% YoY), operating profit ¥530.7B, a record for any quarter and up 117% YoY, with no EV-related charges. Motorcycles earned a record ¥233.9B (20.5% margin) on India and Brazil, and automobiles swung to a ¥192.1B profit from a ¥29.6B loss a year earlier as North American hybrid demand held up while China sales kept falling.

Guidance: Honda raised its FY2027 operating-profit forecast from ¥500B to ¥650B (net income ¥400B vs a ¥423.9B loss in FY2026). Earlier guidance had assumed about ¥500B of further EV-related losses, which management said would take adjusted profit to roughly ¥1.0T. Separately, Honda is reported to be pushing suppliers for more than $9B of cost cuts over four years, and agreed with Nissan (Aug 31, 2026) to jointly develop core vehicle software and ECUs for cars from FY2029. Tariffs and the Chinese price war remain headwinds. Guidance is management's estimate, not a reported result, and is not scored.

Trailing & Forward P/E — FY22-26 & Current
Trailing P/E sat at 7.1-9.0x through FY25 (Honda has traded at a persistent low multiple, 0.3-0.5x sales) and is not meaningful in FY26 and now. Forward P/E is about 9.9x at ¥1,687.5, versus an FY22-26 average of 7.99x — a ratio of 1.24x, i.e. a modest premium to its own history.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers, and why this one is awkward: current P/E is today's price divided by the last 12 months of actual earnings; the trailing series above is that ratio at each fiscal year-end. Forward P/E divides today's price by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings. For Honda the trailing number does not exist right now (TTM net income is −¥169.7B), so the framework falls back on forward vs forward: ≈9.9x now against a 7.99x average, ratio ≈1.24, which lands in the 1.15-1.30 band (1/5, Not met). Two cautions: the consensus forward P/E series is third-party data, and it sits well below what Honda's own guidance implies — net income guidance of ¥400B on a ≈¥6.6T market cap is about 16x. Honda's low multiples historically reflect cyclicality, thin auto margins and large cash tied up in captive finance. A single PEG figure is not meaningful with trailing earnings negative and consensus growth driven by a rebound from an impaired base.
Balance sheet: on an industrial (non-financial-services) basis Honda held net cash of ¥3,324.5B at Mar 31, 2026 (company-disclosed), which auto-qualifies the net-debt/EBITDA criterion. Consolidated total debt was ¥13.8T against ¥5.4T of cash and investments (net debt ¥8.4T at FY2026 and ¥8.9T at Jun 30, 2026), but most of that funds customer loans and leases in the captive-finance arm and is matched by receivables. Net debt has risen steadily from ¥4.5T in FY2022 as the finance book grew. Industrial free cash flow was ¥1,058.0B in FY2026, and dividends of ¥70 a share (≈4.1% yield) plus buybacks returned cash even in a loss year. Quarterly capex jumped to ¥579.7B in Q1 FY27 on S&P's measure (which includes vehicles on operating lease), so FY2027 capital intensity is worth watching.

What's Working

Honda is the world's #1 motorcycle maker and that franchise is at record profit (¥731.9B in FY2026, 20.5% margin in Q1 FY2027). The industrial balance sheet is net cash (¥3.3T), industrial free cash flow stayed positive every year of the window (¥1,058.0B in FY2026), the share count is down about a quarter since FY2022, and capex is modest at 3.4% of revenue. Q1 FY2027 delivered a record ¥530.7B operating profit with no EV charges, revenue growth re-accelerated to +13.5%, and guidance was raised to ¥650B.

What to Watch

FY2026 was a loss-making year (operating margin −1.90%), and the reported-history scorecard is correspondingly weak: negative EBIT, negative ROIC spread, no conversion band cleared and a gross margin 3pp below FY2023. Automobiles, two-thirds of revenue, face US tariffs, Chinese price competition and structural over-capacity, and the EV strategy has been reset at a cost of ¥1.58T with roughly another ¥500B of EV-related charges flagged in earlier FY2027 guidance. Consolidated debt keeps growing with the finance book, the multiple depends on a rebound that guidance (not yet history) describes, and there is no founder or controlling holder. Whether Q1 FY2027's record quarter proves durable once tariffs and the EV transition bite is the central open question.
