Cadence Design Systems, based in San Jose, California, sells the software (and some emulation hardware) that engineers use to design, simulate and verify integrated circuits, circuit boards and, via its System Design and Analysis business, whole electronic systems. Chipmakers, hyperscalers, automakers and aerospace firms license it on multi-year terms, which is why revenue is highly recurring: 18% recurring-revenue growth in Q3 2026, a record backlog of $8.2B and $4.2B expected to be recognised over the next 12 months. Revenue has grown every year at 11-19% (FY2025 $5.30B, +14.1%) and growth has accelerated in 2026 to +18.7%, +24.2% and +22.0% in the first three quarters, with Cadence guiding to about +21% for the year; the company points to AI-driven chip designs, record hardware sales and growing demand for its IP. Profitability is high but looks lower on a GAAP basis: GAAP operating margin was 28.2% in FY2025 (non-GAAP 44.6%) because of stock compensation (about 9% of revenue), amortisation and acquisition costs, and it dipped to 26.2% in Q3 2026. Gross margin has slipped from 89.6% (FY2022) to 86.4% (FY2025), the main blemish on the scorecard. The balance sheet moved from net cash to net debt of $1.18B in 2026 after roughly $2.1B of acquisitions. The shares are far from cheap: a trailing GAAP P/E of 65x (about 35x forward on non-GAAP-style consensus), though that is below the company’s own FY-end average of 70x. After the Q3 report the stock rose to about $343 and then gave back 4.3% on Oct 30 to $327.89, 21% below its June high.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) GAAP vs. non-GAAP — Cadence’s own headline margins and EPS are non-GAAP (44.1% margin, $2.11 EPS in Q3 2026) and exclude ~$0.53 per quarter of stock compensation plus amortisation and restructuring; this page scores on GAAP, which is why the margin looks about 15 points lower. (2) Acquisitions and the 53-week year — FY2021 had 53 weeks, and 2026 growth is partly acquired (goodwill rose from $2.7B to $4.9B in nine months), so 2026 organic growth is lower than the headline +22%. (3) Legal matters — FY2025 includes a $128.5m charge tied to the July 2025 settlements with the U.S. Department of Justice and Bureau of Industry and Security (export controls); China was 16% of Q3 2026 revenue. Estimates, ROIC/WACC and the market-share call are third-party or judgment and are flagged as such.

Latest Quarter & Momentum (Q3 2026)
Reported Oct 26, 2026 after the close (quarter ended Sep 30, 2026). Calendar quarters; FY = calendar year.
What the quarter actually showed

Revenue was $1,633m (+22.0% YoY, +3.1% QoQ), with double-digit growth in every product category: Core EDA +20%, semiconductor IP +24% and System Design and Analysis +30%; recurring revenue grew 18%. Non-GAAP EPS was $2.11 (vs $1.93) and above the ~$2.05 consensus, but GAAP EPS was flat at $1.05 and GAAP operating margin fell to 26.2% from 31.8% (non-GAAP 44.1% vs 47.6%) on higher amortisation (4.9% of revenue vs 1.9%), restructuring and acquisition costs. Backlog reached a record $8.2B. Cadence used $400m on buybacks in the quarter and the board added $2.5B to the authorisation. Free cash flow was $362m (22% of revenue), below Q2’s $582m. Cash paid for acquisitions was $2.14B in the first nine months, funded from cash and a revolver, and net debt is now $1.18B.

Revenue — Last 8 Quarters ($m)
Revenue has risen sequentially in each of the last six quarters, from $1,242m (Q1’25) to $1,633m. YoY growth: Q4’24 +26.9%, Q1’25 +23.1%, Q2’25 +20.3%, Q3’25 +10.2%, Q4’25 +6.2%, Q1’26 +18.7%, Q2’26 +24.2%, Q3’26 +22.0%. The Q3’25 and Q4’25 slowdown reflected tough comparisons and hardware timing.

Share Count & Buybacks

Basic shares were 273M in Q4 2021 and 274M in Q3 2026 (+0.4%); the YoY change is +0.3% (S&P). Cadence repurchased $800m of stock in the first nine months of 2026 ($400m in Q3) and the board just added $2.5B to the authorisation, but stock-based compensation (~$430m in 9M 2026) and tax-withholding offsets keep the count flat. Founders, insiders & honest caveats: Cadence has no founder or controlling holder. Net debt/EBITDA is ~0.55x on TTM GAAP EBITDA (~$2.16B); S&P shows 0.52x. Inventories rose to $449m from $304m at end-2025 (emulation hardware). Leverage is modest, but the shift from $489m net cash at end-2025 to $1.18B net debt came in nine months.

What's Working

Durable, recurring revenue (record $8.2B backlog, 18% recurring growth) in a three-vendor industry where Cadence is a co-leader, growth that has accelerated to +22% with guidance raised to about +21% for 2026, GAAP operating margin around 28-31%, FCF at 106% of EBIT in FY2025, capex under 3% of revenue and modest leverage (0.55x). The shares are about 21% below their June high, trailing GAAP P/E (65x) is below the company’s 70x FY-end average, and forward P/E (~35x) is well below its ~45x average.

What to Watch

Valuation is still very high in absolute terms (65x trailing GAAP, ~15x sales) and the stock needs sustained mid-teens growth. Gross margin has fallen 3.2pp in three years (score 0 on criterion 14), GAAP margin dropped to 26.2% in Q3 2026 on acquisitions, ROIC has halved from its 2022 peak, and FCF CAGR (11.2%) is below the 15% bar. Net debt appeared in 2026 after $2.1B of acquisitions, so integration risk is live. Other risks: customer concentration, export controls and the July 2025 DOJ/BIS settlement obligations (China was 16% of Q3 revenue), and competition from Synopsys and Siemens.
