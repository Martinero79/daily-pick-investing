Celestica, headquartered in Toronto and led by CEO Rob Mionis, designs and manufactures electronics for other companies. Its Connectivity & Cloud Solutions segment (Communications plus Enterprise, about 74% of FY2025 revenue per S&P segment data) makes the networking switches, servers and storage that hyperscalers deploy for AI and cloud; its Advanced Technology Solutions segment (aerospace and defence, industrial, health-tech and capital equipment, about 26%) is slower and steadier. Revenue grew from $5.6B in FY2021 to $12.4B in FY2025 (+22% a year), gross margin widened from 8.9% to 12.1% and EBIT margin from 3.4% to 8.6%, which drove EBIT up about 5.7x and net income about 8x. Q2 2026 revenue reached $4.70B (+62% YoY, CCS +84%), with a record 8.2% adjusted operating margin and adjusted EPS of $2.54; the 2026 outlook was raised to ~$20.5B revenue, $11.30 adjusted EPS and $600M of free cash flow. The flip side is that this is a low-margin, working-capital-heavy business with extreme customer concentration: the three largest customers were 32%, 14% and 12% of FY2025 revenue (58% together) and the top ten were 79%. The stock has risen from $11.13 at the end of 2021 to $371.57, roughly 33x, while trailing EPS rose about 12x, so most of the move is earnings growth but the P/E has also expanded from ~14x to ~39x.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Customer concentration — three customers (all in CCS) were 58% of FY2025 revenue and the top ten 79%, up from 64% in FY2023; the 10-K names Amazon, Meta, Google, Ciena, Dell, HPE and IBM among CCS customers but does not say which is the 32% customer. (2) Thin margins and heavy working capital — a 12% gross margin leaves little room for error, inventories rose from $2.2B to $3.4B in the first half of 2026, and FCF was only ~43% of EBIT in FY2025 while capex steps up toward ~$1B in 2026 (company plan from January). (3) Valuation and volatility — ~39x trailing earnings is about 1.9x the stock's own FY21-25 average, and the shares move sharply (beta ~1.5; -13.3% on Aug 6, +11.6% on Sep 3 and -4.3% on Oct 7, 2026).

Quarterly Trend (Q3 2024 – Q2 2026)
Eight quarters. YoY revenue growth has stepped up from ~20% to 62% as hyperscale networking and compute programs ramped, while gross margin has stayed in a 10–13% band. Q3 2026 results are due Oct 26, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was $4,699M (+62% YoY, +16% on Q1), against a guide of $4.15–4.45B. CCS revenue was $3.81B (+84%, segment margin 8.7%), of which Hardware Platform Solutions (the switch and platform business) was ~$1.9B (+58%); ATS was $0.89B (+8%, segment margin 6.3%). GAAP earnings from operations were $458M (9.8% of revenue, including a $104M total-return-swap gain); the adjusted operating margin of 8.2% was a company record. Adjusted EPS was $2.54 (guide $2.14–2.34) and GAAP EPS $3.17. Free cash flow was only $147M because capex jumped to $264M and inventories rose by $729M in the quarter. The 2026 outlook was raised to ~$20.5B revenue (from $19.0B), $11.30 adjusted EPS (from $10.15), 8.4% adjusted operating margin and $600M free cash flow, and management expects 2027 growth to exceed the 65% anticipated for 2026.

What's Working

Revenue is accelerating (+62% in Q2 2026) as AI and cloud customers order 800G and 1.6T networking and compute programs, gross margin has risen four years in a row, and adjusted operating margin reached a record 8.2%. Return on invested capital is ~37% with almost no net debt, the share count is ~9% lower than in FY2021, and management has raised the 2026 outlook several times this year and guides to faster growth in 2027.

What to Watch

The valuation: ~39x trailing and ~25–33x forward earnings versus a ~21x trailing average, with the stock volatile. Concentration: three customers were 58% of FY2025 revenue. Margins are thin (12% gross, 8.6% EBIT) and free cash flow lags profit (43% of EBIT) as inventories, receivables and a ~$1B capex program absorb cash. A pause in hyperscaler AI spending or a program loss would hit a business with little pricing power.
