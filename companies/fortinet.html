Market concentration score: 4 / 5 — network security is fragmented
Fortinet is one of the most profitable large security vendors: GAAP operating margin of 30.5% in FY2025, free cash flow above EBIT, net cash of $4.3B and founders who still own roughly 19% between them. The business sells its own firewall hardware (product revenue) and then attaches security subscriptions and support, so margins depend on both hardware costs and the services mix. 2026 has been a hardware upgrade cycle: product revenue grew 52% in Q2 and 37% in Q3 as customers bought ahead of memory-driven price increases. The question after the Q3 print on Oct 28 is how long that cycle lasts, and what it does to gross margin. The stock fell from $185.00 (Oct 27) to $139.10 (Oct 29), taking the trailing GAAP P/E from about 60x to about 45x.
On the “gross margin miss”: the drop is attributed to gross margin in market commentary, but the numbers are more nuanced. Q3 non-GAAP gross margin was 79.6% against a guided 79-81% range (midpoint 80.0%), so it landed inside the range, 0.4pp under the midpoint and 2.0pp below a year ago. What changed the picture was the outlook: Q4 gross margin guided to 78-80% (79.0% midpoint), a $40M cut to full-year service revenue guidance, hardware growth implied to slow to about 20% in Q4, and inventory up 39% in one quarter to $591M. Explanation of the reaction beyond these facts is third-party opinion (see page 3).

Quarterly Development — Q3 2026 and the Gross-Margin Question
Latest reported quarter: Q3 2026 (three months to Sep 30, 2026), reported Wed Oct 28, 2026 after the close. Q3 revenue $2.078B, +20.5% YoY, versus the company's $2.010-2.100B guide; billings $2.336B, +29.2%.
What moved the stock: gross margin, guidance and the hardware cycle

The margin facts. GAAP gross margin was 78.95% in Q3 2026 versus 80.77% a year earlier (-1.8pp) and 80.24% in Q2. Non-GAAP gross margin was 79.6% (-2.0pp YoY) against guidance of 79-81%. The hardware (product) gross margin fell about 3.3pp to 64.4% as memory-chip and other component costs rose faster than pricing; product is now 37% of revenue (765 of 2,078), so a falling product margin pulls the blended margin down. Q4 non-GAAP gross margin is guided to 78-80% (79.0% midpoint), lower than Q3's 79.6%, and FY2026 stays at 79.7-80.3%. Operating margin still beat: 38.2% non-GAAP versus a 35-37% guide, because sales and R&D grew slower than revenue.

Other things in the print. Full-year revenue guidance was raised $70M to $8.125-8.215B (the smallest of three raises), but service revenue guidance was cut $40M to $5.15-5.17B and service growth slowed to 12.7%. Product revenue growth cooled from 52% in Q2 to 37%, and the Q4 guide implies about 15.5% total revenue growth. Inventory rose 39% in the quarter to $591M. Free cash flow was strong at $865-882M (about 42% of revenue) and the buyback authorization was raised by $1.2B, although no shares were repurchased in Q3.

Interpretation (third-party, not company statements). Analyst commentary reported after the print (for example Barron's: a key revenue driver may have peaked) points to a pull-forward of firewall purchases ahead of price increases, a services ramp that has not yet followed, and margin pressure from memory costs. Roughly a dozen brokers cut price targets on Oct 29. Whether gross margin is the main reason for the drop, versus the guidance and growth deceleration, cannot be separated from the price move alone; the stock was also already down 5.9% on Oct 28, the day of the report.

What's Working

Margins and cash: gross margin is 5pp above FY2022, GAAP operating margin has gone from 19% to 31%, FCF exceeds EBIT and the company holds $4.3B net cash. Founders own about 19% and the share count is down about 11% since FY2021. Q3 revenue grew 20% and billings 29%, deferred revenue is building, and full-year revenue, billings and EPS guidance were all raised.

What to Watch

Gross margin is now falling: -1.8pp YoY in GAAP terms with Q4 guided lower again, tied to memory-chip costs and a bigger hardware mix. Service revenue growth (12.7%) is lagging hardware (37%) and the service guide was cut; inventory is up 39% and purchase commitments are $1.67B; the Q4 guide implies hardware growth slowing sharply, with a tough 2027 comparison if 2026 pulled demand forward. The valuation is still a premium to a typical software multiple (about 45x trailing GAAP) after the fall. Market share and ROIC are judgment or estimate-based.
