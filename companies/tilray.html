Tilray Brands is a lifestyle consumer-products company built around cannabis cultivation, processing and distribution, plus a fast-growing beverage-alcohol business and a smaller wellness/CBD segment. It traces its roots to Tilray Inc. (founded 2013/2014 in Nanaimo, BC, then centered on Leamington, Ontario) and took its current form via the 2021 all-stock merger with Aphria, after which the combined company retained the Tilray name and relocated its principal executive offices to New York while keeping the bulk of its cultivation and processing footprint in Canada. Since then it has pursued an aggressive, debt-and-equity-funded acquisition strategy that has fundamentally reshaped the business: HEXO Corp and Truss Beverage (2023), and a run of U.S. craft-beer and spirits acquisitions — SweetWater Brewing, Breckenridge Distillery, Montauk Brewing, Shock Top, 10 Barrel, Hop Valley, Sweetwater and, most recently, the UK's BrewDog — have turned Tilray into a genuine multi-brand alcohol company as much as a cannabis one; beverage-alcohol is now roughly 28% of revenue. Tilray also owns CC Pharma and Tilray Medical, positioning it as a leading distributor and grower for the fast-growing European medical-cannabis market (Germany, Portugal, UK). The result is a real, ~$915m-revenue operating business — genuinely different from this site's pre-revenue psychedelics-biotech names — but one with a deeply messy financial history: multiple large acquisitions funded mostly by new share issuance, a currency- and segment-complex P&L spanning cannabis, alcohol, distribution and wellness in several countries, and a string of large non-cash goodwill and intangible-asset writedowns as the original cannabis-boom valuations of acquired assets proved unsupportable. GAAP profitability has never been achieved; the FY2025 net loss alone was -$2.19 billion, driven almost entirely by a $2.1 billion non-cash impairment charge, not an operating cash crisis.
Reverse split & dilution, read together: Tilray executed a 1-for-10 reverse stock split on December 2, 2025 to regain Nasdaq minimum-bid-price compliance after the shares fell toward $1. That reset the nominal share count, but it did not stop dilution: shares outstanding (split-adjusted throughout for comparability) have risen from roughly 53.3 million (FY2022) to 136.99 million currently — up ~157% over four-plus years, including +25.6% in just the past twelve months, funded almost entirely by at-the-market equity issuance. Every price and CAGR figure on this dashboard is split-adjusted; treat the reverse split itself as a cosmetic reset, not a fix for the underlying dilution problem.
The FY2025 writedown, in plain terms: Goodwill fell from $2.64 billion (FY2022) to $752 million (FY2025-26) and other intangible assets fell from $915m (FY2024) to $21m (FY2025) as Tilray wrote down the carrying value of acquired cannabis and beverage assets that hadn't lived up to their purchase price. This produced the -$24.56 FY2025 EPS figure and the -$2.19bn FY2025 net loss shown on page 2 — a one-off accounting event, not a sign the underlying business collapsed that year (FY2025 revenue actually grew 4.1% and TTM revenue growth has since re-accelerated to +11.5%). Read GAAP net income/EPS on this dashboard with that context; operating cash flow and EBIT are the more useful lines for judging the ongoing business.

Segment Mix & Recent Performance
Four segments spanning cannabis, alcohol, wholesale distribution and wellness/CBD — a genuinely diversified but genuinely complex P&L, not a single-product cannabis pure-play.
Four segments, one P&L

Distribution (35.7% of TTM revenue, $327.2m) is Tilray's largest segment by dollars — low-margin European pharmaceutical and wellness-product wholesale distribution via CC Pharma and Alcohol Countries, which brings scale but thin margins. Cannabis (29.3%, $268.3m) covers Canadian adult-use and medical cannabis plus international medical cannabis exports (Germany, Portugal, UK, Australia). Beverage (27.7%, $254.0m) is the fastest-growing and highest-margin segment, built through the SweetWater, Breckenridge, Montauk, Shock Top, 10 Barrel, Hop Valley and (most recently) BrewDog acquisitions — Tilray now describes itself as one of the largest craft beverage-alcohol platforms in the U.S. by volume. Wellness (7.2%, $65.9m) is the smallest segment, hemp-based food and wellness products. This mix is the direct explanation for the gross-margin improvement on page 2: as beverage-alcohol (higher-margin) grows faster than distribution (lower-margin), blended gross margin rises even without any single segment getting structurally better on its own.

What's Working

Genuine, real operating revenue with positive growth (+11.5% TTM, ~9.9% 4-year CAGR) — not a pre-revenue story like Tilray's psychedelics-biotech siblings on this site. Operating losses have narrowed by roughly 77% since FY2022 (-$230.4m to -$52.6m TTM) as the business scales. Gross margin has structurally improved, from 18.94% (FY2022) to 28.68% (TTM), driven by the higher-margin beverage-alcohol segment (now ~28% of revenue) diluting the historically thin, oversupplied Canadian wholesale-cannabis mix. Tilray holds genuine #1 positions — largest cannabis company in Canada by revenue and a leading global medical-cannabis distributor across Europe via CC Pharma/Tilray Medical — and its TTM P/S of ~0.615x sits well below its own 5-year average of ~1.90x, a statistically cheap entry point by this narrow measure. Balance-sheet liquidity is adequate for now (current ratio 2.21x, quick ratio 1.17x, Debt/Equity 0.25x), and a real U.S. craft-beverage platform (BrewDog, Breckenridge, Montauk, Shock Top and others) has been built to diversify away from cannabis-only exposure.

What to Watch

This is a name with almost every quality-compounder metric failing, and several failures are severe rather than marginal. Shares outstanding have roughly tripled since FY2022 (53.3m to 136.99m currently, +25.6% in the past year alone) even after the December 2025 1-for-10 reverse split reset the nominal count — dilution has been relentless, funded mostly by at-the-market equity issuance rather than debt. FY2025 alone absorbed a $2.1 billion non-cash writedown that dragged goodwill from $2.64bn (FY2022) to $752m (FY2025-26) and produced a -$2.19bn net loss (EPS -$24.56) — Tilray's serial acquisitions have repeatedly outrun the earnings power those deals actually delivered. Net debt/EBITDA is effectively unusable as a ratio (TTM EBITDA is barely positive at $15.0m against $167.1m of net debt), ROIC (-3.11%) remains deeply negative against an ~11.2% WACC, insider ownership is thin (2.23%), and the stock's Altman Z-Score of -1.63 sits in the model's distress zone with a low Piotroski F-Score of 3/9. Short interest remains elevated at ~13% of float. Free cash flow has been negative in 4 of the last 5 fiscal years, so this is a name to track for an actual, sustained inflection in cash generation and dilution discipline — not one to buy on narrative or federal-legalization optionality alone.
