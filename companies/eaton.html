Eaton Corporation plc is an Irish-domiciled power-management company headquartered in Dublin. Its largest and fastest-growing franchise is electrical equipment — switchgear, busway, UPS, circuit protection and power distribution for data centres, utilities, commercial buildings and industry — which together made up about 73% of FY2025 revenue. Aerospace (fuel, hydraulic and motion-control systems) added 15%, and Vehicle and eMobility the rest; in 2026 Eaton agreed to separate its Mobility business through a Reverse Morris Trust. Over FY2021-25 revenue grew from $19.6B to $27.4B, GAAP operating income more than doubled from $2.5B to $5.3B as operating margin widened by 6.5 points, and free cash flow rose from $1.6B to $3.6B. The story turned more acquisitive in 2026: roughly $11B of cash acquisitions in the twelve months to June (mainly Boyd Thermal, a liquid-cooling business) lifted net debt to $20.6B and pushed Q2 2026 sales up 21% to a record $8.5B, with 14% organic growth, while GAAP margins dipped on amortisation and deal costs. Backlogs are the bullish datapoint: at June 2026 they were up 33% year on year in Electrical Americas, up 103% in Electrical Global and up 28% in Aerospace. The stock sits about 23% below its 52-week high of $478 after falling about 6% on each of Oct 22 and Oct 30 (no company-specific cause identified in the sources reviewed), ahead of Q3 results on Nov 3.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) GAAP vs adjusted — this page scores GAAP figures; Eaton's adjusted EPS excludes amortisation, deal and restructuring costs ($1.04 per share in Q2 2026: $3.15 adjusted vs $2.11 GAAP). (2) M&A — 2026 growth and leverage both include Boyd Thermal, so FY2021-25 CAGRs are mostly organic but TTM figures are not. (3) Pending catalyst — Q3 2026 results (Nov 3) will update every TTM number here; this page is based on Q2 2026 reported data and the Oct 30 price.

What actually moved the numbers

Q2 2026 sales were a record $8,531m, up 21.4% year on year (14% organic, 7% from acquisitions, per the company). Electrical Global was the standout: sales rose to $2,517m from $1,753m, with Boyd Thermal contributing about 25 points in its first full quarter and 18% organic growth. Electrical Americas grew 18% to $3,951m. Operating margin was 16.6% on a GAAP basis (Eaton's segment margin: 23.1%, 80bp below Q2 2025) and GAAP EPS fell 16% to $2.11 as amortisation and acquisition charges ($0.99 per share together) hit; adjusted EPS was a Q2 record of $3.15. Free cash flow was $874m (Q2 2025: $716m). The share price moves in October (-6.3% on Oct 22, -6.0% on Oct 30) were not tied to company news in the sources reviewed.

What's Working

Operating income has compounded at ~21% a year since FY2021 and FCF at ~22%, helped by a 5.5-point gross-margin gain, and the business is exposed to data-centre, utility and aerospace demand with record backlogs (Electrical Americas +33%, Global +103%, Aerospace +28% year on year at June 2026). Capex is a steady ~3% of sales, the dividend has risen 16 years in a row, and management raised FY2026 organic growth guidance to 11-13% with a Q3 guide of 13.5-15.5%. The forward P/E (~24x) is in line with its own FY2021-25 average even though the trailing GAAP multiple looks high.

What to Watch

The $11B+ of 2026 acquisitions took net debt to $20.6B (3.1x TTM EBITDA) and cut GAAP margins and ROIC; buybacks are paused and more deals are pending (COL Group). GAAP EPS fell 16% in Q2 2026 even as revenue rose 21%. The stock is already down ~23% from its high on an AI/data-centre sentiment swing, and the trailing P/E (37.7x) is 24% above its own average. Eaton is #3 in a competitive global market, has no insider block, and FCF conversion (67%) is below the 80% bar. Q3 results on Nov 3 will reset every TTM figure here. Market-share, ROIC/WACC and employee-ownership inputs are estimates or unverified.
