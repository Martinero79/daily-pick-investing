PepsiCo is really two businesses wearing one ticker. Frito-Lay is a dominant, hard-to-dislodge #1 in savory snacks with a distribution network competitors can't easily replicate. The beverage side is a genuine duopoly story: Pepsi is a durable, real #2 to Coca-Cola in carbonated soft drinks, while Gatorade is the clear #1 in sports drinks. Both halves are under real pressure right now for the same underlying reason — GLP-1 weight-loss drug adoption and value-seeking consumers have driven North America snack and soda volumes lower for multiple consecutive quarters, and it shows up directly in the growth numbers: five-year revenue CAGR of just 4.3% and EBIT CAGR of 6.0%, both far short of this framework's 15% bar for a mature consumer-staples giant. That growth pressure, combined with a roughly 25% drop in market value from PepsiCo's 2023 peak, is what drew activist investor Elliott Management to build a ~$4 billion stake in September 2025 and publicly push the board to refranchise its company-owned bottling operations and fix what it calls execution missteps in North America beverages. The balance sheet and returns picture is more reassuring: ROIC has held a steady 18-21% for five years, comfortably clearing an assumed 7.5% WACC, and the current trailing P/E of 18.04x sits well below PepsiCo's own FY21-25 average of roughly 26.3x. But that "cheap vs. history" read comes with a catch — the PEG ratio, which adjusts for expected growth, sits around 3.1x, well above the traditional 1.0 fair-value line, so the discount isn't a screaming bargain once slower growth is priced in. Add in a Net Debt/EBITDA that's been stuck just above 2x for five years, FCF conversion running roughly 45-59% against an 80% bar, and no founder or controlling shareholder (insiders own just 0.61% of shares), and the checklist lands at 7 of 14 criteria met and a Martinero Index of 56/100 — a real, durable business with two genuinely strong brand franchises, scored down by mature growth mechanics and cash-conversion math that this framework is built to catch. This isn't a call to avoid or buy PepsiCo — just what the checklist says today.

Quarterly Development, GLP-1 Headwinds & the Elliott Campaign
PepsiCo's fiscal quarters run roughly Jan-Mar, Apr-Jun, Jul-Sep, Oct-Dec (12-13 week periods). Latest reported period: Q2 FY2026 (12 weeks to Jun 13, 2026, reported Jul 9, 2026).
Q2 FY2026 Headlines

Reported net revenue was $24.18B, up 6.4% YoY, but roughly 2.2 percentage points of that was a foreign-exchange tailwind. Organic revenue — PepsiCo's preferred underlying growth measure, which strips out FX, M&A and divestitures — grew a slower 2.4%, on 3% global food volume growth and 2% beverage volume growth. Nearly all of that volume growth came from international markets: North America foods volume was flat and North America beverages volume fell 4% in the quarter, which is exactly the gap between "reported" and "organic" growth, and between the U.S. and the rest of the world, that Elliott's campaign and the GLP-1 narrative below are both about.

Gross Margin Trend (FY2021-FY2025)
Gross margin has actually held up better than the revenue growth story suggests — 53.39% (FY2021) to 54.46% (FY2025), a +1.16pp improvement versus the FY2022 base used for criterion #14, as pricing/mix and productivity programs offset input-cost inflation.
GLP-1 & the demand backdrop: PepsiCo's North American snack and soda volumes have declined for multiple consecutive quarters. Analysts at firms including Barclays and Morgan Stanley have flagged GLP-1 weight-loss drug adoption (used in an estimated one in five U.S. households) as a real, structural drag on calorie-dense snack and soda consumption, compounding more ordinary value-seeking and inflation pressures. PepsiCo's response has leaned into portion control, higher-protein items and "better-for-you" reformulations (Sun Chips with legumes, Stacy's whole-grain pita chips) rather than deny the shift is happening.
The Elliott campaign: In September 2025, activist investor Elliott Investment Management disclosed a roughly $4 billion stake in PepsiCo — among its top-five active holdings — and publicly pushed the board to refranchise its company-owned bottling operations and address what it called strategic missteps and market-share losses in North America beverages, arguing the stock had 50%+ upside from where it sat. PepsiCo's market value had fallen from a 2023 peak near $270B to roughly $190-200B by the time of the campaign. Management said it welcomes shareholder dialogue while defending its current strategy; as of this writing, it remains an unresolved, material overhang on the stock.

Trailing & Forward P/E — FY21-25 & Current
Current trailing P/E of 18.04x sits well below the FY21-25 average of roughly 26.3x — a genuine discount versus PepsiCo's own recent history, driven by the stock's decline from 2023 highs amid the GLP-1/volume/activist overhang rather than a change in trailing earnings. Forward P/E (26.10x–16.93x FY21-25, now 15.80x) has run below trailing P/E throughout — see explainer below.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed. For PepsiCo specifically: both trailing (18.04x) and forward (15.80x) P/E sit well below their FY21-25 averages, a real discount versus its own history. But the PEG ratio — forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether a "cheap" multiple is actually cheap once growth is accounted for — currently runs around 3.1x for PepsiCo, well above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line. That's the honest read: the stock is cheaper than its own history, but not cheap relative to how slowly earnings are now expected to grow.
Balance sheet: net debt of $43.50B against EBITDA, for a Net Debt/EBITDA of 2.39x (FY2025) — just above this framework's <2x bar, and essentially rangebound for five years: 2.52x (FY2021), 2.42x (FY2022), 2.21x (FY2023), 2.16x (FY2024), 2.39x (FY2025). ROIC has stayed remarkably steady — 18.11% (FY2021) → 19.49% (FY2022) → 20.57% (FY2023) → 21.35% (FY2024) → 20.10% (FY2025) — clearing both the assumed 7.5% WACC and this framework's 15% bar every year in the window. What's Working

Two genuinely strong brand franchises — Frito-Lay's dominant snacks position and a real Coke/Pepsi duopoly in beverages with Gatorade's outright #1 in sports drinks. ROIC has held a steady 18-21% for five years, comfortably clearing WACC. The entry multiple is meaningfully below PepsiCo's own recent history, share count keeps drifting lower, capex intensity is low and improving, and gross margin has actually improved versus three years ago despite the growth slowdown.

What to Watch

Growth is genuinely mature — 4.3% revenue CAGR and multiple quarters of North America volume declines tied in part to GLP-1-driven demand shifts. FCF conversion is structurally weak (45-59% vs. an 80% bar) and net debt/EBITDA has been stuck just above 2x for five years. There's no founder or controlling shareholder to anchor governance, which is part of why activist investor Elliott built a $4B stake in September 2025 and is publicly pushing for a bottling refranchise and a North America beverages turnaround — an unresolved overhang heading into 2026-27.
