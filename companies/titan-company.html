Titan Company Ltd was founded in 1984 as a joint venture between the Tata Group and the Tamil Nadu Industrial Development Corporation (TIDCO). It began as a watch maker and is now mostly a jewellery company: Tanishq, Mia, Zoya and CaratLane sell gold and studded jewellery through a network of owned and franchised stores, and in Q4 FY2026 Titan completed the purchase of a 67% stake in Damas, a GCC jewellery retailer. Total stores reached 3,758 at September 2026. The model is high-turnover but capital-heavy in working capital terms: Titan must hold large gold and diamond inventories (inventory days ~220), funded partly by borrowings and customer gold-exchange schemes, while capex is only ~1% of revenue. Revenue has grown from ₹28,799 cr in FY2022 to ₹87,584 cr in FY2026 (+32% CAGR), helped by market-share gains from unorganised jewellers, a higher gold price, and the Damas consolidation. Q2 FY2027 (Sep 2026 quarter) was reported on Nov 5, 2026: statutory revenue ₹20,614 cr (+10.1% YoY; Titan's ex-bullion total income ₹20,533 cr, +25%), operating profit ₹2,983 cr, and net profit ₹2,393 cr (+114%). Most of that profit jump is not operating: it includes a ₹536 cr customs-duty inventory gain and a ₹671 cr non-cash gain from remeasuring the Damas minority put option; the company's adjusted PAT was ₹1,722 cr (+54%) and a third-party estimate of fully normalised growth is ~16%. The share trades at ~61x GAAP trailing earnings (~68x excluding the put-option gain), below its own FY2022-26 year-end average of ~84x but far above most large-cap peers on this site.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Gold price & bullion — gold is the main input; statutory revenue includes bullion/digi-gold sales (about ₹2,300 cr in Q2 FY2026 vs very little in Q2 FY2027), so statutory growth is lumpy and not like-for-like; Titan's ex-bullion figures are shown separately where used. (2) Customs duty — the government's gold duty increase gave Titan inventory gains of ₹407 cr (Q1) and ₹536 cr (Q2 FY2027), which lifted margins; management did not guarantee they persist, and a duty cut would reverse the benefit. (3) Damas & put-option accounting — Damas is consolidated from Q4 FY2026 (international jewellery revenue +105% YoY in Q2 FY2027); the ₹671 cr put-option credit is non-cash and non-operating.

Quarterly Trend & Latest Quarter (Q2 FY2027, Sep 2026)
Eight quarters from Q3 FY2025 (Dec 2024) to Q2 FY2027 (Sep 30, 2026), statutory consolidated, ₹ crore. Q2 FY2027 was reported on Nov 5, 2026. Q3 is the festive/wedding quarter, so sequential comparisons are seasonal.
What the quarter actually showed

Reported net profit more than doubled to ₹2,393 cr (statutory), but about ₹1,200 cr of the year-on-year jump is non-operating: a ₹671 cr non-cash credit on the Damas put option and a ₹536 cr customs-duty inventory gain. Titan's adjusted PAT was ₹1,722 cr (+54%), and a third-party estimate of fully normalised growth is ~16%. Titan's ex-bullion total income was ₹20,533 cr (+25%); jewellery grew 24% to ₹17,495 cr (India +21%, CaratLane +31%, international +105% with Damas), watches +27% to ₹1,881 cr at a 21.9% EBIT margin, EyeCare +28%. India jewellery EBIT margin was 13.1% reported but 10.7% excluding the duty gain, consistent with management's ~11% “centre of gravity”. Jewellery revenue fell ~6.7% sequentially as the festive season shifted into Q3, and the Oct 6 business update had already shown growth below some analyst forecasts. The weak spot was cash: third-party analysis shows H1 FY2027 operating cash flow of about −₹11,177 cr on an ~₹8,065 cr inventory build, funded partly by a newly approved ₹2,500 cr NCD programme.

Share Count & Dividends

Equity capital has been ₹89 cr (₹1 face value, ~88.8 crore shares) throughout FY2022-26: no buybacks, no dilution. The board recommended a ₹15 per share dividend for FY2026 (~26% payout; yield ~0.3% at the current price). Promoter holding has been exactly 52.90% for the whole period.

Founders, insiders & honest caveats: Titan has no individual founder block. The promoter group is the Tata Group (with Tata Sons the largest) and TIDCO, the Tamil Nadu state development corporation, which together hold a controlling 52.9%; management (MD Ajoy Chawla) holds little. The scorecard treats this as a controlling, aligned stake but it is institutional, not founder-led. The Q2 FY2027 filing also announced the retirement of Noel Tata from the Board.
Balance sheet & cash flow: Screener shows borrowings of ₹27,638 cr and investments of ₹2,672 cr at Sep 2026 (Mar 2026: ₹30,621 cr and ₹3,506 cr), against equity of about ₹18.3k cr; third-party summaries put financial borrowings at ₹24,265 cr and debt-to-equity at 1.51x versus 0.93x at March 2026, so definitions differ and the leverage figure here (~2.4x net debt/EBITDA) should be read as an approximate, conservative estimate. Titan fully redeemed ₹1,000 cr and ₹850 cr of commercial paper in November 2026, and has approved a ₹2,500 cr NCD programme to fund the festive inventory build. H1 FY2027 operating cash flow was negative (third-party figure), which makes FY2026's 63% FCF conversion a high-water mark rather than a trend.

What's Working

Titan has grown revenue ~32% a year for four years and EBIT ~26% a year, while share count is flat and capex is ~1% of revenue. It is the clear leader in Indian branded jewellery and watches, trusted brands and the Tata name help it take share from unorganised jewellers, and the store network reached 3,758 at Sep 2026. Q2 FY2027 showed broad growth (jewellery +24% ex-bullion, watches +27% with a 21.9% EBIT margin, EyeCare +28%), international is now positive at the EBIT line, and ROIC appears to be above the cost of capital. A controlling Tata/TIDCO owner provides stability, gross margin has held within about 1pp over three years, and the trailing P/E is below Titan's own five-year average.

What to Watch

The headline profit jump in Q2 FY2027 is mostly one-offs (₹536 cr duty gain, ₹671 cr put-option credit); normalised growth was ~16%, and a customs-duty cut would reverse the tailwind. EBIT margin is only ~8.6% on statutory revenue and jewellery margins are structurally thin. Cash conversion is weak: H1 FY2027 operating cash flow was negative, borrowings are up sharply and net debt/EBITDA is ~2.4x. The stock is still ~61x GAAP trailing earnings, so it needs 15–20% compounding to hold up. Growth faces very high bases in the Dec, Mar and Jun quarters, gold-price volatility affects demand and inventory, Damas is still loss-making at the core, and the market is fragmented.
