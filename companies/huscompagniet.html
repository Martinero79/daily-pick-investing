HusCompagniet is Denmark's largest builder of single-family detached and semi-detached houses, founded in 1972 and headquartered in Tilst near Aarhus. It designs, sells and builds customizable houses on customer-owned land across Denmark, plus prefabricated wood-framed houses in Sweden under the VårgårdaHus brand — a genuinely asset-light model since it rarely owns land or heavy fixed plant. It previously also operated in Germany (the Danhaus brand, acquired under EQT ownership) but exited that market in 2021; today the footprint is Denmark and Sweden only. The company IPO'd on Nasdaq Copenhagen in June 2020, backed at the time by private equity firm EQT, which has since fully exited its stake. This is a small, thinly-traded, deeply cyclical stock — not a steady compounder. Danish house-building went through a severe downturn as interest rates spiked in 2022-23: revenue nearly halved from a FY2022 peak of DKK 4,330m to DKK 2,297m by FY2024, and operating margin fell from 6.9% to 2.4% over the same stretch. FY2025 brought a genuine volume recovery (revenue +28.7% to DKK 2,957m) as the Danish economy and employment held up, and HusCompagniet used the downturn to take further market share (now ~22% of Danish detached houses, more than double the nearest rival) — but profitability kept falling anyway, because three troubled Semi-detached B2B (professional investor) projects, signed at the wrong time on the wrong terms, are still working their way through the order book and are expected to drag on results into 2027. Gross margin fell to 16.5% in FY2025 from 20.7% in FY2024, and full-year EBIT margin was just 0.52%. Management's own long-term target is an 8-10% EBITDA margin — a long way from today's ~2% — and they've been explicit it won't be reached until the legacy problem projects roll off, "post-2027." This is a name for people who believe in a multi-year Danish housing-market and margin-normalization story and are willing to sit through more bad-news quarters to get there, not a name to buy for what the trailing numbers show today.
Three problem B2B projects are the whole current story: HusCompagniet flagged in 2025 that three Semi-detached projects sold to professional/institutional investors were loss-making or margin-negative, driving the FY2025 EBITDA/EBIT collapse and pushing 2026 guidance down. Management's response has been to shrink and simplify — divesting the Esbjerg prefabrication factory in April 2026 to "return to the core of the Semi-detached business" and shift toward a more asset-light posture. FY2026 guidance: revenue DKK 3.0-3.3bn, EBITDA DKK 70-130m (a wide range reflecting genuine uncertainty on how fast the legacy projects clear). No dividend is planned until net debt/EBITDA falls back under 2x (it stood at 6.96x at FY2025-end).
Early 2026 read: Q1 2026 revenue grew 25% YoY to DKK 791m, but EBIT was only DKK 5m (margin ~0.6%) as gross margin fell further to 16.4% (Q1 2025: 19.5%) — partly the coldest Danish January in 16 years, partly the same legacy B2B project drag. Management said H1 2026 results "executed on strategic priorities" and maintained full-year guidance. Read this as: the volume/demand recovery is real and market share keeps growing, but margin recovery is not yet visible in the reported numbers — it's a 2027-and-beyond thesis, not a today thesis.

Recent Performance & 2026 Guidance
The cyclical recovery is real on the top line; profitability recovery is not yet visible and isn't guided to show up meaningfully until after 2027.
The three problem projects, in plain terms

Through 2025, HusCompagniet disclosed that three Semi-detached projects sold to professional/institutional (B2B) investors were structurally loss-making or margin-negative — likely fixed-price contracts signed before construction-cost and financing conditions moved against the company. Q3 2025 alone saw EBITDA fall to DKK 8m (0.9% margin) from DKK 32m (5.5%) a year earlier, and EBIT turn negative (-DKK 4m vs +DKK 20m). Management's full-year 2025 EBITDA guidance was cut mid-year as a result. The company's response has been to shrink the B2B/Semi-detached footprint and simplify: it divested its Esbjerg prefabrication factory in April 2026, explicitly to "return to the core of the Semi-detached business" and move toward a more asset-light model, rather than owning manufacturing capacity for a segment that just burned it.

Treat backlog growth and margin recovery as two separate questions until the legacy projects have fully rolled off (guided into 2027).

Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership

Price / Free Cash Flow — FY-End vs. Current
Current P/FCF (~5.5x, using TTM FCF) sits below HusCompagniet's own FY21-25 average (~6.9x) — cheap-looking on this basis. But see the callout below: this metric is flattered the same way FCF/EBIT is, by a cash-generative-but-shrinking-earnings business, and shouldn't be read in isolation.

Why P/E isn't usable here: trailing P/E was 8.20x (FY21) and 4.33x (FY22) in the pre-downturn years, then spiked to a meaningless 68.21x in FY23 as earnings neared zero, and is simply not computable in FY24, FY25 or currently because the company reported a net loss each period. Forward P/E (consensus next-12-month estimate) currently sits around 40-56x depending on the exact date/estimate revision — also not a useful "cheap vs. expensive" signal, since it's dividing today's price by a still-tiny, early-recovery earnings estimate rather than reflecting genuine overvaluation. The PEG ratio (~1.2x on the latest estimate vintage) is similarly distorted by the tiny earnings-growth denominator this early in a recovery and shouldn't be trusted either. P/FCF is used above instead because free cash flow has stayed a real, positive number throughout the cycle — but even that flatters a business whose core EBIT has nearly vanished. Bottom line: there is currently no clean, single-number way to say whether HUSCO is "cheap" — you're pricing a recovery in earnings that hasn't shown up yet, on very thin trading volume (52-wk range DKK 29.50-52.00, average 20-day volume under 9,000 shares).

Share Count Trend

Shares outstanding rose from an EPS-implied ~19.3m (FY2021) to 21.43m currently, up roughly +11% — mostly IPO-era option/RSU-related dilution rather than large follow-on equity raises. Dilution has slowed sharply recently (+0.44% in the past year), and there is no buyback program in place.

Ownership note: HusCompagniet IPO'd on Nasdaq Copenhagen in June 2020 backed by private equity firm EQT, which has since fully exited (EQT's own site now lists it as a "former portfolio company"). The largest current shareholder is Lind Invest ApS, the investment vehicle of Danish investor Henrik Lind, at ~20.0% of shares and votes (notified Oct 2025) — a meaningful, concentrated stake, but a financial investor's holding rather than founder or operating-management skin in the game. Narrowly-defined insider ownership (board and executives) is only ~2.3%. Other 5%+ holders are conventional Danish/Nordic institutional asset managers (PFA, Danske Invest, BI Asset Management) and Danske Bank, holding voting-rights notification thresholds rather than strategic stakes.

What's Working
  • Genuinely dominant, growing Danish market position — ~22% share of detached houses, more than double the nearest rival, and share gains continued through the downturn and recovery alike
  • Structurally very low capex (<1% of revenue every year) — builds on customer-owned land, no land bank, a real asset-light characteristic of the business model
  • Free cash flow has stayed positive and comfortably ahead of EBIT throughout the cycle, aided by working-capital/customer-deposit dynamics specific to house-building
  • Volume/demand recovery is real: FY2025 revenue +28.7%, Q1 2026 +25% YoY, order backlog reported up ~40% YoY through H1 2025
  • Management is actively simplifying the troubled Semi-detached B2B business (Esbjerg factory divestment, April 2026) rather than doubling down on it
  • Net debt in absolute DKK terms is at a 5-year low (~DKK 197m) — the leverage ratio problem is an earnings problem, not a balance-sheet-risk problem, and the current ratio (1.39x) and quick ratio (1.14x) show no near-term liquidity strain
What to Watch
  • Every growth and profitability metric on this scorecard is currently failing — this is a name priced on a margin-normalization story that management itself says won't show up until "post-2027"
  • Three legacy Semi-detached B2B projects are still working through the order book and are the single biggest swing factor on 2026-27 earnings — a name-specific execution risk on top of the macro cycle
  • ROIC (~0.7%) is nowhere near covering even a conservative cost of capital estimate (~7.4%) — capital currently being deployed is not clearing a reasonable hurdle rate
  • Net debt/EBITDA of 6.96x looks alarming in isolation; it will only normalize if EBITDA recovers, which is exactly the uncertain part of the thesis
  • Very thin trading liquidity (52-wk range DKK 29.50-52.00, ~9,000 shares/day average volume) — a small, illiquid Nordic micro/small-cap, not a name you can size up carelessly
  • No dividend, and none planned until leverage falls back under 2x — this is a pure earnings-recovery bet with no income cushion while you wait
  • Only 3 sell-side analysts cover the stock — limited independent scrutiny of management's guidance and recovery timeline
