Prosegur is one of Europe's largest private security companies, founded in Madrid in 1976 and still controlled today by the Gut Revoredo family (Helena Revoredo, widow of founder José María Gut Santaularia, and her children — CEO Christian Gut Revoredo among them) through the family holding vehicle Gubel S.L. It is a genuinely diversified conglomerate, not a single-product business: Security (guarding, technological security, ~53% of FY2025 revenue) protects premises and people; Cash (cash-in-transit, ATM management, coin/note processing — competing globally with Brink's and Loomis) is the largest single profit contributor; Alarms (branded Prosegur Alarms, including the Movistar Prosegur Alarms joint venture in Spain) provides residential and small-business monitored-alarm subscriptions; and AVOS (added-value outsourcing services) rounds out the portfolio. This is the critical framing difference versus today's daily pick, Verisure: Verisure is a pure-play, subscription-driven alarm-monitoring business with a single, high-margin, recurring-revenue model and market leadership across Europe and Latin America. Prosegur Alarmas is Verisure's most direct European/LatAm competitor by product, but it is a small (~4.8% of group revenue), faster-growing, higher-margin division embedded inside a much larger, lower-margin, more cyclical, more capital-intensive parent. Comparing the two stocks directly without this context would be comparing a specialist to a conglomerate.
Alarms is the bright spot, but it's small: Prosegur Alarms' customer base grew 10.7% to 1.06 million connections in FY2025 (117,000 net new adds), churn improved from 12% to 9%, and ARPU rose 4.8% — genuinely strong subscription-business metrics. But at €234.6m of FY2025's €4,930m consolidated revenue, Alarms is roughly one-twentieth the size of the whole group, and still a distant #2/#3 to Verisure's several-million-customer European/LatAm footprint.
Currency is a real, recurring swing factor: Prosegur has meaningful exposure to Argentina and Brazil (Cash and Security operate across most of Latin America). FY2025 results explicitly cited Argentine peso and macro headwinds holding back reported (EUR) growth even as local-currency/organic growth stayed solid, and net profit still surged +44-53% on operating leverage and easier prior-year comparisons. Read every reported EUR growth figure here alongside the organic-growth figure — they can diverge meaningfully in a given year.

Prosegur vs. Verisure — the honest comparison: Verisure is a single-product, high-margin, recurring-revenue alarm-monitoring specialist and the clear #1 in Europe by customer count. Prosegur Alarms is a genuinely strong, improving business on its own metrics (churn, ARPU, growth) but is #2/#3 in its category, and sits inside a parent company whose other three segments (guarding, cash logistics, outsourcing) are structurally lower-margin, more cyclical, and far more capital- and labor-intensive. An investor buying PSG stock is buying all four businesses, currency exposure to Argentina/Brazil, and a controlling family shareholder — not a leveraged bet on the European alarm-monitoring market specifically.

This is a meaningful structural strength on the ownership-alignment criterion, though a ~75% controlling stake also means minority shareholders have limited influence over strategy or capital allocation decisions.

What's Working

A genuinely deep, controlling family ownership stake (~75%, with a family member as CEO) that is rare for a company of this size, an essentially flat share count with no dilution, and remarkably stable gross margins despite meaningful Argentina/Brazil currency exposure. EBIT has compounded at over 16% annually over five years, faster than revenue, showing real operating leverage. The Alarms segment specifically — the part of the business that actually competes with Verisure — is executing well on its own terms: customer base up 10.7%, churn improving from 12% to 9%, and ARPU growing, even though it remains small relative to the group. Net debt/EBITDA has been trending down toward the company's own 2.0-2.2x target.

What to Watch

This is a structurally low-margin, capital-intensive conglomerate, not a high-margin compounder — EBIT margin (6.5%) is nowhere near the 20% bar, and free cash flow has not grown in five years despite EBIT nearly doubling, meaning cash conversion and working-capital discipline deserve scrutiny each quarter. Leverage (2.4x net debt/EBITDA on the company's own measure) remains above its own 2.0-2.2x target. Consolidated ROIC isn't separately disclosed, but the listed Cash subsidiary reporting ROIC near 0% against its own cost of capital is a caution flag for capital efficiency group-wide. Currency (Argentine peso, Brazilian real) will keep distorting reported EUR growth figures year to year — always check organic growth alongside the headline number. And remember the core framing: Alarms, the Verisure-comparable business, is only ~5% of this company: buying PSG is a bet on guarding and cash logistics first, alarms a distant third.

Maarten's note

I'm building this one purely as a competitor reference next to Verisure, not as a pick in its own right. The two companies look similar on the surface — both Spanish-rooted, both deep in European and Latin American residential security — but they're actually very different investments. Verisure is a clean, single-product subscription bet: you're paying for churn, ARPU, and market leadership in one specific business. Prosegur is a diversified holding company wrapped around four businesses with different margin profiles and different competitive dynamics, where the part that resembles Verisure (Alarms) is a rounding error next to guarding and cash-in-transit.

The family control is the part I find most interesting here, for better and worse. ~75% ownership by the Gut Revoredo family, with a family member as CEO, is the kind of skin-in-the-game alignment that's genuinely rare at this size — but it also means minority shareholders are along for whatever capital-allocation ride the family chooses, with limited ability to push back. Combined with real Argentina/Brazil currency exposure and margins that are structurally thin (not temporarily depressed), I read Prosegur as reasonably priced for what it is — a stable, family-run, cash-generative-if-unglamorous conglomerate — rather than either cheap or expensive. It's not obviously a value trap and it's not obviously undervalued; it's a fairly transparent trade-off between diversification/stability and margin/growth ceiling. Worth watching mainly to see whether Alarms keeps outgrowing the rest of the group fast enough to eventually move the needle on consolidated margins.
