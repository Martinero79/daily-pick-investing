Affirm is a US buy-now-pay-later and consumer-installment network founded in 2012 by Max Levchin, a PayPal co-founder, and listed on Nasdaq in January 2021. It underwrites each purchase in real time, then earns money four ways: network fees from merchants and its Affirm Card ($1.44B in FY2026), interest on loans it holds ($2.05B), gains on loans sold to capital-markets partners ($0.60B) and servicing income ($0.17B). Volume is the engine: GMV crossed $50B in FY2026 and by Sep 30, 2026 had 28.7 million active consumers (+19%), 621 thousand active merchants (+48%) and 6.0 million Affirm Card holders, while the top five partners fell to 42% of GMV from 46% in FY2026. The shift to profit has been rapid: GAAP operating income went from -$1.16B in FY2023 to +$417M in FY2026, helped by revenue less transaction costs growing faster than costs and hiring discipline described in the shareholder letter. The stock, however, sits about 34% below its August spike: it reached $90.44 intraday on Aug 28, the day after fiscal Q4 results, closed that day at $77.76 and fell to $59.44 by Nov 17, including a drop of roughly 8% on Nov 5, the day of the Q1 FY2027 print. Q1 FY2027 was strong — GMV $14.4B (+34%), revenue $1,262M (+35%), operating income $187M (14.8% margin) — though provisions for credit losses rose 59% to $259M, and FY2027 guidance now calls for more than $65.25B GMV and a GAAP operating margin above 14.75%.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Tax benefit — FY2026 net income of $1.93B (and Q4's $1.62B) includes a ~$1.46B deferred-tax benefit from releasing a valuation allowance; GAAP operating income ($417M) is the cleaner profit measure, and the trailing P/E (about 10.3x on my TTM estimate) is not meaningful. (2) Funding model — FCF of $993M looks like 238% of EBIT but excludes loan originations and the debt that funds them; Affirm is closer to a lender than a software company, and credit losses (provision $797M, 18.7% of revenue) rise with volume. (3) Negative base years — EBIT and FCF were negative in FY2021-23, so growth rates from FY2021 are not computable; read the trajectory, not a CAGR.

Latest Quarter (Fiscal Q1 FY2027, July – September 2026)
The investment case now turns on whether 30%+ volume growth can hold while operating margin widens toward the 14.75%+ FY2027 guide and credit costs (provisions +59% in Q1) stay in check. Reported on Nov 5, 2026; fiscal Q2 2027 is due around February 2027 (estimated).
What the quarter actually showed

Q1 FY2027 revenue was $1,262M (+35.2% YoY), the 12th consecutive quarter of 30%+ GMV growth (GMV $14.4B, +34%). Revenue less transaction costs (RLTC) grew 36% to $617M (4.3% of GMV), GAAP operating income nearly tripled to $187M (14.8% margin vs 6.8%) and adjusted operating income rose 44% to $380M (30.1% margin). Net income of $164M (+103%) carried $41M of income tax expense versus $2M a year ago, and GAAP diluted EPS was $0.47. Affirm Card GMV grew 117% to $3.3B, active cardholders reached 6.0 million (+112%) and direct-to-consumer GMV grew 55% to $5.1B. Quality flags: provision for credit losses rose 59% to $259M against 38% growth in average loans held for investment, 30+ day delinquencies (ex-Peloton) rose 35 bps sequentially to 2.9%, the allowance rose to 6.3% of loans from 5.9%, funding costs rose 27 bps sequentially, and $51M of enterprise warrant expense is inside GAAP operating income. Revenue was 8.79% of GMV (+11 bps YoY) even as network revenue grew only 23% on the D2C mix shift. The stock fell roughly 8% on Nov 5 (Trefis; about 9% intraday per 24/7 Wall St.) and closed at $59.44 on Nov 17. Third-party sources disagree on whether GAAP EPS beat or missed consensus; the figures above are from the company's shareholder letter.

What's Working

Growth plus a visible path to profit: revenue has compounded at +37% a year for five years, GMV has grown 30%+ for 12 straight quarters, and GAAP operating income swung from -$1.16B (FY2023) to +$417M (FY2026) with FY2027 guided above a 14.5% margin. Network effects are showing up in the numbers — 621 thousand active merchants (+48%), 28.7 million active consumers, Affirm Card GMV +117% and a top-five-partner share falling to 42% (FY2026) — and the customer mix is broadening beyond the original large e-commerce partners. Liquidity is solid (about $3.0B vs $1.13B of converts), funding capacity keeps growing, and margin stability scores well because gross margin has risen 6.4pp since FY2023. Forward-looking, the stock trades at 24.7x trailing FCF versus a 32.2x three-year average, with analysts' late-October average target (pre-print) about 65% above the price.

What to Watch

Credit and funding are the real risks: provisions were $797M in FY2026 (18.7% of revenue), the loan book is financed with $9.1B of funding debt and securitizations, Q1 FY27 provisions rose 59% to $259M with 30+ day delinquencies at 2.9%, and the guidance assumes rising short-term rates. Profit is thin and young — a 9.8% GAAP operating margin, ROIC of 3.6% against a 17.7% estimated cost of capital, and net income that is mostly a one-off tax benefit. Annual growth decelerated (+38.8% to +32.2% revenue in FY2026), though Q1 FY2027 re-accelerated to +35.2% and the FY2027 GMV guide is now +30%, the share count has risen about 19% since FY2022, and revenue per dollar of GMV is drifting down as lower-yield direct-to-consumer volume grows. Reading the comparison: Affirm grows about as fast as Shopify (+35% vs +34% latest quarter) and more than twice as fast as Visa and Mastercard, but sits well below all three on Martinero (62 vs 74–83). The gap is not growth; it is quality of earnings. Visa and Mastercard are asset-light toll booths with ~60% operating margins and return on capital far above their cost of capital, and Shopify has net cash with a near-zero capital burden. Affirm is a balance-sheet business: it takes credit risk and funds loans with $9.1B of debt, earns a 9.8% operating margin (FY2026) with ROIC of 3.6%, and is still diluting shareholders. The most relevant connection is that Shop Pay Installments, which Shopify offers, is powered by Affirm (launching in Australia, per the FQ4 letter), so Shopify is both a peer and a distribution partner. Affirm's momentum is accelerating toward profitability, not toward the stability the Visa/Mastercard scores reflect.
