Affirm is a US buy-now-pay-later and consumer-installment network founded in 2012 by Max Levchin, a PayPal co-founder, and listed on Nasdaq in January 2021. It underwrites each purchase in real time, then earns money four ways: network fees from merchants and its Affirm Card ($1.44B in FY2026), interest on loans it holds ($2.05B), gains on loans sold to capital-markets partners ($0.60B) and servicing income ($0.17B). Volume is the engine: GMV crossed $50B in FY2026 with 27.8 million active consumers (+21%), 571 thousand active merchants (+51%) and 5.2 million Affirm Card holders, while the top five partners fell to 42% of GMV from 46%. The shift to profit has been rapid: GAAP operating income went from -$1.16B in FY2023 to +$417M in FY2026, helped by revenue less transaction costs growing faster than costs and hiring discipline described in the shareholder letter. The stock, however, sits about 23% below its August spike: it reached $90.44 intraday on Aug 28, the day after fiscal Q4 results, closed that day at $77.76 and has drifted to $69.23. Q4 FY2026 was strong — GMV $14.1B (+36%), revenue $1,166M (+33%), operating income $147M (12.6% margin) — and FY2027 guidance calls for more than $64B GMV and a GAAP operating margin above 14.5%.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Tax benefit — FY2026 net income of $1.93B (and Q4's $1.62B) includes a ~$1.46B deferred-tax benefit from releasing a valuation allowance; GAAP operating income ($417M) is the cleaner profit measure, and the reported 12.5x P/E is not meaningful. (2) Funding model — FCF of $993M looks like 238% of EBIT but excludes loan originations and the debt that funds them; Affirm is closer to a lender than a software company, and credit losses (provision $797M, 18.7% of revenue) rise with volume. (3) Negative base years — EBIT and FCF were negative in FY2021-23, so growth rates from FY2021 are not computable; read the trajectory, not a CAGR.

Latest Quarter (Fiscal Q4 FY2026, April – June 2026)
The investment case now turns on whether 30%+ volume growth can hold while operating margin widens from 10% to more than 14.5%. Reported on Aug 27, 2026; fiscal Q1 2027 is due around Nov 5, 2026 (estimated).
What the quarter actually showed

Q4 FY2026 revenue was $1,166M (+33.0% YoY), the 11th consecutive quarter of 30%+ GMV growth (GMV $14.1B, +36%). Revenue less transaction costs (RLTC) grew 39% to $589M, GAAP operating income more than doubled to $147M (12.6% margin vs 6.6%) and adjusted operating income rose 49% to $353M (30.3% margin). Net income of $1.62B is mostly the $1.45B tax benefit and should be ignored for run-rate purposes. Affirm Card GMV grew 124% to $2.8B, Card holders passed 5.2 million (19% of active consumers) and transactions grew 41% to 52.9 million as lower-ticket Pay-in-X grew 41%. Two quality flags: a promotional event at a large enterprise partner shifted from FQ1 to FQ4, adding a low-single-digit benefit to the 36% GMV growth, and revenue as a share of GMV slipped 17 bps to 8.29% as direct-to-consumer volume (lower network revenue per dollar) grew faster than integrated checkout volume. The stock jumped intraday to $90.44 the next day, closed at $77.76 and has since given back about 11%.

What's Working

Growth plus a visible path to profit: revenue has compounded at +37% a year for five years, GMV has grown 30%+ for 11 straight quarters, and GAAP operating income swung from -$1.16B (FY2023) to +$417M (FY2026) with FY2027 guided above a 14.5% margin. Network effects are showing up in the numbers — 571 thousand active merchants (+51%), 27.8 million active consumers, Affirm Card GMV +124% and a top-five-partner share falling to 42% — and the customer mix is broadening beyond the original large e-commerce partners. Liquidity is solid (about $2.6B vs $1.13B of converts), funding capacity keeps growing, and margin stability scores well because gross margin has risen 6.4pp since FY2023. Forward-looking, the stock trades at 23.5x FCF versus a 32.2x three-year average, with analysts' average target about 43% above the price.

What to Watch

Credit and funding are the real risks: provisions were $797M in FY2026 (18.7% of revenue), the loan book is financed with $8.7B of funding debt and securitizations, and the guidance assumes rising short-term rates. Profit is thin and young — a 9.8% GAAP operating margin, ROIC of 3.6% against a 17.7% estimated cost of capital, and net income that is mostly a one-off tax benefit. Growth is decelerating (+38.8% to +32.2% revenue; FY2027 GMV guide of +27%), the share count has risen about 19% since FY2022, and revenue per dollar of GMV is drifting down as lower-yield direct-to-consumer volume grows. The stock is volatile (beta 3.8, a 52-week range of $42.10 to $90.44) and sold off about 11% after its post-earnings spike. Several inputs could not be fully verified: market-share percentages, Levchin's current voting power, WACC, and the Jun 2021 share price.
