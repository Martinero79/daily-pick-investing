Dr. Ing. h.c. F. Porsche AG is the Stuttgart sports-car maker founded in 1931 and part of the Volkswagen Group. It sells the 911, 718 Boxster/Cayman, Panamera, Cayenne, Macan and Taycan, and runs a captive finance arm (Porsche Financial Services, €4.4B of FY2025 revenue). It listed in September 2022 at €82.50 per preferred share, one of the largest European IPOs on record, with Volkswagen AG keeping control and Porsche SE taking a blocking minority of the ordinary shares. For its first two years as a listed company it reported operating margins around 18% in FY2022-23. Then demand in China weakened, the electric strategy (Taycan, battery unit Cellforce) underdelivered and US import tariffs arrived: FY2025 revenue fell 9.5% to €36.3B and operating profit fell from €5.6B to €0.4B, a 1.1% margin, after about €3.9B of extraordinary expenses. Management under CEO Michael Leiters has since shifted to a “value over volume” approach and a “Sportwagenschmiede 35” strategy: H1 2026 operating margin recovered to 7.8% (partly helped by €0.3B of provision releases), automotive net cash flow was €1.0B and the full-year outlook (5.5-7.5% margin) was reaffirmed, while deliveries fell 16.5%. The Bugatti Rimac stake sale (about €1B) closed in September 2026 and the MHP consulting unit is being sold to Tata Consultancy Services for €320M. The share price is about 48% below the IPO price. Read FY2025 as a reset year, not a run-rate, and note that the board has not yet shown that the 18% margins of FY2022-23 are recoverable.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) FY2025 charges — roughly €3.9B of extraordinary expenses (my arithmetic: adding them back would put FY2025 operating profit near €4.3B, an ~11.9% margin) make every FY2025-based ratio extreme: FCF/EBIT of 366%, P/E of 96x and a -47% EBIT CAGR are all artefacts of a trough year. (2) Captive finance — consolidated cash flow and debt include the Porsche Financial Services lease/loan book; this page uses the automotive (industrial) figures for FCF and net liquidity. (3) Controlled company — Volkswagen AG (75% minus one ordinary share) and Porsche SE (25% plus one) hold all voting shares; the listed preferred shares carry no vote, so the free float has no say over strategy.

Quarterly Trend & H1 2026 Reset
Eight quarters, Q3 2024 to Q2 2026. Revenue has fallen year on year in seven of the last eight quarters; profit hit a trough in Q3 2025 when the product-strategy realignment was booked, and has recovered since.
What actually moved the numbers

H1 2026 group sales were €17.23B (-5.1%) and operating profit €1.35B (+33.9%), a 7.8% margin versus 5.5% a year earlier. Deliveries fell 16.5% to 122,306 and the battery-electric share dropped to 19.4% (23.5%), so the profit recovery came from price/mix discipline and cost cuts, not volume. Realignment charges (~€0.4B) were largely offset by ~€0.3B of provision releases from supplier settlements, a one-off that flatters the half. Automotive net cash flow rose to €1.02B (H1 2025: €0.39B), a 6.7% margin, and automotive net liquidity stood at €7.3B. A “Future Package” agreement with the works council (job and site protection to 2035, €2.1B of investment in Zuffenhausen and Weissach) will add three-digit-million realignment costs in H2 2026 and again in 2027. Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance, and a strategy update follows at the Capital Markets Day on Oct 7.

What's Working

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the case: €7.3B of automotive net cash, an undrawn €2.5B credit line and positive automotive net cash flow every year, even FY2025 (€1.5B). H1 2026 showed a real recovery in profitability (7.8% margin versus 5.5%) and cash flow (€1.0B versus €0.4B) despite 16.5% fewer deliveries, and management reaffirmed its FY2026 guidance. The brand still commands pricing power relative to mass-market peers (gross margin was 29% in FY2023), Porsche is the clear volume leader among luxury sports-car makers, and portfolio pruning (Bugatti Rimac, MHP) is bringing in cash and focus. Control by Volkswagen and Porsche SE means a stable, long-term shareholder base.

What to Watch

Margins have not yet been proven recoverable: FY2025 was 1.1%, the FY2026 target is only 5.5-7.5% versus 18% in FY2022-23, and part of H1's improvement came from one-off provision releases. Volumes are falling (-16.5% in H1) with China down sharply and US tariffs ongoing, the electric strategy is being scaled back, and the Future Package will cost another three-digit million in H2 2026 and 2027. ROIC is below WACC, capex plus capitalised development is ~10% of sales, the dividend was cut and the FY2025 payout exceeded earnings. At ~24x forward earnings the shares are not cheap against their own history. As a controlled company with non-voting listed shares, minority holders have little influence. Items I could not verify: exact 2026 ownership percentages, management holdings, and the new FY2026 cash-flow outlook after the Bugatti Rimac sale.
