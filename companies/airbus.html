Airbus is one half of the commercial-aircraft duopoly, building the A220/A320/A330/A350 families against Boeing's 737/777/787. Its order backlog runs to 9,222 aircraft as of mid-2026 — more than ten years of production already locked in at current delivery rates. But a duopoly position doesn't automatically make a great scorecard: aerospace manufacturing is capital-intensive and structurally thin-margin, and on the model's own numbers, growth, margin, cash conversion and valuation all miss the bar. What clears comfortably: the duopoly position itself, a healthy return on capital, a stable share count, and long-term-oriented government ownership.

What's Working

A genuine duopoly position with Boeing; a record order backlog of 9,222 aircraft, over ten years of production already locked in; revenue and deliveries both growing every year; a healthy return on capital versus its cost; a stable share count; and a long-term-oriented government ownership pact that isn't going anywhere.

What to Watch

Structurally thin margins (9.7% vs. the 20% screen bar) are simply the nature of aircraft manufacturing, not a fixable flaw. FCF conversion weakened in FY2025 as capex ramped for production expansion. The stock has re-rated above its own 5-year average P/E. Pratt & Whitney engine supply constraints have pushed the "Rate 75" production target out to late 2027. A stricter balance-sheet view shows the net-cash cushion has swung to a small net debt position by mid-2026, even as Airbus's own headline metric stays positive. China's COMAC is a real but distant threat — flying commercially at home and now internationally, but still years from EU/US certification.
