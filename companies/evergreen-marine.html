Evergreen Marine is the purest way to see container shipping's brutal cyclicality in a single scorecard. This is a genuinely real, extraordinary story, not a data error: net income went from NT$239.0B (FY2021) to NT$334.2B (FY2022) — among the highest EPS prints in the world at the time, NT$86-113 per share, during the COVID-era port-congestion freight-rate spike — then crashed to just NT$35.3B in FY2023 as rates normalized, before a second, smaller spike in FY2024 (Red Sea/Houthi rerouting via the Cape of Good Hope) pushed operating margin back to 33.37%, and FY2025 settled lower again at 19.07%. Because this framework's growth criteria compare FY2021 (already inside the boom) to FY2025 (a post-boom normal year), Revenue, EBIT and FCF CAGR are all structurally negative — not because the underlying business shrank, but because the starting point was itself an unrepeatable peak. Stripped of that framing distortion, the more useful facts are: Evergreen is the youngest, most fuel-efficient fleet among the top 20 carriers (9.3-year average age), stayed inside the Ocean Alliance through 2025's industry-wide reshuffle, carries very little net leverage, and is controlled by the Chang family and Evergreen Group affiliated entities at a combined stake comfortably above this framework's 5% bar. Set against that: it's only the #7 carrier globally in a scale-driven industry, ROIC (11.28%) clears the assumed WACC but misses the absolute 15% floor, and the stock's current P/E sits roughly 54% above its own post-supercycle (FY2023-25) average even as a large, newly committed $8B+ newbuild orderbook (59 ships, LNG and methanol dual-fuel, delivering 2026-2028) is set to push capex intensity up sharply from here.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio at each fiscal year-end, badly distorted here by the 2021-22 freight-rate supercycle. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings; it spikes to 24.51x at FY2023 because consensus was pricing in a sharp earnings decline as the supercycle unwound, then falls back into a more normal 4x-10x band from FY2024 onward, in line with trailing P/E's post-supercycle range. A PEG ratio (forward P/E ÷ expected annual EPS growth) is not meaningfully computable for a shipping cyclical like Evergreen Marine, whose earnings swing with freight rates rather than a steady structural growth rate — the forward P/E level itself, not a PEG multiple, is the more honest cross-check here.

What's Working

Very low net leverage even after the recent net-cash-to-net-debt flip, strong FCF conversion, a roughly flat share count, and a meaningful, disclosed Chang family/Evergreen Group ownership stake well above this framework's 5% bar. Operationally, Evergreen runs the youngest, most fuel-efficient fleet among the top 20 global carriers and was the only top-10 carrier that didn't need to restructure its alliance in 2025's industry-wide reshuffle.

What to Watch

This is a #7 global player in a scale-driven, moderately concentrated industry, and every growth criterion is structurally negative purely because the 5-year window starts inside an unrepeatable freight-rate supercycle — a real reminder of just how cyclical container shipping is. ROIC clears WACC but misses the absolute bar, the stock trades at a real premium (≈1.54x) to its own post-supercycle average even with earnings well off 2021-2022 highs, and an $8B+ newbuild orderbook is about to push capex and possibly leverage meaningfully higher through 2026-2028. Add the TWSE-only listing (no liquid U.S. ADR) and this is a name most Western retail investors would find operationally harder to actually buy than most others on this site.
