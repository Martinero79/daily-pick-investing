Deere & Company, founded in 1837 and headquartered in Moline, Illinois, is the world's largest maker of agricultural machinery and a major player in construction and forestry equipment. It runs three equipment segments — Production & Precision Agriculture (large tractors, combines, planters, sprayers), Small Agriculture & Turf, and Construction & Forestry — plus John Deere Financial, a captive finance arm that funds dealer inventory and customer purchases. The business is intensely cyclical: consolidated revenue rose from $44.0B (FY2021) to a $61.3B peak (FY2023) as farmers enjoyed record incomes, then fell 25% to $45.7B by FY2025 as grain prices, farm income and used-equipment overhang turned against large-ag demand. Equipment operating profit fell harder: from $12.2B (21.9% margin) in FY2023 to $4.9B (12.6%) in FY2025. That is what a five-year window that starts near a cycle trough and ends near another one looks like — revenue CAGR of just +0.9%, EBIT CAGR of -8.1%. Deere itself says fiscal 2026 marks the bottom of the ag equipment cycle: Q3 FY2026 revenue grew +4.9% YoY, net income rose 7% to $1.379B ($5.10 per share) and the equipment margin recovered to 14.4%, with net income guidance for the year raised to $4.75-5.00B. The market has moved faster than the earnings: the stock is up 48.6% over 52 weeks to $689.59, leaving it at about 38x trailing earnings against a 17x average at fiscal year-ends over the past five years. Quality is not the question here — a dominant franchise, an almost debt-free equipment business, and a share count down ~14% since FY2021. Price paid for a recovery that has not yet shown up in the numbers is.
Two books, one company: Deere reports consolidated results that include John Deere Financial ($54.5B of debt funding ~$49B of financing receivables and $7.4B of leased equipment). Recent Performance, Guidance & Cycle Themes
Management says fiscal 2026 is the bottom of the ag equipment cycle: growth has returned at the consolidated level, but large-ag (Production & Precision Ag) is still shrinking while construction and small-ag carry the year.
The cycle in plain terms

Large-ag equipment (the high-margin combines and big tractors that make up Production & Precision Ag) sold at a record pace in FY2022-23 on strong farm incomes, then fell as grain prices and farm profitability weakened and used-equipment inventories built up. Deere deliberately cut production to protect pricing rather than discount, which is why profit fell faster than sales. In Q3 FY2026 Production & Precision Ag sales were still down 6%, but Small Ag & Turf (+12%) and Construction & Forestry (+18%) grew, and Deere says early-order-program trends and improving used inventories support 2026 as the low point. 2026 industry guidance still has U.S./Canada large ag down 15-20% and South American tractors and combines down 15-20%, so the recovery Deere is pointing to is a 2027 story, not something visible in this year's numbers.

Tariffs: Deere recorded tariff recoveries of $110M in Q3 and $382M in the first nine months of FY2026 (press release). Earnings-call coverage from secondary outlets puts gross direct tariff expense at roughly $1.1B for FY2026 (about $750M net of the recoveries) and about $1B net for FY2027, with revisions to Section 232 tariff treatment of European imports cited as the main improvement — treat those two figures as call commentary I could not tie to a filing. Deere's own risk factors also name contested tariffs and retaliatory trade measures as a demand-side risk for its farm customers.
Precision agriculture and recurring revenue: the technology story is the reason bulls argue Deere is less cyclical than it used to be. Earnings-call coverage says See & Spray (camera-guided targeted herbicide application, claimed >50% herbicide savings) is on about one-third of North American sprayers on order, and management cites about 190 million highly engaged digital acres. Deere bills precision-guidance and telematics subscriptions in advance and reported deferred revenue of about $2.1B at Aug 2, 2026 (per 10-Q coverage). What I could not find is a separate reported line for recurring software revenue or its margin, so the shift from one-time machine sales to subscriptions is a thesis rather than something the financials let me measure.

How to read the multiple: the raw comparison (38.4x trailing vs 17.1x average, i.e. 2.25x) is a clear fail on the entry-multiple criterion. But the multiple is partly a cycle artefact — earnings are near the bottom, so the same price on FY2023 peak EPS of $34.63 would be only 19.9x. The market is paying up in advance for a recovery, which is a normal pattern for cyclicals off the trough and also exactly why a stock up 48.6% in a year, with the consensus price target ($691.83) sitting on top of the current price, leaves little room for the recovery to disappoint. The stock also moved from $457.51 at the FY2025 year-end to $689.59 while trailing EPS fell.

What's Working

An unmatched franchise: the #1 position in large agricultural equipment, a dealer network competitors cannot easily copy, and pricing discipline that let it cut production rather than discount through the downcycle (Q3 FY2026 equipment margin recovered to 14.4% with sales still declining in large ag). The equipment business is close to debt-free (~0.4x net debt/EBITDA) with ROIC around 16% even at trough earnings, the share count is down ~14% since FY2021, and diversification is helping: Construction & Forestry (+18%) and Small Ag & Turf (+12%) grew strongly in Q3. Management says the order book supports FY2026 as the bottom, net income guidance was raised to $4.75-5.00B, and precision technology (See & Spray on about a third of sprayers on order, per call coverage) gives a credible path to a less cyclical earnings stream.

What to Watch

The price already discounts the recovery: 38x trailing earnings against a 17x fiscal-year-end average, the stock up 48.6% in 52 weeks, and the consensus price target barely above spot. Large ag, the highest-margin part of the business, is still guided down about 10% for FY2026, and the industry is guided down 15-20% in the U.S./Canada and South America; if the 2027 recovery is slower or shallower than the roughly 20% margin peak-to-trough would imply, the multiple is exposed. Tariffs are a real cost (roughly $1.1B gross in FY2026 per call coverage) and depend on policy that can change; farm-income dependence on trade disputes and commodity prices is unavoidable. Growth criteria fail across the board, FCF conversion is below 80% on a consolidated basis, insiders own almost nothing, and I could not verify a separate reported recurring-software revenue line, so the technology story rests on management commentary.

Maarten's note

I keep Deere next to Caterpillar because they are the two names I would use to sanity-check anything in heavy machinery, but they are exposed to very different cycles. Deere is really a bet on the farm economy: grain prices, farm incomes and the health of large-ag demand, plus construction where it is a smaller player. Caterpillar leans more on mining, energy and construction, and as far as I can tell has a larger services and aftermarket business, so I see its earnings as a little less tied to one crop cycle. On pure business quality Deere stacks up well — #1 share, an equipment arm with almost no net debt, ROIC well above its cost of capital — but it has visibly been through the ag downcycle in the numbers: equipment operating profit fell about 60% from the FY2023 peak.

The part of Deere I find genuinely interesting is precision ag: See & Spray, autonomy, and the subscription and software layer on top of the machines. If that becomes a large recurring-revenue business, Deere deserves a higher and more stable multiple than a normal machinery maker, which is the argument for today's price. What holds me back is that I cannot find recurring software revenue reported separately, so I treat it as an option rather than something I can see in the financials. At 38x trailing earnings versus its own 17x average the market is paying for both the cycle recovery and the tech story before either shows up. I would rather wait to see whether the 2027 recovery arrives and what the margin looks like, and compare that to what I can get at Caterpillar's multiple. Not a recommendation, just how I think about it.
