Eni is Italy's integrated energy group: upstream oil and gas (≈1.8M boe/d), a global gas and LNG portfolio, refining and chemicals, and two growing retail businesses — Enilive (fuels, biorefining, mobility) and Plenitude (power retail and renewables). Reported results are strongly cyclical: revenue peaked at €133.6B and operating income at €19.0B in FY2022, then fell to €83.6B and €5.2B by FY2025, so every FY2021-25 growth CAGR is flat or negative. The latest reported quarter is Q3 2026 (reported Oct 23, 2026) — Q3 is not pending. Net debt is at a record-low gearing of about 9% on Eni's own pro forma basis. The share price has risen about 76% in 12 months, so the market is already discounting part of the 2026 rebound.

What's Working

Strong cash returns and balance-sheet repair: FCF conversion of 88% on FY2025 numbers, net debt/EBITDA of 1.57x and a record-low pro forma gearing of about 9%, a dividend raised every year of the window plus an extraordinary €0.46 payout, and roughly 18% fewer shares than in FY2021. Q3 2026 revenue grew 26.7% with 7% production growth, Enilive and Plenitude add retail earnings that are less tied to the crude price, the Italian state anchors about a third of the shares, and the trailing P/E is a little below its own FY21-25 average.

What to Watch

Reported earnings fell sharply from FY2021 to FY2025 (operating income −19% a year, FCF −12.5% a year), margins compressed (gross margin −3.4pp since FY2022), ROIC is at or below the cost of capital, and capex has nearly doubled to about 10% of revenue. The 2026 rebound rests on Brent near ≈$114 and strong refining margins — a cyclical high that can reverse, and the extraordinary dividend is conditional on it. Dividends plus buybacks (about €8.2B planned in 2026) are large relative to FCF; the shares have risen about 76% in a year, the Italian state has policy influence, and geopolitical exposure in Libya, Egypt and Mozambique remains.
