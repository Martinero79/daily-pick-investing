GE Aerospace designs, builds and services jet engines and aircraft systems. Its two segments are Commercial Engines & Services (CES), which sells new engines (largely through the 50/50 CFM joint venture with Safran, whose LEAP and CFM56 engines power the Airbus A320neo and Boeing 737 families) and then earns a long stream of high-margin spare parts and shop-visit revenue over each engine's 25-30 year life, and Defense & Propulsion Technologies (DPT), which covers military engines, Avio Aero, Unison and Dowty propellers. The economics are the classic razor-and-blades model: engines are sold at thin margins, then services drive profit — services were about 66% of FY2025 revenue. After the 2023-24 break-up (GE HealthCare, then GE Vernova), the company is a pure aerospace business with restated revenue of $45.9B in FY2025 (+18.5%), an S&P operating margin of 20.7% and free cash flow of $7.3B. Q3 2026 was strong again: total orders $19.0B (+49%), adjusted revenue $13.3B (+18%), adjusted EPS $2.25 (+36%), and 2026 guidance was raised for the second time this year. The stock nevertheless fell 3.7% on the day and has dropped to about 22% below its 52-week high, because the shares already priced a lot of that growth: the trailing P/E is about 35x versus roughly 27x on average for FY2023-25, and defense stocks and aviation sentiment have been weak into the report.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Restated base — the comparable history starts in FY2022 (revenue $29.1B, operating margin 13.7%, which still carried large costs), so growth rates measured from FY2022 are flattered by a depressed starting year; FY2021 on the same basis is not available. (2) GAAP vs adjusted — GAAP net income swings with spin-off gains, discontinued operations and investment marks (FY2023 net income $9.2B included large one-off gains), so the P/E history is noisy; company-adjusted EPS ($8.00-8.10 guided for 2026) is higher quality but non-GAAP. (3) Spin-off adjusted prices — the five-year price history is adjusted by the data provider for the two spin-offs, so it shows value creation of the remaining business, not what a 2021 holder's whole stake did.

What the quarter actually showed

Total orders were $19.0B (+49%): Commercial Engines & Services orders $15.3B (+58%, with services orders +23% and equipment orders more than tripling on large engine commitments) and Defense & Propulsion orders $4.3B (+18%). GAAP revenue was $14.1B (+16%; adjusted revenue $13.3B, +18%). Company operating profit was about $3.0B (+30%, margin 22.5%, +220bps) and adjusted EPS $2.25 (+36%) versus a consensus near $2.01. Free cash flow of $2.0B was down 14% year on year and year-to-date FCF conversion was 105%. Management raised 2026 guidance across the board and said it expects commercial services revenue to grow at a low-double-digit rate in 2027, supported by a commercial-services backlog of more than $170B. The shares still fell 3.7% on the day, and analysts trimmed price targets (e.g. JPMorgan $400 to $375, Morgan Stanley $400 to $375, Bernstein $421 to $376, Jefferies $435 to $425), with brokers pointing to a challenging market backdrop and the initial 2027 commentary (Reuters separately noted surging fuel costs squeezing airlines). A TipRanks piece notes the stock has fallen about 5% on average after each of the last four reports despite beats.

What's Working

Services-led economics: an installed base of ~50,000 commercial engines keeps generating spare-parts and shop-visit revenue, with commercial services up 23% in Q3 and a backlog above $170B. Operating margin has risen from 13.7% to 20.7% over three years, gross margin by 5.5pp, and ROIC is far above any plausible cost of capital. Revenue growth has accelerated (FY2024 +9.5%, FY2025 +18.5%, Q3 2026 +16-18%), 2026 guidance has been raised twice, capex is under 3% of revenue, leverage is under 1x, and the share count is shrinking while the dividend has grown from $0.32 to $1.88. Orders (+49%) point to continued growth in 2027.

What to Watch

Valuation: about 35x trailing and 34x forward earnings leaves little room for disappointment, and the stock has fallen after each of the last four beat-and-raise reports. FCF conversion is below 80% of operating income and 3-year FCF growth (11%) trails earnings growth; Q3 FCF fell 14% year on year. Aviation is cyclical and exposed to fuel prices, tariffs, supplier constraints and engine-quality events; defense budgets are a political risk. GE9X certification timing and heavier equipment mix are compressing CES margins slightly. The history is restated and short, GAAP net income is distorted by spin-off gains, and I could not verify named shareholders or a market-share percentage.
