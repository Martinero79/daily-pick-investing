MercadoLibre is the clearest "just buy the market leader" story in this entire e-commerce batch. It's the dominant commerce-and-fintech platform across 18 Latin American countries — more than 570,000 small and medium businesses actively sell on the marketplace, and for over 1.8 million families across the region, MercadoLibre income is their primary source of livelihood. Unlike Alibaba, PDD, Sea, Coupang or JD elsewhere in this batch, MELI's growth story isn't complicated by tariff politics, a self-inflicted price war, or founder-control drama — it's simply compounding: revenue has grown from $7.1B (FY2021) to $28.9B (FY2025), a 42% CAGR, while free cash flow has grown even faster, from $392M to $10.8B. The two things holding this dashboard's scorecard back from a clean sweep are both modest, not alarming: operating margin has actually compressed slightly from its FY2023 peak as the company reinvests aggressively in logistics and its fast-growing Mercado Crédito lending business, and ROIC (14.20%) sits just a hair below this framework's 15% bar despite clearing WACC comfortably. Meanwhile the stock is down 18.53% over the past year even as the business just posted its fastest revenue growth in nearly four years — a genuine and, on this dashboard's numbers, hard-to-explain gap between price and fundamentals.

Current 53.5x sits below the FY22-25 average of 66.3x (ratio ≈0.81) — a genuine, if more modest, discount to MELI's own trading history than the P/FCF read suggested.
Balance sheet nuance — read the debt carefully: MELI carries $13.21B of total debt against $5.75B of cash and investments, a net debt position of $7.46B (1.92x TTM EBITDA, just inside this framework's 2x threshold). Unlike traditional corporate leverage, most of this debt funds MELI's own consumer and merchant lending book through Mercado Crédito — a fintech "loans funded by borrowings" structure similar to how a bank or BNPL lender operates, not debt taken on to prop up the core commerce business. Quick ratio is a thin 0.40 and the Altman Z-Score of 2.58 sits below the traditional "safe" threshold of 3 — both numbers that would normally raise a flag, but are partly an artifact of a business model with a large, rapidly growing lending portfolio on the balance sheet. Worth watching as the credit book scales, but not read as classic balance-sheet distress.

What's Working

MELI is the clearest dominant-market-leader story in this batch: a widening #1 position across an 18-country region, revenue and FCF both compounding at rates well above this framework's bars, a current valuation meaningfully below its own trading history, and a nearly flat share count. What to Watch

Operating margin has compressed from its FY2023 peak and quarterly margins have kept drifting lower through Q2 2026, as the company reinvests aggressively into logistics and its fast-growing credit business. ROIC sits just under this framework's 15% bar. The extreme FCF-conversion ratio likely reflects fintech float dynamics rather than pure efficiency, and the growing lending book funded by debt is worth monitoring as it scales, even though it isn't classic corporate leverage risk today.
