Apple is an extraordinary business by almost any operating measure — a 32% operating margin, a ROIC-WACC spread few companies can match, a genuinely net-cash balance sheet even after hundreds of billions in buybacks, and a share count that keeps shrinking. What this framework catches, though, is that Apple today is a mature, wonderful-quality compounder rather than a growth story: revenue compounded at just 3.3% a year over FY2021-25 (FY2026's +15% is the first sharp re-acceleration), and free cash flow had not grown over that span despite record profits, largely because operating cash flow growth has been mostly offset by tax and working-capital effects. None of that makes Apple a bad business — it's a case of this framework's heavy growth weighting doing exactly what it's designed to do.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading trailing vs. forward P/E: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. The trailing-P/E series above is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each year-end, showing how the market has re-rated Apple relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E divides today's price by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and it captures expected growth (or decline) that trailing P/E can't see. For Apple, forward P/E (≈38.1x, stale-consensus estimate) tracks close to trailing (≈38.9x), only a modest discount — consistent with a mature, slower-growing business where the market isn't pricing in much of an earnings inflection either way. That same maturity shows up starkly in the PEG ratio: dividing forward P/E by consensus EPS growth (low-double-digits) puts Apple's PEG north of 3 — well above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, meaning Apple's multiple isn't being justified by growth at all, but by the market's confidence in the ecosystem, buybacks, and earnings durability instead.
Balance sheet: Apple held a net cash position of roughly $57B at Sep 26, 2026 (cash and marketable securities of $136.9B less $80.1B of commercial paper and term debt; up from ~$34B at Sep 2025 on the same method) — comfortably net cash even after FY2026 buybacks of $86.4B, a deliberate capital-structure choice rather than a sign of financial strain. Inventories rose to $14.5B from $5.7B a year earlier.

What's Working

Apple runs one of the most efficient, capital-disciplined businesses of any large company — a 32% operating margin, a ROIC-WACC spread most companies can't approach, and a genuinely net-cash balance sheet despite hundreds of billions returned to shareholders over the years. FY2026 was a real acceleration (+15% revenue, +18% net income; Q4 +12% revenue), the December FY2026 holiday quarter set an all-time record, and Services keeps compounding as a high-margin, recurring layer on top of the hardware base. The premium smartphone tier has become a genuine Apple-Samsung duopoly, with Apple alone capturing roughly half of industry revenue.

What to Watch

The core scorecard miss here is growth: revenue compounded at just 3.3% a year over FY2021-25 and free cash flow did not grow over that span even as profits hit records — a maturing business, though FY2026 (+15% revenue, FCF $136B) is a sharp break from that pattern, and the tariff refunds and memory-cost pressure flatter or cloud the margin picture. There's no founder or meaningfully-sized insider stake, the current multiple sits modestly above its own five-year average, and the multiple has expanded to ~38.9x trailing, about 25% above its own five-year average, at a record share price. Tariff policy is also a live, non-trivial swing factor given Apple's reliance on Chinese and broader Asian manufacturing — Q3 and Q4 FY2026 results already included tariff-refund tailwinds (about 2 and 1 points of gross margin) that may not repeat.
