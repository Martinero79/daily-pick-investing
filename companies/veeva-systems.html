Veeva Systems is a Pleasanton, California software company that sells cloud applications, data and (increasingly) AI agents to the global life sciences industry. Its two subscription families are Commercial Solutions (Vault CRM, data, Crossix analytics; $1.33B trailing subscription revenue) and R&D and Quality Solutions (clinical, regulatory, quality, safety; $1.56B), now the larger and faster-growing half. Revenue is almost entirely recurring subscription plus professional services, billed largely annually in advance, so cash flow runs seasonally ahead of earnings (Q1 free cash flow is the heavy collection quarter). The business is asset-light (capex under 1% of revenue), holds $7.2B of cash and investments against negligible debt, and converted 146% of FY2026 GAAP operating income into free cash flow. Q2 FY2027 revenue was $928.0M (+17.6% YoY), subscription revenue $766.8M (+16%), GAAP operating income $275.0M (+40%, helped by a $30.6M litigation charge in the prior-year quarter) and non-GAAP operating income $415.9M (+18%); the company raised FY2027 guidance to $3.682-3.687B revenue and about $1.64B non-GAAP operating income. The share price has more than doubled from its April 2026 low of $148.05 as software-sector worries about AI disruption eased, so the valuation debate is now about how long growth of mid-to-high teens can be sustained at ~50x trailing GAAP earnings.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) GAAP vs non-GAAP — Veeva's non-GAAP operating margin (~45%) excludes ~$0.5B a year of stock-based compensation; this page scores the GAAP figures. (2) Fiscal-year labels — Veeva's FY2026 ended Jan 31, 2026, so “FY2022-26” spans Feb 2021 to Jan 2026 and the 4-year CAGRs below start from FY2022 (Jan 2022). (3) Investor Day — Veeva held a virtual Investor Day on Nov 5, 2026; any new long-term targets from it are not incorporated here.

What the quarter actually showed

Revenue of $928.0M grew 17.6% (the fastest in eight quarters) and beat the company's own guidance; subscription revenue grew 16.3% and professional services 24.1%. GAAP operating income rose 40% to $275.0M, but roughly 10 points of that is a base effect from a $30.6M litigation charge in Q2 FY2026; non-GAAP operating income grew 18% to $415.9M (44.8% margin, flat YoY). Veeva said Vault CRM had its best quarter ever (180+ customers live, 12 top-20 biopharma commitments) and that its Falcon agent platform is moving toward first go-lives this year. Operating cash flow was flat YoY at $238.7M (seasonally small Q2). The company bought back $472.7M of stock in H1 and spent $81.8M on acquisitions (including Copli).

What's Working

A rare combination of 15%+ growth, ~30% GAAP operating margin, 146% FCF conversion, 0.9% capex intensity and $7.1B of net cash. Growth re-accelerated to +17.6% in Q2 FY2027 and guidance was raised to $3.68B revenue; gross margin has risen 3.8pp in three years; Vault CRM wins at top-20 biopharmas continue; and share repurchases have restarted. The R&D and Quality half of the business is growing faster than Commercial and deepens the customer relationship beyond CRM.

What to Watch

At ~50x trailing GAAP earnings (~32x forward) the stock already discounts sustained mid-to-high-teens growth; the average target implies only ~4% upside. The revenue CAGR sits just under 15%, the stock has doubled from its April low, and AI-disruption sentiment moves the shares sharply in both directions. GAAP margins depend on stock-based compensation of ~14-15% of revenue, roughly a fifth of pretax income is interest income on the cash pile, and customer concentration in a pharma industry sensitive to policy and funding is structural. Market-share figures and Mr. Gassner's exact stake were not independently verified.
