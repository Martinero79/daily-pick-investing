Intuitive built and has defended a genuine two-decade near-monopoly in soft-tissue robotic surgery — over 11,700 da Vinci systems installed worldwide, 60%+ market share, and a recurring-revenue model (instruments, accessories and service tied to every procedure) that keeps compounding as the installed base grows. On the model's own numbers it clears growth, margin, returns, balance-sheet and even valuation bars comfortably. What it misses: EBIT and FCF growth have lagged the 15% bar during a heavy 2023–24 capex ramp to build da Vinci 5 manufacturing capacity, and ownership is almost entirely institutional — there's no founder or family stake anchoring the register the way there is at, say, Keyence or FANUC.

Quarterly Revenue ($B)
Six straight quarters of double-digit YoY growth, with a seasonal step-up each Q4 as system placements cluster toward year-end.
da Vinci Systems Placed — Total, by Generation
da Vinci 5 (the newest, highest-ASP platform) has grown from ~40% of placements in Q1 2025 to over half of every quarter since Q3 2025 — the mix shift underpinning both revenue and margin.
da Vinci 5 Other generations (Xi, X, SP)
Competitive landscape shifting: for nearly two decades da Vinci had no real soft-tissue rival. That changed in 2025-26: Medtronic's Hugo (modular, $1.5-2M price point) cleared the FDA in late 2025; Johnson & Johnson's Ottava won approval mid-2026; CMR Surgical's Versius is already live across Europe, the UK and Asia on a per-procedure leasing model, with US clearance still pending. None has meaningfully dented Intuitive's installed base yet — it's still growing 12% YoY — but this is the first year the market concentration score genuinely has downside risk to watch.

What's Working

A genuine two-decade near-monopoly with a recurring-revenue razor/razorblade model; revenue growth that actually clears the 15% bar; a fast-improving margin and FCF-conversion trend as the da Vinci 5 capex cycle normalizes; a debt-free balance sheet; consistent share-count discipline; and, unusually for a "growth" name, a current valuation multiple well below its own five-year average.

What to Watch

2025-26 marks the first year Intuitive faces real, FDA-cleared soft-tissue competition (Medtronic Hugo, J&J Ottava, CMR Surgical Versius) after two decades essentially unchallenged — installed-base growth hasn't slowed yet, but this is the criterion with the most genuine multi-year downside risk. Ownership is almost entirely institutional with no anchoring founder stake. EBIT and FCF growth lagged the 15% bar during the 2023-24 capacity build, and the stock has been volatile and largely range-bound for years despite strong fundamental compounding (see page 6).
