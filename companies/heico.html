HEICO is a Florida-based aerospace, defense and electronics group, founded in 1957 and still led by the founding family (Co-Chairmen and Co-CEOs Eric and Victor Mendelson). It has two segments. The Flight Support Group (FSG) (about 70% of sales) makes FAA-approved PMA replacement parts that compete with OEM spare parts at lower prices, plus specialty products (such as repair, avionics and thermal products), repair and overhaul services and distribution. The Electronic Technologies Group (ETG) makes niche electronic, microwave, power and infrared products for defense, space, medical and industrial customers. The model is a long compounding story built on organic niche growth plus a steady stream of small-to-mid-sized acquisitions funded by cash flow and moderate debt: capex is only ~1.6% of sales, FCF was $861m in FY2025 and 84% of operating income. Fiscal 2025 revenue grew 16.3% to $4.49B with a 23.0% operating margin; the first nine months of fiscal 2026 accelerated to +21% sales and +30% operating income, with Q3 FY2026 setting records for sales ($1.41B), operating income ($355m, 25.1% margin) and net income ($235m, $1.67 per share). The shares trade at about 54x trailing GAAP earnings — roughly in line with HEICO's own history (average ~55x at year-ends FY2021-25) but a very high absolute multiple, so the stock is far more sensitive to any slowing of growth than the fundamentals suggest.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Fiscal year — FY2026 (Nov 2025–Oct 2026) has ended but is unreported, so the latest annual figures are FY2025 and 'current' growth comes from the TTM and nine-month figures. (2) M&A — reported growth includes acquisitions (FY2023's +34% included the Wencor deal; Q3 FY2026's +23% is 14% organic plus acquisitions), so reported CAGRs flatter organic growth. (3) Two share classes — HEI (1 vote) and HEI.A (1/10 vote) trade at different prices (HEI.A roughly 24% lower); the price, P/E and market cap on this page use the HEI common share, and the same GAAP EPS applies to both, so HEI.A trades at a lower P/E.

Quarter ended Jul 31, 2026, reported Aug 25, 2026. Fiscal Q4 (Aug–Oct 2026) ended on Oct 31 and is unreported. HEICO does not publish numeric annual guidance; the commentary below is management's qualitative outlook.
What the quarter actually showed

Net sales rose 23% to a record $1,413.1m (consolidated organic growth 14%); operating income rose 34% to a record $355.2m (margin 25.1% vs 23.1%); net income rose 33% to $235.4m ($1.67 per diluted share vs $1.26); EBITDA rose 31% to $415.2m and operating cash flow rose 49% to $345.3m. The Flight Support Group grew sales 18% (organic 12%) to $947.8m, with operating income +24% to $245.3m (25.9% margin). The Electronic Technologies Group grew sales 36% (organic 18%) to $483.5m and operating income 55% to $125.6m (26.0% margin), helped by aerospace, defense and other electronics. Over the nine months, sales rose 21% to $3.97B, operating income 30% to $965.5m and EPS 31% to $4.67. Management funded the quarter's acquisitions by issuing $1.2B of senior notes ($550m 4.95% due 2031 and $650m 5.40% due 2036), repaying revolver borrowings; net debt/EBITDA improved to 1.57x.

What's Working

Compounding with discipline: 4-year CAGRs of 25% (revenue), 27% (EBIT) and 21% (FCF), a 23% operating margin that is still rising (TTM 24.3%), a steady ~39-40% gross margin, and capex of only ~1.6% of sales. Growth has accelerated to +23% in Q3 FY2026 with 14% organic growth and record margins in both segments. A founding-family management team, an acquisition record that has so far earned more than its cost of capital (ROIC 13% vs ~10% WACC), leverage of 1.57x after a $1.0B acquisition year, and FCF conversion above 80% combine growth with quality.

What to Watch

Valuation is the main risk: ~54x trailing and ~46x forward earnings means the stock needs growth to stay near 20% a year, and the 'met' on the entry-multiple criterion only means the multiple is near HEICO's own long-run (always high) average. Reported growth includes acquisitions (organic is 12-18%), goodwill and intangibles are 62% of assets, and leverage has been rising with acquisition spend ($1.0B in nine months). ROIC is only ~3pp above WACC. Aftermarket PMA parts depend on FAA approvals and OEM reactions, ETG is exposed to defense and space budgets, and commercial aviation is cyclical. HEI and HEI.A trade at different prices, so market-cap and P/E figures depend on which class is quoted. FY2026 numbers are not yet public.
