AppLovin's scorecard is, mechanically, the strongest on this entire site — and that number needs real context before it means anything. In February 2024, AppLovin agreed to sell its legacy mobile-games/apps business (about 10 studios) to Tripledot Studios for roughly $400M cash plus a 20% stake in Tripledot; the deal closed June 30, 2025. What's left is AXON, AppLovin's AI-driven ad-recommendation and user-acquisition engine, now effectively the entire company — and it runs at software-platform economics rather than ad-tech economics: 75.75% operating margin, 132.73% ROIC, and revenue that's more than tripled since the restated FY2023 base. Because the divestiture changed what "AppLovin" actually is, FY2021-22 figures reflect the old, larger, lower-margin combined business and aren't comparable to FY2023 onward — this dashboard uses FY2023, the first full year on a clean continuing-operations basis, as the base year for every CAGR and valuation-history calculation, flagging rather than hiding the older data where it's shown. Two things temper the eye-popping scorecard. First, the underlying business is genuinely young in its current form — two clean fiscal years is a short track record for a 12/13 score. Second, the stock has been through a rough stretch: short-seller Muddy Waters Research published allegations in March 2025 of Terms-of-Service violations and overstated return-on-ad-spend claims (stock fell 20% that day), several other short firms piled on afterward, and Q2 2026 earnings on August 5 — despite 53% revenue growth and a 58% jump in adjusted EBITDA — sent the stock down another 16-21% on concerns about the pace of AppLovin's newer e-commerce advertising push and the CEO's own comments about "lumpy" timing in ad-model improvements. The net effect: the stock closed FY2025 at $673.82 and sits at $317.76 today, down 53% from that peak even as the fundamentals underneath have kept compounding.

Q2 2026 Headlines

Revenue reached $1.92B, up 52.83% YoY but a slight miss against the roughly $1.94B analyst consensus. Adjusted EBITDA rose 58% YoY to $1.61B (84% margin) and net income rose 54.54% to $1.27B — strong absolute growth by almost any measure. Despite that, the stock fell 16-21% on the print (sources vary on the exact figure), driven by CEO Adam Foroughi's comments describing "lumpy" timing on improvements to AppLovin's advertising models, plus market skepticism about the pace and scalability of the company's newer e-commerce advertising expansion, which management itself has characterized as early-stage with creative tooling still a bottleneck. Q3 2026 guidance calls for revenue of $2.055-2.085B, still 46-48% YoY growth.

Short-seller overhang: Muddy Waters Research published a report on March 27, 2025 alleging AppLovin violated the terms of service of Meta, Google, TikTok, Snap and Reddit to extract user ID-graph data, and that the company overstated its return-on-ad-spend claims to advertisers; the stock fell roughly 20% (from about $325 to $260) on the day. Several other short-selling firms (Fuzzy Panda Research, Culper Research, The Bear Cave) published similar allegations in the following weeks. CEO Adam Foroughi publicly disputed the claims and retained outside counsel (Alex Spiro of Quinn Emanuel) to conduct an independent review. This overhang, more than any single fundamental miss, is a meaningful part of why the stock has underperformed its own scorecard over the past 18 months.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY23-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — it's the more useful lens for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed. For AppLovin, forward P/E (17.63x) sits well below trailing P/E (24.4x) and below every prior fiscal year-end in the clean FY23-25 window, consistent with consensus expecting continued earnings growth. The PEG ratio — forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth — sanity-checks that further: AppLovin's PEG currently runs around 0.57, comfortably under the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, suggesting the stock isn't just cheap on a backward-looking basis, it's cheap relative to how fast earnings are expected to keep growing.
Methodology note: AppLovin's FY2021-22 GAAP earnings are excluded from the historical average as near-zero/negative-earnings noise, not because those years were structurally a different business (they were, but that's not why they're excluded here). FY2021 net income was just $31.7M against a $2.79B revenue base — a real but economically trivial GAAP profit that produces a nonsense 1,000x+ P/E; FY2022 was an outright GAAP net loss (-$192.75M), where P/E is undefined. Both were legacy-apps-business years before the AXON pivot took hold. FY2023 onward, GAAP earnings became real and meaningful (a 40.7x-71.5x range), so those three years are used in full for the average — unlike the CAGR calculations on page 2, this isn't about excluding "the old business," it's about excluding multiples that carry no real information. Even judged only against its own three clean fiscal years, AppLovin trades meaningfully below its FY23-25 average today.
Balance sheet: AppLovin holds $3.05B in cash against $3.52B in total debt, a net debt position of $461.77M ($1.38/share) — not net cash, but trivial at 0.09x TTM EBITDA. Current ratio of 4.30 and quick ratio of 4.17 are strong, and interest coverage stands at 25.79x, so near-term debt service is not a concern despite the technically negative net cash figure.

What's Working

Every growth and profitability criterion in this framework is comfortably met — revenue, EBIT and FCF have all more than doubled since the FY2023 restatement, operating margin and ROIC are the strongest of any company on this site, capex is negligible, and the balance sheet carries only trivial leverage. The current valuation, even judged only against AppLovin's own clean two-year trading history, sits meaningfully below average.

What to Watch

This is a genuinely young business in its current form — two clean fiscal years is a short track record to build a 12/13 score on, and the FY2021-22 exclusions, while well-justified by the Tripledot divestiture, mean this scorecard has less historical depth behind it than most others on this site. The stock remains under a live short-seller cloud (Muddy Waters and others), is down 53% from its FY2025 peak, and just posted a rough earnings reaction despite strong absolute growth — the newer e-commerce ad expansion is explicitly early-stage per management's own comments. Market concentration is the one structural scorecard miss, in a large, well-funded, fast-moving competitive field.
