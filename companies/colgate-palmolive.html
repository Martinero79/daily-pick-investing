Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed. For Colgate specifically: trailing P/E (35.03x) is meaningfully above its own FY21-25 average (30.84x), while forward P/E (22.58x) sits modestly below its own average (24.08x) — the gap between the two exists because trailing earnings were temporarily depressed by an unusually weak Q4 2025 (net income briefly went negative on a one-off charge), which mechanically inflates the trailing multiple without reflecting the underlying run-rate. One more lens worth a glance: the PEG ratio, forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether a multiple is cheap once growth is accounted for. Colgate's PEG currently runs around 4.25 — well above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, and a reminder that even the modestly-discounted-looking forward multiple is still a real premium for a company growing EPS in the mid-single digits.
Balance sheet: net debt of $6.40B against TTM EBITDA of roughly $4.98B, for a Net Debt/EBITDA of about 1.29x — comfortably under this framework's <2x bar, and net debt has stayed in a tight $6.4B-$8.3B band throughout FY2021-2025 (FY2021 $6.98B, FY2022 $8.32B, FY2023 $7.92B, FY2024 $7.26B, FY2025 $7.16B). ROIC of 45.56% clears both the 15% absolute bar and the assumed 7.5% WACC by a wide margin, though this is significantly amplified by an unusually thin book-equity base ($566M) after decades of sustained buybacks — a mechanical effect of the capital structure as much as a sign of an unusually capital-light business. Share count has declined every year of the window, from roughly 849M (FY2021, implied) to 797.17M today, down 1.49% in the past year alone.

What's Working

A durable #1 global position in toothpaste (41.3% share, still gaining), an operating margin that has held a tight, healthy band for five years, gross margin that has genuinely improved since the FY2022 inflation trough, a comfortably low net-debt/EBITDA under 2x throughout the window, high FCF conversion, consistent buybacks, low capex intensity, and 63 consecutive years of dividend growth — plus Hill's Pet Nutrition, a real second growth leg that's been outgrowing the core business.

What to Watch

And the stock is not statistically cheap: the current 35.03x trailing P/E is a real premium to Colgate's own recent history, and a PEG of 4.25 suggests investors are paying up for stability and moat quality rather than getting a growth bargain.
