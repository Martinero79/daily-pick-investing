Pandora A/S is a Copenhagen-based jewellery group founded in 1982 by Per Enevoldsen, best known for its customisable charm bracelets. It designs and crafts its own jewellery (production sites in Thailand, with a large new factory in Vietnam opened in October 2026), uses 100% recycled silver and gold, and sells through about 7,000 points of sale, including more than 2,800 own and partner concept stores, plus online and wholesale. Revenue is split between Core (Moments charms and bracelets, Collabs, ME: DKK 24.2B, about 74% of FY2025 revenue) and Fuel with More (Timeless, Signature, Essence and lab-grown diamonds: DKK 8.3B). Gross margin is very high (about 79%) because the products are made of relatively inexpensive metals, and the business is strongly seasonal: Q4 is about 36% of annual revenue and about 51% of annual EBIT. The 2026 story is a reset: a new CEO (Berta de Pablos-Barbier) is course-correcting toward design-led product, a new local marketing model and a shift to platinum-plated jewellery in 2027 to reduce exposure to volatile silver, while US tariffs and metal costs squeeze margins. After a 2025 share-price drop from DKK 1,317 (end-2024) to DKK 708 (end-2025) and a low of DKK 430 in the 52-week window, the shares have recovered to DKK 933 on the back of two guidance upgrades. Q3 2026 organic growth of 6% (LFL +4%, positive in all four regions) was the best in several quarters, but 370bp of the Q3 EBIT margin came from a one-off tariff refund.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) One-offs — a partial refund of Pandora's US IEEPA tariff claim lifted Q2 gross margin by 230bp and Q3 by 340bp (EBIT margin +250bp and +370bp); FY2026 EBIT-margin guidance of 22.5–23% includes it, so reported 2026 margins are not a clean run-rate. (2) Seasonality — Q4 carries about half of annual EBIT, so any single quarter (including the strong Q3) says little on its own about the year. (3) Currency & silver — FY2025 reported revenue grew +2.7% vs +6% organic because of FX; silver and gold costs and tariffs were about 270bp of external headwind to Q2 2026 gross margin before offsets.

Latest Quarter (Q3 2026) & Eight-Quarter Trend
Reported Nov 4, 2026 (Company announcement No. 1019). The shares closed DKK 919.80 on the day, +10.9% from DKK 829.40 the evening before (Yahoo Finance / S&P daily data), and reached DKK 958.60 on Nov 10 before easing to DKK 933.00 on Nov 13.
What the quarter actually showed

Q3 2026 revenue was DKK 6,694m (+6.8% reported vs DKK 6,269m; organic +6%: like-for-like +4% and network expansion +2%). LFL growth was positive in all four regions: North America +3% (accelerating), EMEA +1%, Asia-Pacific +20% (led by Japan; management expects Japan's contribution to moderate) and Latin America +6%. Core returned to +1% LFL and Fuel with More accelerated to +9%. EBIT was DKK 1,064m (margin 15.9%, +190bp), but 370bp of that margin was a one-off from a partial refund of Pandora's US IEEPA tariff claim, so the underlying margin was broadly flat year on year. Gross margin was 83.7% (340bp of it from the refund; above 80%, up 100bp, excluding it). October LFL growth was +1% (hurt by lower promotional activity; management says the underlying trend is about +3%), and management cautioned the recovery may not be linear in an uncertain consumer environment.

What's Working

Exceptional unit economics: ~79% gross margin, ~24% EBIT margin and a 27% ROIC against a ~10% WACC. Gross margin has improved about 3pp in three years, buybacks have cut the share count by about a quarter since 2021 and the dividend has grown to DKK 22. Momentum is turning: organic growth went from 2% (Q1) to 3% (Q2) to 6% (Q3 2026), LFL turned positive in all four regions, Asia-Pacific is growing ~20% LFL, and management narrowed FY2026 guidance up twice. The stock trades at ~13.5x trailing GAAP earnings versus a ~15.3x own five-year average, and the platinum-plated transition and 90–100% silver hedge for 2027 address the main cost risk.

What to Watch

Growth over four years is modest (revenue CAGR +8.6%, EBIT +7.7%, FCF flat), FY2025 EBIT fell 2.4%, and FY2026 margins are flattered by one-off tariff-refund income (370bp in Q3), so the underlying margin is lower than the 22.5–23% guide. Core LFL has been weak (−1% in Q2, +1% in Q3), October LFL was only +1%, Japan's contribution is expected to moderate, and the consumer backdrop (especially the US) is soft. Net debt has risen from 0.4x to ~1.9x EBITDA while FCF was flat, equity is thin, the market is highly fragmented, and there is no founder or controlling holder. The forward P/E (~18x) is above its own history even though the trailing P/E is below; the 2027 margin target as printed (14–15%) could not be reconciled with the current guidance.
