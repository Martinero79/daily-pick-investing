Nordea is the largest financial services group in the Nordic region, with leading positions in personal banking, business banking, large corporates and asset and wealth management across Finland, Sweden, Denmark and Norway, about 28,400 employees and a G-SIB designation. FY2025 operating income was €11.7bn (-2.8%) and net profit €4.8bn (-4%), a 17.8% return on tangible equity (15.5% ROE), with a 46.0% cost/income ratio and a 15.7% CET1 ratio. Over FY2021–FY2025 operating income compounded at ~5.1% a year and net profit at ~6.0% — well below this framework's 15% bar, and measured across an IFRS 17 break and a FY2022 year that includes Russia-related items. The strength is returns and capital: ROTE has stayed 15–19% since FY2023, FY2025 dividends of €0.96 per share plus about €0.75bn of executed buybacks were roughly 84% of profit, and the share count is down ~13% since FY2021. The caveats are that falling policy rates have cut net interest income (-6% in FY25), Q3 2026 profit was 4% below a year earlier, and at ~11.2x trailing earnings and ~1.66x book value the shares trade above their own FY21–FY25 averages of ~9.8x and ~1.31x after a sharp 2025 re-rating (+53%).
Framing note — why this scorecard looks different for a bank: Nordea is a bank, so the standard revenue/EBIT/FCF/net-debt framework does not map cleanly onto it. The same substitutions used on the JPMorgan Chase page are applied here, all flagged inline: Total Operating Income stands in for revenue (criterion 1); Net Income substitutes for both EBIT (criterion 2) and Free Cash Flow (criterion 4) — a bank's cash flow statement is dominated by deposit and trading-book swings that do not map to a “free cash flow” concept, so criteria 2 and 4 are necessarily identical here, a genuine limitation of the adaptation and not a workaround; ROTE vs. an assumed ~10% cost of equity substitutes for ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7; Nordea reports return on tangible equity (ROTE) alongside ROE, so ROTE is used for the spread. A ~10% cost of equity is used, the low end of the 10–11% range, rather than the 9–10% allowed for low-beta Nordic banks, to stay conservative); CET1 ratio vs. the regulatory requirement substitutes for net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8), scored on the cushion over the requirement; and (dividends + buybacks) ÷ net income substitutes for FCF conversion (criterion 9). Entry multiple (criterion 10) is scored on the average of the P/E and P/B ratios, each current value divided by its own FY2021–FY2025 year-end average (P/TBV history is not available, so P/B is used). Criterion 14 uses the efficiency (cost/income) ratio because banks report no gross profit; a falling ratio counts as a margin rise. Everything is reported in EUR and nothing is converted.

What the latest quarter showed (Q3 2026)

Q3 2026 operating income was €2,968m (+2% YoY, -2% on Q2), with net interest income €1,799m (+1% YoY), fees €880m (+9%) and net fair value result €198m (-19%). Operating profit was €1,541m (-4% YoY) and net profit €1,181m (-4%), with a cost/income ratio of 45.0% (excluding regulatory fees), ROE of 15.1% and ROTE of 17.6%, and diluted EPS of €0.35. Net loan losses were €74m (7bp), within the group's long-term expectation of about 10bp, driven by a few corporate customers. The CET1 ratio was 15.8%, 1.9pp above the requirement, after assuming a 70% dividend deduction subject to ECB approval. Lending rose 6% YoY to €399.6bn, deposits 15% to €259.0bn and assets under management 13% to €509.4bn. Nordea kept its 2026 outlook of ROE above 15% and a cost/income ratio of 44–45%, and its 2030 target of ROE above 15% with a cost/income ratio of 40–42%. The shares closed at €15.65 on results day against €16.98 the day before (-7.8%), and were at €15.27 on Oct 23.

Governance & Capital Notes

Nordea has no controlling shareholder or founder; the largest holders at Sep 30, 2026 (per Nordea's factbook, approximate) are global asset managers, the Danish foundation Nordea-fonden (3.9%) and the activist investor Cevian (3.1%), several of which sit on the shareholders' nomination board. Management ownership is well under 1%. Under the criterion's bands the 3.9% foundation stake is scored as a 1–5% anchor holder (2 points) and flagged as not meaningful insider alignment. Nordea's policy is a 60–70% dividend payout with excess capital returned through buybacks; it executed three €250m buyback programmes in 2025 and started a €500m programme in December 2025.

What's Working

The most profitable large bank in its region on this framework: ROTE of 17.8% (ROE 15.5%), a 41% net margin, a 46% cost/income ratio and a 15.7% CET1 ratio about 1.9pp above the requirement. Capital return is substantial — roughly 84% of FY25 profit via dividends and buybacks, with the share count down ~13% since FY2021 — credit quality is strong (7bp of loan losses in Q3 2026), fees and assets under management are growing, and management guides to ROE above 15% through 2030 with a cost/income ratio falling to 40–42%.

What to Watch

Growth has stalled: operating income has been flat at €11.7–12.1B for three years, net profit fell 4% in FY2025 and again in Q3 2026 as lower policy rates cut net interest income (61% of income), and the five-year CAGRs are 5–6% against the framework's 15% bar. The shares trade at ~11.2x earnings and ~1.66x book, above FY21–FY25 averages, after a 53% re-rating in 2025 and a sharp fall on the Q3 print. Restructuring costs hit Q1 2026, the CET1 figure relies on a 70% dividend-deduction assumption pending ECB approval, and the Russia-related items in FY2022 and the IFRS 17 break reduce comparability. Insider ownership is below 5%.
