PDD Holdings scores as one of the strongest businesses on this entire site — 11 of 14 criteria met and a Martinero Index of 85 — and yet the stock has fallen almost 30% over the past year. That gap is the whole story here. Domestically, Pinduoduo has grown to overtake JD.com by GMV, positioning PDD as arguably China's #2 e-commerce platform, and the business remains extraordinarily capital-efficient: a 21.56% operating margin, a 10.7-point ROIC-WACC spread, essentially no debt, and $66.5 billion of net cash — more than half of the company's entire market capitalization. What's weighing on the stock is Temu, PDD's international cross-border arm: the U.S. eliminated the "de minimis" duty-free exemption for small Chinese packages in May 2025, and the EU followed with its own per-item customs duty in July 2026, together pushing Temu's global monthly active users down from roughly 525 million to 467 million as the company scrambles to shift from direct-from-China shipping to local warehousing. Layer on intensifying domestic competition with Alibaba and JD (including spillover from their quick-commerce subsidy war) and growth has cooled from 89.68% (FY2023) to just 9.65% (FY2025). None of that shows up as a fundamental scorecard problem yet — it shows up as multiple compression, which is exactly why the valuation looks so cheap: trailing P/E 9.35x, forward P/E roughly 7.70x (as shown in the chart on page 4), PEG 0.88, P/FCF 7.41x, EV/EBITDA just 3.80x.

Forward P/E (48.08x–8.57x FY21-24, now roughly 7.70x) has run below trailing P/E in every year shown, reflecting consensus expectations of continued earnings growth even as the multiple itself has compressed — see explainer below.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading trailing vs. forward P/E: trailing P/E (the solid line) is price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings, calculated at each fiscal year-end; forward P/E (the dashed line) is price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings, so it's forward-looking rather than a backward snapshot. Forward P/E has sat below trailing P/E in every year shown, including today (9.35x trailing vs. roughly 7.70x forward), because the market has consistently expected PDD's earnings to keep growing from wherever they currently stand. The PEG ratio — forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth — sanity-checks that a low multiple isn't just cheap in isolation but cheap relative to growth too; PDD's PEG currently runs around 0.88, comfortably under the 1.0 "fair value" line, reinforcing that the current discount isn't simply a growth-expectation illusion.
Balance sheet: PDD carries just $713M of total debt against $67.19B of cash, a net cash position of $66.48B ($46.70/share) — equal to more than half of the entire market cap. Current ratio of 2.62 and quick ratio of 2.21 are both very strong, and Debt/EBITDA of just 0.05x is about as clean as a balance sheet gets. What's Working

PDD is one of the most capital-efficient businesses covered on this site: near-zero debt, $66.5B of net cash, a 10.7-point ROIC-WACC spread, and a domestic platform that has overtaken JD.com by GMV. Free cash flow has compounded at over 40% annually for four years, and the current valuation (9.35x P/E against a 25.10x five-year average, 0.88 PEG) prices in far more pessimism than the fundamentals currently show.

What to Watch

Temu's international growth engine is genuinely impaired by the US and EU tariff/de minimis changes, and monthly active users have already fallen meaningfully as the company retools its logistics model. Domestic margin is under real pressure from Alibaba and JD.com competition. ROIC came in just a hair under this framework's 15% threshold despite an excellent WACC spread, and the same China-ADR structural risk that applies to Alibaba applies here too.
