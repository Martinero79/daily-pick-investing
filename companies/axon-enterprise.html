Axon Enterprise (formerly TASER International) is a public-safety technology company founded in 1993 and led by co-founder Rick Smith. It sells four things: TASER conducted energy devices ($914M, 33% of FY2025 revenue), Personal Sensors including body cameras ($397M, 14%), Platform Solutions such as in-car video, fixed cameras, drones and counter-drone systems from Dedrone ($266M, 10%) and Software & Services ($1,203M, 43%), the Axon Cloud subscription layer (digital evidence, Fusus real-time operations, Records, Axon 911 dispatch and the Draft One / AI Era Plan products). The model is hardware-led land-and-expand: agencies sign multi-year bundles that pair TASERs and cameras with cloud software, and the software line is now the largest and fastest-growing (+43% in Q3 2026, 48% of quarterly revenue). Annual recurring revenue reached $1.75B (+40%), net revenue retention is 126% and future contracted bookings stand at $15.8B. The tension is profitability. Gross margin has slipped from 62.7% (FY2021) to 59.8% (FY2025) and 56.7% in Q3 2026 as counter-drone hardware, memory costs and newer product categories dilute the mix, and GAAP operating income has been close to zero for two years because SBC (including the broad Employee XSP plan and a CEO performance award) runs at roughly 17% of revenue. Q3 2026 (reported Nov 5) added a second problem: revenue of $909M (+28%) missed consensus of about $937M, and about $130M of US federal Dedrone counter-drone deployments slipped out of 2026, cutting full-year growth guidance from 32–34% to about 30%. The stock fell 18.2% on Nov 6 and is now about 54% below its 52-week high. Axon also announced the largest investment in its history, $1.2B into drone maker Skydio, soon after a $1.15B convertible note issue in September, which pushes leverage up just as free cash flow guidance was lowered.
Four distortions to keep in mind: (1) GAAP vs adjusted — Adjusted EBITDA (25.9% margin) excludes SBC and amortisation; GAAP EBIT (4.6% margin in Q3 2026, −1.1% in FY2025) is what the scorecard uses. (2) Negative EBIT bases — FY2021 (−$168M) and FY2025 (−$30M) GAAP operating income were negative, so an EBIT CAGR does not exist and FCF/EBIT is meaningless; both are N/A and dropped from the index denominator rather than scored 0 or 5. (3) Investment gains in net income — 9M 2026 net income of $256M includes ~$256M of “other income” (largely strategic-investment fair-value changes), more than double operating income of $118M, so GAAP EPS and P/E are noisy. (4) Lumpy federal hardware — counter-drone deployments are large, hardware-heavy and timing-sensitive, which is what caused the Q3 miss.

Quarterly Trend — the Q3 2026 Miss & Guidance Cut
Q3 2026 was reported on Nov 5, 2026 after the close. The stock had already fallen 9.8% that day (the Skydio announcement came the same day; the cause of that day's move is not verified), then dropped a further 18.2% on Nov 6 to $313.00, and closed at $303.18 on Nov 9.
What actually moved the stock

Revenue of $909M (+27.9% YoY) was a record but missed consensus of about $937M, and non-GAAP EPS of $1.72 missed the roughly $1.89–1.90 expected (consensus figures vary by source). Growth decelerated for a third consecutive quarter. The cause was about $130M of US federal Dedrone counter-drone deployments (a ~$30M one expected in Q3 plus two totalling ~$100M expected in Q4) that Axon now excludes from 2026, which cut the full-year growth outlook to ~30% from 32–34%. Beneath the headline, Software & Services accelerated to +43% ($437M) and Platform Solutions grew +106% ($125M), but Connected Devices grew only +16% ($472M). Gross margin fell to 56.7% from 60.1% on counter-drone equipment mix, higher memory costs and new product scaling. Free cash flow was −$19M because ~$100M of cash went into inventory (full-year inventory investment ~$225M). GAAP operating income was $42M (4.6% margin) vs −$2M a year ago; Adjusted EBITDA was $236M (+33%, 25.9% margin). Management frames the miss as timing; the open question is whether the delayed deployments land in Q4 or 2027, and whether memory-cost pressure on hardware margin persists.

Share Count & Dilution

Shares outstanding rose from ~68.5M at end-2021 to ~81.4M now (about +19%) through SBC, equity raises (~$362M in 9M 2025, ~$100M in 9M 2026) and conversions of earlier convertible notes. There is no buyback programme and no dividend. SBC of ~$570–600M a year (~16% of revenue) is the economic cost of that dilution and is why GAAP profit is so much lower than Adjusted EBITDA. The diluted count (83.7M in Q3 2026) also reflects convertible-note dilution.

Balance sheet & convertible notes: At Sep 30, 2026 Axon held $1.46B cash, $0.18B short-term investments and $0.01B marketable securities ($1.66B total) against $2.87B of long-term notes (company: “$2.9B of outstanding convertible and senior notes”), a net debt position of about $1.2B, up $182M in the quarter. In Q3 it issued $1.15B of 0% convertible senior notes due Sep 2031 (conversion price reported at ~$652, a ~47.5% premium, per TIKR; not verified against the filing) and bought capped calls for $115M, while also refinancing its revolving credit facility. Interest expense runs ~$28M a quarter. Axon was net cash of $255M at end-FY2024 and $602M at end-FY2023, so the swing to net debt is recent (business combinations of $552M in 9M 2026; strategic investments on the balance sheet of $925M). The Skydio payment, if made in cash, would add to net debt. Net debt/EBITDA: ~5.6x on TTM GAAP EBITDA of ~$217M (scored); ~1.3x on the FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guide midpoint of ~$926M (forward, adds back ~$585M of SBC; not scored). Conversion of the notes into shares is a further dilution source if the stock recovers toward the reported conversion price.
Insiders & honest caveats: co-founder Rick Smith is CEO and holds roughly 3.1M shares (~3.8% per Intrinsic Alpha; a March 2026 Form 4 shows ~3.09M shares directly held after a sale). Benzinga lists total insiders at ~7.5%. I did not retrieve the 2026 proxy's beneficial-ownership table, so these are third-party figures and the founder stake is below 5% on its own. The CEO Performance Award and broad Employee XSP plan (about $240M of FY2026 SBC) tie pay to stock-price, operational and time-based hurdles; with the shares down 54% from the high, the value of those awards has fallen too.

What's Working

Demand and contract visibility are strong: revenue grew 34% a year over FY2021–25, Axon Cloud ARR is $1.75B (+40%) with 126% net revenue retention, and future contracted bookings are $15.8B (+39%), with about 20–25% to be fulfilled in the next 12 months. Software & Services accelerated to +43% and is now about half of quarterly revenue, a higher-margin mix (71% gross margin, over 80% for software alone). GAAP operating income has been positive for three quarters and Adjusted EBITDA grew 33% in Q3 with a 25.9% margin. New products (Body 5, TASER Ion, AI Era Plan, Axon Guardian) refresh the installed base, five-year bookings are up 25% year to date, the founder is still CEO, and the shares trade at about 35x forward earnings versus an 85x own-history average after a 54% drawdown.

What to Watch

GAAP profitability is near zero: operating margin was −1.1% in FY2025, FCF was flat over four years and SBC takes ~16–17% of revenue, so the gap between a 25.9% Adjusted EBITDA margin and a 4.6% GAAP operating margin is the central quality question. Gross margin is eroding (56.7% in Q3, Connected Devices 43.0%) on counter-drone mix and memory costs. Q3 missed on revenue and EPS and FY guidance was cut, with ~$130M of federal Dedrone revenue deferred and FCF guidance lowered to $350–400M. Net debt has swung to ~$1.2B with $2.9B of convertible and senior notes, the $1.2B Skydio investment is still to be funded, the share count is up 19%, and the 37% Skydio stake is a large, hard-to-value strategic asset (strategic investments already $925M, with large fair-value changes running through net income). Federal budget timing, law-firm securities-investigation notices (not a filed case) and Motorola Solutions in drones add risk.
