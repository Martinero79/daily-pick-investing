IonQ is the lead name in a new four-company Quantum batch on this site, alongside Rigetti Computing, D-Wave Quantum and Quantum Computing Inc — all pure-play, publicly traded quantum computing stocks. IonQ was picked to go first because, among the four, it is the clearest leader on the metrics that matter most for evaluating whether a pre-revenue-scale deep-tech company can survive long enough to matter: it has by far the largest revenue base (TTM $246.47M vs. low-to-mid single-digit millions per quarter for its closest pure-play peers), the deepest cash position (net cash of roughly $2.06B), and the most diversified customer base spanning commercial partnerships (Airbus, Hyundai Motor, GE Research, Goldman Sachs) and a genuinely substantial and growing slate of US government and defense relationships (Air Force Research Lab, DARPA, the Missile Defense Agency, the National Reconnaissance Office, and a newly launched IonQ Federal unit). Revenue growth is real and dramatic — five consecutive record quarters, +287% YoY in Q2 2026, backlog (remaining performance obligations) up 297% YoY to $485M. But this dashboard uses a different scorecard from the rest of this site on purpose: IonQ lost $510.4M in FY2025 on $130.0M of revenue, burned roughly $300M of free cash flow doing it, and diluted shareholders enormously to fund it — shares outstanding have roughly doubled from FY2024's weighted-average count of 213.0M to 397.3M today, partly through a $372M at-the-market equity program in 2025 and partly through the July 2026 acquisition of SkyWater Technology, a $1.8B cash-and-stock deal that gives IonQ its own US-based semiconductor foundry but added a large new block of shares. Gross margin has actually compressed as the business has grown, from 60.61% a year ago to 26.26% last quarter, as lower-margin hardware and systems sales take up a larger share of the mix. None of this makes IonQ a bad business relative to its quantum-computing peers — on every metric in this scorecard it is the strongest of the four candidates. It does mean this is a survival-and-execution story, not yet a profitability story, and the 79/100 Martinero Index below should be read that way: a real, weighted assessment of growth and balance-sheet strength within a still-unprofitable, still-dilutive, still-unproven sector — not a signal that this is a safe or conservative holding.
Read this before the scorecard: IonQ has never been profitable, has never generated positive free cash flow, and has no publicly stated timeline for GAAP profitability. Reported GAAP net income has also been distorted in recent quarters (e.g. a $1.87B net loss in Q2 2026, but a $753.7M net gain in Q4 2025) by non-cash, mark-to-market swings in warrant and earnout liabilities tied to IonQ's own stock price — these are not signals of operating performance and are excluded from this dashboard's narrative wherever possible in favor of operating-line figures. A 79/100 Martinero Index on this scorecard measures growth, cash survival and dilution risk for a speculative, pre-profit company — it is not comparable to, and should not be read the same way as, this site's standard mature-company Martinero Index used elsewhere.

What's Working

Revenue growth is real and accelerating (five consecutive record quarters), backlog is growing even faster than revenue, the balance sheet has roughly $2.1B in cash against almost no debt, and IonQ has the most diversified commercial-plus-government customer base of any pure-play quantum stock. Operating margin, while still deeply negative, has narrowed consistently for four straight years — the losses are shrinking as a share of a fast-growing revenue base, not widening.

What to Watch

Gross margin is compressing, not improving, as hardware/systems revenue scales — the opposite direction you'd want to see from a company trying to prove a profitable business model. Dilution has been severe (shares roughly doubled in under two years) and the SkyWater acquisition adds integration risk and a large new cost base on top of an already heavy cash burn. There is no stated timeline to GAAP profitability, and reported net income is currently unreliable quarter-to-quarter due to non-cash warrant/earnout revaluations. This remains, fundamentally, a bet on a technology and a company surviving and scaling faster than it dilutes and burns cash — not a bet on a proven, profitable business.
