Sanofi is the honest weak point among this batch's pharma names (7/14 criteria met, Martinero Index 52/100), and the gap is real rather than an accounting artifact. ROIC has sat between 8-10% every single year since FY2021, never approaching this framework's 15% bar and only narrowly clearing an estimated cost of capital — a genuine, structural profitability issue, not a one-off. Revenue, EBIT, and free cash flow have all grown in the low single digits or worse over the five-year window, and the current 23.85x P/E sits meaningfully above the company's own recent history even after excluding the artificially depressed FY2025 reading. Two real one-off items are worth flagging honestly: FY2022's unusually high 25.76% operating margin was boosted by gains tied to Sanofi's Regeneron equity stake sales, and FY2025's net income was lifted by a €2.61bn gain from divesting the Opella consumer-healthcare business to CD&R in April 2025 (Sanofi retained a 48.2% stake in the new joint venture and received €10.4bn in cash, funding an active buyback program). What is genuinely working: Dupixent, Sanofi's flagship immunology drug, just crossed €5B in quarterly sales for the first time (+37.6% in Q2 2026), the balance sheet carries only moderate leverage, and the share count has declined a real 18% over five years. L'Oréal remains Sanofi's largest shareholder at 7.2% of capital and 13.1% of voting rights — a real, disclosed strategic stake, though a corporate cross-holding rather than founder ownership.

Current, trailing & forward P/E: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. The trailing P/E series above is that same backward-looking ratio at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated Sanofi relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's price divided by consensus estimates of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and it captures expected growth that trailing P/E can't see. Sanofi's forward P/E of 8.57x is dramatically cheaper than its 23.85x trailing P/E — the widest forward-vs-trailing gap in this batch — implying the market expects a sharp earnings recovery from the currently depressed profit base (net margin just 8.09% TTM), consistent with consensus expecting margin normalization as one-off costs roll off. The PEG ratio backs this up: with consensus EPS growth forecast at around 8% annually over the next three years, Sanofi's forward P/E implies a PEG near 1.1 — right around the traditional 1.0 fair-value line, suggesting that once the depressed earnings base and the recovery embedded in forward estimates are both accounted for, the stock isn't priced as a bargain, but isn't priced as expensive either.
Balance sheet: Sanofi held about €23.6B in total debt against €6.4B of cash & investments as of the latest reported quarter — a net debt position of roughly €17.2B, or 1.27x EBITDA (1.00x at FY2025-end). This is moderate, manageable leverage, though it has crept up somewhat as the €10.4B Opella proceeds have been partly redeployed into an active share buyback program.

What's Working

Dupixent just crossed €5B in quarterly sales for the first time, growing 37.6% and returning to strong growth in 2026 after a 2025 price adjustment. The balance sheet carries only moderate leverage, FCF conversion clears 93%, and the share count has declined a genuine 18% over five years, aided by the €10.4bn in cash Sanofi received from the 2025 Opella divestment. L'Oréal's continued 7.2% stake and demonstrated willingness to influence governance is a real, if unconventional, form of long-term shareholder alignment.

What to Watch

This is a genuine, structural profitability gap, not a one-off: ROIC has sat in the 8-10% range every year since FY2021 and has never approached this framework's 15% bar. Revenue, EBIT, and FCF growth have all been modest-to-flat over the full five-year window. The current 23.85x P/E sits roughly 30% above Sanofi's own recent average even after excluding the distorted FY2025 reading — a rich price for a business with this growth and return profile. Reported quarterly operating margins are notably volatile (14-29%) due to recurring R&D impairments, restructuring, and legal charges, which is worth remembering when comparing quarter to quarter.
