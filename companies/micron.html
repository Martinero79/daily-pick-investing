Micron is the US-based leg of the memory trio covered in this batch, and the one where the gap between "the stock" and "the scorecard" is most pronounced. The stock's one-year move (market value up about +225%, though roughly 38% below its $1,255 high) is driven by the same AI memory supercycle as its Korean peers: Micron holds roughly 21% of the HBM market (a solid #2 behind SK hynix), and its DRAM share has been closing in on the #2 spot held by SK hynix, with Nvidia's CEO publicly citing memory as a supply constraint on AI accelerator production. The most recent quarter (Q4 FY2026, ended Sep 3, 2026) posted $54.23B in revenue, up 379.1% year-over-year, with an 80.7% GAAP operating margin (FY2026 revenue $133.19B, operating income $99.34B) — numbers that would look extraordinary anywhere on this site. Where this dashboard's scorecard pulls back from the enthusiasm: Micron's fiscal year (ending in late August) means the FY2021-FY2025 window used for this framework's CAGR calculations mostly predates the sharpest part of the ramp, which shows up in the FY2026 quarters (now fully reported) instead — so the 5-year revenue, EBIT and FCF growth rates all miss this framework's 15% bar despite the current run-rate being spectacular. More structurally, Micron also runs the highest capex intensity of the three memory names (23% of TTM revenue, funding a large US manufacturing buildout), has the weakest FCF conversion of the group, and — unlike Samsung and SK hynix — has no chaebol-style anchor shareholder to fall back on for the insider-ownership criterion; it's a straightforwardly widely-held US public company with just 0.22% insider ownership. None of this makes Micron a bad business. It makes it the more speculative, momentum-driven member of this three-company memory group, with a scorecard that says "real and improving fast" rather than SK hynix's "already excellent."

What's Working

The current run-rate is genuinely extraordinary: revenue up 379.1% YoY and GAAP margin at 80.7% in the latest quarter, the steepest acceleration of any company in this memory batch. ROIC/WACC spread and balance sheet strength are both best-in-group, and with free cash flow catching up, the entry multiple (14.9x P/FCF) now sits far below its own recent average even after a +225% one-year gain in market value.

What to Watch

This is the most momentum-driven of the three memory names: weaker FCF conversion, the highest capex intensity, a more contested (rather than dominant) market position, and no anchor shareholder to fall back on if sentiment turns. The FY2021-FY2025 CAGR misses are a genuine artifact of timing rather than weak fundamentals, but they're a reminder that this framework's backward-looking criteria haven't yet caught up with what's happening in FY2026.
