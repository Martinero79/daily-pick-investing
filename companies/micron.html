Where this dashboard's scorecard pulls back from the enthusiasm: Micron's fiscal year (ending in late August) means the FY2021-FY2025 window used for this framework's CAGR calculations mostly predates the sharpest part of the ramp, which shows up in FY2026's quarters instead — so the 5-year revenue, EBIT and FCF growth rates all miss this framework's 15% bar despite the current run-rate being spectacular. None of this makes Micron a bad business. It makes it the more speculative, momentum-driven member of this three-company memory group, with a scorecard that says "real and improving fast" rather than SK hynix's "already excellent."

What's Working

The current run-rate is genuinely extraordinary: revenue up 345.72% YoY and margin at 80.37% in the latest quarter, the steepest acceleration of any company in this memory batch. ROIC/WACC spread and balance sheet strength are both best-in-group, and even after a +692% year, the entry multiple sits modestly below its own recent average.

What to Watch

This is the most momentum-driven of the three memory names: weaker FCF conversion, the highest capex intensity, a more contested (rather than dominant) market position, and no anchor shareholder to fall back on if sentiment turns. The FY2021-FY2025 CAGR misses are a genuine artifact of timing rather than weak fundamentals, but they're a reminder that this framework's backward-looking criteria haven't yet caught up with what's happening in FY2026.
