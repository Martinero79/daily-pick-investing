Maarten picked Nintendo the way he picked Booking Holdings — not because a screen flagged it, but because he's been playing Mario and Zelda since he was a kid and wanted to see what the checklist actually says about it. The honest answer: it's a genuine mixed bag, and this happens to be about the worst fiscal year to look at it in. Nintendo just launched Switch 2 (June 2025), and hardware launch years are structurally brutal on this framework — revenue nearly doubled to ¥2,313.1B (+98.56%) as tens of millions of new consoles shipped, but gross margin collapsed from 60.96% to 39.30% and operating margin fell to 15.57% (from 34.96% just four years ago) because early-cycle hardware is sold at thin or negative margins before the high-margin software attach rate catches up. That single dynamic is why revenue CAGR (+8.1%), EBIT CAGR (-11.7%), FCF CAGR (-1.8%) and the new margin-stability criterion all land as misses despite Switch 2 objectively outselling the PS5 at launch. What isn't a launch-year artifact: Nintendo carries zero debt and a ¥2.2T net cash pile, buybacks have quietly shrunk the share count by roughly 11% over five years, capex is essentially nothing (1.17% of revenue — Nintendo doesn't manufacture its own hardware), and at a trailing P/E of roughly 19.75x the stock actually sits modestly below its own five-year average multiple (≈22.90x). The clear miss, and the one that matters most for a "beloved brand" thesis: insider and founder ownership is under 1% — the Yamauchi family's direct control ended after Hiroshi Yamauchi's 2013 death, and Nintendo today is fully institutionally owned like Booking Holdings, not founder-run like Oracle. Net: 7 of 14 criteria met, Martinero Index 55/100 — middle of the pack, not a standout. This is sentiment more than a screaming value case right now; the checklist mildly likes the balance sheet and the valuation, is neutral on the market position, and actively dislikes this specific fiscal year's growth and margin trend. That's an honest, unglamorous result for a stock Maarten picked mostly because he loves the games.

Nintendo's fiscal quarters run Apr-Jun, Jul-Sep, Oct-Dec, Jan-Mar. Latest reported period: Q1 FY2027 (three months to Jun 30, 2026, reported Aug 6, 2026).
Q1 FY2027 Headlines

Revenue was ¥517.8B, down 9.5% YoY as the quarter lapped the initial Switch 2 launch frenzy. Operating profit still rose 150.5% YoY to ¥142.6B, helped substantially by roughly $300M in U.S. IEEPA tariff refunds booked as a reduction to cost of sales — a one-off that flatters this quarter's margin and shouldn't be extrapolated. Nintendo reaffirmed full FY2027 guidance: net sales of ¥2.05T (-11% YoY), operating profit of ¥370B, and net income of ¥310B (-27% YoY) — management itself expects this year's launch-year revenue spike to partially reverse as Switch 2 hardware unit sales step down from FY2026's pace to a guided 16.5M units. Switch 2 lifetime shipments reached roughly 23.68M units by Jun 30, 2026, already Nintendo's fastest-selling console ever.

Gross Margin Trend (FY2022-FY2026)
Gross margin held in a tight 55-61% band for four years, then fell off a cliff in FY2026, from 60.96% to 39.30% — a −15.98pp drop versus FY2023 alone (the window used for criterion #14), driven almost entirely by the cost of mass-producing Switch 2 hardware in its first launch year.
Market position, honestly stated: within dedicated video game consoles, Nintendo is the clear current-generation leader — Switch 2 outsold the PS5 in the Jan-Mar 2026 quarter, the original Switch is Nintendo's best-selling console ever at 155.92M lifetime units, and Nintendo's share of the global active console installed base is projected to climb to roughly 49% by 2027. Scored here as a "Clear #1" on that basis. But zoom out to the total gaming industry — which also includes PC gaming (Steam, Epic) and mobile gaming (a larger revenue pool than console gaming globally) — and Nintendo's overall share of "gaming" spend is far smaller and more fragmented; Nintendo has repeatedly struggled to translate its IP into meaningful mobile revenue (Mobile & IP-related income was just ¥67.7B in FY2025, a small slice of the total). Both things are true: dominant in dedicated console hardware, a minor player in the much larger all-platforms gaming market.

Trailing & Forward P/E — FY22-26 & Current
Current trailing P/E of roughly 19.75x sits modestly below the FY22-26 average of ≈22.90x (itself dragged up by FY2025's 42.22x spike, when earnings were depressed mid-transition but the multiple hadn't caught down yet). Unusually, forward P/E (27.24x currently) now sits above trailing P/E — the reverse of the normal pattern — because FY2026's trailing earnings were flattered by one-off tariff refunds and FX gains, while FY2027 guidance points to profit falling 27% as the launch-year hardware surge fades.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers, and the PEG check: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY22-26 series above) is that same ratio computed at each fiscal year-end. Forward P/E is today's price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings. Nintendo is one of the few names on this site where forward P/E currently runs above trailing P/E (27.24x vs 19.75x) — the opposite of the usual growth-discount pattern. That's consistent with Nintendo's own guidance: FY2026's trailing earnings were lifted by one-off items (U.S. tariff refunds, FX gains) on top of the Switch 2 launch surge, while FY2027 guidance calls for net income to fall 27% as hardware volumes normalize and those one-offs don't repeat. The PEG ratio (forward P/E ÷ expected earnings growth) reinforces this: stockanalysis.com puts it around 2.5, well above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line and above the 1.0-2.0 "reasonably priced growth" band this framework generally likes to see — because near-term consensus earnings growth is actually negative, not positive, making a simple PEG read less useful here than the entry-multiple-vs-own-history comparison above.
Balance sheet: Nintendo carries zero net debt — net cash of ¥2,216.9B against FY2026 EBITDA gives a Net Debt/EBITDA of roughly -5.9x (i.e., net cash, comfortably inside and beyond this framework's <2x bar). ROIC of 38.25% (FY2026) clears the assumed 8.0% WACC by a wide margin, though this figure is flattered by an unusually small invested-capital base once the enormous cash pile is netted out — a more conservative, cash-adjusted view of returns on operating capital would be lower, but still clearly profitable. Share count has declined steadily via modest buybacks, from roughly 1.294B shares (FY2022-equivalent) to about 1.153B today — a reduction of roughly 10.9% over four years, without ever taking on debt to fund it.

Governance note: Nintendo is not a founder-controlled company today. Hiroshi Yamauchi, whose family ran Nintendo for four generations, retired as president in 2002 and died in 2013; the Yamauchi family's direct control ended with him, and no successor family stake anywhere near that scale exists on the current shareholder register. The largest disclosed shareholder, The Master Trust Bank of Japan (Trust Account), holds roughly 16.5% — but that's an institutional trust bank administering assets on behalf of thousands of pension funds and pooled investors, not a founder or insider with personal skin in the game. That makes Nintendo's ownership structure much closer to Booking Holdings' fully-institutional model than to Oracle's Larry-Ellison-style founder concentration — a genuine trade-off for anyone drawn to this stock partly because of its founder-driven creative legacy (Miyamoto, Iwata) rather than its current cap table.

What's Working

Zero debt and a ¥2.2T net cash pile; genuinely dominant, launch-validated position in dedicated console hardware (Switch 2 already outselling the PS5); capex is close to nothing since Nintendo doesn't own its own factories; the share count has quietly shrunk about 11% over five years without leverage; and the entry multiple is modestly below Nintendo's own five-year average despite the stock's run-up.

What to Watch

This is a genuine console-cycle trough for margins: gross margin cratered 16pp in a single year on Switch 2 hardware-ramp costs, and management's own FY2027 guidance calls for revenue, operating profit and net income to all decline as the launch-year unit surge normalizes — the checklist's growth and margin misses aren't a one-off data quirk, they're the current reality of the business. Insider/founder ownership is under 1%; the Yamauchi era is over and this is now an institutionally-owned company. And software/IP monetization outside dedicated hardware (mobile, in particular) remains a persistently small part of the business relative to Nintendo's brand reach.
