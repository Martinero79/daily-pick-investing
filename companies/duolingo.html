Duolingo, founded in 2011 by Luis von Ahn and Severin Hacker and listed on Nasdaq in July 2021, runs a mobile-first, game-like language-learning app. Anyone can use it free with ads; paying users on Super Duolingo and Max tiers generated $873M of FY2025's $1,038M revenue (84%), with advertising ($80M), the Duolingo English Test ($42M) and in-app purchases ($40M) making up the rest. The financial turnaround is real: revenue grew from $251M (FY2021) to $1,038M (FY2025), GAAP operating income went from -$60M to +$141M and free cash flow from $6M to $370M (35.6% margin), all with a net-cash balance sheet and ~1.7% capex. The stock, however, has been de-rated: S&P shows it down about 53% over the 52 weeks to Oct 26, as growth decelerated through 2026 (revenue growth +26.5%, +18.3% and +13.7% in Q1, Q2 and Q3, versus +41% in mid-2025). Q3 2026, reported on Nov 3, showed revenue of $309.0M (+14%), bookings of $316.1M (+12%), daily active users of 64.9M (+29%) and GAAP EPS of $0.54, with FY2026 guidance of $1.221B revenue and $323M adjusted EBITDA. The shares jumped 26% the next day ($128.00 to $161.27) and closed at $154.25 on Nov 9. Note the gap between user growth (+29%) and revenue/bookings growth (+12–14%): users are growing faster than monetisation, which is the central thing to follow.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Tax benefit in GAAP earnings — FY2025 GAAP net income of $414M and EPS of $8.57 are far above pre-tax income of ~$182M (derived: pre-tax margin 17.6% on $1,038M); the gap (~$232M) is a net income-tax benefit, and Q3 2025 EPS of $5.95 was one quarter of it. A trailing P/E of 19.6x at end-FY2025 therefore overstated earnings power; on the current trailing basis it is ~52x ($154.25 / $2.98 over the last four quarters). (2) Negative early EBIT — FY2021-23 operating income was negative, so EBIT CAGR is undefined and FCF CAGR (+185%) is a base effect. (3) Stock compensation and two classes — GAAP EBIT is after, and FCF is before, stock compensation, and share count has risen ~26% since 2021; founders' Class B shares give them voting power beyond their economic stake.

What's Working

A very large and fast-growing consumer subscription business: revenue up 4x in four years, 64.9M daily users (+29%), 72% gross margin, free cash flow of $370M in FY2025 (35.6% margin) and $1.29B of net cash with almost no capex. Operating margin turned positive in 2024 and was 13.6% in FY2025. Subscription revenue is growing about 18% YoY and daily users keep growing faster than revenue. Founders remain in charge, and on price-to-free-cash-flow the stock is at 17.9x versus an own-history average of ~49x after a roughly 53% decline over the year to Oct 26.

What to Watch

Growth is decelerating quickly (Q3 revenue +13.7% vs +41% a year earlier; bookings +12%), users are growing twice as fast as revenue, and GAAP operating margin fell to 8.5% in Q3 from 14.6%. GAAP earnings and P/E are distorted by a ~$232M tax benefit, FCF flatters because stock compensation is added back, and the share count is up ~26% since 2021. The market is fragmented with AI chatbots and free alternatives as competition, the stock is volatile (+26% in a day, -53% over a year), short interest is high, and securities-class-action notices are in the news. The FY2026 targets (revenue $1.221B, adjusted EBITDA $323M) imply a Q4 growth rate near 14%. Unverified here: the 52-week high, market share, Class B voting power and the Q4 report date.
