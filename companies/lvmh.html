Half-Year Development — Latest Results
LVMH publishes quarterly sales updates but reports full P&L semi-annually. Latest reported period: H1 2026 (six months to June 30, 2026).
H1 2026 Headlines

Revenue was €38.6B, down 3% as reported, but organic growth turned positive at +2% — the reported decline reflects a 5% negative currency headwind and a 1% negative portfolio/perimeter effect, not weaker underlying demand. Growth accelerated sequentially: Q1 organic growth was +1%, improving to +3% in Q2, a genuine positive inflection after the 2023-2025 slowdown. Profit from recurring operations was €8.7B, an operating margin of 22.5% — down just 10 basis points year-over-year, reflecting strong cost discipline through the downturn. By segment: Watches & Jewelry led with 9% organic growth (11% in Q2), Wines & Spirits grew 5% organically, Selective Retailing grew 5% organically to €8.40B, while Fashion & Leather Goods (the largest and most-watched segment) declined 5% reported but returned to +1% organic growth in Q2 — an important signal given this division's size and margin contribution.

Half-Year Group Revenue (€B)
Reported revenue declined again year-over-year, but the underlying organic trend turned positive for the first time in several halves — a genuine, if early, sign of stabilization after the 2023-2025 slowdown.
Why the FX/organic gap matters: LVMH earns revenue globally but reports in EUR, so a strong euro against the US dollar, Japanese yen, and Chinese yuan mechanically shrinks reported growth even when constant-currency demand is improving — as it was here. The organic-growth figure strips out currency and portfolio (M&A) effects and is the more reliable read on underlying demand trends.

What's Working

LVMH remains the unambiguous global #1 in luxury goods, with the most direct founder-family control of any GRANOLAS name (Arnault family, >50% of capital, >65% of voting rights). Leverage is moderate, FCF conversion clears 80%, the share count has declined modestly, and the current 20.87x P/E sits about 22% below its own five-year average — the deepest valuation discount in this batch. H1 2026 showed a genuine positive inflection: organic growth turned positive and accelerated through Q2, even as reported figures were held back by currency effects.

What to Watch

This is a real, ongoing industry-wide luxury demand slowdown, not a one-off distortion — revenue has declined for two straight years from the FY2023 peak, EBIT growth has been essentially flat over the full window, and free cash flow is down outright since FY2021. ROIC has fallen from ≈20% to 12.55%, still clearing an estimated cost of capital but well off its prior level. Global luxury remains a genuinely fragmented, well-competed market. The organic-growth recovery seen in H1 2026 is early and not yet confirmed over multiple quarters, and Fashion & Leather Goods — LVMH's largest, highest-margin division — only just returned to positive organic growth in Q2 2026 after a prolonged decline.
