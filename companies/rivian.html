Rivian Automotive is a US electric-vehicle maker founded in 2009 by CEO R.J. Scaringe and listed on NASDAQ in November 2021 at $78 a share. It sells the R1T pickup and R1S SUV, a commercial delivery van developed with Amazon, and since 2026 the smaller and cheaper R2 SUV, built to widen the addressable market. A second segment, Software & Services, covers vehicle architecture and software work (including the Volkswagen joint venture), Autonomy+, repair and maintenance, accessories and financing; it grew from $0.48B in FY2024 to $1.56B in FY2025 (29% of revenue). The business case is a bet that unit volumes from R2 and software revenue can finally outgrow a fixed cost base that has produced $6.9B, $5.7B, $4.7B and $3.6B of operating losses in FY2022-25. The progress is real: gross margin has gone from -188% to +2.7% in three years, Q2 2026 revenue was $1.66B (+27.2% YoY) with a 10.8% gross margin, and Q3 2026 deliveries hit a record 19,248. But the company still burned $3.5B of free cash flow over the last twelve months, the share count is up about 61% since end-2021 (+15% in the last year alone), and net cash has gone from $16.5B to roughly zero. Read the revenue CAGR with care: it is measured from a near-zero base.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Tiny FY2021 base — revenue was $55M in FY2021, so the +214.6% 4-yr revenue CAGR says little about the business today; the 3-yr FY2022-25 CAGR is +48.1% and annual growth has slowed to +12.1% (FY2024) and +8.4% (FY2025). (2) Negative-over-negative ratios — EBIT and FCF are negative in every year, so EBIT and FCF CAGRs are not meaningful and “FCF conversion” (FCF/EBIT = +69% arithmetically) only compares two losses; all are scored 0, not as passes. (3) Partner funding and dilution — the VW joint venture and equity raises have kept the balance sheet funded, but they show up as a 61% larger share count; per-share figures are not comparable with FY2021 (FY2021 EPS of -$22.98 reflects a pre-IPO share base).

R2 Ramp & Latest Quarter (Q2 2026)
The investment case for the next 12-18 months is whether R2 volume and software revenue lift gross profit faster than cash burns. Q2 2026 reported on Jul 30, 2026; Q3 2026 financial results are due Oct 29, 2026.
What actually moved the stock

Q2 2026 revenue was $1,658M (+27.2% YoY): Automotive $1,143M (+23.3%) and Software & Services $515M (+37.0%). Gross profit was $179M (10.8% margin), the best margin since Q1 2025 (16.6%), and the operating loss was $836M, little changed from the prior four quarters (-$833M to -$1,114M) because R&D, SG&A and R2 launch costs are rising alongside volume. Free cash flow was -$849M (TTM -$3.5B). Net cash flipped to net debt of $43M, with S&P data showing about $1.3B of common-stock issuance in Q2 2026 and the share count up 6.1% QoQ (15.2% YoY). On Oct 2, 2026 Rivian reported record Q3 deliveries of 19,248 (+46% YoY; production 19,751, +84%) and reaffirmed its 65,000-70,000 delivery guidance for 2026; the stock still fell 3.1% to $14.30 as investors had hoped for a guidance raise (Barron's), and Rivian also announced an R2 recall for battery-pack fasteners. The CFO announced on Aug 27 that she will step down at the end of October 2026.

What's Working

Gross margin has moved from -188% (FY2022) to +2.7% (FY2025) and +7.5% TTM, with a 10.8% gross margin in Q2 2026; Software & Services tripled to $1.56B in FY2025 and is growing 37% YoY. Q2 2026 revenue grew 27% and Q3 2026 deliveries were a record 19,248 (+46%) as the cheaper R2 SUV begins to ramp, with 2026 delivery guidance reaffirmed. The operating loss has shrunk from $6.9B (FY2022) to $3.6B (FY2025), and FCF burn narrowed from $6.4B to $2.5B over the same period. Strategic partners (Volkswagen, Amazon, Uber) provide funding, validation and a route to autonomy and software revenue that pure EV makers lack, and consensus expects revenue of about $11.8B by FY2027.

What to Watch

The company still loses roughly two-thirds of every revenue dollar at the operating line, burned $3.5B of free cash flow in the last twelve months, and has converted $16.5B of end-2021 net cash into a net-debt position, with the share count up 61% along the way. Annual revenue growth has slowed to +8.4%, and FY2025 Automotive revenue fell 14.6%, so the next leg depends on R2 volume, pricing and unit costs. Capex is still above 30% of revenue, ROIC is -20.6% against an 11.3% cost of capital, and the auto industry is structurally overcapacity-prone with thin margins even for scaled players. Early R2 recalls, a CFO departure at the end of October, and a stock that fell after record deliveries show how much good news is already expected. Several inputs could not be verified: founder and employee ownership, strategic holders' current stakes, the exact size of VW-related revenue, and a 2026 US EV market-share rank.
