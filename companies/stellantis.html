Stellantis was created in January 2021 by merging Fiat Chrysler and PSA, and for three years it looked like the merger's synergy case was working: AOI margin ran 12–13% from FY2021 to FY2023 and industrial free cash flow reached €12.9B in FY2023, which funded a €1.55 dividend and large buybacks. Then the cycle turned. FY2024 revenue fell 17% as North American dealer inventories were cut and pricing weakened, AOI margin dropped to 5.5% and industrial free cash flow turned negative (-€6.0B). FY2025 was worse at the headline level: revenue was €153.5B (-2%), AOI was a loss of €842M (-0.5%), industrial free cash flow was -€4.5B, and €25.4B of unusual charges tied to a strategic reset (including a shift away from some EV programmes as regulation changed) produced a net loss of €22.3B. The board suspended the 2026 dividend. Under CEO Antonio Filosa, 2026 has started to show stabilisation: H1 revenue grew 10% to €81.6B, AOI was +€1.73B (2.1% margin), and Q2 industrial free cash flow was +€1.0B, but Q2 AOI of €773M still missed analyst estimates and management only targets positive industrial free cash flow in 2027. The balance sheet is the main cushion: industrial net cash of €6.7B at end-2025, though it has fallen sharply each year since 2023. On this site's scorecard the company meets 4 of 14 criteria with a Martinero Index of 22/100.

What's Working

The industrial balance sheet is still net cash (€6.7B) with €46B of industrial liquidity, giving room to fund the turnaround. Revenue growth returned in H2 2025 and accelerated to +13% in Q2 2026, with North America up 32%. AOI and industrial free cash flow turned positive in Q2 2026 under new CEO Antonio Filosa, and management has reaffirmed 2026 guidance and a 2027 target for positive industrial free cash flow. The anchor shareholders (Exor, Peugeot family, Bpifrance) are long-term aligned, and the diluted share count is about 8.5% lower than in FY2021.

What to Watch

Profitability and cash have collapsed: AOI margin went from 13.4% (FY2022) to -0.5% (FY2025), gross margin fell 14.5 points, and industrial free cash flow has been negative for two years, so FCF conversion and growth criteria all score zero. The recovery so far is small (H1 2026 AOI margin 2.1%, Q2 missed estimates), the industrial net cash cushion has shrunk from €29.5B to €6.7B, the dividend is suspended, and the shares sit near record lows. Risks named in the company's reporting and in the press include US tariffs and fuel-economy rule changes, weakness in Jeep volumes, European competition from Chinese brands, EV programme resets and further restructuring charges. Q3 2026 results on Oct 28 are the next test of whether the H2 improvement the company has guided for is arriving.
