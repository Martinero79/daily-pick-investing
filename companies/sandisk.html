SanDisk makes NAND flash memory — the storage chips behind everything from SD cards and USB drives to the massive enterprise SSDs hyperscalers now need to feed AI workloads. It's an unusual company to research: spun off from Western Digital in February 2025 as an independent public company, it swung from a $1.64bn net loss in its first fiscal year to an $11.43bn profit in its second, as a structural NAND shortage — AI data centers competing with everyone else for the same flash memory — handed the industry extraordinary pricing power almost overnight. The stock followed: from around $235 at the start of 2026 to an all-time high near $2,354 by late June, before a violent pullback (a forced hedge-fund unwind, plus a scare from a giant Chinese memory IPO) took it down roughly a third. It has since partly recovered on a bullish Investor Day. The honest problem for a checklist like this one: SanDisk doesn't have five years of standalone financial history to test growth durability against, and NAND has a long history of boom-bust cycles — this scorecard is unusually light on "met" criteria not because the business is bad, but because there isn't yet enough track record to confirm today's numbers are durable rather than a supply-shock peak.
This is a "supply-shock peak or new normal" case: gross margins near 84–85% and a >100% ROIC reading are extraordinary for a commodity memory business with a multi-decade history of brutal boom-bust cycles. The bull case (per SanDisk's own Investor Day framework) is that AI-driven demand and disciplined supply keep pricing elevated through 2030. The bear case is that this is the sharpest point of a cyclical spike that Chinese capacity (YMTC, CXMT) and the industry's own capacity response will eventually erode.

What actually moved the stock after the June 25 peak

Late July 2026: Situational Awareness, an AI-focused hedge fund that had built a ~$5.7bn concentrated stake in SanDisk (alongside a similar-sized Micron position), was forced to unwind after the stock fell in a single month — the fund's leveraged book sank sharply and Ken Griffin's Citadel bought the bulk of the position to stabilize it. Jul 27, 2026: China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) — a DRAM maker, not a direct NAND rival — IPO'd and surged, reviving broad fears about Chinese memory capacity; SanDisk fell double digits that session even though some coverage noted the market may have conflated DRAM with NAND. Aug 13, 2026: an Investor Day unveiling an FY2028-30 framework (mid-to-high-teens revenue growth, ~80% gross margins, a new "High Bandwidth Flash" AI product line) sent the stock up double digits and kicked off a partial recovery.

What's Working
  • Genuinely extraordinary current profitability: TTM operating margin ~61.6%, non-GAAP gross margin hit ~84.6% by Q4 FY2026, net-cash balance sheet with FCF conversion around 92%
  • Revenue ramped from $1.90bn (Q1 FY26) to $8.97bn (Q4 FY26) in a single fiscal year as NAND pricing power kicked in
  • Management is backing confidence with real money: a $15.5bn total buyback authorization, one of the largest relative to market cap in this whole screen
  • A genuine multi-year structural angle — AI data centers need storage (SSDs), not just GPUs, and SanDisk's new "High Bandwidth Flash" architecture targets exactly that for 2027 and beyond
  • The Kioxia joint-venture manufacturing model shares R&D and capex risk rather than SanDisk bearing 100% of fab costs alone
What to Watch
  • No five-year standalone track record exists — SanDisk has only been an independent filer since Feb 2025, so four of the thirteen criteria here are scored N/A rather than tested
  • NAND flash has a long history of brutal boom-bust pricing cycles; SanDisk itself posted a $1.64bn net loss the year before this year's $11.43bn profit
  • Market share (~11-14%) trails Samsung, SK Hynix and Kioxia — a strong #4-5 player, not the #1/#2 this model screens for, and Chinese suppliers (YMTC, CXMT) are closing fast
  • A concentrated, crowded trade: hedge fund Situational Awareness built a ~$5.7bn stake, then was forced to unwind it in July 2026 after a 47% one-month drawdown — a reminder of how much of the move has run through momentum positioning, not just fundamentals
  • ~0.6% insider ownership and no founder/controlling holder; capex intensity looks light on SanDisk's own reported line but real capital commitments flow through the Kioxia JV
