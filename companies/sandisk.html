SanDisk makes NAND flash memory — the storage chips behind everything from SD cards and USB drives to the massive enterprise SSDs hyperscalers now need to feed AI workloads. It's an unusual company to research: spun off from Western Digital in February 2025 as an independent public company, it swung from a $1.64bn net loss in its first fiscal year to an $11.43bn profit in its second, as a structural NAND shortage — AI data centers competing with everyone else for the same flash memory — handed the industry extraordinary pricing power almost overnight. The stock followed: from around $235 at the start of 2026 to an all-time high close of $2,335 on June 25, then a violent pullback that bottomed near $1,016 on July 29 (down ~56% from the peak, on a forced hedge-fund unwind plus a scare from a giant Chinese memory IPO), before a bullish Investor Day drove a partial recovery to today's $1,568.87 (still down ~33% from the peak). The honest complication for a checklist like this one: three of the five fiscal years shown on page 2 are Western Digital's Flash segment on a predecessor, gross-profit-only basis — not standalone SanDisk — and free cash flow and a "vs. own trading history" valuation read still can't be tested at all before the 2025 spin-off.
This is a "supply-shock peak or new normal" case: gross margins near 84–85% and a >100% ROIC reading are extraordinary for a commodity memory business with a multi-decade history of brutal boom-bust cycles — the FY2023 trough (7.1% gross margin) to FY2026 peak (71.5%) swing on page 2 shows exactly how violent those cycles can be. The bull case (per SanDisk's own Investor Day framework) is that AI-driven demand and disciplined supply keep pricing elevated through 2030. The bear case is that this is the sharpest point of a cyclical spike that Chinese capacity (YMTC, CXMT) and the industry's own capacity response will eventually erode.

Quarterly Trend & the August Whiplash
Eight fiscal quarters, Q1 FY2025 through Q4 FY2026 — spanning the tail of the prior NAND downturn through the current supply-shock ramp. The stock's round trip after the June 25 peak was driven by positioning and sentiment shocks, not by any of the numbers below.
What actually moved the stock after the June 25 peak

The stock bottomed at $1,015.89 on July 29, 2026 — down ~56% from the June 25 close of $2,335.00 — before recovering to $1,786.85 by Aug 17. Late July 2026: Situational Awareness, an AI-focused hedge fund that had built a ~$5.7bn concentrated stake in SanDisk (alongside a similar-sized Micron position), was forced to unwind after the stock fell sharply in a single month — the fund's leveraged book sank and Ken Griffin's Citadel bought the bulk of the position to stabilize it. Jul 27, 2026: China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) — a DRAM maker, not a direct NAND rival — IPO'd and surged, reviving broad fears about Chinese memory capacity; SanDisk fell double digits that session even though some coverage noted the market may have conflated DRAM with NAND. Aug 13, 2026: an Investor Day unveiling an FY2028-30 framework (mid-to-high-teens revenue growth, ~80% gross margins, a new "High Bandwidth Flash" AI product line) sent the stock up double digits and kicked off the recovery.

What's Working
  • Genuinely extraordinary current profitability: TTM operating margin ~61.6%, non-GAAP gross margin hit ~84.6% by Q4 FY2026, net-cash balance sheet with FCF conversion around 92%
  • Revenue ramped from $2.31bn (Q1 FY26) to $8.97bn (Q4 FY26) in a single fiscal year as NAND pricing power kicked in, after two years of flat-to-declining quarterly revenue in the prior downturn
  • Management is backing confidence with real money: a $15.5bn total buyback authorization, one of the largest relative to market cap in this whole screen
  • A genuine multi-year structural angle — AI data centers need storage (SSDs), not just GPUs, and SanDisk's new "High Bandwidth Flash" architecture targets exactly that for 2027 and beyond
  • The Kioxia joint-venture manufacturing model shares R&D and capex risk rather than SanDisk bearing 100% of fab costs alone
What to Watch
  • Most of this five-year picture is a predecessor entity, not standalone SanDisk — FY22-24 are Western Digital's Flash segment on a gross-profit-only basis, so the revenue and "EBIT" CAGRs on page 2 bridge two different reporting entities
  • NAND flash has a long history of brutal boom-bust pricing cycles; gross margin swung from 36.2% (FY22) to a 7.1% trough (FY23) to today's 71.5% (FY26), and SanDisk itself posted a $1.64bn net loss the year before this year's $11.43bn profit
  • Market share (~11-14%) trails Samsung, SK Hynix and Kioxia — a strong #4-5 player, not the #1/#2 this model screens for, and Chinese suppliers (YMTC, CXMT) are closing fast
  • A concentrated, crowded trade: hedge fund Situational Awareness built a ~$5.7bn stake, then was forced to unwind it in July 2026 after a 47% one-month drawdown — a reminder of how much of the move has run through momentum positioning, not just fundamentals
  • ~0.6% insider ownership and no founder/controlling holder; capex intensity looks light on SanDisk's own reported line but real capital commitments flow through the Kioxia JV
