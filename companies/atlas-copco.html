Atlas Copco, founded in 1873 and headquartered in Nacka outside Stockholm, makes compressors, vacuum pumps and abatement systems, industrial power tools and assembly systems, and portable power and pumping equipment. Its mining and infrastructure arm was split off as Epiroc in 2018. Revenue grew from SEK 110.9bn in FY2021 to SEK 168.3bn in FY2025 (+11% a year, helped by acquisitions), with a reported EBIT margin steady at 20–21.6%, and a dividend payout of roughly half of earnings (SEK 3.00 ordinary plus an SEK 2.00 extra distribution for 2025). The business is about 60% equipment and 40% aftermarket: service (spare parts, maintenance, repairs) was 38% of FY2025 revenue and 43% in Compressor Technique. 2025 was a soft year for reported numbers (revenue -5%, EBIT -11%) mostly because a strong krona shaved about 6% off revenue. In 2026 demand turned: Q2 orders hit a record SEK 51.0bn (+27%, organic +26%), led by a +60% jump in Vacuum Technique orders on semiconductors, and the share price reached a record SEK 212.60 on Oct 6, 2026. The flip side is price: the stock trades at ~35x trailing earnings against a ~31x own average, and organic growth has been low single digit.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) The currency is part of the return — Atlas Copco books revenue in dozens of currencies and reports in SEK, so a strong krona (as in 2025) lowers reported revenue and profit, and a euro, dollar or Danish-krone investor also takes the SEK exchange rate on the share price itself (see the Currency Exposure page). (2) Order strength is cyclical and concentrated — the 2026 order surge is led by semiconductor vacuum equipment, a famously cyclical end market; service (38% of revenue) cushions downturns but is not immune. (3) Valuation has re-rated — ~35x trailing against ~31x average, with the A share about 9% below its Oct 6 record and management guiding only that customer activity will “remain at the current level”.

What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 orders were a record SEK 50,951m (+27%, organic +26%; currency -4%), well above the SEK 44.9bn analyst average, with management citing double-digit order growth in every business area and a +60% jump in Vacuum Technique (semiconductors). Revenue was SEK 44,974m (+9.1%; organic +8%, acquisitions +4%, currency -3%). Operating profit was SEK 9,249m, a 20.6% margin (flat year on year), or 21.0% adjusted (20.4% a year earlier). Basic EPS was SEK 1.45 (Q2 2025: 1.34). The CEO said demand “improved significantly, particularly from the semiconductor industry”; the A share rose 4.4% on the report day. Orders are running ahead of revenue (a book-to-bill above 1.1), so Q3 and Q4 revenue should lean on the order backlog, but reported SEK figures stay exposed to the exchange rate. Net indebtedness was SEK 22.3bn (net debt/EBITDA 0.5x).

Currency Exposure — a Stockholm Listing, a Global Earnings Stream
Atlas Copco reports in SEK and its shares trade in SEK, but almost all of its revenue is earned outside Sweden. That creates two currency layers for an investor outside Sweden: the company’s own translation effect on reported results, and the exchange rate on the share price itself. Theme page linked to the site topic on how currencies affect investment returns.
Currency exposure (company disclosures, FY2025): revenue by region was Asia/Oceania 36%, Europe 28%, North America 26%, Africa/Middle East 6% and South America 4%. Sweden is not broken out in the material I reviewed; as a proxy only 1,721 of 55,549 average employees (3%) are in Sweden, so almost all revenue is earned in foreign currencies. Estimated currency mix (not disclosed in the sources I reviewed): roughly a quarter US-dollar-linked, a bit over a quarter euro and other European currencies, and a third spread across the Chinese yuan, yen, won, rupee and other Asian currencies — inferred from the regional split, not reported by the company. Manufacturing is also global (Belgium, US, China, India, Germany, Italy), which gives a partial natural hedge on costs.

How the translation effect works

When the krona strengthens, Atlas Copco’s foreign sales and profits translate into fewer kronor: 2025 revenue fell 5% in SEK with organic volumes -1%, and the group margin lost ~0.6 points. When the krona weakens, as it has done against the dollar and the euro in 2026, the effect reverses (Q2 2026 currency drag shrank to -3%). The company reports in SEK, so local-currency strength is invisible unless you read the organic line.

What it means for non-SEK investors

A euro, dollar or Danish-krone investor holds a SEK asset whose earnings are mostly in other currencies. If SEK strengthens, reported SEK profits fall but the share is worth more in the investor’s own currency; if SEK weakens, the reverse. Over this window the krona’s weakness cut the gain by roughly 9–11 points in each currency versus the SEK return. The exposure is real in both directions and can be hedged by the investor or by the choice of listing or fund share class — this page is not advice.

What's Working

A global leader in compressors and vacuum with a 20%+ EBIT margin through a currency headwind, ROIC about 12 points above its cost of capital, net debt of only 0.5x EBITDA and a decentralised model that keeps compounding through small acquisitions. Service is 38% of revenue and growing faster than equipment, orders hit a record SEK 51.0bn in Q2 2026, gross margin is 0.9 points above FY2022, and Investor AB gives the register a stable anchor. Cash conversion is high enough to fund a SEK 5.00 dividend and ~SEK 11.6bn of bolt-ons in 2025.

What to Watch

Growth: a 11% revenue CAGR is mostly price, acquisitions and the 2021-23 inflation spike, and 2025 organic revenue fell 1%. Currency has moved reported revenue by 3–11% in each of the last four quarters and EBIT by SEK 1.7bn in a quarter. Valuation is ~13% above its own average at ~35x, with the A share near its record. The order surge leans on semiconductor vacuum equipment (+60% in Q2 2026), which is cyclical, and automotive tooling is weak. Ingersoll Rand, Kaeser and Hitachi in compressors, and Ebara, Busch and Shimadzu in vacuum, compete directly.
