Copart is a Dallas-based operator of online vehicle auctions. Insurers, fleets, rental companies and dealers consign damaged or used vehicles; Copart stores them in its own yards, markets them to registered buyers worldwide through its auction platform and collects fees from both sides plus transport and storage. About 85% of FY2026 revenue ($3.97B) was service fees and 15% ($0.70B) came from selling vehicles it owns The model has compounded for decades because a large yard network and a deep buyer pool are hard to copy, and Copart's net cash of $4.4B (and no dividend) funds yard expansion and buybacks. FY2026 was the weakest year in a long time: revenue grew 0.4% to $4.67B, operating income fell 2.6% to $1.65B (margin 35.4%) and net income fell 4.4% to $1.48B, with Q4 net income down 17.4% on a 32.0% operating margin. U.S. service revenue declined while international service revenue grew 12.4% to $581M. In September 2026 Copart agreed to buy ACV Auctions for $10.50 per share in cash (about $1.9B), a digital wholesale marketplace; the deal closed on Oct 15, 2026 and management has said it expects it to be accretive in FY2028. The shares have fallen ~35% over the last year (market cap $27.9B) and now trade at ~19x trailing earnings against a five-year fiscal-year-end average of ~29x, a gap the market is pricing as the cost of lower growth.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Slower growth, not a loss of position — FY2022 grew 30% on post-pandemic used-vehicle and insurance-claims inflation, so a four-year CAGR from that base understates the earlier run-rate while FY2026's +0.4% shows how quickly volumes can stall; I did not find a clean volume-versus-price split. (2) Interest income — roughly $182M of FY2026 pre-tax income is interest on cash, so net income margin (31.8%) overstates operating performance and will shrink if cash is spent on acquisitions or buybacks. (3) ACV — the deal closed after the fiscal year-end, so none of ACV's revenue, costs or integration expenses are in the figures on this page.

What's Working

A dominant position in U.S. salvage auctions (one of two scaled platforms) has produced a 35% EBIT margin, $4.4B of net cash and ROIC near 29%, with no debt. International service revenue is growing double digits (+12.4% in FY2026) and the business still converts 77% of EBIT to free cash flow. The multiple has compressed from ~34-37x to ~19x, so the starting valuation is far below its five-year average even though earnings are near their highs. Share count fell ~4.7% in a year and management has cash to keep buying back stock and to integrate ACV without borrowing.

What to Watch

Growth has stalled: FY2026 revenue +0.4%, EBIT -2.6%, net income -4.4%, and Q4 net income -17.4% with a 32% operating margin, the lowest of the last eight quarters. U.S. service revenue is shrinking and the drivers (volumes, pricing, competition) are not clear from the data used here. EBIT margin has dropped four years running from 39.3% to 35.4%. Capex is heavy and uneven ($337M vs $569M the year before), so FCF growth is partly timing. ACV (~$1.9B) adds integration risk and uses cash that earned interest. The score would look different if the low P/E turns out to reflect a lasting slowdown rather than a pause; analysts are split (HSBC Hold, others Buy).
