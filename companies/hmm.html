HMM is Korea's largest ocean carrier and a direct global competitor to Maersk — but at roughly a fifth of Maersk's fleet size and ranked #9 worldwide by capacity, it plays in a different weight class. The scorecard here is genuinely rough, and it's important to read why: this framework's fixed FY2021-25 window happens to start at the single most extraordinary year in container-shipping history — the pandemic freight-rate supercycle, when HMM's operating margin briefly exceeded 53% — and ends in a normalized, post-boom 2025. Every growth CAGR in this dashboard (revenue, EBIT, FCF) is therefore negative, not because the underlying 2023-25 business collapsed, but because the FY2021 base was a one-off windfall unlikely to recur. That context matters, but it doesn't rescue the rest of the picture: HMM diluted shares by 92.9% during its state-backed restructuring (CB conversions and rights issues), still trades at roughly double its own five-year-average P/E even after excluding the boom years, and remains majority-owned by two state creditors, KDB and KOBC (≈65% combined), that have been trying and failing to exit since 2023 — a Harim Group deal collapsed in February 2024, and POSCO Holdings is now conducting preliminary due diligence on KDB's stake with no decision expected before December 2026. What does hold up: a genuinely strong, consistently net-cash balance sheet, real capital returns (a completed ₩2.14T buyback, a ₩700/share dividend), and a sharp Q2 2026 revenue rebound (+29.7% YoY) showing the top line can still move quickly when freight rates cooperate.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio at each fiscal year-end, distorted here by the 2021-22 pandemic freight-rate windfall. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings and tracks the trailing series closely, including the same FY2023 spike to 17.41x as consensus priced in a sharp post-boom earnings normalization. Today's forward P/E (13.72x) sits almost exactly on top of trailing P/E (13.70x), suggesting the market doesn't expect much near-term change in HMM's earnings power from here. A PEG ratio (forward P/E ÷ expected annual EPS growth) is not meaningfully computable for a shipping cyclical like HMM, whose earnings are driven by freight rates rather than a steady structural growth rate — the level of forward P/E itself, not a PEG multiple, is the more honest cross-check here.

Gross Debt/EBITDA is a modest 2.01x (FY2025), but the net position is net cash overall; current ratio 5.81x and quick ratio 5.62x (FY2025) both point to ample near-term liquidity. Governance note — a temporary state-creditor stake, not founder alignment: HMM has no founder or family controlling shareholder. Its two largest holders, KDB and KOBC, are state-run entities that rescued the company (then Hyundai Merchant Marine) from bankruptcy with a 2016 public-fund injection and together still hold roughly 65% of HMM. Both have been trying to exit since 2023: a ₩6.3T deal to sell a majority stake to poultry group Harim collapsed in February 2024 over management-rights disputes, and as of September 2026 POSCO Holdings is conducting preliminary due diligence on KDB's ≈32.6% stake (≈₩7T / ≈$5B), with HD Hyundai and Hanjin Group also reportedly circling; POSCO has said no decision is expected before Dec 29, 2026. This criterion is scored Met on raw stake size — ≈65% is far above the ≈5% "meaningful" bar — but the Martinero Index score below is docked one band from the theoretical maximum: this is a temporary, actively-for-sale creditor position explicitly seeking an exit, not a long-horizon aligned owner in the founder or Lauritzen-Fonden mold, and a change of control could arrive at any time with terms not yet known.

What's Working

The balance sheet is genuinely strong — net cash throughout the entire five-year window, ample liquidity (current ratio 5.81x), and a P/B of just 0.73x. HMM has also returned real capital to shareholders (a completed ₩2.14T buyback, a ₩700/share FY2025 dividend) even while ramping fleet investment. Q2 2026's freight-rate rebound (+29.7% YoY) shows the top line can still move sharply higher when rates cooperate.

What to Watch

Nearly every growth and profitability metric here is negative or well below this framework's bars, though a large part of that is the fixed FY21-25 window capturing an extraordinary, one-off pandemic freight-rate peak as its starting point rather than a genuine multi-year decline — worth reading in context, not at face value. The bigger open question is ownership: KDB and KOBC together still hold roughly 65% of the company as explicitly-for-sale state creditors, and POSCO's preliminary due diligence (no decision before Dec 29, 2026) means a change of control, and the terms that come with it, could reshape the stock at any time. Heavy historical dilution (+92.9% share count since FY2021) and a rich valuation relative to its own (admittedly boom-distorted) history are real, independent concerns.
