Cisco Systems, founded in 1984, sells the switches, routers, wireless gear, security software, collaboration tools and (since the March 2024 Splunk deal) observability and data-analytics software that run most corporate networks, plus a $15B-a-year services business. Revenue was $51.6B in FY2022 and $63.3B in FY2026 (+5.3% a year), with GAAP operating income of $14.0B rising to $16.1B (+3.5% a year) and free cash flow flat at about $12.8B. The record is lumpy: FY2023 was inflated by a supply-chain order backlog (revenue $57.0B, FCF $19.0B), FY2024 fell 5.6% as customers digested that inventory, and Splunk, bought for roughly $28B, then added scale but also $22B of net new debt and heavy amortisation, which pulled GAAP operating margin from 27.4% (FY2023) to 22.1% (FY2025). The turn came in FY2026: revenue grew 11.8%, GAAP operating margin recovered to 25.4% and the networking segment grew 22.5% as hyperscaler AI orders reached $9.3B for the year. The shares rose from about $68 to about $116 between July 2025 and July 2026, then fell about 8% on Aug 13 despite a beat-and-raise because gross margin slipped 210 basis points on memory costs and hardware mix. The result is a mature, cash-generative company that the market now values like a growth stock: ~35x trailing earnings against a ~23x average since FY2022.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Growth numbers are distorted by Splunk and the pandemic cycle — FY2022 had no Splunk, so the 4-year revenue CAGR of +5.3% includes acquired revenue; excluding the whole Security segment, revenue grew only ~3.6% a year (a rough proxy, see page 2). (2) The multiple has doubled — P/E rose from ~16x (Jul 2022) to ~35x while EPS grew 18%; the FY2027 guide of $5.05–5.11 non-GAAP EPS puts the stock at ~23x forward. (3) Margins now depend on memory prices and AI mix — management guided Q1 FY2027 gross margin to 65–66% and warned of a slight headwind through the year.

Quarterly Trend (Q1 FY2025 – Q4 FY2026)
Eight fiscal quarters (Q1 FY2025 = Aug–Oct 2024; Q4 FY2026 = May–Jul 2026). Year-on-year growth has stepped up from ~7.5% to ~17.6% as AI infrastructure orders convert to revenue. Q1 FY2027 is due around Nov 11, 2026 (estimated date).
What the quarter actually showed

Q4 FY2026 revenue was a record $17.25B (+17.6% YoY, +8.9% on Q3), above the ~$16.8B consensus; product revenue rose 24% to $13.5B and services were flat at $3.8B. Networking revenue was $9.8B, orders for networking rose about 40% and Cisco booked $4B of hyperscaler AI orders in the quarter, $9.3B for the year (about 4.5x FY2025). GAAP operating margin reached a record-for-the-window 27.7% and GAAP EPS was $0.97 (+52%). But non-GAAP gross margin fell to 66.3% from 68.4% a year earlier (GAAP 64.1%) on memory costs and hardware mix, and the shares fell about 8% on Aug 13 (from $123.88 to $113.47) even though FY2027 guidance was above consensus. Guidance: revenue $72.2–73.4B (vs ~$68.7B consensus), non-GAAP EPS $5.05–5.11, Q1 FY2027 gross margin 65–66%, hyperscaler AI revenue ~$7.5B in FY2027 (from ~$4B). Guidance and order figures are as reported in press coverage of the Aug 12 release; check Cisco's release for exact wording.

Share Count & Capital Return

Shares outstanding fell from ~4.2B (FY2022) to ~3.94B, about −5% over four years (aggregator-derived, so the exact figure could be −4% to −6%); buybacks of $5–8B a year barely outrun stock compensation of $3.8B. Ownership caveats: Cisco has no founder or controlling holder; the 0.03% insider figure and 82.2% institutional figure are S&P Global data. Cisco runs a broad employee stock purchase plan, but I could not verify that employees hold 5% or more, so it is not credited under criterion #12.
Balance sheet: cash and investments of $15.9B against total debt of $31.2B at Jul 25, 2026 gives net debt of $15.3B, ~0.8x EBITDA (gross debt ~1.7x). The aggregator's total-debt line may include lease liabilities and does not net off Cisco's customer-financing receivables. Debt was $9.5B before Splunk; Cisco issued ~$32B of debt in FY2024 and has since repaid about $12–22B a year while refinancing. Interest coverage is ~11x.

What's Working

A 25% GAAP operating margin, 64.5% gross margin, 2% capex intensity and net debt of only ~0.8x EBITDA. Growth has re-accelerated from −5.6% (FY2024) to +11.8% (FY2026) and +17.6% in the latest quarter, with hyperscaler AI orders of $9.3B in FY2026 and FY2027 guidance of $72–73B. Cisco is still the #1 Ethernet switch vendor overall, pays a growing dividend and returns ~$14.5B a year to shareholders.

What to Watch

The valuation: ~35x trailing and ~23x forward, 55% above its own four-year average, after the shares rose ~70% in a year. Four-year growth is still low (revenue +5.3%, EBIT +3.5%, FCF flat), partly because the Splunk deal added debt, amortisation and a flattering base. Gross margin is under pressure from memory costs and hardware mix (non-GAAP −210bp in Q4). Cisco is only #3 in data-centre Ethernet behind NVIDIA and Arista, and AI orders depend on a few hyperscalers.
