Shell is one of the world's largest energy companies and the biggest LNG portfolio among the Western majors. For a Dutch reader: Royal Dutch Shell was long dual-headquartered in The Hague and London; in January 2022 it moved its headquarters and tax residence to London, dropped "Royal Dutch" from its name and collapsed its two share classes into one, Shell plc. The shares still trade on Euronext Amsterdam (ticker SHELL, an AEX constituent) alongside London and New York; this dashboard follows the Amsterdam line in euro, while Shell reports in US dollars. The scorecard shows a mature, cash-generative, lightly levered business: net debt is under 1x EBITDA, free cash flow covers about 80% of operating income, and buybacks have cut the share count by roughly 27% since FY2021. What it does not show is growth: revenue is flat since FY2021, FCF has declined, and the FY2022 earnings peak means the 5-year P/E average looks low. Earnings are strongly tied to oil and gas prices — with Brent near $114, Q3 2026 followed Q2 with income before tax of about $18.0B (against $29.8B for all of FY2025), which should not be read as a baseline.

Q3 2026 Headlines

Revenue $92.8B, +36.2% YoY (vs $68.2B in Q3 2025) and slightly below Q2's $94.7B. Income before taxation, used here as the operating-income proxy, was $18.0B (19.4% margin, vs 11.3% on the provider's figure a year earlier). Net income attributable to shareholders was $12.1B, EPS $2.15 (vs $0.91). Free cash flow was $16.3B (operating cash flow $21.0B; cash capex $7.9B, which included the ARC Resources acquisition). Net debt fell to $39.7B from $41.8B at the end of Q2 (gearing 16.6%). Shell declared a Q3 dividend of $0.3906 per share (unchanged versus Q2) and announced a new $5.0B share buyback, expected to run until the Q4 2026 results, after completing the $4.2B programme announced with Q2. Shell attributes the result mainly to higher refining margins, higher realised LNG prices and higher Integrated Gas volumes, partly offset by lower trading and optimisation. Basis note: Shell's own headline Adjusted Earnings were $10.8B (vs $9.8B in Q2 and $5.4B in Q3 2025); the figures on this page are IFRS reported, and net income includes identified items and a current cost of supplies adjustment of $0.4B. This is a high-price-cycle quarter, not a new baseline.

Shell's earnings, cash flow and buyback capacity move with oil, gas and refining margins; a price reversal would reverse the 2026 picture quickly. Shell has also been reshaping its portfolio (recent disposal announcements include its French mobility business and its Nigeria domestic gas business, and the Infineum JV is reportedly exploring a sale per press reports); in Q3 it completed the ARC Resources acquisition and agreed to take full ownership of Tri Star Energy.

Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's price over the last 12 months of reported earnings; the FY21-25 series is the same ratio at each year-end; forward P/E is today's price over analysts' consensus for the next 12 months. For a cyclical commodity producer all three are noisy: trailing earnings were at a trough-to-mid-cycle level at the end of FY2021, a record in FY2022, and are now being lifted by $100+ oil (three of the last four quarters are 2026 quarters). A low P/E at a cycle peak can mean the market expects earnings to fall. The provider's PEG ratio is about 1.14 (third-party figure).
Balance sheet: at Sep 30, 2026 Shell reported $76.1B of total debt (including lease liabilities) and net debt of $39.7B (gearing 16.6%), down from $41.8B at Jun 30, 2026 and $45.5B at FY2025-end; the ARC Resources acquisition added $2.5B of net debt and leases, of which $1.9B of debt was repaid in September. Net debt has stayed within $38-52B over the FY21-25 window while Shell spent roughly $15B a year on buybacks and $8-9B on dividends. Net debt/EBITDA of 0.85x (FY25) is moderate, but note it includes leases and that buybacks absorb a large share of free cash flow.

What's Working

Low leverage for an oil major (net debt/EBITDA ≈0.85x), free cash flow that covers roughly 80% of operating income, a share count down about 27% since FY2021 through buybacks, a dividend that has grown every year of the window (USD 0.893 to 1.446 per share), and a gross margin that has held at 24-26% through the whole cycle. Q3 2026 delivered $16.3B of free cash flow, cut net debt to $39.7B and brought a new $5.0B buyback.

What to Watch

Growth is absent: revenue is flat and FCF has declined since FY2021, operating margin is only 11% and ROIC (11.4%) sits modestly above an estimated 8% cost of capital. The TTM P/E (about 9.1x) now sits slightly below its FY21-25 average, but only because trailing earnings are inflated by the 2026 price spike; the criterion flips back if earnings normalise. Earnings track oil and gas prices — Brent near $114 is a cycle high. Climate litigation, portfolio disposals and buyback pace versus free cash flow are further variables.
