Shell is one of the world's largest energy companies and the biggest LNG portfolio among the Western majors. For a Dutch reader: Royal Dutch Shell was long dual-headquartered in The Hague and London; in January 2022 it moved its headquarters and tax residence to London, dropped "Royal Dutch" from its name and collapsed its two share classes into one, Shell plc. The shares still trade on Euronext Amsterdam (ticker SHELL, an AEX constituent) alongside London and New York; this dashboard follows the Amsterdam line in euro, while Shell reports in US dollars. The scorecard shows a mature, cash-generative, lightly levered business: net debt is under 1x EBITDA, free cash flow covers about 80% of operating income, and buybacks have cut the share count by roughly 27% since FY2021. What it does not show is growth: revenue is flat since FY2021, FCF has declined, and the FY2022 earnings peak means the 5-year P/E average looks low. Earnings are strongly tied to oil and gas prices — with Brent near $114, H1 2026 operating income already ran at a $39B annualised pace, which should not be read as a baseline.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E (current only, estimate)
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's price over the last 12 months of reported earnings; the FY21-25 series is the same ratio at each year-end; forward P/E is today's price over analysts' consensus for the next 12 months. For a cyclical commodity producer all three are noisy: trailing earnings were at a trough-to-mid-cycle level at the end of FY2021, a record in FY2022, and are now being lifted by $100+ oil. A low P/E at a cycle peak can mean the market expects earnings to fall. The provider's PEG ratio is about 1.14 (third-party figure).
Balance sheet: at Jun 30, 2026 Shell held $31.4B of cash and investments against $73.1B of total debt (including lease liabilities), a net debt of $41.7B; at FY2025-end it was $45.5B. Net debt has stayed within $38-52B over the FY21-25 window while Shell spent roughly $15B a year on buybacks and $8-9B on dividends. Net debt/EBITDA of 0.85x (FY25) is moderate, but note it includes leases and that buybacks absorb a large share of free cash flow.

What's Working

Low leverage for an oil major (net debt/EBITDA ≈0.85x), free cash flow that covers roughly 80% of operating income, a share count down about 27% since FY2021 through buybacks, a dividend that has grown every year of the window (USD 0.893 to 1.446 per share), and a gross margin that has held at 24-26% through the whole cycle. Q2 2026 delivered $17.4B of free cash flow and cut net debt.

What to Watch

Growth is absent: revenue is flat and FCF has declined since FY2021, operating margin is only 11% and ROIC (11.4%) sits modestly above an estimated 8% cost of capital. The P/E is about 22% above its FY21-25 average (itself flattered low by FY2022). Earnings track oil and gas prices — Brent near $114 is a cycle high, and Q3 2026 results on Thu Oct 29 are pending. Climate litigation, portfolio disposals and buyback pace versus free cash flow are further variables.
