C.H. Robinson Worldwide is a Minnesota-based, asset-light third-party logistics company with roots in 1905. It owns almost no trucks, ships or aircraft: it matches roughly 75,000 customers with about 450,000 contracted carriers, moving some 37 million shipments a year (about $23B of freight) and earning the spread. North American Surface Transportation (NAST: truckload and LTL brokerage) is the engine at $11.6B of FY2025 revenue and 62% of AGP; Global Forwarding (ocean, air, customs) adds $3.1B; Robinson Fresh and Managed Solutions make up the rest. Capex is under 0.5% of revenue, so when volumes are weak the business sheds working capital rather than burning cash. Since CEO Dave Bozeman took over in 2023 the story has been “Lean AI”: automating the quote-to-cash process, cutting average headcount about 11% year on year (11,471 in Q2 2026) and lifting productivity by more than 60% since the end of 2022. That is why operating income has risen while AGP and revenue have not. The latest quarter (Q2 2026) showed the pattern clearly: revenue +19.3% to $4.93B on higher truckload, LTL, air and ocean prices, but AGP only +6.5% and income from operations +18.4% to $255.7M, with NAST volume +1.5% against a Cass Freight Shipment Index down 3.3% (13th straight quarter of outgrowth). Read revenue growth with care: it is largely price pass-through.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Post-COVID freight cycle — FY2021-22 revenue ($23.1B, $24.7B) and EBIT were inflated by extraordinary freight rates, then fell 29% in FY2023 with EBIT down 59%, so CAGRs measured from FY2021 are noisy; the Cass index has now fallen year on year for 15 straight quarters, so the business is arguably at a cyclical trough. (2) Divestiture — the Europe Surface Transportation business was sold effective Feb 1, 2025 (“All Other” revenue fell $0.6B in FY2025), which depresses reported FY2025 growth. (3) Legal risk — on May 14, 2026 the US Supreme Court ruled 9-0 in Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II (a case involving CHRW) that negligent-hiring claims against freight brokers are not pre-empted by federal law, and management has referred to a large verdict it intends to appeal; I could not verify the dollar amount, so none is stated.
Maarten's note

CHRW is the cleanest US example on this site of an asset-light broker that has learned to make more money from a smaller top line. Revenue is down about a third from the FY2022 peak and AGP is down about 24%, yet FY2025 operating income of $795M is about 63% of the peak and ROIC is 21.9%. Capex is around 0.4% of revenue, the share count has fallen from roughly 144M to 117M since 2021, and the dividend has grown for 28 years. If the freight cycle ever turns, the operating leverage built since 2022 should show up quickly.

What makes me cautious is price, not quality. The stock is at about 28x trailing earnings against a five-year average near 23.5x (about 21x forward, roughly in line with its own forward average), so most of the good news looks priced in even after a ~30% drop from the high. Gross-billing growth this year is inflated by price pass-through, and ocean and truckload gross profit are still falling. The Supreme Court ruling adds a legal and insurance-cost tail risk to the whole brokerage model that is hard to size.

Against DSV (Martinero 48) the two score the same, for opposite reasons: CHRW wins on returns, balance sheet and buybacks, DSV on global scale and a lower valuation versus its history, and both lose on growth. Maersk (44) and DFDS (47) carry ships and ferries and score lower. I would want to see a volume recovery, not just price, before getting more constructive. My own reasoning only, not advice.

Latest Quarter (Q2 2026): Price Up, Productivity Up, Cash Down
Reported Jul 29, 2026. The case for the next 12 months is whether volume recovers while the cost base stays lean; Q3 2026 is due around Oct 28, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was $4,934M (+19.3% YoY), driven by higher truckload, LTL, air and ocean prices, while adjusted gross profit rose only 6.5% to $738M. Income from operations grew 18.4% to $255.7M (adjusted +19.5% to $263.2M), diluted EPS +23.8% to $1.56 (adjusted $1.61, +24.8%) and the adjusted operating margin on AGP reached 34.7% (+360bp). Operating expenses were up just 1.0% with average headcount down 10.8%. NAST AGP grew 8.6% on 1.5% volume growth, with LTL AGP up 21.8% but truckload AGP down 1.4%. Global Forwarding revenue grew 12.4% but AGP only 0.7% (ocean -2.7%, customs -9.4%, air +22.9%), and its margin ex-restructuring was 33.4%. The weak spot was cash: operating cash flow was $35.9M vs $227.1M a year earlier as receivables rose $704M in H1, while buybacks ($226M) and dividends ($75M) returned $301M in the quarter.

What's Working

Lean AI is converting a weak freight market into higher earnings: Q2 2026 income from operations rose 18.4% on AGP growth of 6.5%, headcount is down about 11%, and productivity is up more than 60% since 2022. NAST has outgrown the Cass index for 13 straight quarters. Returns are excellent (ROIC 21.9% vs WACC 8.7%), capex is only ~0.4% of revenue, FCF conversion was 106% in FY2025, the share count is down 19% in five years and the dividend has risen for 28 years. Industry consolidation after the Supreme Court ruling could push smaller brokers out and favour a scaled player with a deep balance sheet.

What to Watch

Headline revenue growth is price pass-through, not volume: Q2 revenue +19.3% but AGP +6.5%, and ocean and truckload AGP are still falling. Valuation is full at ~28x trailing earnings versus a ~23.5x own-history average, even after a ~30% fall from the $210 high. Operating cash flow dropped 84% in Q2 on receivables, and net debt/EBITDA of 1.95x is at the edge of the 2x bar with buybacks running above free cash flow. The Montgomery ruling exposes brokers to negligent-hiring liability and could raise insurance and legal costs, with a large verdict under appeal. Growth criteria score zero, insiders own 0.27%, and several inputs could not be verified (exact market share, management holdings, top-holder stakes beyond Vanguard).
