Chevron Corporation is the second-largest US integrated oil and gas company, with Upstream (crude, natural gas and LNG; $53.5B of FY2025 sales) and Downstream (refining, marketing and chemicals; $130.9B of FY2025 sales) segments. Its profits follow oil and refining margins: operating income was $16.1B in FY2021, $42.0B at the FY2022 peak, and $17.6B in FY2025. The largest recent event is the all-stock acquisition of Hess (announced October 2023, closed July 2025 after an arbitration ruling), which brought a share of the Stabroek block in Guyana and Bakken assets and added roughly 300M shares; the share count is up about 2% over five years net of buybacks. Capex has doubled since FY2021 to $17.3B, so FCF is lower than in FY2021 even though operating cash flow is higher. In late October 2026 Brent trades near $114 on the Iran and Red Sea/Saudi tensions, the stock is near its 52-week high ($234.08), and Q2 2026 delivered $14.8B of operating income and $18.1B of FCF; the five-year growth rates below do not yet capture that. Read every ratio as cycle-dependent: Q3 2026 results are due Friday October 30 and are not in these numbers.
Cycle caveat: (1) Oil price — Brent near $114 (late Oct 2026) is far above the price environment behind FY2025's 9.6% margin, so TTM and Q2 2026 figures are flattered relative to a mid-cycle year. (2) Hess — FY2025 includes only about 5.5 months of Hess, and S&P shows shares +12% YoY; per-share figures mix old and new share counts. (3) Reported vs adjusted — Chevron's own adjusted earnings exclude special items and will differ from the GAAP-based figures here. Chevron also reported Oct 6, 2026 that it will divest its Hess Midstream interest and DJ Basin crude midstream assets (Reuters/Business Wire), and press reports point to a CEO succession in progress; neither is reflected in the numbers.

What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was $67.9B (+53.5% YoY, +42% QoQ) and operating income $14.8B (21.9% margin) against $3.8B a year earlier, as oil and product prices jumped with the Middle East conflict. Net debt fell from $40.1B to $28.5B in the quarter; dividends ($3.5B) and buybacks ($3.1B) continued. Treat this as a spike quarter: the prior seven quarters were in a $3.5B-$5.4B operating-income range. I could not verify the composition of Q2 net income (one-off gains vs operations), so no adjusted figure is shown.

What's Working

Balance sheet and cash conversion: net debt/EBITDA is 0.56x, FCF was 94% of EBIT in FY2025 and Q2 2026 produced $18.1B of FCF. Dividend per share has grown every year (39 straight years per S&P) and the TTM payout is about half of FCF. Hess adds long-life Guyana barrels, ROIC is above WACC, gross margin is higher than in FY2022, and the stock trades at ~12x forward earnings.

What to Watch

This is a commodity price-taker at a cycle high: Brent near $114 vs a FY2025 9.6% margin, so the TTM numbers and ~12x forward P/E depend on prices staying high. Five-year revenue, EBIT and FCF CAGRs are weak or negative, capex has doubled, trailing P/E (21.9x) is 1.43x its FY21-25 average (flattered low by the FY2022 peak), and the stock is within 2% of the analyst target. Hess integration, the Q3 report on Oct 30 and the reported CEO succession are open items. No insider block.
