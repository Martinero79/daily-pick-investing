Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio at each fiscal year-end, distorted here by FY2021's near-zero-earnings base and FY2023's one-off tax benefit. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings and isn't affected by those historical distortions — the more useful lens for judging whether ServiceNow is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed. Forward P/E has fallen steadily from 96.16x (FY2021) to 19.29x today, a much smoother and more credible de-rating story than the noisy trailing series. The PEG ratio — forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth — adds a growth-adjusted check: ServiceNow's PEG currently runs around 0.79, below the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, after the roughly 46% share-price decline over twelve months; trailing GAAP earnings ($1.39 per share over the last twelve months) are depressed by acquisition amortization and stock comp, so the trailing multiple overstates how expensive the shares look on the company's own non-GAAP measures.
Balance sheet: ServiceNow carries $8.42B of total debt (including leases; $2.09B short-term debt/commercial paper and $5.44B long-term debt) against $6.96B of cash and investments, a modest net debt position of $1.45B ($1.40/share) at Sep 30, 2026. Current ratio of 0.72 and quick ratio of 0.58 are tight (typical for a subscription business with large deferred-revenue liabilities), and interest coverage of about 12.5x (stockanalysis.com) is down from 22.0x as interest expense rose to $81M in Q3 from $6M a year ago. No shares were repurchased in Q3 (vs $2.2B in the first nine months). Net debt/EBITDA of about 0.5x is comfortably below this framework's 2x threshold — a real contrast with some SaaS peers that have leveraged up purely to fund buybacks.

What's Working

ServiceNow has the strongest growth durability of any core-enterprise-SaaS name covered on this site — 20%+ revenue growth sustained for five straight years and still 20.5% in Q3 2026 off an already-large base, a dominant #1 ITSM market position with real distance from its nearest competitor, and a genuinely fast-scaling AI product line (Now Assist/agentic AI) whose 2026 ACV target has been raised twice, to $1.7B. The balance sheet remains conservatively levered even after a large strategic acquisition, free-cash-flow guidance was raised to a 36% margin, and the stock trades far cheaper than its own recent history (19.3x forward P/E, PEG 0.79).

What to Watch

GAAP operating margin is below this framework's 20% bar and was 9.4% in Q3 (vs 17% a year earlier) on acquisition amortization, stock comp and severance; GAAP gross margin fell to 70.5% from 77.5%. Q4 cRPO growth is guided to 18.5% (from 20.5%), ROIC has fallen below WACC, and insider/founder ownership is the thinnest of any name on this site. The stock's -46% 52-week decline (-27% on Oct 29 alone) reflects fears that AI agents erode seat-based software; whether that is an overreaction or a fair repricing is the open question, so treat the lower multiple as a signal to understand, not a clean discount.
