DSV A/S is a Danish global transport and logistics group, founded in 1976 by nine independent Danish hauliers and built almost entirely through acquisitions. Its three divisions are Air & Sea (freight forwarding, the profit engine at DKK 13.0B of FY2025 EBIT), Road (European groupage and trucking networks) and Contract Logistics (warehousing). The model is asset-light — DSV owns no ships or aircraft and buys capacity from carriers, so capex is only ~1% of revenue — and the playbook is repeatable: buy a large competitor, strip out overlap, and lift the target's profit conversion to DSV's own level. It did that with UTi (2016), Panalpina (2019) and Agility's Global Integrated Logistics (GIL, 2021), and in 2025 attempted the biggest version yet: DB Schenker for EUR 14.3B, funded with roughly DKK 37B of new equity (Oct 2024) plus debt. The result is a company whose trailing revenue is roughly 70% above FY2024 and a stock about 36% below its 52-week high, because the market is waiting to see whether DKK 9B of annual synergies show up in EBIT while a Road division integration stumbles and the freight cycle stays volatile. Q2 2026 was the best quarter since the deal: revenue DKK 76.7B (+23.7% YoY), EBIT before special items DKK 6.26B (+32.5%), full-year 2026 EBIT guidance narrowed to DKK 23.5-25.5B. Read FY2025 growth rates with care: they are mostly the Schenker purchase, not organic momentum.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) COVID boom — FY2021-22 revenue and EBIT were inflated by extraordinary freight rates (revenue DKK 235.7B and EBIT DKK 25.2B in FY2022), then collapsed in FY2023 (revenue -36%, EBIT -30%), so any CAGR measured from or through that window is noisy. (2) M&A — GIL (closed Aug 2021) flatters FY2021-22, and Schenker (closed Apr 30, 2025) drives most of the FY2025 jump; DSV does not publish a clean organic-vs-acquired revenue bridge in the sources I could access, so none is shown. H2 2026 (when Q3 2025's Schenker base is first like-for-like) will be the first clean read. (3) Special items — EBIT before special items excludes ~DKK 11B of deal costs, which is why FY2025 net income (DKK 8.5B) fell even as EBIT rose 22%.
Maarten's note

DSV is the cleanest example on this site of an asset-light compounder built by serial acquisition: Panalpina in 2019, GIL in 2021, and now Schenker. Capex is around 1% of revenue, FCF has averaged about 65% of EBIT over five years, and with GIL absorbed the conversion ratio ran at 40-48% in FY2021-23. That track record is why I take the DKK 9B synergy target seriously rather than dismissing it as a slide-deck number.

What makes me cautious is that this deal is a different size of problem. Road is already below expectations in a few European countries, Germany is the hardest integration, and operating margin has gone from 11.8% to 7.9% in two years. Then there is the freight cycle: FY2022 to FY2023 showed that nearly a third of EBIT can vanish when rates normalise, and the 2026 Middle East disruption and fuel-price spike are a reminder that working capital and yields swing with events DSV cannot control. Leverage at 2.7x with buybacks suspended also means the equity gets no help from capital returns until the integration is done.

On valuation, the stock is not dramatically cheap on trailing adjusted earnings (about 23x versus a five-year average near 25x) but it looks cheaper on forward earnings (about 18x versus 24x), which only works if EBIT really reaches the guided DKK 23.5-25.5B. Compared with Maersk (Martinero 44) and DFDS (46), which are already on this site, DSV at 46 scores better mainly because it does not carry ships or ferries and its returns do not depend on charter and freight rates alone; but it is still a mid-table score because the growth criteria are distorted by M&A and ROIC is below WACC. I would want to see H2 2026 EBIT and the Road recovery before deciding how much conviction to have. My own reasoning only, not advice.

Schenker Integration & Latest Quarter (Q2 2026)
The investment case for the next 18 months is whether synergies reach EBIT faster than integration problems and freight-cycle noise take it away. Reported on Jul 22, 2026; Q3 2026 is due Oct 21, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was DKK 76,688m (+23.7% YoY; DSV cites +23.4%, likely constant-currency) and EBIT before special items DKK 6,255m (+32.5%), the best quarter since the deal. The +23.7% is still flattered by one extra month of Schenker (April 2026 vs a pre-closing April 2025), so it is not a clean organic number. Air & Sea EBIT grew 9.4% to DKK 3,776m and its conversion ratio rose to 42.4%, the first increase since integration began. Road's EBIT jumped 90.5% to DKK 999m, helped by property-disposal gains, while operations in several European countries still ran below expectations and management changes were made. Contract Logistics EBIT more than doubled to DKK 1,531m on data-centre and technology customers. The weak spot was cash: adjusted FCF was only DKK 786m in Q2 (H1: DKK 2.3B vs 7.1B a year earlier) on higher working capital.

Share Count & Buybacks

Roughly flat over the window (~240.1M at end-2021, ~238.8M now) but with two big swings: buybacks of DKK 17.8B, 20.3B and 14.0B in 2021-23 and DKK 3.3B in 2024 shrank the count to ~212M, then the Oct 2024 DKK 37.3B equity issue for Schenker restored it. The buyback programme was discontinued when the Schenker deal was announced; none ran in 2025 and none is planned until leverage is back under 2.0x. Dividend held at DKK 7.00/share (FY2023-25).

Founders, insiders & honest caveats: DSV has no founder or controlling family; it was founded by nine hauliers in 1976, and the Group CEO (Jens H. Lund, since Feb 2024), CFO (Michael Ebbe) and Chair (Thomas Plenborg) are professional managers. I could not verify management or board shareholdings, so I make no claim about them. The S&P-sourced “insider” figure of ~9.8% almost certainly reflects the Göhner foundation rather than management. The two largest holders (Göhner and Agility, ~17.7% combined) are long-term strategic holders but not insiders or founders, and I could not verify Göhner's current exact stake or the date of the ~9.7% figure. Names such as Brian Hansen could not be confirmed as significant holders and are not asserted here.
Deleveraging path: net interest-bearing debt (incl. leases) was DKK 87.3B at Q2 2026, a gearing of 2.7x EBITDA before special items (2.8x the quarter before), versus a policy ceiling of 2.0x. DSV ranks capital allocation as: deleverage above the target, then M&A or organic investment, then buybacks and dividends. Illustrative arithmetic only, not guidance: with debt flat, adding the targeted ~DKK 9B of synergies to LTM EBITDA (~DKK 32B implied) would bring gearing to roughly 2.1x, so reaching <2.0x likely needs both synergies and several quarters of normal free cash flow; H1 2026 FCF (DKK 2.3B) was weak. No official date for hitting 2.0x was found.

What's Working

A repeatable acquirer: GIL was integrated within a year and management called the Panalpina integration successful, and Schenker's integration is now expected to finish by end-2026 instead of 2028, with 60+ countries done or in process. Asset-light economics are real (capex ~1% of revenue), FCF conversion was 83% in FY2025 and the FCF CAGR clears the 15% bar. Q2 2026 delivered the highest quarterly EBIT since the deal (DKK 6.26B, +32.5%) and Air & Sea conversion turned up for the first time, FY2026 guidance implies +20-30% EBIT growth, and the stock trades at ~18x forward earnings versus a ~24x own-history average after falling ~36% from its high. Management has also retained a clear capital-allocation hierarchy (deleverage first), and 2030 targets (~45% conversion, ~20% pre-tax ROIC) give something measurable to hold them to.

What to Watch

The headline growth is acquired and the underlying trend is not strong: FY2021-25 EBIT CAGR is only 4.9%, EBIT margin has slid from 11.8% to 7.9%, and ROIC is below WACC until the DKK 9B of synergies appear. Integration risk is live: Road is below expectations in several European countries and management changes were needed. Leverage is 2.7x with buybacks paused, Q2 free cash flow fell 80% on working capital, and forward P/E only looks cheap if EBIT really reaches DKK 23.5-25.5B. The business is also exposed to freight cycles and geopolitics (FY2023 showed revenue -36% and EBIT -30% in one year), and there is no founder or controlling insider. Several inputs could not be verified: market-share percentages, management shareholdings, an organic-versus-acquired revenue bridge and the exact Göhner stake.
