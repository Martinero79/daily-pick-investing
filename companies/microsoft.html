Microsoft is the clearest "picks-and-shovels-plus-application-layer" way to own the AI cycle: Azure is the #2 cloud platform and is compounding at 40%+ growth on a base that just crossed $100B in annual revenue, OpenAI has committed to $250B of Azure consumption through 2030, and Copilot is starting to monetize across the Microsoft 365 and GitHub installed base (30M+ paid Copilot seats). Operating margins keep expanding even as the company scales, and the franchise moats (Windows, Office/M365, Azure switching costs) remain intact. The honest tension is capital intensity: capex jumped from ~23% of revenue in FY25 to ~35% in FY26, free cash flow actually declined in dollar terms even as operating income rose 21%, and the balance sheet flipped from comfortably net-cash to net-debt for the first time in over a decade. None of this threatens solvency — leverage is still trivial relative to EBITDA — but it does mean the "asset-light software compounder" version of the Microsoft story no longer fully applies, and near-term free cash flow will likely stay depressed while the AI infrastructure build continues. At ~28x trailing earnings (below the 5–10yr average of ~31x), the valuation is not demanding for the growth on offer, but it is pricing in continued Azure/Copilot execution, not a margin of safety.
Watch item: FY26 capex (cash purchases of property & equipment) was ~$116B, about 35% of revenue, and management has guided to ~$175B of calendar-2026 capital spending (including finance leases) — nearly triple the FY24 level. FCF/EBIT conversion has fallen from ~56% (FY25) to ~43% (FY26). If Azure/AI demand growth ever disappoints relative to this spend, margin and FCF downside could be sharp — this is the single biggest swing factor in the thesis.

What's working

Azure is #2 in cloud and growing 40%+ with AI as an accelerant, not a headwind; the OpenAI partnership (equity stake + $250B committed Azure spend through 2030) is a structural demand anchor few peers can match; Copilot is starting to monetize across a massive installed base (30M+ paid seats); operating margins keep expanding despite the investment cycle; the balance sheet, while now net-debt, carries essentially no leverage risk relative to earnings power; and the valuation is not stretched relative to Microsoft's own history.

What to watch

Free cash flow has stalled and conversion has roughly halved in four years as capex outruns operating cash flow growth — the market is trusting that this spend converts to durable Azure/AI revenue, and any sign of overbuild or slowing AI monetization would hit the thesis hard. Governance is a legitimate (not a flaw, just a fact) difference from founder-led names in this portfolio — no aligned controlling owner. Depreciation from the AI buildout will be a growing drag on reported margins in coming years even if the cash spend itself moderates.
