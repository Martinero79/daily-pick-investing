Block, Inc. is a US financial-technology company founded by Jack Dorsey and Jim McKelvey in 2009 as Square and renamed in December 2021. It runs Square, which sells point-of-sale hardware, payment processing, software and working-capital loans to small and mid-sized sellers, and Cash App, which lets about 59 million monthly users send money, spend through a debit card, bank, buy and sell bitcoin, borrow short-term and use Afterpay buy-now-pay-later (acquired January 2022). Reported revenue splits into Commerce Enablement ($11.5B in FY2025), Financial Solutions ($4.2B) and the Bitcoin ecosystem ($8.5B). That last line is low-margin pass-through, so gross profit is the better yardstick: it has grown from $4.4B in FY2021 to $10.4B in FY2025, and Block guides for $12.5B in FY2026 (+21%).
Four distortions to keep in mind: (1) Bitcoin revenue — roughly a third of revenue but only ~2% of Q2 2026 gross profit, so margins on revenue look low and revenue growth looks weak. (2) Low base — GAAP operating income was $161M in FY2021 and negative in FY2022-23, so the +82% EBIT CAGR is a base effect, not a run-rate. (3) Cash-flow quality — free cash flow here is operating cash flow minus capex and benefits from stock-based compensation add-backs (about $1.2B a year) and swings in settlement balances. (4) Afterpay — the all-stock deal that closed in January 2022 explains the higher share count and the large goodwill base that depresses ROIC.

What the quarter actually showed

Revenue was $6,618M (+9.3% YoY) but gross profit grew +25% to about $3.17B (Cash App +31%, Square +13%) and adjusted operating income rose 57% to a record $864M (27% of gross profit); adjusted EPS was $1.02 (+65%). Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership
Trailing GAAP P/E is not a usable signal (about 125x on $0.59 of TTM EPS, and negative or meaningless in most of FY2021-23), so the entry-multiple criterion is scored on forward P/E against its own FY-end history. Leverage is low; the debates are returns on capital and dilution.
Forward P/E — FY-End vs. Current
Current forward P/E of 16.1x (next-twelve-month consensus EPS) compares with a FY2021-25 year-end average of 43.0x, or 27.3x if the FY2021 growth-stock peak (105.8x) is excluded. Either way the ratio to history is well under 0.70. Forward P/E rests on adjusted EPS guidance ($4.02 for 2026); on GAAP earnings the stock looks far more expensive (trailing P/E 125x).

Share Count & Buybacks

The count rose from roughly 0.5B at end-2021 to ~577M after the Afterpay stock deal (January 2022), then drifted to ~622M at end-2025 on stock compensation before buybacks cut it to 604.5M (-3.7% over the past year). Net, it is up about 22% on the FY2021 base, which scores zero on the share-count criterion even though it has been falling for a year. Block pays no dividend.

Founder control & honest caveats: Block has two share classes. Class B carries ten votes a share and is held almost entirely by insiders; per the 2026 proxy, Jack Dorsey holds ~47.8M Class B shares (about 80% of that class) plus ~1M Class A, which gives him roughly 42% of total voting power on an economic stake of about 8%. That is real alignment but also limited accountability to outside holders. The insider figure used for scoring (10.6%) is S&P's; I have not independently reconciled it with the proxy table.
Balance sheet: cash and investments $6.74B against total debt (incl. leases) $7.17B at Jun 30, 2026, a net debt of $0.43B. Total debt was $9.0B at year-end 2025 and $8.1B at March 31, 2026, and has since been reduced. Block also holds bitcoin on its balance sheet, which is excluded from the cash figure and marked to market through the income statement (a source of GAAP volatility). It operates an industrial bank (Square Financial Services) and applied in September 2026 to charter a national trust bank for bitcoin and stablecoin custody.

What's Working

Gross profit has more than doubled in four years and is still growing 20%+, with Cash App up 31% in Q2 2026 on lending and card usage while Square re-accelerated to +13%. Profitability has inflected: adjusted operating margin reached 27% of gross profit and guidance implies about $3.5B of adjusted operating income in FY2026, against GAAP operating income of $1.8B in FY2025. Free cash flow is strong ($2.4B in FY2025, $3.9B TTM), the model is asset-light (capex 0.6% of revenue), net debt is close to zero, and the forward P/E of 16x is well below its own history. Gross margin has expanded 8.5 points in two years.

What to Watch

GAAP profits are thin and noisy: TTM operating income is $1.2B on $25B of revenue, net income is $357M, and bitcoin remeasurement and restructuring charges move the quarterly result. ROIC (2.7%) is below any reasonable cost of capital. Growth now depends on consumer lending (Borrow, Afterpay) where credit losses are untested in a downturn, on Cash App user growth that is only +3%, and on Square keeping its US momentum against Toast, Clover and Stripe. The share count is up ~22% since 2021, the Class B structure leaves control with the founder, the beta of 2.5 means large drawdowns (the stock was $48 in February), and the cheap forward multiple relies on adjusted EPS that excludes real costs.

Where Block Compares Well

Cash conversion and balance sheet. Block's FCF conversion (138%) is above Visa (90%) and Mastercard (88%) and in line with Shopify (106%), and its net debt is near zero. On the forward multiple it is also the cheapest of the group relative to its own history (ratio 0.37, versus 0.88-0.99 on trailing P/E for Mastercard and Visa and ~0.70 on P/FCF for Shopify; different bases, so indicative only). On gross profit rather than revenue, its growth (+16% in FY2025, +25% in Q2 2026) is competitive with the card networks, though still behind Shopify.

Where It Lags

Returns and moat. Visa and Mastercard earn ROICs of 45%+ and 90%+ on duopoly economics with 59-60% operating margins; Shopify earns 26% ROIC. Block's ROIC is 2.7% and its GAAP margin 7%. It also has no network-scale moat comparable to the card duopoly: it competes in contested markets, is exposed to consumer credit, and its share count has grown rather than shrunk. That is the main reason the index is 7-17 points below the peers despite the cheaper multiple.
