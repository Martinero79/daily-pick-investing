Parker-Hannifin is an Ohio-based maker of motion and control technologies — hydraulics, pneumatics, filtration, sealing, electromechanical systems and aerospace actuation, fuel and engine components. It sells through two segments: Diversified Industrial ($14.4B of FY2026 sales, split between North America and International businesses and sold largely through a distribution network) and Aerospace Systems ($7.1B, about one third of sales and the fastest-growing part). Management runs the company through “The Win Strategy” and has built scale by acquisition: Meggitt (aerospace, closed Sep 2022), Curtis Instruments (FY2026), and in August 2026 the ~$9B purchase of Filtration Group (about $1.8B of FY2027 sales in its first 10.5 months), with the CIRCOR commercial and defense aerospace business still pending. FY2026 was a record: sales $21.5B (+8.3%, organic +6.6%), GAAP EPS $28.48 (adjusted $32.31, +18%), operating cash flow $4.4B (20.3% of sales) and about $2B returned to shareholders, with the dividend raised 11% for a 70th consecutive annual increase. Orders accelerated through the year (+19% in Q4) and backlog hit a record $12.8B. FY2027 guidance (excluding Filtration Group and CIRCOR) calls for sales +5.5% to +8.5%, adjusted segment margin 27.5-27.9% and adjusted EPS of $34.25-35.25. The shares are ~18% below the 52-week high but trade at ~31.6x trailing GAAP earnings, well above their own five-year fiscal-year-end average of ~26x.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) M&A — Meggitt lifted FY2023 revenue (+20.2%) and the four-year growth CAGRs; the Filtration Group deal will do the same for FY2027, so reported growth will mix acquired and organic. (2) Non-GAAP vs GAAP — Parker's headline adjusted EPS ($32.31) excludes ~$4.6 per share of acquired-intangible amortisation and other items; this page scores on GAAP figures (S&P-standardised) and shows adjusted numbers separately. (3) One-offs — FY2026 includes an $84M IEEPA tariff refund credited to cost of sales (excluded from adjusted results) and FY2025 net income included a $252M divestiture gain and $215M of discrete tax benefits, which is why FY2026 GAAP net income grew only 3% while adjusted grew 16%.

What's Working

Revenue growth has re-accelerated (organic +8.0% in Q4 FY2026), orders rose 19% and backlog is a record $12.8B, so FY2027 starts with visibility. Quality metrics are strong and improving: EBIT margin 22.0% (adjusted segment margin 27.3%), gross margin +3.75pp in three years, FCF conversion 83%, capex ~2% of sales and ROIC of ~16%. FY2026 free cash flow was a record $3.9B (18.2% margin), the dividend has risen for 70 straight years, and management has a record of buying and integrating businesses (Meggitt, Curtis) while lifting margins above its own target. Net debt/EBITDA of 1.45x at June gave room for the Filtration Group deal.

What to Watch

Valuation is the main tension: trailing GAAP P/E of ~31.6x is ~22% above the five-year average and the stock is only ~18% off its high, so expectations are already high; the Oct 22 drop of 6.8% shows how sensitive it is. Filtration Group (>$9B) pushes leverage back up (my estimate ~3x pro forma) and adds integration risk, and ROIC will dip with the added goodwill. Growth is cyclical: FY2025 was -0.4% and international organic growth was flat in EMEA and negative in Latin America in FY2026. Part of FY2026's margin is a tariff refund. Aerospace (a third of sales) carries the growth and concentrates exposure to commercial and defense build rates. There is no founder or large insider stake.
