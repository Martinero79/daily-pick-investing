Alibaba is the most complicated name in this batch, and arguably on the whole site: a genuinely dominant, decades-old platform business currently absorbing two large, self-inflicted-but-strategic hits to its numbers at once. First, a quick-commerce subsidy war: Alibaba committed roughly ¥50B ($7B) over 12 months to instant-delivery incentives via Ele.me and Taobao Instant Commerce, directly targeting Meituan's leadership, with JD.com fighting on the same front. That single decision took GAAP operating margin from a healthy 15.22% (FY2025) to 6.15% (FY2026) and just 4.56% on a trailing basis — the entire margin story for this scorecard. Second, an AI/cloud capex ramp: capital expenditure has more than tripled from ¥32B (FY2024) to over ¥126B (FY2026), pushing free cash flow negative for the first time in years, mirroring the same infrastructure investment cycle every major cloud provider is running through right now. Set against this: Alibaba remains net cash ($17.5B), has cut its share count by over 13% since FY2022, and trades at a PEG ratio of just 0.44 — genuinely cheap if you believe both the margin compression and the capex cycle are temporary rather than structural. The market concentration story has also shifted: Taobao+Tmall's combined ≈40-46% GMV share is still the largest in China, but PDD and Douyin have both grown into real, double-digit-share competitors, meaning this is no longer the uncontested moat it was a decade ago.

What's Working

Alibaba remains the largest single e-commerce and cloud group in China by revenue, with a comfortable net-cash balance sheet, a shrinking share count, and a genuinely low valuation on a PEG basis (0.44) if the current margin compression proves temporary. Quarterly operating margin has already begun recovering from its March 2026 trough (1.01% → 7.29%), and cloud revenue growth is re-accelerating on the back of Alibaba's own AI model family.

What to Watch

Two live, ongoing events are doing almost all of the damage to this scorecard: the quick-commerce subsidy war against Meituan and JD.com, and a tripling of capex to fund AI/cloud infrastructure that's pushed FCF negative. Neither is necessarily permanent, but neither has fully resolved either — watch whether GAAP operating margin keeps recovering quarter over quarter, and whether capex growth moderates relative to revenue. Add to that genuine, multi-way competitive erosion in e-commerce (PDD and Douyin both now real, growing rivals) and the structural China-ADR/regulatory considerations noted on page 4.
