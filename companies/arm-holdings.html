Arm is the default instruction-set architecture of the mobile era and, increasingly, of the AI/datacenter era too: its IP sits in roughly 99% of smartphone application processors and is now the CPU core inside Nvidia's Grace/Grace Blackwell platforms, AWS Graviton, Google Axion and Microsoft Cobalt. The real engine is the royalty line — it's compounding as Armv9 and Compute Subsystems (CSS) licenses carry structurally higher per-chip rates, and Q1 FY2027 revenue grew 22% YoY on a base that's already several billion dollars, with data-center royalties more than doubling. But the model's own numbers say this quality is priced for a lot: trailing P/S of ~56x sits roughly 1.8x above Arm's own FY25–FY26 average (~30x), GAAP operating margin is volatile and depressed by heavy stock-based comp (18.3% latest FY vs. a non-GAAP figure north of 40%), and ROIC (14.2%) doesn't clear even a conservative WACC estimate, let alone the elevated one used here. The governance structure is unlike anything else in a typical portfolio: SoftBank owns roughly 87% of shares, leaving a public float of barely 13% — a minority holder is, in practice, a passenger in a SoftBank-controlled vehicle, and that float thinness likely explains a meaningful share of the stock's extreme volatility (beta 3.91, a $100–$452 52-week range). Share count is also drifting higher via RSU issuance rather than shrinking. Honest read: a durable, strengthening franchise wrapped in an expensive, volatile, and structurally minority-unfriendly ownership structure.

Governance note: SoftBank's ~87% stake makes Arm functionally a controlled subsidiary trading on Nasdaq rather than a widely-held public company. SoftBank holds voting control, can direct major strategic decisions, and its own capital needs or strategic priorities (its broader AI/robotics/Vision Fund ambitions) could diverge from minority shareholders' interests at any time — SoftBank has already sold down its stake once before (a ~25% tranche to its own Vision Fund pre-IPO) and could do so again. The public float is only around 13% of shares outstanding (~140M of 1.07B shares) — thin enough that it likely amplifies the stock's already-extreme volatility (beta 3.91) and could worsen liquidity in a stress scenario. This is a materially different risk profile than a widely-held, professionally-governed company with dispersed institutional ownership.

What's Working

Royalty engine is reaccelerating (Q1 FY27 revenue +22% YoY, data-center royalties more than doubled) on the Armv9/CSS rate-uplift transition; genuine architectural lock-in with ~99% smartphone penetration and expanding footprint in AI datacenter silicon and automotive; balance sheet is debt-free in substance (net cash $3.4B, Debt/EBITDA 0.43x); FCF has recovered strongly off FY25's working-capital-driven trough.

What to Watch

Valuation remains rich even after the ~39% post-ATH pullback — P/S is still roughly 1.8x its own FY25–FY26 average; GAAP margins are volatile and well below headline non-GAAP due to heavy RSU-based comp, and both EBIT CAGR and latest-FY EBIT margin miss the growth-quality bar; ROIC doesn't clear even a base-case WACC estimate; SoftBank's ~87% stake means minority holders have essentially no influence and the ~13% float amplifies volatility (beta 3.91); RISC-V is a real long-horizon architectural threat in AI accelerators, automotive and China even though it hasn't touched Arm's core smartphone and premium-compute franchise yet.
