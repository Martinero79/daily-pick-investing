Coupang holds the clearest #1 market position of any name in this e-commerce batch — 37.7% platform usage share in Korea versus Naver's 27.2% — built on a genuinely differentiated same/next-day Rocket Delivery logistics network that took years and billions of dollars to build. That's exactly why this dashboard is uncomfortable: a company with that kind of moat just posted its worst quarter since its 2021 NYSE listing. In June 2026, Korea's Personal Information Protection Commission fined Coupang $410 million over a November 2025 data breach that exposed data on more than 37 million customers — a real, disclosed, one-off charge that alone explains most of Q2 2026's $556 million operating loss. But it isn't the whole story: Q1 2026 was already running an operating loss before the fine hit, and revenue growth has decelerated hard, from double digits in FY2024-25 to just 3.90% YoY in the most recent quarter. Net cash has shrunk to a thin $477 million buffer, down from $2.46 billion at the end of FY2023, as heavy capex on fulfillment infrastructure and international expansion (Taiwan, the Farfetch luxury platform) continues. None of this changes the underlying market position — Coupang is still clearly winning in Korea — but the financial picture right now is genuinely the weakest in this entire batch outside of Alibaba.

Q2 2026 Headlines

Revenue reached $8.86B, up just 3.90% YoY (the slowest quarter in Coupang's public history) but up ~10% on a constant-currency basis. The company posted an operating loss of $556M — its largest ever — driven primarily by the $410M Korea Personal Information Protection Commission fine over the November 2025 data breach (37M+ customers affected). Management stated the adjusted operating loss, excluding the fine, was $146M, and that "customers now spend above their pre-incident levels." Q1 2026 had already posted a smaller operating loss of $242M before the fine was recorded, pointing to real underlying margin softness alongside the one-off charge.

What's Working

Coupang's core market position is genuinely unshaken: it remains Korea's clear #1 e-commerce platform with a real logistics moat in Rocket Delivery, and management says the November 2025 breach did not meaningfully dent customer spending. Revenue growth, while decelerating, remains positive, the balance sheet is still technically net-cash, share count is roughly flat with recent buybacks, and capex intensity remains reasonable for a company still building out fulfillment infrastructure.

What to Watch

This is genuinely the weakest financial picture in this e-commerce batch outside Alibaba: ROIC deeply negative, FCF collapsing toward breakeven, and revenue growth decelerating to the slowest pace in Coupang's history even before the fine hit. The $410M fine is disclosed and one-off, but Q1 2026's loss predates it, meaning real margin pressure exists independent of the breach. Net cash has shrunk fast, and founder Bom Kim's 76% voting control via super-voting shares concentrates governance risk in a way that drew public criticism after the breach.
