Palantir builds software platforms — Gotham, Foundry, and now the AI Platform (AIP) — that let organizations fuse messy, siloed data and run AI models against it. It started almost entirely as a U.S. government and intelligence contractor and has spent the last few years pushing hard into commercial AI deployments. The financial turnaround has been dramatic: GAAP operating losses every year from IPO through 2022 gave way to a first profitable year in 2023, and by Q2 2026 (reported Aug 3, 2026) revenue was growing 93% year over year, with U.S. commercial revenue up 149%. The stock has tracked that story almost exactly — from around $16 in late January 2024 to an all-time-high close of $207.18 on November 3, 2025, before cooling to today's $173.96. What makes this one harder to read cleanly than most names on this site is the ownership structure: a Founder Voting Trust gives Peter Thiel, CEO Alex Karp, and Stephen Cohen up to 49.99% of total voting power through Class F shares, regardless of how much stock they actually own or how much further the company issues — a genuine governance story, not just a simple insider-ownership percentage.
This is a "growth is real, valuation is the argument" case: on the operating numbers, Palantir clears almost every bar in this model — growth, margins, cash generation, balance sheet, even entry multiple relative to its own extreme 2025 peak. The genuine debate is whether 149x trailing earnings and roughly 68x sales is a fair price for a company still early in monetizing an AI-driven commercial pivot, or a valuation that has run well ahead of any realistic long-run cash flows. Investor Michael Burry has taken the other side of that argument publicly and sized a large short position on it.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E
The first seven points are quarter-end trailing/forward P/E readings; the last point ("Now") is the current multiple as of the Aug 20, 2026 close, so it isn't on the same fixed quarterly cadence as the rest.
Trailing vs. forward P/E: current trailing P/E (148.8x) divides today's price by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings; the quarterly series above is that same backward-looking ratio at each quarter-end, tracking how the market has re-rated Palantir relative to earnings it had already booked. Forward P/E instead divides today's price by consensus estimates of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and better at capturing the growth trailing P/E can't see. For Palantir, forward P/E (91.4x) has run persistently below trailing across this whole window, including at both multiples' 2025 peaks, and the gap between the two has narrowed as trailing earnings have caught up — the market has consistently expected, and largely gotten, sharp near-term earnings growth from Palantir's government and commercial AI ramp. The PEG ratio (forward P/E divided by expected EPS growth) comes in around 1.7 — above the traditional 1.0 fair-value line, but far more reasonable than the triple-digit trailing and forward P/E multiples alone would suggest, since it credits Palantir for the exceptional growth rate behind those multiples.

Balance Sheet Note

Net cash of roughly $9.2bn against debt of only ~$211M, mostly operating lease liabilities — essentially debt-free. FY2025 free cash flow of $2,101M against FY2025 GAAP EBIT of $1,414M is comfortably above 100% conversion, helped by deferred revenue growth and non-cash stock-comp addbacks.

Governance note, not a simple ownership %: direct economic insider stakes are modest by dollar value (Peter Thiel's disclosed stake is roughly 4% per SEC filings; totals vary by source), but the three-class share structure (Class A = 1 vote, Class B = 10 votes, Class F = variable "Founder" shares) locks founder voting control at up to 49.99% regardless of future dilution or share sales. That's a durable control mechanism, but a different story from a founder who simply owns a large chunk of the company outright.

What's Working
  • Explosive, broad-based growth: TTM revenue $6.16bn (+78.9% YoY), Q2 2026 revenue +93% YoY, with U.S. commercial specifically up 149%
  • GAAP profitability scaled fast: operating margin went from -26.7% (FY2021) to 31.6% (FY2025) to 47.1% (Q2 2026), with FCF conversion comfortably above 100% of EBIT
  • Essentially debt-free with roughly $9.2bn in net cash — plenty of room to keep investing without diluting through debt
  • Today's 148.8x trailing P/E is well below its own 2025 peak (~395-421x) — the multiple has compressed sharply as earnings caught up to the stock price
  • A durable, if unusual, governance structure: the Founder Voting Trust locks in founder control regardless of future dilution or share sales
What to Watch
  • Valuation is still extreme in absolute terms — 148.8x trailing earnings and roughly 68x sales prices in a lot of future growth that still has to show up
  • The commercial side (now nearly half of revenue) faces genuine competition from Databricks, Snowflake, Microsoft Fabric and C3.ai — a more contested moat than the government side
  • Heavy dilution: shares outstanding up ~18.4% since 2021, with FY2025 stock comp ($684M) far outpacing ~$57M of TTM buybacks
  • A high-profile bear case: investor Michael Burry has disclosed a large notional short position, arguing the stock is worth "less than $1" long-run — a genuinely contested stock, not a consensus one
  • Reputational/political exposure cuts both ways: deep defense and intelligence ties plus an outspoken CEO, and a 2026 report highlighted only ~£2m of UK corporate tax paid against ~£670m of UK government contracts
