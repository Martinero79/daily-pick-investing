TransDigm Group designs and makes highly engineered aircraft components — actuators, ignition systems, pumps and valves, power-conditioning devices, latches, connectors, seat belts, cockpit displays and many others — reported in three segments (Power & Control, Airframe and Non-aviation). The company describes most of its sales as proprietary and sole-source, and sells into three channels: commercial OEM (new aircraft), commercial aftermarket (spares and repairs for aircraft in service) and defense. The model is to buy niche proprietary-product businesses, run them with a decentralised, value-driven playbook (pricing discipline, cost control, lean operations), and finance the whole with high leverage and cash returns to shareholders. FY2025 revenue was $8.83B with a 47.6% GAAP operating margin; Q3 FY2026 revenue grew 22.5% (13% organic) and FY2026 guidance was raised in August to $10.47-10.55B of sales and $5.49-5.55B of EBITDA As Defined. The cost of the approach is on the balance sheet: $33.5B of debt, a $9.8B stockholders' deficit created largely by special dividends ($90 per share in September 2025 on top of $75 in October 2024) and buybacks, and about $2.0B of net interest expense guided for FY2026. The stock is about 22% below its 52-week high and several brokers cut price targets in October.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Negative equity — book equity is a deficit because the company returns cash through special dividends and buybacks funded by debt, so ROE, P/B and debt/equity are not meaningful; this page relies on ROIC and EBITDA-based leverage instead. (2) M&A — Jet Parts Engineering and Victor Sierra ($2.2B, closed Apr 2026) and Prince & Izant ($1.07B, closed Sep 2026) inflate reported growth (Q3 FY2026: +22.5% reported, 13% organic) and add leverage. (3) Non-GAAP vs GAAP — adjusted EPS ($37.33 in FY2025; $40.62-41.46 guided for FY2026) excludes stock compensation and acquisition costs; GAAP EPS ($32.06 in FY2025) is lower and is further reduced in years with dividend-equivalent payments. Special dividends make the historical dividend line lumpy: unadjusted year-end prices below do not adjust for them.

What the quarter actually showed

Q3 FY2026 net sales were $2,741m, up 23% from $2,237m (organic growth 13%). Net income was $540m (+10%), GAAP EPS $9.39 (+11%) and adjusted EPS $10.87 (+13%; company non-GAAP). EBITDA As Defined rose 19% to $1,447m, but the margin slipped to 52.8% from 54.4% as acquisitions diluted it; the base businesses expanded margin, according to the CEO. Commercial aftermarket grew 17%, commercial OEM and defense both grew at double-digit rates. Net interest expense rose to $514m from $397m on higher debt, which is why net income grew far slower than sales. The company bought back $1.0B of stock in the quarter (809,101 shares at an average of $1,208), taking nine-month repurchases to $1.8B, and raised debt by $1.5B in April.

What's Working

Exceptional unit economics: a 47.6% GAAP operating margin, gross margin up 3.2pp in three years, 25% EBIT CAGR and 22% FCF CAGR over four years, capex of about 2.5% of revenue, and ROIC about twice the estimated WACC. Q3 FY2026 growth accelerated to 22.5% (13% organic), commercial aftermarket grew 17%, guidance was raised, and defense backlog is building. The stock trades at 34.8x trailing and 24.6x forward earnings versus 44.8x and 32.6x own-history averages, after a decline of about 22% from the 52-week high. Buybacks have resumed in size.

What to Watch

Leverage of about 5.8x net debt/EBITDA (before the latest acquisition), a −$9.8B stockholders' deficit and roughly $2.0B of annual interest leave less room for error, and FCF conversion is only about 43% of EBIT. Reported growth is helped by acquisitions, and acquisitions are diluting EBITDA margin (52.8% vs 54.4%). Full-year revenue growth decelerated in FY2025 (+11.2% vs +20.6%), several brokers cut targets in October, and commercial aerospace demand, pricing scrutiny on proprietary parts and defense budgets are standing risks. No founder or insider block. Inputs that are third-party or my own estimates: ROIC/WACC, LTM leverage, insider ownership, market-position ranking and the TTM column.
