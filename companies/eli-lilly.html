Gross Margin Trend (FY2021-FY2025)
Gross margin has expanded every year, from 75.38% to 83.04% — a +6.27pp gain versus FY2022, three fiscal years earlier (the window used for criterion #14) — driven by GLP-1 volume and mix, the opposite of the margin compression seen in some other capacity-buildout stories on this site.
Market position, honestly stated: in the GLP-1/incretin obesity and diabetes category, Lilly and Novo Nordisk together control almost the entire market — a genuine duopoly, criterion #6 scored 2/5 (tight, high barriers to entry: biologics manufacturing at scale, years of clinical trial data). Lilly has overtaken Novo as the larger player, roughly 60% vs. 39% combined US share as of early 2026, driven by tirzepatide's superior head-to-head efficacy data. That said, the moat is not unlimited: oral GLP-1 pills (Lilly's own Foundayo/orforglipron and Novo's oral Wegovy) are just beginning to scale, and Roche, Amgen and Pfizer all have GLP-1/incretin programs in development that could erode the duopoly over the back half of this decade. Beyond GLP-1, Lilly's oncology ($9.4B FY2025), immunology ($5.2B) and neuroscience ($1.4B, including donanemab for Alzheimer's) segments provide real diversification against a GLP-1-specific setback, even though they are currently dwarfed by the cardiometabolic franchise in growth terms.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed. For Lilly specifically: trailing P/E (38.58x) already sits well below its own FY21-25 average (62.6x) and the FY2023 peak (100.04x, when one-time acquired-IPR&D charges compressed reported earnings), while forward P/E (28.18x) is cheaper still, implying continued earnings growth ahead. One more lens worth a glance: the PEG ratio, forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether a "cheap-relative-to-history" multiple is actually cheap once growth is priced in. Lilly's PEG currently runs around 1.33 — modestly above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, meaning the stock is not undervalued on a growth-adjusted basis even though it looks cheap purely against its own trading history; it's a fair-to-full price for an exceptional growth rate, not a bargain.
Balance sheet: net debt of $45.16B against EBITDA gives a Net Debt/EBITDA of 1.08x (1.15x at FY2025 year-end) — comfortably inside this framework's <2x bar, though up from 1.20x in FY2021 as debt has been raised to fund manufacturing capacity and bolt-on M&A (including the $2.9B Merida Biosciences deal announced in late August 2026). ROIC has actually improved through the buildout, from 36.42% (FY2021) to 43.74% (FY2025, 42.24% current) — unlike some other capacity-driven stories on this site, the extra capital deployed here is earning a very high return, not diluting it. Shares outstanding declined 0.62% year-over-year, with net buybacks in 4 of the last 5 fiscal years and only a marginal dilution blip in FY2024 (-0.09%).

What's Working

Revenue and EBIT have both compounded at extraordinary rates (23.2% and 37.6% CAGR) as the tirzepatide franchise scaled, operating margin has expanded every single year (29.2% → 45.6%), ROIC is rising rather than falling through the capacity buildout (42-44%), Lilly has taken the #1 spot in the GLP-1 duopoly from Novo Nordisk, gross margin has genuinely expanded (not just held steady), and the trailing P/E is meaningfully below its own five-year average despite the growth.

What to Watch

Free cash flow has been the genuine weak link: capex (~12% of revenue) and working-capital build for manufacturing capacity have kept FCF/EBIT conversion around 30%, well short of this framework's 80% bar, and the 5-year FCF CAGR misses the 15% growth bar despite revenue and EBIT comfortably clearing it. The GLP-1 category is currently a tight duopoly, but oral pills and new entrants (Roche, Amgen, Pfizer) are a real long-term threat to that structure, and Lilly's non-GLP-1 pipeline (oncology, immunology, donanemab) will need to keep growing to diversify away from concentration in a single drug class. Forward-looking PEG (~1.33) suggests the stock isn't mispriced relative to its own growth rate, even though it looks cheap against its own trading history.
