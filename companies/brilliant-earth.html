Brilliant Earth Group, Inc. designs and sells ethically sourced diamond engagement rings, wedding and anniversary bands and fine jewellery, directly to consumers through its website and, increasingly, through showrooms (43 in the US). It was founded in 2005 by Beth Gerstein (CEO) and Eric Grossberg (Executive Chairman), who still control about 90% of the votes through Class C shares, and it listed on Nasdaq on Sep 23, 2021 at $20; the stock closed at $1.16 on Nov 16, 2026. The model is asset-light: made-to-order inventory, leased showrooms and capex of about 1% of sales, with no borrowings and $76.2M of cash. The record, though, is a company whose gross margin has improved (49.3% in FY2021 to 57.5% in FY2025) while everything below it has deteriorated: revenue growth slowed to +3.6% in FY2025 after a -5.4% year, operating income went from +$40.1M in FY2021 to -$5.4M in FY2025, and FCF fell from $40.5M to $5.8M. 2026 so far is a mixed recovery: net sales grew +6.0%, +5.7% and +2.3% in Q1-Q3 and fine jewellery reached 16% of Q3 bookings, but operating income is still -$6.5M for the nine months. Q3 2026 beat the company's own guidance (net sales $112.8M, adjusted EBITDA $5.1M, gross margin 59.7%, up 210bps), yet the shares have fallen about 24% from the Nov 4 close ($1.53) to Nov 16 ($1.16). Read the low valuation with care: market cap is about $118M on a fully-exchanged basis, so small absolute dollars of profit move the picture a lot.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Short history — listed since Sep 2021, so the “5-year” windows here are FY2021-25 (4 years of growth) and the own-history multiple (#10) is N/A. (2) Up-C structure — most of the equity and profit belongs to the founders and Mainsail via LLC units; the consolidated net loss attributable to public Class A holders differs from the group figure, and the public float is small and thinly traded. (3) Non-GAAP vs GAAP — the company's adjusted EBITDA ($13-15M guided for FY2026) excludes stock compensation and other items; GAAP operating income is lower and was negative for FY2025 and the first nine months of 2026.

What moved the stock

Q3 2026 was a beat on the company's own guidance: net sales $112.8M (+2.3% YoY, above guidance), gross margin 59.7% (+210bps YoY), GAAP net income $2.4M against a $0.7M loss a year earlier and adjusted EBITDA $5.1M (guided $3-5M). Orders grew 5.6% but average order value fell 3.1% to $2,140. Even so, the shares closed at $1.53 on Nov 4, $1.47 on Nov 5 and $1.16 on Nov 16 (-24% from Nov 4). I did not verify the cause; third-party commentary pointed to the full-year net sales growth guide of +2.75% to +4%, which it read as a trim, with the unchanged $13-15M adjusted EBITDA target now leaning on gross margin rather than sales. Arithmetic on the guide (an estimate, not company guidance): nine-month sales were $327.4M, so the range implies Q4 net sales of roughly $122M to $128M versus $124.4M a year earlier, i.e. about -2% to +3%, and Q4 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $7M to $9M after $6.2M for the nine months. Q4 is the seasonally strongest quarter.

Share Count & Dilution

About 102.1M shares and exchangeable units at Sep 30, 2026 (17.27M Class A, 35.74M Class B, 49.12M Class C), versus ~97M in FY2021 and 100.5M at Dec 2025. Class A rises as LLC units are exchanged and equity awards vest; stock-based compensation was $4.0M in the first nine months of 2026 (down from $7.0M). No buyback or dividend programme is in place.

Founders, insiders & control: per the Apr 27, 2026 proxy (share counts as of Apr 22, 2026), Beth Gerstein and Eric Grossberg jointly own and control Just Rocks, Inc., which holds all 49.1M Class C shares (ten votes each): 90.4% of combined voting power. They are also CEO and Executive Chairman, each on a $600,000 base salary. Mainsail GP III holds 31.9M Class B shares (89% of Class B, 5.9% of votes; its partner Gavin Turner is a director). The Just Rocks holding rests on a 2023 Schedule 13G/A and the Mainsail figure on a May 2025 filing, so current exact stakes may differ slightly. This control means public Class A holders have little say, which cuts both ways for alignment.
Balance sheet (Sep 30, 2026): cash $76.2M (Dec 2025: $79.1M), inventory $50.3M, total assets $196.9M, total liabilities $122.8M (of which operating-lease liabilities $42.7M, deferred revenue $24.1M), total equity $74.2M (of which $61.6M is the non-controlling interest). No borrowings: the term loan was extinguished in Q3 2025, with a $0.6M loss on extinguishment. Cash fell by about $83M in FY2025 as the debt was repaid.

What's Working

A clean, debt-free balance sheet ($76.2M cash, no borrowings) in a business that needs almost no capex (about 1% of sales). Gross margin has risen from 49.3% to 57.5% over four years and reached 59.7% in Q3 2026, and nine-month 2026 net sales are up 4.6%. Q3 2026 returned to GAAP operating profit (+$1.9M) and beat the company's own sales and adjusted EBITDA guidance. Fine jewellery (16% of Q3 bookings) and showrooms are diversifying the business beyond bridal. The founders still run the company and control about 90% of the votes, a strong alignment with the long-term brand even if it limits outside influence.

What to Watch

Growth is low (revenue CAGR +3.6%, FY2026 guided to +2.75-4%) and operating income has fallen every year since FY2021, to GAAP losses in FY2025 and the first nine months of 2026; adjusted EBITDA is only $13-15M guided on about $450M of sales. SG&A has outgrown gross profit for four years. Average order value fell 3.1% in Q3, tariffs and metal and diamond prices are cost risks, and lab-grown diamond price deflation affects the category. The shares are down about 94% from the IPO price and about 24% since the Q3 beat, on a thin float (~17M Class A shares) and an Up-C structure with a tax receivable agreement.
