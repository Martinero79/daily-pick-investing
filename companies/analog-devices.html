Analog Devices designs and makes the chips that sit between the real world and digital systems: converting sensor signals to data, managing power and processing signals in industrial automation, cars, data-centre and communications equipment. It was founded in 1965, has paid a dividend for decades and is vertically integrated, running its own wafer fabs as well as using foundries. The model is high gross margin, long product lives and sticky design wins, but it is cyclical: after the post-COVID inventory build, FY2024 revenue fell 23% and GAAP operating income fell 48%. The recovery is now steep. Q3 FY26 revenue of $4.02B was +39.6% YoY with a 39.5% GAAP operating margin, and the company guides Q4 to about $4.3B (±$0.1B) with adjusted EPS of $3.86 (±$0.15). The stock has risen about 75% in a year and the trailing GAAP P/E is ~49.5x, roughly in line with its (distorted) five-year average, so the debate is how far the upcycle, data-centre demand and the new Alif acquisition can carry earnings. Read the five-year growth rates with care: FY2021 is the base and was a mid-cycle year that only included the Maxim acquisition from late August 2021.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Cycle — FY2022-23 were peak years (revenue $12.0-12.3B), FY2024 the trough ($9.4B); the 4-year CAGR from FY2021 understates the rebound and overstates stability. (2) Maxim — the all-stock deal closed in Aug 2021, so FY2021 contains only about two months of Maxim and FY2022 is the first full year; GAAP earnings carry heavy intangible amortisation, which is why FY2021's P/E (67x) and FY2024's (68x) look inflated. (3) GAAP vs adjusted — this page uses GAAP throughout; adjusted EPS is roughly 25% higher than GAAP (Q3 FY26: $3.45 vs $2.74).

What the quarter actually showed

Q3 FY26 revenue was $4.02B, up 39.6% YoY (and +11% sequentially), YoY growth has accelerated every quarter since Q4 FY24. Industrial was $1.97B (+52% YoY), Automotive $998M (+16%), Communications $655M (+84%) and Consumer $397M (+6%). GAAP gross margin was 67.3% and GAAP operating margin 39.5% (vs 28.6% a year earlier), so operating income grew 93% to $1.59B. GAAP EPS was $2.74 (vs $1.04) and adjusted EPS $3.45 (vs $2.05). Free cash flow was $1.46B (36% of revenue); the TTM FCF is $4.94B. Management described demand as strengthening across products and regions, led by data-centre and industrial, per the earnings release and call summary.

What's Working

Margins and cash generation are exceptional for a hardware company: 39.5% GAAP operating margin and 67% gross margin in Q3 FY26, FCF conversion above 100% of GAAP operating income, and net debt of only ~1x EBITDA. Revenue growth has accelerated every quarter since Q4 FY24 (+39.6% in Q3), the Q4 guide implies roughly +40% again, and demand is broadening from industrial and automotive into data-centre and communications. The share count is down ~6% since FY2021, the dividend has grown 22 consecutive years and ADI is investing in capacity and adjacent technology (Alif).

What to Watch

This is a cyclical business: FY2024 showed revenue -23% and GAAP operating income -48% in one year, and the 4-year revenue and EBIT CAGRs (10.8% and 9.2%) are well below the 15% bars. The stock is up ~75% in twelve months at ~49.5x trailing and ~26x forward earnings, i.e. above its own trailing average, so a pause in the upcycle would hit both earnings and the multiple. ROIC (11.0%) barely exceeds the estimated WACC (10.7%). GAAP earnings carry large acquisition amortisation. There is no founder or insider block. Unverified: market-share percentage, the exact FY2021 share count and the Alif deal's closing timing.
