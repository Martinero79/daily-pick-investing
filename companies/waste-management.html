Waste Management (WM) is the largest waste and environmental services company in North America. Its core is Collection and Disposal: residential, commercial and industrial collection, transfer stations and a network of landfills, with about 72% of collected waste internalised into its own disposal sites, which is the source of its pricing leverage. Around that core sit Recycling Processing and Sales, Renewable Energy (landfill gas turned into renewable natural gas, RNG, and electricity) and, since the Stericycle acquisition closed in November 2024, Healthcare Solutions (regulated medical waste and secure document destruction). Stericycle was the biggest deal in WM's history: roughly $7.5B of cash acquisitions in FY2024 (S&P cash flow), funded with debt, which lifted total debt from about $16.7B to $24.9B, pushed net debt/EBITDA from 2.8x to 3.7x (S&P basis) and added roughly $2.5B of annual revenue at a much lower margin than solid waste. Two years in, WM is bringing it into line: Healthcare Solutions' adjusted operating EBITDA margin was 22.4% in Q3 2026 (17.5% a year earlier), SG&A is down to 15.2% of its revenue and its core price is 5.5%. Leverage has eased to 2.9x and share repurchases (about $2B planned for 2026) have restarted. In the core, WM keeps pushing price (core price 5.8% in Q3, collection and disposal yield +3.4%) while volumes are slightly negative (-0.4%) as it sheds low-margin residential contracts. The stock trades near the top of its 52-week range at 31x trailing GAAP earnings and 27.5x forward, roughly in line with its own five-year average. A CEO handover is also coming: Jim Fish retires in early January 2027 and John Morris, currently President, takes over.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) M&A — Stericycle contributed two months in FY2024 and a full year in FY2025, so FY2025 revenue growth of +14.2% is not organic; the acquisition effect annualised in Q4 2025 and growth has since normalised to +3.5% to +7.1% a quarter. (2) Adjusted vs GAAP — WM adds back Stericycle integration costs and impairments: Q3 2026 EPS was $2.15 adjusted vs $2.05 GAAP, and FY2025 was $7.50 adjusted vs $6.70 GAAP. GAAP is used for P/E here. Q3 2025 GAAP EPS ($1.49) was depressed by about $197M of impairments, which flatters the +37.6% GAAP EPS growth in Q3 2026. (3) Energy surcharges and commodity prices — Q3 2026 revenue growth of 6.3% included $112M (about 1.7pp) from energy surcharges recovering record diesel costs, and higher recycling and RNG volumes; fuel is a pass-through for revenue but a margin drag (-40bp in Q3).

Stericycle, Pricing & Latest Quarter (Q3 2026)
The case for the next 12 months is whether price-to-cost spread in solid waste, Healthcare margin recovery and a maturing RNG build-out can lift cash flow faster than fuel, insurance and landfill-compliance costs and flat-to-negative volumes erode it. Reported Oct 27, 2026 (call Oct 28).
What the quarter actually showed

Q3 2026 revenue was $6,846M (+6.3% YoY) and adjusted operating EBITDA $2,121M (+5.8%), a 31.0% margin vs 31.1%. Adjusted EPS was $2.15 (GAAP $2.05) against a consensus of about $2.18, a small miss; revenue beat (about $6.81B expected). Core price was +5.8% and collection and disposal yield +3.4%, with volume -0.4% (-0.5% workday-adjusted) from deliberate residential shedding and prior national-account broker losses. Operating expenses rose to 59.8% of revenue (58.9%): collection and disposal efficiency improved the ratio by 40bp, but fuel and increased risk-management (insurance and claims) costs added 130bp; SG&A improved 70bp to 9.3% adjusted. Healthcare Solutions revenue grew 3.7% and adjusted EBITDA rose 33% to $146M (22.4% margin). Free cash flow (WM definition) was $985M (+20%), $3.01B for nine months (+42%), and WM returned $957M to shareholders in the quarter ($580M buybacks, $377M dividends).

Guidance (FY2026, reaffirmed Oct 27): revenue $26.275-26.475B (now expected at the high end); adjusted operating EBITDA $8.15-8.25B (within range; FY2025 about $7.6B at a 30.1% margin, derived); free cash flow $3.75-3.85B (high end; built from ~$6.5B operating cash flow, ~$2.5B sustaining capex and ~$0.25B sustainability growth capex). About $2B of buybacks and, with dividends of roughly $1.5B, around $3.5B of capital returns planned. Volume still about -1% for the year (-0.5% excluding prior-year wildfire cleanup); SG&A about 10% of revenue.
Stericycle (Healthcare Solutions): closed Nov 2024 for a reported ~$7.2B including debt (announced $62/share; not re-verified here). Healthcare revenue: $0.4B in FY2024 (two months), $2.5B in FY2025, flat since (9M 2026 $1,903M vs $1,893M; internal growth -$42M). Both year-end 2026 targets — core price 5.5% and SG&A of 15-16% of revenue — were hit in Q3, and the full cost-synergy target is now expected around mid-2027, slightly ahead of plan. Integration costs are still being added back ($30M in Q3).
Pricing power in collection and landfills: WM has pushed yield above volume loss for years: collection yield is +4.2% (9M) and landfill/transfer pricing is where management says the opportunity lies, because its post-collection assets are scarce and critical (internalisation 72.1%). New California landfill-monitoring rules and rising leachate costs are being passed through in price. The trade-off is volume: collection and disposal volume was -1.3% over nine months. Gross margin rose 2.85pp in three years, which is the clearest evidence that price has beaten cost; the risk is that fuel, insurance and compliance costs (and the abandonment of low-margin residential volume) absorb part of it.
RNG and sustainability: WM brought its 18th renewable natural gas plant online in Q3 2026 (since 2022); one more is expected in Q4 and the last in early Q1 2027. Sustainability growth capex has fallen sharply (9M 2026 $191M vs $429M in 9M 2025; $250M guided for FY2026), which is a main reason free cash flow is rising faster than EBITDA. Management says more than 90% of 2026 RNG production and about 40% of 2027 production is contracted and cites a long-term realised-price outlook of about $26 (about $27 expected for 2026; the unit was not stated in the transcript excerpt I used). RINs averaged $2.32 per credit in Q3 vs $2.56 a year earlier. Q3 plant start-up shortfalls hurt RNG output, with a rebound expected in Q4. RNG earnings remain exposed to RIN, natural gas and policy changes.

Share Count & Buybacks

Diluted shares fell from ~421M (Q4 2021) to ~400M (Q3 2026) and shares outstanding are 397.6M (-0.29% YoY). Repurchases were $1.4B (2021), $1.5B (2022), $1.3B (2023), then only $0.3B (2024) and $0.05B (2025) while cash went to Stericycle. They resumed in 2026: $1.58B in nine months, with $2B planned for the year. Dividend per share rose 10% in 2025 and 14.5% from Q1 2026 to $0.945 a quarter (22 consecutive years of growth, per stockanalysis.com); the payout ratio is ~50% of GAAP earnings.

Founders, insiders & caveats: WM has no founder or controlling holder; leadership are professional managers (CEO Jim Fish retires in early January 2027; John Morris, President, succeeds him; CFO David Reed). Insider ownership of ~0.17% is a third-party figure. Cascade Investment (Bill Gates) reported 4.0% in a Feb 2022 Schedule 13G/A; I could not verify its current holding and it is not an insider stake.
Leverage after Stericycle: net debt / EBITDA (S&P basis) was 2.79x, 2.77x and 2.76x in FY2021-23, jumped to 3.73x at end-2024 after the debt-funded purchase, eased to 3.14x at end-2025 and 2.93x at Sep 30, 2026 (WM: 2.92x, inside its 2.5x-3.0x target range). Total debt (S&P) was $16.7B at end-2023, $24.9B at end-2024 and $23.4B now. EBITDA growth (guided ~+8% for FY2026 at the midpoint vs FY2025, derived) and ~$3.8B of FCF are doing the work, while ~$3.5B a year of dividends and buybacks keep net debt roughly flat. The $250M acquisition signed for early 2027 and tuck-in M&A would add to debt.

What's Working

A clear #1 with scarce, permit-protected landfill assets and 72% internalisation, which is why price keeps beating volume loss (core price +5.8%, gross margin +2.85pp in three years). ROIC (11.8%) sits about 6pp above an estimated WACC, EBIT has compounded at 12% a year, and FCF is inflecting (TTM $3.7B, +56% YoY) as sustainability growth capex falls away. Stericycle is being worked into shape: Healthcare Solutions' adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 22.4%, SG&A and pricing targets were hit early, and leverage has come down from 3.7x to 2.9x. Buybacks ($2B in 2026) and a 14.5% dividend raise are back, FY2026 revenue and FCF are guided to the high end of ranges, and about 90% of 2026 RNG output is contracted.

What to Watch

Growth is moderate once Stericycle is stripped out (about +4.8% in FY2025; +3.5% to +6.3% a quarter in 2026) and volumes are negative (-1.3% for nine months). Q3 adjusted EBITDA margin dipped to 31.0% as fuel (-40bp) and insurance/risk costs (-40bp) rose, and adjusted EPS missed by about $0.03. Leverage is 2.9x, above this site's 2x bar, and capex remains 11-13% of revenue; only 60% of EBIT converts to FCF. RNG earnings depend on RIN prices (lower year on year) and policy, and Q3 plant start-up delays showed execution risk. Landfill compliance costs (California) are rising.
