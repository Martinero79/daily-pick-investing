Roche is one of the most profitable and capital-efficient large companies on this entire site: a 33% operating margin, a 30.8% ROIC against an estimated 7-8% cost of capital, and comfortable leverage at 0.74x net debt/EBITDA. It's also a genuine rarity in large-cap pharma — 130 years after founding, the Hoffmann-Oeri family still controls roughly 45% of voting shares through a formal shareholder pool, a real and unusually durable form of founder alignment (the family pool held just over 50% until 2024, when a departing family member reduced it slightly). What the scorecard doesn't reward is growth: revenue has been essentially flat since FY2021 (-0.96% CAGR), weighed down by the post-pandemic normalization of Roche's COVID-testing-driven Diagnostics boom and biosimilar erosion on older oncology blockbusters, while free cash flow has also drifted lower. FY2024's net income dip to CHF 8.3B was driven by a real, disclosed CHF 5.7B asset-writedown and restructuring charge — a lumpy but genuine expense, not an accounting artifact, and profitability rebounded strongly in FY2025.

Balance sheet: Roche held roughly CHF 33.2B in total debt against CHF 15.5B of cash & investments as of FY2025-end (Dec 31, 2025) — a net debt position of about CHF 17.7B, or 0.74x EBITDA. That's low, comfortable leverage for a company generating well over CHF 15B of free cash flow a year.

Governance note: Roche has a genuinely unusual dual-class structure — 160 million voting "bearer shares" and over 700 million non-voting "Genussscheine" (participation certificates, the security type most investors, including via the RHHBY ADR, actually hold). Voting control sits with the Hoffmann-Oeri founding family pool at roughly 45% (down from just over 50% after a family member, Maja Oeri, exited the pool in 2024) — a real, 130-year-old family-control structure that's genuinely rare at this scale. Novartis previously held a large Roche voting stake from a 2001-era investment but sold it back to Roche in 2021, ending two decades of an unusual cross-holding between the two Swiss rivals.

What's Working

Exceptional profitability (33% operating margin) and capital efficiency (30.8% ROIC, well clear of an estimated 7-8% cost of capital), low leverage, a declining share count, and low capex intensity typical of an R&D-driven business. Most unusually for a company this large, Roche remains a genuine family-controlled business 130 years after founding — the Hoffmann-Oeri pool still controls roughly 45% of voting shares, a real and durable form of ownership alignment this framework specifically rewards.

What to Watch

Growth has genuinely stalled — revenue, EBIT and free cash flow have all been flat-to-slightly-declining since FY2021, as Diagnostics normalizes past its pandemic-era COVID-testing peak and older oncology blockbusters face biosimilar competition, only partly offset by newer drugs (Ocrevus, Tecentriq, Vabysmo, Hemlibra, Polivy). The current valuation (23.71x TTM P/E) sits roughly 14% above Roche's own FY21-25 average, making this a somewhat richer entry point than its recent history. Both of Roche's core markets — diagnostics and oncology pharma — are genuinely fragmented and competitive, and a persistently strong Swiss franc is a real, ongoing headwind to reported (though not constant-currency) results for a company that earns the vast majority of its revenue outside Switzerland.
