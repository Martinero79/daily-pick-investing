Current, trailing & forward P/E: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. The trailing P/E series above is that same backward-looking ratio at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated SAP relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's price divided by consensus estimates of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and it captures expected growth that trailing P/E can't see. SAP's forward P/E of 23.51x is meaningfully below its 28.67x trailing P/E, implying the market expects continued cloud-driven earnings growth, consistent with the double-digit EPS growth analysts have forecast for the next few years. Running the PEG ratio confirms that read: with consensus EPS growth forecast at nearly 17% annually over the next three years, SAP's forward P/E implies a PEG around 1.4 — modestly above the 1.0 fair-value line, indicating the stock is priced a bit rich relative to its growth outlook but not dramatically so.
Balance sheet: SAP held about €9.94B in total debt against €11.63B of cash & investments as of the latest reported quarter — a genuine net cash position of roughly €1.69B, or -0.14x EBITDA. This is the strongest balance sheet found anywhere in this GRANOLAS batch, and has been net-cash-or-near-net-cash since FY2023, funded in part by strong free cash flow generation from the cloud transition.

What's Working

The strongest balance sheet and cleanest all-around fundamentals in this GRANOLAS batch: a genuine net cash position, operating margin expanding to a record 28.31%, ROIC of 16.82% comfortably clearing an estimated cost of capital, and FCF conversion clearing 80%. Cloud revenue growing 23-24% year-over-year with cloud backlog up 26-27% points to durable forward momentum, and at 28.67x earnings versus a 34.4x recent average, the stock trades at a meaningful discount to its own history. Co-founder Hasso Plattner remains actively involved as Chairman Emeritus with a real, disclosed ownership stake.

What to Watch

Revenue, EBIT, and FCF growth, while genuinely strong, all fall short of this framework's 15% bar — SAP's transformation from on-premise licenses to cloud subscriptions is a real growth driver but has not yet produced hypergrowth-level CAGRs. FY2024's €3.14bn restructuring charge, while disclosed and complete, is a reminder that large-scale organizational transformation carries real execution and cost risk. The enterprise software market beyond core ERP remains genuinely competitive, with Microsoft, Salesforce, Workday and ServiceNow all pushing into adjacent categories SAP also competes in.
