Ford Motor Company, founded in 1903 and run from Dearborn, Michigan, sells Ford and Lincoln trucks, SUVs and vans through three operating segments plus a captive lender. Ford Blue (gas and hybrid vehicles, $101B of FY2025 revenue, $3.0B EBIT) and Ford Pro (commercial vehicles and software, $66B revenue, $6.8B EBIT, 10.3% margin) are the profit engines. Ford Model e (electric vehicles) lost $4.8B in FY2025 on $6.7B of revenue, and Ford Credit earned $2.6B before tax. The Ford family still controls the company through Class B shares worth 40% of the votes while owning only about 2% of the equity. FY2025 was messy: adjusted EBIT fell 34% to $6.8B on tariffs and the Novelis aluminum plant fire, and the GAAP net loss of $8.2B reflected $17.4B of special items, mostly EV program cancellations announced in December 2025. 2026 looks better on adjusted numbers: H1 adjusted EBIT was $6.0B (vs $3.2B), guidance was raised to $10-11B and adjusted FCF to $6-7B, helped by Ford Blue pricing and a $1.3B tariff reimbursement booked in Q1. But the GAAP picture is still red, with a $3.6B largely non-cash charge in Q2 for the disposal of the BlueOval SK battery joint venture, a trailing net loss of $7.4B and Model e still guided to lose about $4B this year. The stock sits about 32% below the average analyst target and about 32% below its 52-week high.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Adjusted vs GAAP — Ford's headline EBIT and FCF strip out special items that recur nearly every year (restructuring, program cancellations, recalls), so adjusted figures flatter the underlying economics; FY2025 GAAP net income was -$8.2B against adjusted EBIT of +$6.8B. (2) Captive finance — Ford Credit adds $10.3B of revenue and $137B of debt to the consolidated statements; consolidated net debt (~$141B) and consolidated operating cash flow are not comparable with industrial peers. (3) Cyclical, policy-driven earnings — tariffs and their reimbursement (a $1.3B IEEPA refund in Q1 2026), the Novelis supply shock (about $1B swing in 2026) and EV subsidies all moved EBIT by billions, so single-year figures are noisy.

What actually moved the stock

Q2 2026 revenue was $48.3B (-3.8% YoY) with adjusted EBIT of $2.5B (+17% YoY, 5.2% margin) and adjusted FCF of $2.1B, and Ford raised full-year guidance to adjusted EBIT $10-11B and adjusted FCF $6-7B. Ford Blue earned $1.1B (+72%) on pricing and mix, Ford Pro earned $1.7B (9.7% margin, down from 12.3%) as it recovered from the Novelis aluminum shortage, and Model e lost $0.9B. Q1 2026 was the standout quarter ($3.5B adjusted EBIT) because it included a $1.3B IEEPA tariff reimbursement, so H1 2026 adjusted EBIT of $6.0B overstates the run-rate. The headline GAAP result was a $1.3B net loss because of $4.2B of special items, mainly a $3.6B largely non-cash charge for disposing of the BlueOval SK battery venture. The stock nevertheless fell from $15.28 on Jul 29 to $12.15 by Oct 5 as U.S. market share softened and investors weighed 2026 guidance quality.

What's Working

Ford Pro is a real franchise (FY2025 $6.8B EBIT, 10.3% margin, paid software subscriptions +30%), Ford Blue is earning on pricing and hybrids (Q2 EBIT +72%), and Ford Credit is steady ($2.6B EBT in FY2025, above $2.5B guided for 2026). Industrial balance sheet is near net-debt-neutral with $43B of liquidity, 2026 guidance was raised, Model e losses are narrowing for the third straight quarter, and quality is cited as improving. Capex is a moderate 4-5% of revenue, the multiple is below Ford's own five-year average (on adjusted earnings), the dividend yields ~4.9%, and the family's Class B control gives long-horizon stewardship.

What to Watch

Earnings and cash flow have shrunk while revenue grew: adjusted EBIT -32% and adjusted FCF -24% since FY2021, gross margin down 5pp in three years, and GAAP results were deeply negative in FY2022 and FY2025 and in the trailing twelve months, so “adjusted” metrics carry a lot of weight. Model e still burns about $4B a year, the BOSK battery venture is being disposed of at a $3.6B charge, tariffs and the Novelis aluminum shock moved EBIT by billions, and U.S. share is under pressure from Asian rivals (Q3 sales -6.6%). Q1 2026 included a one-off $1.3B tariff refund, so the H1 run-rate is flattered. Consolidated debt is ~$163B because of Ford Credit, ROIC clears WACC only on the adjusted basis, and some inputs are unverified (family economic stake, institutional holders, FY2021-23 adjusted EPS, my WACC estimate).
