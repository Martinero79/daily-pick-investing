Interactive Brokers is a highly automated, global electronic broker serving individual investors, hedge funds, advisors and proprietary trading firms on 175+ markets. Revenue grew ~23% a year over FY2021–FY2025 ($2.71bn to $6.21bn of net revenues) and the pre-tax margin rose from 66% to 77%, because net revenues scaled far faster than costs. Two structural points matter for reading the numbers. First, ownership: the public company (IBG, Inc.) owns only ~26.6% of the operating company IBG LLC; IBG Holdings, controlled by founder Thomas Peterffy and affiliates, owns the other 73.4%. Headline net income and EPS ($984M, $2.22 in FY25) reflect only the public slice, so this page uses pre-tax income of the whole business as the EBIT proxy. Second, split history: IBKR did a 4-for-1 forward split on June 18, 2025; all prices, EPS and dividends here are split-adjusted. About 57–60% of revenue is net interest income on customer margin loans and cash balances, so the model is rate-sensitive, and trading activity (DARTs) is cyclical: Q3 2026 DARTs fell 11% from Q2 and October was reported 4% below a year earlier. The stock trades at ~29.6x trailing earnings (about 30% above its own FY21–FY25 average) and has fallen about 20% from its late-Aug 2026 high.
Framing note — why this scorecard is adapted for a broker: Like JPMorgan and NN Group on this site, IBKR is a financial company whose customer cash and securities flows dominate its cash-flow statement, so the standard industrial framework is adapted, with every substitution flagged inline: Net revenues stand in for revenue (criterion 1); pre-tax income stands in for EBIT (criteria 2 and 3) and net income to common for FCF (criterion 4); ROE vs. an assumed ~11.5% cost of equity replaces ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7); corporate debt and capital position replace net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8; IBKR does not report CET1); (dividends + buybacks) ÷ net income replaces FCF conversion (criterion 9); criterion 10 uses P/E vs. own history; and criterion 14 uses the pre-tax (efficiency) margin because there is no gross-profit line.

Governance & Capital Notes

Thomas Peterffy (founder, Chairman) controls IBG Holdings and the voting Class B stock. The headline "~75% economic ownership" is a third-party rounding: the filing-based figure is that IBG Holdings holds 73.4% of IBG LLC, and Holdings itself has other member-owners besides Peterffy. Public shareholders therefore own a 26.6% economic claim on the operating business, and as Holdings members exchange into Class A shares the public share count rises without new capital being raised. Insiders own ~2.8% of the Class A float (S&P Global). Customer-protection rules require segregation of customer cash and securities; IBKR also runs a currency-diversification strategy (equity held in a basket of 10 currencies) that moves reported other income and OCI.

What's Working

Revenue compounding near 20% a year with a pre-tax margin of ~77% and a capex bill of ~1% of revenue. Account growth is strong (+35% YoY to 5.58M) and customer equity ($965B) and margin loans ($105B, +36%) are growing, which feed net interest income. ROE of ~24% clears any reasonable cost of equity, the corporate balance sheet carries no meaningful debt, and the founder-controlled structure (73.4% via Holdings) gives strong alignment. The quarterly dividend was raised 71% to $0.15.

What to Watch

Valuation: ~29.6x trailing earnings is ~30% above IBKR's own FY21–FY25 average. About 60% of revenue is net interest income, so falling short-term rates or smaller customer margin balances would hit the largest line (NIM already slipped to 1.99% from 2.16%). Trading activity is cyclical (Q3 DARTs −11% QoQ; October −4% YoY). Payout is low (~14% of net income to common) and the Class A share count rises as Holdings members exchange. Market position is strong but mid-pack among U.S. brokers by assets, and the public holds only a 26.6% economic interest, so EPS understates whole-company earnings (and the tax rate on them is low at ~9%).
