A.P. Møller - Mærsk is the world's #2 container shipping line by capacity, running an integrated Ocean, Logistics & Services and Terminals business built on 120+ years of continuous control by the founding family's foundation. The scorecard here is dominated by one fact: the FY21-25 window brackets the entire 2021-22 freight-rate supercycle (EBIT peaked at $30.7bn in FY2022) and the brutal crash that followed (FY2025 EBIT of just $3.0bn, a tenth of the peak) — so revenue, EBIT and FCF all show negative 5-year CAGRs despite nothing structurally wrong with the business. What holds up through the cycle is the balance sheet and capital discipline: Maersk ran a net-cash position in every single year of this window, including the worst of the downturn, free cash flow has exceeded EBIT in four of the last five years, and the share count has shrunk by more than a fifth since FY2021 on consistent buybacks. The A.P. Møller Foundation's ~41.5% stake (51.45% of votes) is about as long-horizon and aligned as ownership gets anywhere on this site. The catch is valuation: the stock is up ~41% over the past year as 2026's freight rates have firmed sharply (Q2 2026 EBIT more than doubled year-over-year), pushing the trailing P/E to 20.75x — roughly 3.6x its own FY21-25 average of 5.80x. Investors are now paying a real premium for a cyclical recovery that has only just begun to show up in trailing earnings.

Q2 2026 Headlines

Revenue reached $15.76bn, up 20.0% YoY from $13.13bn. EBIT more than doubled to $1.43bn from $647m a year earlier (+121.6%), lifting operating margin to 9.10% from 4.93%. This followed a near-breakeven Q4 2025 (-$18m EBIT, -0.14% margin) — a rare loss-adjacent quarter for Maersk. Management raised its FY2026 underlying EBIT outlook to $4.5-6.5bn, a $2.5bn upgrade, citing surging ocean freight rates, the new Gemini Cooperation network (with alliance partner Hapag-Lloyd) improving schedule reliability, and continued cost discipline.

Quarterly Operating Income / EBIT ($bn)
Q4 2025 was effectively break-even (-$18m, -0.14% margin) — a rare near-loss quarter for Maersk — before Q1 and especially Q2 2026 rebounded sharply as freight rates firmed.
What changed: 2025 was a soft year for ocean freight rates as new vessel capacity outpaced demand growth, squeezing margins across the industry (not just Maersk). 2026 has seen rates firm meaningfully — partly tied to continued Red Sea/Suez rerouting keeping effective capacity tighter, and to the Gemini Cooperation network (Maersk + Hapag-Lloyd, launched Feb 2025) improving schedule reliability and cost efficiency — pushing Q2 2026 EBIT to more than double the year-ago quarter and prompting a $2.5bn guidance upgrade.

Forward P/E is shown for FY21-24 and current (3.15x–11.64x, now 9.61x); FY2025's data-sourced forward reading (175x) is a near-zero-next-year-earnings estimate artifact and is flagged rather than plotted — see explainer below.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio at each fiscal year-end, distorted by the 2021-22 freight-rate supercycle and the 2023 crash that followed it. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings; FY2021-24 and today's reading (3.15x-11.64x, now 9.61x) are sensible, but the data source's FY2025 forward reading (≈175x) reflects a near-zero next-twelve-months consensus earnings estimate at that snapshot date — a data artifact, not a real valuation signal — so it's flagged on the chart rather than plotted as if meaningful. Today's forward P/E (9.61x) sits well below trailing P/E (20.75x), consistent with consensus expecting Maersk's earnings to recover from the current cyclical trough. A PEG ratio (forward P/E ÷ expected annual EPS growth) isn't meaningfully computable for a shipping cyclical like Maersk, whose earnings swing with freight rates rather than a steady structural growth rate — the forward P/E level itself, not a PEG multiple, is the more honest cross-check here.

What's Working

A genuine #2 global container carrier by capacity; a rock-solid, net-cash balance sheet in every one of the last five years, even through the worst freight-rate crash in over a decade; free cash flow has exceeded EBIT in four of the last five years thanks to heavy depreciation and disciplined capex; the share count has shrunk by more than a fifth since FY2021 through consistent buybacks; and the A.P. Møller Foundation's ~41.5%/51.45% stake is about as aligned and long-horizon as ownership gets on this site.

What to Watch

This is one of the most cyclical scorecards on the site: revenue, EBIT and FCF all show negative 5-year CAGRs, not because the business is structurally shrinking but because the window brackets the 2021-22 freight-rate supercycle and the crash that followed. ROIC has sat below even a conservative 8.5% WACC assumption in 3 of the last 4 years. And after the stock's ~41% run over the past year on 2026's rate recovery, the trailing P/E (20.75x) sits at roughly 3.6x its own 5-year average — investors are now paying a real premium for a cyclical recovery that has only just begun to show up in trailing earnings.
