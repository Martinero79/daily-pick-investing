China Construction Bank is China's third-largest bank by assets (RMB 45.6tn at FY25, RMB 47.3tn at June 2026) behind ICBC and Agricultural Bank, and the second-most profitable (RMB 338.9bn net profit attributable to the shareholders of the bank in FY25), with particular strength in infrastructure, housing finance and corporate lending. It is majority state-owned (Central Huijin holds ~54.6%) and part of the policy-directed “Big Six”. The honest case for including it on a growth-oriented scorecard rests on scale, capital strength and sustained high returns, not growth: operating income fell from RMB 764.7bn (FY21) to RMB 740.9bn (FY25) as net interest margin compressed from 2.13% to 1.34%, profit to ordinary holders compounded at only ~2.9%/yr, ROE slid from 12.55% to 10.04% and the cost-to-income ratio rose from 27.6% to 29.4%. It remains very well capitalised (CET1 14.24% in June 2026, boosted by a RMB 105bn state placement in 2025 that raised the share count 4.6%) with a low NPL ratio (1.31%), and H1 2026 showed an income rebound (operating income +10.5%, net profit to the parent +4.6%, NIM 1.37% vs. 1.34% in FY25). The catch: the H-share has re-rated from ~2.6x to ~6.6x earnings and from ~0.3x to ~0.65x book in five years, so much of the recovery is in the price, and the payout is only ~31%.
Framing note — why this scorecard looks different for a bank: CCB is the second Chinese bank on this site alongside ICBC and, like JPMorgan and Citigroup, requires bank substitutions, all flagged inline: Operating income (total net operating income, IFRS, RMB) stands in for revenue (criterion 1); Net profit attributable to ordinary shareholders substitutes for both EBIT (criterion 2) and Free Cash Flow (criterion 4) — bank cash flow is dominated by deposit and interbank swings, so the two are correlated by construction; ROE vs. an assumed ~9.5% cost of equity (midpoint of 9–10%, justified by a low-beta, state-backed franchise; tangible equity is not reported so ROE stands in for ROTCE) substitutes for ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7); CET1 ratio vs. regulatory requirement substitutes for net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8); (dividends + buybacks) ÷ net profit substitutes for FCF conversion (criterion 9); the cost-to-income ratio replaces gross margin (criterion 14, lower = better, bands inverted). Reporting currency is RMB; the share price is the H-share in HKD, and valuation ratios convert EPS and book value per share to HKD at year-end FX (approximate). Nothing else is converted.

ROE Trend — bank analog to ROIC
Return on weighted-average equity as reported, vs. an assumed ~9.5% cost of equity (midpoint of 9–10%). ROE has fallen every year (12.55% to 10.04%) but FY2025 is still above the hurdle by ~0.5pt; annualised H1 2026 ROE is 9.52%, back to the hurdle.
Market position, honestly stated: CCB is the #3 bank in China by total assets (RMB 45.6tn at FY25) behind ICBC (RMB 53.5tn) and Agricultural Bank of China (RMB 48.8tn), and #2 by net profit — so on the standard assets measure criterion #5 (#1 or #2) is Not Met (#3 = 2/5). Its strengths are housing finance, infrastructure lending and a leading technology/ESG lending reputation. The structure behind that rank matters: the six biggest Chinese banks are all state-controlled, prices (loan prime rate, deposit-rate guidance, mortgage-rate and fee concessions) are heavily shaped by policy, and CCB is expected to support national priorities. That makes China's banking market an oligopoly at the top but with thousands of smaller institutions, joint-stock and city commercial banks at the margins, so concentration is scored 3/5 (Not Met). G-SIB status brings higher loss-absorbency requirements.

Reading the valuation: At ~6.6x trailing earnings and ~0.65x book value, the CCB H-share trades well above its own FY2021–FY2025 year-end averages (P/E ~3.4x; P/TBV ~0.37x). Governance & Capital Notes

CCB is controlled by the Chinese state: Central Huijin Investment owns about 54.6% (54.51% via H shares plus 0.10% via A shares, per the Q1 2026 report), with the H-share public float held mainly through HKSCC Nominees (~32.9%). The Ministry of Finance subscribed to the RMB 105.0bn A-share placement in 2025 (11.6bn new shares, +4.6% share capital; ~4.4% of the enlarged capital, a derived estimate, not a disclosed holding). That is a controlling stake in the framework's >30% band, but it is state, not founder or management, ownership — alignment with minority holders is not guaranteed and the bank may be asked to prioritise policy goals (rate and fee concessions, lending to priority sectors) over return on equity. The 2025 recapitalisation also shows the state supports bank capital, but at the cost of minority dilution. Management ownership is negligible. Ownership percentages are approximate.

What's Working

Very strong capital (CET1 14.24%, total CAR 19.42%) and a low-cost franchise (cost-to-income 29.4%, ~22% in H1) with ROE still above a ~9.5% cost-of-equity assumption. Asset quality has improved slowly (NPL ratio 1.42% → 1.31%, provision coverage 233%), net profit to ordinary holders has grown every year (RMB 298bn → 333bn) even as net interest margin fell, and H1 2026 shows an income rebound (+10.5%) with NIM stabilising at 1.37%. Capex is low (3.2% of income) and the dividend yields ~4.6% on the H-share.

What to Watch

Growth is absent (operating income below FY21, ROE down ~2.5pts in four years, cost-to-income up ~1.8pts) and the margin recovery is only a few basis points so far. After the H-share re-rating (~2.6x → ~6.6x earnings, ~0.3x → ~0.65x book), the valuation test against its own history is failed, and the payout (~31%) is modest for a bank with this much capital. State control cuts both ways: policy support and recapitalisation (the 2025 placement diluted shares 4.6%) but also policy-directed lending and rate concessions. CCB is only #3 by assets. Property and local-government debt exposure remain the main asset-quality swing factors.
