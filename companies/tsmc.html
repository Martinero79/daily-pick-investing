TSMC is the world's dominant contract chipmaker, manufacturing the most advanced chips for NVIDIA, Apple, AMD and nearly every major fabless designer — roughly 70% of global foundry revenue, and a near-monopoly (74–77% of wafer revenue) at the leading edge (7nm and below). HPC/AI-related demand has pushed the HPC platform to 66% of revenue by Q2 2026, up from 53% seven quarters earlier, as datacenter AI-chip demand outpaces smartphones. The model's own numbers say: growth, margin, ROIC, balance-sheet strength, market position and share count all clear the bar comfortably — the misses are heavy capex intensity (a structural feature of the foundry model, not a red flag on its own), FCF conversion trailing the fab buildout, a valuation that has re-rated above its own recent average multiple, and thin insider/founder ownership since Morris Chang's retirement.

Operating Margin % (quarterly)
Margin climbed from 49.0% to 60.3% in seven quarters — a genuine step-change, not a seasonal blip, coinciding with the HPC/AI mix shift and 2nm ramp.
Pattern to note: unlike a fabless chip designer, TSMC's margin expansion is coming while capex is also running at record dollar levels ($52–56B guided for 2026) — the company is funding next-generation 2nm/A16 capacity from its own operating cash flow while still growing net cash every quarter. That combination is the core of the investment case.

What's Working

Near-monopoly at the leading edge (74–77% of wafer revenue at 7nm-and-below); HPC/AI demand now 66% of revenue and climbing; operating margin expanded from 49% to over 60% in seven quarters; net cash position growing every quarter despite record capex; revenue, EBIT and FCF all compounding at 20%+ CAGR.

What to Watch

Capex running 33–53% of revenue for fab construction — the trade-off for staying ahead is a genuinely capital-intensive model, not the asset-light economics of the fabless names elsewhere on this site; FCF conversion trails growth as a result; current P/E (28x) sits above its own 2-year average (23x) after a strong re-rating; Taiwan Strait concentration risk is widely cited by analysts as a factor that can compress the multiple; no controlling founder-CEO since Morris Chang's retirement.
