CATL is the world's largest EV battery and energy-storage maker by a wide margin — roughly 2.4x the size of #2 BYD by global installations, and #1 for nine consecutive years. Revenue has compounded at over 30% a year since 2021 despite a rare down year in 2024, profitability has kept expanding even as raw-material costs swung wildly, and the balance sheet sits comfortably net cash. The one real structural weak point this framework flags is capex intensity: building battery gigafactories at this scale is genuinely capital-hungry, unlike an asset-light software or platform business. Reading trailing vs. forward P/E: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings; the trailing-P/E series above is that same ratio at each year-end. Forward P/E divides today's price by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and it captures expected growth that trailing P/E can't see. Western sell-side coverage of this Shenzhen-listed name is thinner than for the U.S. mega-caps on this site, so only a single current reading (≈18.3x, 2026 consensus estimate via MarketScreener) is shown rather than a full historical line. It sits well below trailing (≈31.9x), implying the market expects meaningful further earnings growth from CATL's continued gigafactory expansion and dominant battery market share. A clean PEG ratio isn't reliably published for this Shenzhen-listed name either, for the same thin-coverage reason the forward-P/E history is incomplete, but the same math applies conceptually: dividing an ≈18.3x forward P/E by CATL's still-elevated expected earnings growth would plausibly land near or below the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, consistent with the stock not looking expensive once its growth is taken into account.

What's Working

A structurally dominant #1 in a market that's only getting bigger — global EV and grid-storage battery demand keeps climbing, and CATL's share has been rising, not fading, against every real competitor. Revenue and profit have both compounded at 30%+ a year since 2021, the balance sheet is comfortably net cash, the founder remains the largest shareholder and actively engaged as chairman, and the current multiple sits modestly below the stock's own FY21-25 average despite the growth. This is a rare combination of scale, growth and a statistically reasonable entry point.

What to Watch

Battery manufacturing at gigafactory scale is genuinely capital-intensive — capex intensity is the one clear structural miss here, and it's a real, ongoing cash drain rather than a one-off. Two criteria (FCF CAGR and FCF conversion) are marked N/A because reliable multi-year capex data wasn't available in public English-language sourcing — worth digging into CATL's original-language annual reports before relying on this dashboard's FCF conclusions. There's also real geopolitical exposure: a China-domiciled company sitting at the center of a strategically sensitive supply chain, which brings export-control, tariff and sanctions-list risk this framework's financial criteria don't capture at all. Notably, CATL has kept expanding its own margins even as China's broader EV industry has been squeezed by a brutal price war — a sign of real pricing power and some insulation from pressure on its automaker customers.
