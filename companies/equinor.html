Equinor is Norway's national energy champion and the main marketer of Norwegian gas into Europe. Its FY2022 numbers were extraordinary (49.7% EBIT margin on the post-invasion gas spike) and every year since has been a normalization: EBIT fell from $74.1B to $26.4B, free cash flow from $26.4B to $6.0B, and gross margin by about 20 points, while capex climbed from $8.8B to $14.0B. What the scorecard does reward is a still-healthy margin, low leverage, a share count that has shrunk roughly a quarter in five years through buybacks, and a controlling state shareholder (≈67%). The share price (NOK 440.50) has nearly doubled over the last 12 months as oil and gas prices rose again, so the current P/E (≈12.3x) sits about 50% above its FY21–25 average (≈8.2x) — a comparison flattered downward by FY22's peak earnings, so treat it with care.

What's Working

A 25% EBIT margin and ~37% gross margin, low net debt (≈0.3x EBITDA), a share count down roughly a quarter in five years, a controlling and stable state owner (≈67%), and a central role as Europe's largest pipeline gas supplier. Q2 2026 operating income of $12.5B shows the earnings power at current prices, and the dividend has grown each year (from $0.71 to $1.50 per share).

What to Watch

Earnings and free cash flow have trended down since FY2022 (EBIT CAGR −6.3%, FCF CAGR −26.8%), capex has risen to 13% of revenue, and FCF covers only about 23% of EBIT. The share price has roughly doubled in a year and trades at about 1.5x its own five-year average P/E, with analysts' average target (≈NOK 380) below the price. Results swing with oil and gas prices (Brent ≈$114 now), Norwegian petroleum taxation, impairments and energy-transition spending, and the state's priorities differ from those of minority holders.
