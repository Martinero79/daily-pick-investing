Kuehne+Nagel International AG is a Swiss global logistics group founded in Bremen in 1890 and run from Schindellegi near Zurich, with roughly 88,000 employees at 1,300+ sites in close to 100 countries. It earns its money in four units: Sea Logistics (CHF 8.8B net turnover in FY2025, 4.3M TEU), Air Logistics (CHF 7.3B, 2.2M tonnes), Road Logistics (CHF 3.5B, mainly European groupage) and Contract Logistics (CHF 4.8B, warehousing). Like DSV it is asset-light: it charters rather than owns ships and aircraft, so capex is about 1% of net turnover, and the economics are driven by the gross profit it keeps per container or kilo (the “conversion rate” of EBIT to gross profit) rather than by freight rates themselves. The share price tells a story of the freight cycle. EBIT peaked at CHF 3.8B in FY2022, fell to CHF 1.2B in FY2025 as yields normalised and the Red Sea, tariff and European-demand shocks hit, and the dividend was cut from CHF 14.00 to CHF 6.00. Management responded with a cost programme of more than CHF 200m (fully in place by Q4 2025), a push into small and medium-sized customers (now about half of sea volumes), and AI-driven automation it expects to add CHF 100-150m of EBIT by 2027. Q2 2026 was the first clear sign it is working: net turnover CHF 6.6B (+7.6% YoY), EBIT CHF 381m (+11%), Air Logistics EBIT +35% on cloud and data-centre freight, and 2026 recurring EBIT guidance raised to CHF 1.35-1.55B (initially CHF 1.2-1.4B). The shares have re-rated about 49% in a year and now sit about 5% below their September high, so the market is already paying for a recovery that is still early.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) COVID base effect — FY2021-22 net turnover (CHF 32.8B / 39.4B) and EBIT (CHF 2.9B / 3.8B) were inflated by extraordinary freight rates, so every FY2021-25 CAGR here is negative largely because of the starting point, not only because the business shrank; I did not verify pre-2020 earnings levels. (2) Control — Klaus-Michael Kühne, the long-time majority owner (through Kühne Holding AG, ~53%), died on Aug 23, 2026 at 89; press reports say his stake passes to the Kühne Foundation, which should keep control stable but is a change worth watching. (3) Earnings-based multiples look high (trailing P/E ~30x) because EPS is near a cyclical low; that is a feature of the trough, not necessarily of the price paid for normal earnings.

Turnaround Evidence & Latest Quarter (Q2 2026)
The investment case for the next 12 months is whether cost cuts, market-share gains in air freight and AI productivity can lift EBIT back toward CHF 1.5B without a new yield shock. Reported on Jul 23, 2026; the nine-month report is due Oct 22, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 net turnover was CHF 6,616m (+7.6% YoY; the company cites +8%, or +11% in constant currencies, because the Swiss franc strengthened) and EBIT was CHF 381m (+11%, or +17% at constant currency), the best quarter since Q1 2025 (CHF 402m), against a Q2 2025 that was itself weak. Recurring EBIT rose about 6% YoY and about 24% on Q1, and the H1 2026 total of CHF 724m reported (CHF 689m recurring) was enough for management to lift 2026 guidance to CHF 1.35-1.55B. Air Logistics was the star: net turnover +20% and EBIT +35% to CHF 154m on market-share gains in the technology sector (including cloud-infrastructure equipment moves for Google) and a better customer mix. Sea Logistics EBIT was CHF 140m with a 29% conversion rate, four points higher than Q1, though container volumes were about 1% lower YoY and European export demand stayed weak. Road EBIT rose 29% to CHF 36m and Contract Logistics 21% to CHF 51m. Free cash flow in H1 was CHF 310m (vs CHF 295m), fine but not a surge. Gross profit grew only about 3% YoY, so the EBIT gain is mostly cost discipline rather than stronger yields.

Share Count, Dividend & Balance Sheet

Registered shares are unchanged at 120.754M since 2021 (118.8M outstanding after treasury shares; about -1.7% from the 120.75M at end-2021, i.e. flat), with buybacks of CHF 646m in FY2022 and CHF 716m in FY2023, then almost none. The dividend fell from CHF 14.00 (FY2022) to 6.00 (FY2025). Cash was CHF 617m at Jun 30, 2026 against CHF 4.08B of debt (mostly lease liabilities), a net debt of CHF 3.47B including leases (CHF 1.18B excluding), up from net cash of CHF 248m at end-2023 after acquisitions (CHF 559m in FY2025) and dividends.

Controlling shareholder & honest caveats: Kühne Holding AG, controlled by Klaus-Michael Kühne (honorary chairman, died Aug 23, 2026), owned ~53.3% of the shares; press reports say the stake passes to the Kühne Foundation. A controlling holder is good for long-term alignment and makes a hostile bid impossible, but it also means minority holders have little influence and the free float is only ~47%. I did not verify management or board shareholdings, and S&P's “insiders 0.13%” figure excludes the Kühne holding, so the control score rests on the ~53% disclosure, not on that data point. CEO Stefan Paul and Chairman Dr. Joerg Wolle are professional managers.
Leverage path: net debt excluding leases (the company's headline measure) was CHF 1.18B at Jun 30, 2026; including leases it was CHF 3.47B, or ~1.6x my estimate of LTM EBITDA of ~CHF 2.2B. S&P's Debt/EBITDA of 1.97x uses gross debt and its own EBITDA definition. The Apex minority buyout (Partners Group's 24.9%) raises net debt but, per management, is earnings-accretive. With the dividend at ~80% of earnings and capex under 1% of turnover, leverage is not a constraint, though it is no longer the net-cash cushion of 2022.

What's Working

A high-return, asset-light franchise at trough earnings: ROIC of 16.3% against a ~6.9% WACC, capex under 1% of net turnover, and FY2025 FCF up 48% even as EBIT fell. Q2 2026 delivered EBIT +11% and Air Logistics EBIT +35% on genuine market-share gains in technology freight, the cost programme (over CHF 200m) is fully in place, and guidance was lifted to CHF 1.35-1.55B. Net turnover turned positive YoY for the first time in five quarters. The balance sheet is sound (about 1.6x including leases, 0.5x without), the controlling holder gives long-term stability, and Kuehne+Nagel remains the global volume leader in both sea and air freight with SMEs, who pay higher yields, now about half of sea volumes. Management has a clear lever (AI-driven productivity, CHF 100-150m by 2027) that is measurable.

What to Watch

Every growth criterion is negative: FY2021-25 net turnover CAGR is -7.1%, EBIT -19.4% and FCF -15.4%, and EBIT is still about 67% below the FY2022 peak. The stock is up about 49% in a year and trades at ~30x trailing and ~25x forward earnings, with the average analyst target about 10% below the price, so a lot of recovery is priced in while gross profit grew only about 3% in Q2. Sea yields are still under pressure and European export demand is weak, Q2's gain leans on cost cuts and air-freight mix from a few technology customers, and Q1 2026 EBIT appears to include one-off gains. Klaus-Michael Kühne's death in August 2026 changes the shareholder picture even though control should pass to the foundation. The market-share claim is contested (third by revenue behind DSV and DHL), and several inputs could not be verified: management shareholdings, an exact market-share percentage, and company-defined EBITDA for the leverage ratio.
