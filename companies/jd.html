JD.com is China's largest retailer by revenue, built on a first-party inventory and logistics model that's structurally different from Alibaba's or PDD's marketplace approach — and it shows up in the numbers as thin gross margins (9.3%) but a genuinely enormous net cash position ($22.78B, more than half the entire market cap). What's driving this dashboard's weak score is a fight JD itself chose to start: in February 2025, JD launched an aggressive push into food delivery, directly challenging Meituan's dominant position and pulling Alibaba in as well. The resulting subsidy war cost the three companies more than ¥100 billion ($14 billion) combined across Q2-Q3 2025 alone, and it pushed JD to three consecutive quarterly operating losses (Q2, Q3, and a particularly steep Q4 2025 loss of ¥4.55B). The good news: JD returned to modest profitability in Q1 and Q2 2026 (margins around 1.2%), suggesting the worst of the subsidy intensity may be easing, and JD has stated an explicit goal of reaching 30% food-delivery market share in 2026, up from roughly 15%. Whether that goal is worth what it's cost the core retail business's margins is very much an open question this dashboard doesn't try to answer for you.

What's Working

JD is China's largest retailer by revenue, still growing, with a genuinely enormous net cash position that covers well over half its own market cap. Share count has declined a meaningful 13% since FY2021 through consistent buybacks, capex intensity is remarkably low for a company running its own logistics network, and the two most recent quarters show a real return to profitability after three straight quarterly losses.

What to Watch

Unlike Alibaba's and PDD's headwinds, JD's margin damage this cycle is largely self-inflicted: JD chose to enter the food-delivery war in February 2025. Whether the strategic logic (aiming for 30% food-delivery share by 2026) justifies the cost to core retail margins is genuinely unresolved. Revenue growth is structurally slow for a company this size, ROIC sits well below cost of capital right now, and market-share claims relative to PDD are directly contested across sources.
