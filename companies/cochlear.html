Cochlear Limited is the Australian pioneer and dominant global leader in cochlear implants — surgically implanted devices that restore hearing for people with severe-to-profound hearing loss, an entirely different clinical category from the conventional (non-surgical) hearing aids sold by Demant/Oticon, Sonova/Phonak or fitted by Amplifon's retail network. Since its Nucleus implant program in the 1980s, Cochlear has built roughly 50-60% global share in implants, competing mainly with Austria's privately held MED-EL (~25%) and Sonova-owned Advanced Bionics (~20%) — a genuinely concentrated, near-monopoly market structure that shows up clearly in this scorecard's market-share and market-concentration criteria. FY2026, however, was a rough year on the numbers: revenue grew just 0.2% reported (2% in constant currency, decelerating sharply from FY2025's 4.8%) as the rollout of the new Nucleus Nexa implant platform brought manufacturing inefficiencies and unfavourable emerging-market mix, operating margin fell to 17.95% from 22.32%, and statutory net profit collapsed 62% to A$147.3M after A$109.1M in non-cash investment write-downs (mainly the Epiminder neural-monitoring stake) and roughly A$66M of cloud-computing costs — underlying net profit, which strips out those one-offs, was a milder A$322M, down 22% but at the top of guidance. The stock priced in a lot of that stress already: shares crashed roughly 40% on April 22, 2026 after a guidance downgrade citing weak emerging-market demand and Middle East conflict disruption, and still trade about 54% below their 52-week high even after a partial recovery. Balance sheet strength and capital discipline remain intact — Cochlear is effectively net-cash, capex runs under 3% of revenue, and the share count has been roughly flat to slightly down — but growth, margin and FCF-conversion criteria all miss this framework's bars in FY2026, and there is no meaningful insider or founder ownership anchoring the register. Net: a real, durable near-monopoly in a growing implant category, going through a genuine operational rough patch, with 6 of 14 criteria met and a Martinero Index of 55/100.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY22-26 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed. For Cochlear specifically: trailing P/E (63.50x) looks expensive versus its own FY22-26 average (52.16x), but that trailing figure is mechanically distorted by FY2026's write-down-depressed statutory earnings, not by a rising share price (the stock is down over 50% from its 52-week high). Forward P/E (27.41x) is far lower, implying the market expects a substantial earnings recovery as the Epiminder write-down and one-off cloud costs roll off and the Nexa launch matures — consistent with management's FY2027 underlying-profit guidance of A$435-460M. One more lens worth a glance: the PEG ratio, forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether that recovery is already priced in. Cochlear's PEG currently runs around 2.27 — well above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, suggesting that even on a forward, growth-adjusted basis the stock is not cheap; the market is paying up for the expected implant-market recovery and Nexa margin ramp, not getting it at a discount.
Balance sheet: Cochlear has run net-cash every year of this window until FY2026, when a small net debt position of A$25M emerged (net cash A$375.4M in FY2022, A$353.7M FY2023, A$269.9M FY2024, A$40.0M FY2025, -A$25.0M FY2026) against EBITDA of A$479.9M — a Net Debt/EBITDA of roughly 0.05x, still comfortably inside this framework's <2x bar and close to a pure net-cash position. ROIC stands at 16.44% (TTM, per stockanalysis.com), clearing both the assumed 8.5% WACC and the framework's 15% ROIC bar, though the margin of comfort is thin given FY2026's depressed earnings base. Capex ran at roughly 2.6% of revenue (A$61.1M on A$2,348M) in FY2026 — a genuinely low, asset-light capital intensity typical of an IP- and installed-base-driven MedTech franchise. Shares outstanding have been essentially flat (65.40M currently, down 0.11% year-over-year), reflecting modest, consistent buyback activity rather than aggressive capital return.

What's Working

Cochlear holds a genuine near-monopoly in its actual product category (implants, not hearing aids) — roughly 50-60% global share in a concentrated three-player market with MED-EL and Sonova-owned Advanced Bionics. The balance sheet has run net-cash for essentially this entire window, capex intensity is low and asset-light, share count is flat-to-declining, and free cash flow more than doubled year-over-year in FY2026 as post-Nexa-launch working capital normalised. The Nucleus Nexa platform has already captured over 95% of the developed-market implant mix, and management guides underlying profit up 11-17% for FY2027.

What to Watch

FY2026 was genuinely weak on the numbers this framework screens for: revenue growth decelerated to roughly flat, operating margin fell below 20% for the first time in five years, EBIT and FCF five-year CAGRs are both far below the 15% bar, and statutory net profit collapsed 62% on one-off write-downs. There is no meaningful insider or founder ownership anchoring the register, and the trailing P/E looks expensive versus Cochlear's own history — though that's partly a mechanical effect of depressed FY2026 earnings rather than a rising share price, which has in fact fallen roughly 54% from its 52-week high. Whether the Nexa ramp and emerging-market pricing genuinely resolve the margin and growth issues in FY2027, as guided, is the central question here.
