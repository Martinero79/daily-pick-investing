Nu Holdings is the parent of Nubank, founded in 2013 by David Vélez, Cristina Junqueira and Edward Wible and built around a purple credit card with no annual fee and no branches. It now serves 139 million customers in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia and earns money from three sources: credit (cards, unsecured and secured lending, 41% of Q2 2026 gross profit), float on $45.3B of deposits (34%) and fees (25%). The credit book is $39.4B (+37% YoY). In August 2026 Nu launched its bank in Mexico and is adding a full banking licence in Brazil, and in July it launched “Croma” for a more affluent customer tier.
What to keep in mind: (1) Credit cycle — Nu lends mostly unsecured to a customer base that includes many first-time borrowers in a high-rate economy; 90+ day NPLs rose to 6.9% in Q2 2026 and management is deliberately expanding into higher-risk, higher-return segments. (2) FX — earnings are in reais and pesos, reported in dollars, so the USD growth rate swings with the currency (Q2 2025 revenue growth was only +14% reported). (3) M&A noise — on Sep 28, 2026 the stock fell 10% on reports of talks to buy UK bank Monzo; Nu said on Sep 30 it is not pursuing a deal and the shares rebounded in after-hours trading. The Sep 30 close of $12.66 is before that rebound.

Quarterly Trend & Latest Quarter (Q2 2026)
Reported Aug 13, 2026: the first quarter with net income above $1 billion. Next report (Q3 2026): Nov 12, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue (gross-profit basis) was $2,474M, +52.2% YoY in reported dollars (Nu cites +43% on an FX-neutral basis), and net income was $1,060M (+66.5% reported, +49% FX-neutral), with ROE at 33%. Nu's gross revenue was nearly $5.9B (+39% FX-neutral). Net interest margin expanded 180 bps to 22.9% and risk-adjusted NIM to 12.4% (from 9.5% in Q1) as the mix shifted toward unsecured lending and early delinquencies improved seasonally. The efficiency ratio worsened to 19.5% from 17.6% as marketing and real-estate spend moved into the quarter. The 15-90 day NPL ratio improved to 4.8%, but 90+ day NPLs rose to 6.9%. The effective tax rate was a low 14.2%, which flatters net income relative to pre-tax income (+41% YoY at $1,236M).

Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership
Valuation uses trailing and forward P/E from S&P Global; P/FCF is not usable because free cash flow is structurally negative. Only three year-ends (FY2023-25) have positive earnings.
Trailing vs Forward P/E — Fiscal Year-End vs. Current
Current trailing P/E 17.3x ($12.66 / TTM EPS $0.73) is about 56% of the FY2023-25 year-end average of 30.7x; forward P/E 13.0x vs a 22.5x average. PEG is ~0.45 (S&P). The comparison is thin: FY2021-22 had losses, and a share that has fallen 24% from its 2025 year-end price will look cheaper on any ratio.

Book value & other multiples: price/book is 4.6x (vs 7.2x at FY2025 year-end and 6.5x at FY2024), price/sales 7.2x TTM, equity $13.25B, book value $2.74 per share. A 4.6x P/B is only supportable by a ~30% ROE that persists; if returns revert toward a typical bank's mid-teens, the multiple has little cushion.
Why capex and net debt do not tell the story
Capex was $7M in FY2025 (about 0.1% of revenue) and capitalised intangibles $334M; Nu is genuinely asset-light. Reported “total debt” of $5.9B (Jun 2026) against $15.2B cash and investments is a funding-mix detail beside $45.3B of customer deposits and a $39.4B loan book. Net debt/EBITDA is therefore N/A rather than “net cash = full marks”.

What's Working

Scale and unit economics are compounding: 139M customers, ARPAC rising to ~$17, an efficiency ratio below 20% and ROE of 33%. Net income went from a $365M loss in FY2022 to $2.9B in FY2025 and $1.06B in a single quarter, with deposits funding the loan book at 88% of the interbank rate. Revenue growth re-accelerated to +52% in Q2 2026, the Mexican bank launch removes a structural constraint, and the stock trades at 17x trailing and 13x forward earnings, against 30x and 22x at recent year-ends, with a PEG near 0.45.

What to Watch

This is an unsecured-credit business: 90+ day NPLs are 6.9% and management is deliberately taking more risk, so a Brazilian downturn or a regulatory change to interest or card rules would hit revenue and provisions together. Growth has already slowed from +116% to +27% annually, FX can swing reported results by double digits, and the 55% margin is on revenue that has credit costs netted out. The recent Monzo story shows capital-allocation risk, and the stock is down about 21% from its 2025 year-end price. Several inputs are estimates (cost of equity, insider split, the FY23-25 averages) and cash-flow criteria cannot be tested.
