SK hynix is the clearest way to own the physical infrastructure of the AI buildout rather than a bet on who wins the AI race. Every large AI accelerator — Nvidia's GPUs above all — needs high-bandwidth memory (HBM) stacked directly next to the compute die, and SK hynix has held roughly 58-62% of that market through 2026, comfortably ahead of Micron and Samsung. That position shows up directly in the numbers: revenue nearly quadrupled year-over-year in the latest quarter, operating margin climbed from 41% to 76% across five quarters, and the balance sheet flipped from net debt to a ₩66.87T net cash cushion in under two years. Unlike some of the more speculative AI-adjacent names on this site, this isn't a story of hoped-for future profits: ROIC of 61.59% against a 17.27% cost of capital is a genuine, present-tense value-creation engine, and even after the stock's enormous run the current valuation multiple sits almost exactly at its own five-year average. The scorecard isn't perfect — this is still a capital-intensive fab business (capex is 18.6% of revenue) with a history of brutal memory-cycle downturns (FY2023 was a loss year), and free cash flow conversion, while improving fast, hasn't yet cleared this framework's 80% bar. But 10 of 13 criteria met, with a valuation that hasn't run away from its own history, is a genuinely rare combination for a stock that's up over 500% in a year.

What's Working

Nearly every metric on this page is best-in-class: revenue and margin both accelerating for five straight quarters, a genuine ROIC/WACC spread of over 44 points, a rock-solid balance sheet, and a valuation multiple that hasn't run away from its own history despite the stock's enormous move. The #1 HBM position is the single most important fact here — it's the product Nvidia and other AI accelerator makers cannot ship without.

What to Watch

Memory is a famously cyclical, capital-intensive business — FY2023's loss year is a reminder this can turn quickly if AI capex slows or HBM oversupply emerges (Micron and Samsung are both investing hard to close the gap). FCF conversion, while improving, still lags the 80% bar, and capex intensity remains structurally high. A single quarter of AI-demand disappointment could move this scorecard meaningfully.
