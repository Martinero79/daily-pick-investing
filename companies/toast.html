Toast, Inc. sells a cloud-based point-of-sale and restaurant-management platform (handhelds, kitchen displays, online ordering, payroll, marketing and a growing set of AI tools) to restaurants, and earns most of its money from processing the payments that run through it. It was founded in 2011 by Steve Fredette, Aman Narang and Jonathan Grimm, and listed on the NYSE on September 22, 2021. Revenue splits into financial technology solutions ($5.04B of FY2025's $6.15B, mostly payments), subscription services ($0.94B) and hardware and professional services ($0.18B); because payments revenue is booked gross, the 26% gross margin looks low, and management guides instead to recurring (subscription plus fintech) gross profit, +23–25% for FY2026 after an August raise. The business has moved from a $384M GAAP operating loss in FY2022 to a $305M profit in FY2025, and from negative free cash flow to $608M, while building a net-cash balance sheet of $1.7B. Q2 2026 was another clean quarter: revenue $1.91B (+23.1% YoY), a record 9,500 net new locations to ~180,000, ARR of $2.4B (+25%), GAAP net income of $154M and free cash flow of $130M (down 37.5% on a hardware inventory build). The share price has not followed: it is about 27% below its 52-week high and 54% below the IPO-day close, after a 14.7% drop on the Q1 print in May, a recovery to $37 in late August and a slide back to $29 since, with no new earnings in between.
Four distortions to keep in mind: (1) Early-loss years — FY2021-23 were loss-making (GAAP operating income -$228M, -$384M, -$287M), so EBIT and FCF CAGRs from FY2021 cannot be calculated and are marked n.m.; revenue CAGR is clean. (2) Gross revenue accounting — fintech revenue includes interchange passed through to card networks, so the 5% GAAP operating margin and 26% gross margin understate economics relative to software peers, and the 20% EBIT-margin bar is structurally hard to reach. (3) Stock-based compensation — $242M in FY2025 (3.9% of revenue) is deducted from GAAP EBIT but added back in free cash flow, which is why FCF is roughly twice EBIT. (4) Interest and tax — net income ($342M in FY2025) is helped by interest on ~$1.7-2.0B of cash and an effective tax rate of only ~2.4% (loss carry-forwards), so trailing P/E (37x) is not a clean read.

Growth Engine & Latest Quarter (Q2 2026)
The debate is no longer whether Toast can be profitable (it is), but how long 20%+ growth can last and how much of the new profit is durable. Q2 2026 was reported on Aug 4, 2026; Q3 2026 is expected around Nov 3, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was $1,908M (+23.1% YoY), gross profit $507M (+29.0%), GAAP operating income about $142M on a standardised basis (+75% YoY) and net income $154M (+93%). Adjusted EBITDA was $221M (+38%, including a roughly $10M one-time tariff refund) against a guided $185–195M, which more than reversed the shortfall that sent the stock down 14.7% on May 8 after Q1. Operating metrics were strong: ~180,000 locations (+22%, a record 9,500 net adds), ARR $2.4B (+25%), gross payment volume $60.7B (+22%) and recurring gross profit +28%. The soft spot was cash: free cash flow fell 37.5% to $130M because Toast built hardware inventory (TTM FCF is still $576M). The revenue step up from Q1's $1.63B is largely seasonal (Q2 2025 stepped up a similar 16% from Q1). Management raised FY2026 guidance to recurring gross profit growth of 23–25% and adjusted EBITDA of $805–825M, and guided Q3 to 22–24% recurring gross profit growth with adjusted EBITDA of $210–220M.

Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership
Trailing P/E is distorted by the loss-making history and a ~2.4% tax rate, so the scorecard uses price-to-free-cash-flow (the same approach as other GAAP-distorted names on this site). Toast has only three years of positive free cash flow, so its “own history” is short.
Price / Free Cash Flow — Year-End vs. Current
Current P/FCF of 29.1x (market cap $16.76B / TTM FCF $576M) is ~42% of the FY2023-25 year-end average (~69.4x). Caution: the average is inflated by FY2023 (106x on just $93M of FCF) and FY2024 (68x), when profits were only starting to arrive, so the discount flatters the signal. FY2021-22 had negative free cash flow and no meaningful multiple.

Share Count & Buybacks

Shares outstanding rose from ~505M at end-2021 to ~588M at end-2025 (+16%, mostly stock compensation) and have since fallen to ~578M (-2.3% YoY) as Toast began repurchasing stock: $56M in FY2024, $107M in FY2025 and $562M over the last twelve months. Net, the count is still up ~14.5% versus FY2021. No dividend is paid.

Founders, insiders & control: Toast has a dual-class structure: Class B shares carry ten votes each and are held by pre-IPO holders. From the April 2026 proxy, co-founder Jonathan Grimm holds ~11.6M Class B shares (10.1% of total voting power) and former CEO Chris Comparato ~8.4M (7.2%). Working back from those percentages implies Class B is only ~11% of shares outstanding (~64M of 578M, my estimate, approximate) but roughly half of votes. Aman Narang, a co-founder, has been CEO since January 2024. The “~16%” economic stake used for scoring is a data-provider figure and may include 10% holders and affiliates, so it is applied at the conservative end of its range.
Balance sheet: cash and investments of $1.71B (Jun 30, 2026, down from $1.99B at year-end after buybacks) against essentially no financial debt (total debt was $17M at Q1 2026 and none is reported for Q2); current ratio 2.4x. Net cash is ~10% of market cap. Short interest is ~5.5% of shares outstanding (6.5% of float). Net debt/EBITDA is therefore not meaningful in the usual sense and scores as net cash.

What's Working

Toast has scaled to $6.2B of revenue while flipping to profit: GAAP operating income of $305M in FY2025 versus -$384M in FY2022, free cash flow of $608M, a $1.7B net-cash balance sheet and capex under 1% of revenue. Operating momentum is still strong (record 9,500 net location adds, ARR +25% to $2.4B, GPV +22%), gross margin has widened by 7pp since FY2021, guidance was raised in August, and buybacks ($562M over twelve months) are starting to reverse years of dilution. The shares have de-rated from 47x forward earnings at end-2024 to ~19x while the business kept compounding, which puts the stock on a PEG of ~0.8.

What to Watch

Growth is decelerating (annual +28% to +24%, with 22–23% quarterly prints) and the GAAP operating margin is still only 5%, so the profit story depends on operating leverage continuing. Stock compensation of ~$242M a year is large relative to EBIT, share count is up ~14.5% since FY2021, and FCF conversion above 100% is partly a stock-compensation artifact. Revenue is tied to restaurant traffic and card volumes, competition from Square, Clover and new entrants (DoorDash's DashOS is being debated) is real, and the share price has fallen ~21% in five weeks without new results. Several inputs are approximations: the market-share tally, the ROIC/WACC proxy, the ~16% insider figure and the Class B share estimate. The two growth CAGRs are not calculable and rely on judgment.

Where Toast Stands

On growth, Toast sits between the card networks (11–15%) and Shopify (30%+): +24% a year is fast for a business with a real, profitable payments engine, and net cash of $1.7B gives it a balance sheet closer to Shopify's than to a levered fintech. It is the only one of the four whose annual growth is slowing, while Shopify is accelerating and Visa and Mastercard are steady. Entry multiple is the sharpest contrast: Toast trades on ~19x forward earnings and ~29x free cash flow after a large de-rating, whereas Shopify's dashboard flags a 70x P/FCF still priced for growth.

Where Toast Falls Short

Visa and Mastercard earn operating margins well above 20% with a duopoly position and almost no capex; Toast's GAAP margin is 5%, its market is fragmented and its share count is rising. Shopify has the same net-cash, capex-light profile with faster growth. Toast also carries the weakest data quality of the group for this scorecard: a short history of positive earnings, two CAGRs that cannot be computed and a valuation history of just three usable years. Treat 74 as a mid-to-high score with a wider error bar than the 83s, not as evidence that Toast is a better business than Visa.
