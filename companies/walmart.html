Q2 FY2027 Headlines

Revenue reached $177.4B, up 4.8% YoY (+5.6% on a constant-currency basis, an acceleration from the prior quarter's +4.0%). The two genuine growth engines behind the headline number are global e-commerce, up roughly 25% in the quarter, and advertising: Walmart's global ad business (Walmart Connect plus the newly-acquired VIZIO) grew 46% to nearly $6.4B in FY2026, with Walmart Connect itself up 41% in Q4 FY2026 alone. That places Walmart as the #2 retail media network in the US behind only Amazon — a genuinely high-margin, fast-growing business layered on top of the low-margin core.

Gross Margin Trend (FY2022-FY2026)
Gross margin dipped in FY2023 amid inventory/markdown pressure and inflation-driven cost absorption, then recovered and expanded every year since — 24.93% in FY2026 versus 24.14% in FY2023 (the window used for criterion #14), a +0.79pp improvement, not a deterioration.
Market position, honestly stated: Walmart is the #1 US grocer at roughly 20-21% share (per Numerator; down slightly from ~21.1% in 2024, as Costco and Amazon/Whole Foods gain share) and, by total revenue, the largest retailer in the US and in the world — roughly 6x Target's revenue and well ahead of Kroger, Costco and Amazon's retail operations on a total-sales basis. But US retail and grocery remain genuinely fragmented markets: no competitor holds more than about 8-9% share, meaning this is a dominant #1 in a competitive field, not a market with a tight oligopoly structure. Both are true at once: an unmatched scale advantage in physical retail and supply chain, operating in an industry where no single player (including Walmart) can lock out the field.

That re-rating means today's entry point is more expensive than Walmart's own recent history, not cheaper — see explainer below.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY22-26 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed. For Walmart specifically: both trailing (39.29x) and forward (36.17x) P/E sit above the stock's own FY22-26 average of roughly 34.84x, meaning the market has structurally re-rated Walmart higher over this window rather than the multiple compressing — a reflection of the market rewarding steady growth, defensive characteristics, and the advertising/e-commerce optionality described above. One more lens worth a glance: the PEG ratio, forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether a given multiple is cheap or expensive once growth is accounted for. Walmart's PEG currently runs well above 3x — meaningfully above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, underscoring that at today's price the stock is being valued more like a steady compounder/defensive holding than a name priced for its (modest, mid-single-digit) growth rate.

ROIC has ranged 11.97%-15.26% over the same window (14.33% in FY2026, 13.31% on a current/TTM basis) — it clears the assumed 7.5% WACC by a wide margin every year, but has only briefly touched this framework's flat 15% absolute bar (FY2025). What's Working

A fortress balance sheet (net debt/EBITDA of just 1.31x), an unmatched #1 market position in both US grocery and total retail revenue, five straight years of declining share count, a Walton family ownership stake (~44%) that's about as aligned as insider ownership gets anywhere, genuinely low capex intensity relative to revenue, and a stable-to-improving gross margin. Layered on top: Walmart Connect and the broader advertising business grew 46% to nearly $6.4B in FY2026, and global e-commerce is growing roughly 25% — real, high-margin growth engines inside a low-margin core business.

What to Watch

Every growth and margin criterion on this checklist misses its bar — but that's a structural feature of grocery-heavy discount retail at Walmart's scale, not a deteriorating trend: operating margin has been flat at 4.0%-4.5% for five years, and revenue/EBIT/FCF have all grown steadily, just far short of the 15% CAGR bars calibrated for higher-growth businesses. The more relevant watch item is valuation: at a trailing P/E of 39.29x versus Walmart's own FY22-26 average of roughly 34.84x, the market has already re-rated the stock meaningfully higher, so the margin of safety on an entry-multiple basis is thinner than it was a few years ago.
