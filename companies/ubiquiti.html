Ubiquiti Inc. designs and sells networking technology built around a software-driven, low-cost-to-serve model: it develops hardware and its UniFi software in-house, outsources manufacturing, sells mainly through distributors and online stores, and runs a very lean organisation (~1,800 employees, ~$1.8M revenue per employee). The Enterprise Technology segment (UniFi Wi-Fi, switching, cameras, access control, ~91% of FY2026 revenue of $3.27B) is the growth engine; Service Provider Technology (airMAX and related, ~9%) has been flat at $300-375M for four years. Revenue was $1.69B in FY2022, fell slightly in FY2024 after the pandemic hangover, then grew +33% in FY2025 and +27% in FY2026 as margins expanded from 26% to 36%. Q1 FY2027 (Sep 2026) was the first $1B quarter: revenue $1,026.2M (+39.9%), gross margin 47.3%, operating income $391.5M, GAAP EPS $5.29 (non-GAAP $5.32), operating cash flow $318.6M. Ubiquiti gave no numerical guidance and cautioned that component costs have risen since June and availability may stay constrained. The shares have been volatile: from a $1,099.99 high in April 2026 to $500.23 in September, then back to $685.68 after the quarter. Founder Robert Pera controls ~93% of the company, which aligns his interests with the business but leaves a free float of only ~7%.
Risks that sit outside the scorecard: (1) Allegation and legal headlines — in early 2026 a Hunterbrook report alleged Ubiquiti gear is used in Russian military communications, and law-firm “investigation” notices followed; an Ax Wireless patent complaint was filed at the US ITC on Feb 2, 2026. These are allegations or pending proceedings; I did not verify outcomes or the company's responses. (2) Component costs and supply flagged by management. (3) Concentrated control — one person holds ~93% of the equity.

What the quarter actually showed

Revenue reached a record $1,026.2M, up 39.9% YoY and 9.5% on the prior quarter, the first quarter above $1 billion. Enterprise Technology revenue was $941.3M (+43.2%); Service Provider Technology $84.9M (+10.8%). Gross margin rose to 47.3% (46.0% a year ago, 45.8% in Q4 FY2026) on lower indirect costs, partly offset by higher component costs. Operating income was $391.5M (38.2% margin, +49.6%), GAAP net income $320.5M and GAAP EPS $5.29 (non-GAAP $5.32). Operating cash flow was $318.6M and free cash flow $312.4M, lifting net cash to $770M as the company now holds more cash ($845M) than debt ($76M). Management gave no numerical guidance and said component costs have risen since Q4 FY2026 and may continue to rise, with availability possibly constrained, a margin risk. The stock rose ~15.3% on Nov 6 and a further ~15.7% on Nov 9, 2026.

What's Working

Growth and profitability are exceptional for a hardware company: revenue +27% in FY2026 and +39.9% in the latest quarter, a record $1B quarter, operating margin up from 26% to 38% in about two years, gross margin up 7pp, ROIC above 100%, 0.6% capex intensity and a swing from $1.0B net debt to $770M net cash. The quarterly dividend has been raised three times since mid-2025. The founder owns ~93% of the shares, so interests are strongly aligned with the long-term health of the business.

What to Watch

Control and liquidity: ~93% insider ownership leaves a ~7% free float, thin trading and 10-15% single-day swings, and minority holders have no governance leverage. Management warned that component costs are rising, which could pressure the 47% gross margin that drove the earnings jump. The stock trades ~21% above its own 5-year average P/E after a 33% two-day rally, FCF conversion is below 80% and swings with inventory (FY2023 was negative), and market position is mid-tier outside SMB/prosumer. Allegations about Russian military use of its products, a patent complaint at the ITC and law-firm investigation notices add headline risk; outcomes were not verified here.
