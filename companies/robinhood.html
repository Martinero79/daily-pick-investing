Robinhood runs a mobile-first brokerage for U.S. retail investors, with equities, options, crypto, event (prediction-market) contracts, a Gold subscription, retirement accounts and a credit card. Revenue grew from $1.82bn in FY2021 to $4.47bn in FY2025 (~25% a year), and the business swung from a $1.6bn operating loss to a $2.1bn operating profit, a 46.8% margin. That profit record is short: FY2023 was still loss-making (net loss $541M), quarterly profits only became consistent from Q4 2023, and FY2024 net income of $1.41bn was helped by a tax benefit (net income exceeded operating income by ~$360M, which is consistent with a one-off valuation-allowance release). Everything scored on a multi-year basis (EBIT and FCF CAGR, valuation history, ROE) therefore rests on two full profitable years plus 2026 to date. Revenue is also cyclical and concentrated: transaction-based lines (options, crypto, equities, event contracts) were 61% of Q3 2026 revenue and net interest 29%. Crypto revenue fell 55% YoY to $120M in Q3 2026 (from $268M), net interest revenue fell 9% as rates eased, and operating expenses grew 16%, so Q3 net income fell 16% even as revenue rose 12%. The stock fell ~25% in October 2026 and is down from a $139.75 intraday high in Dec 2025.
Framing note — why this scorecard is adapted for a broker with a short profit history: Robinhood is a financial company whose cash flow is dominated by customer cash, margin and fractional-share flows, so the standard industrial framework is adapted, with each substitution flagged inline: total net revenues stand in for revenue; operating income is used as EBIT, but its FY21 base is negative, so criteria 2 (EBIT CAGR) and 4 (FCF CAGR) are N/A and their weights (11 + 8 = 19) are dropped from the Martinero denominator (105 − 19 = 86); ROE vs. an assumed ~13.5% cost of equity replaces ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7); net cash / capital position replaces net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8; no CET1 is reported); (dividends + buybacks) ÷ net income replaces FCF conversion (criterion 9); criterion 10 uses an 8-quarter own-history average because no 5-year P/E record exists; and criterion 14 uses the operating (efficiency) margin because there is no gross-profit line.

Quarterly Trend & Revenue Mix (Q3 2026)
Q3 2026 (quarter to Sep 30) was reported on Oct 27, 2026 and is the latest available. Source: company Q3 2026 release plus S&P Global data for earlier quarters.
What the quarter actually showed

Q3 2026 net revenues were a record $1.42B (+12% YoY, +9% vs Q2), but total operating expenses rose 16% to $739M (provisions for credit losses on the growing credit-card book, spending on Trump Accounts and Rothera, marketing), so operating income was $685M (48.1% margin) and net income $468M ($0.51 per share), down 16% from $556M. Adjusted EBITDA was $808M (+9%, 57% margin). The revenue mix moved sharply: options $399M (+31%), event contracts $173M (from $27M), equities $121M (+41%), while cryptocurrency revenue fell 55% to $120M and net interest revenue fell 9% to $415M. Platform assets reached $396B (+19%), net deposits were $15.7B (17% annualised) and TTM net deposits $71.0B; Gold subscribers rose 34% to 5.2M and funded customers 7% to 28.7M. Buybacks were only $25M in the quarter (~0.2M shares at ~$105; $689M year-to-date). The stock closed at $83.32 on Nov 9, 2026, down from ~$112.5 at end-September.

Governance & Capital Notes

Robinhood has a dual-class structure: Class A (about 791M shares) trades; Class B (high-vote, held by the co-founders) makes up the rest of the ~899M total. Insiders hold ~12.6% of shares per S&P Global, which clears the 5% bar, but their voting share was not verified here. The stock is volatile (beta ~2.35; down ~31% over 52 weeks as of Oct 27, short interest ~4.5% of shares). Regulatory and business-model risks include payment-for-order-flow and crypto/prediction-market rule changes and the credit-card book’s loss provisions.

What's Working

Revenue has compounded ~25% a year since FY21, the business is now solidly profitable (46.8% operating margin, ~24% ROE) and capex is ~0.3% of revenue. Platform assets ($396B, +19%), funded customers (28.7M) and Gold subscribers (5.2M, +34%) keep growing; $71B of net deposits over twelve months funds future activity. Product breadth (options, event contracts, credit card, retirement, crypto) is diversifying the mix, buybacks have roughly offset dilution, and founders hold ~12.6% of shares.

What to Watch

The profit record is short (profitable only since FY24; FY24 helped by a tax benefit), so ROE, margin and valuation comparisons rest on very little history. Q3 2026 net income fell 16% as operating expenses (+16%) outgrew revenue (+12%); crypto revenue fell 55% and net interest revenue 9%. Revenue is activity- and rate-sensitive. Debt has risen sharply ($3.7B to $22.6B) with the credit-card and funding books, the market position is mid-pack by assets, and the stock is volatile (beta ~2.35; fell ~25% in October 2026). Analyst targets (average ~$135) pre-date the Q3 print.
