Market position: FICO Scores are the dominant US consumer credit score used in lending decisions, sold through the three credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) and, since 2025, increasingly via direct licences. The Software segment (fraud, decisioning, the FICO Platform) is the smaller, slower-growing half of the business.
Why the stock de-rated (the key issue): FY2026 was a record year (revenue +27%, EPS +37%), yet the shares fell from a 52-week high of $1,858.91 to $723.76 and the trailing P/E from ~52x (FY21-25 average) to ~20x, because the profit engine — mortgage credit scores sold at rapidly rising prices — is now being challenged by the US housing regulator. In September 2026 FHFA director Bill Pulte let Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac lenders use VantageScore 4.0 (stock -16.7% on Sep 4) and then put VantageScore on the same pricing grid as FICO Classic (stock -26.5% on Sep 29), removing lenders' main cost reason to stay with FICO; the bureau-owned VantageScore is priced at $0.99 versus FICO's ~$10. On Oct 26 the shares fell another 13% after FICO held its mortgage price at $10 for 2027 rather than raising it and Pulte promised “more action”, and on Nov 4 FICO gave no FY2027 guidance. Fair Isaac Corporation (FICO) is a US analytics company founded in 1956 that owns the FICO Score, the credit score most US lenders use to price consumer credit, plus a decisioning and fraud-detection Software business. It has two segments. Scores (65% of FY2026 revenue, $1.65B) earns royalties each time a lender pulls a FICO Score, mostly via the credit bureaus; the largest and fastest-growing piece is US mortgage origination, where unit prices rose sharply (mortgage-origination score revenue grew 72-127% a quarter in FY2026 even as mortgage volumes fell). Software (35%, $883M) sells analytics and the cloud FICO Platform; total ARR was up 14% at September 30, 2026 with platform ARR +72% to $453M while legacy non-platform ARR fell 18%. Revenue has grown from $1.32B (FY2021) to $2.53B (FY2026), operating margin from 38% to 50%, and the share count has fallen about 21% on buybacks that were increasingly financed by debt: FY2026 repurchases of $3.05B were nearly three times FCF, pushing net debt to $5.0B (3.9x EBITDA) and stockholders' equity to -$3.87B. The shares, meanwhile, have lost about 61% from their 52-week high after the regulatory and competitive developments described above.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Growth is price-led. FICO itself attributes the Scores B2B growth “primarily” to higher mortgage origination score unit prices, partly offset by lower mortgage volumes; FICO does not report mortgage revenue as its own line, so the exact dependence on mortgage origination cannot be quantified from its releases. (2) Backward-looking financials. Every number on this page up to FY2026 was earned under the old pricing; what 2027 pricing will look like is explicitly unknown (no guidance, and FICO will no longer disclose mortgage pricing terms). (3) Leverage amplifies both ways. A smaller share count helps EPS per share, but interest expense ($166M of net other expense in FY2026, up from $122M) and a negative equity base leave less room if Scores revenue stalls.

The consequential part was the outlook: FICO is not providing FY2027 guidance, citing the transition to “bespoke mortgage originations pricing”, still-elevated rates and uncertain mortgage markets.

Share Count & Buybacks

Diluted weighted shares fell from 29.3M (FY2021) to 23.0M (FY2026), -21.4%, and the Q4 FY2026 diluted count was 21.6M (vs 24.2M a year earlier). The reduction was increasingly debt-funded. Buybacks are paused while the term loan tied to the accelerated share repurchase is repaid, and FICO said professional-services revenue (already down to $19.5M in Q4) will fall further in FY2027, so the share-count tailwind to EPS per share fades from here. No dividend is paid.

Leverage, calculated: Sep 30, 2026 debt = current maturities $337.5M + long-term debt $4,921.6M = $5,259.1M (carrying value); cash $217.6M; net debt $5,041.5M. EBITDA (GAAP operating income $1,265.7M + depreciation & amortisation $16.7M) = $1,282.4M, so net debt/EBITDA = 3.9x. Adding back stock-based compensation ($185.5M) gives 3.4x, and also adding the $92M of one-offs 3.2x (a third-party summary quoted 3.29x on its own basis). All variants sit in the 3-4x band. Gross debt is $5.26B vs a market capitalisation of ~$15.2B.
Negative equity and ROIC, handled honestly: stockholders' equity is -$3.87B because buybacks ($7.7B in six years) have exceeded cumulative profits; it is a financing choice, not a sign of losses. ROIC can still be computed on operating capital rather than equity: invested capital (debt + equity − cash) ≈ $1.17B (goodwill $0.79B plus working capital) and NOPAT ≈ $961M (operating income $1,266M × (1 − 24.1% tax rate)), giving ~82%. That figure is genuine for an asset-light royalty business but is artificially high because buybacks shrank the capital base. As a stress test, adding all FY21-26 buybacks ($7.7B) back as capital gives ~11%, against an estimated WACC of ~9-10% (my estimate, not a sourced figure), a thin spread. The scorecard therefore scores ROIC 4/5 rather than the mechanical 5/5, flagged as a judgment.

What's Working

Exceptional unit economics: 86% gross margin, ~50% GAAP operating margin and ~$1.0B of FCF on $2.5B of revenue with capex at 1.5% of sales. FY2026 revenue grew 27% and EPS 37%, the Software platform is accelerating (ARR +14%, platform ARR +72%, platform net retention 156%), direct-licence agreements now cover ~73% of reseller volume, the FICO Preferred Program has signed a growing list of large lenders (FICO has listed Guild, New American Funding, PennyMac, DHI Mortgage, loanDepot, CMG Financial, Movement and Rate in Oct-Nov 2026), and the FHA will accept FICO 10T from Jan 1, 2027. The share count is down ~21% since FY2021, and at ~20x trailing GAAP earnings the multiple is well below FICO's own ~52x five-year average. Operating cash generation was unambiguous: $1.07B of operating cash flow in FY2026.

What to Watch — Why the Stock De-Rated
  • The core issue: mortgage-score pricing is now contested by the regulator. FICO's growth was price-led (B2B growth “primarily” higher mortgage unit price). In 2026 FHFA director Bill Pulte, who oversees Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, moved to open the mortgage market to bureau-owned VantageScore.
  • Timeline (price moves are closes): Apr 22: Bloomberg reports Pulte moving to cut credit-score costs, shares slump; Apr 30: analyst downgrade, -6.9%. Sep 4: -16.7% ($1,118.93 to $932.26) as Pulte said Fannie/Freddie securitisations could use VantageScore 4.0 (or both). Sep 29: -26.5% ($840.89 to $617.87) after Pulte announced a single pricing grid with VantageScore alongside FICO Classic (previously VantageScore loans carried higher pricing), TransUnion fixed VantageScore 4.0 at $0.99 through Dec 2028, and Rocket Mortgage (largest originator) said it would default to VantageScore 4.0 in Q4 on Fannie/Freddie/VA loans. Sep 30 close $592.47 (intraday low $586.05). Oct 23-26: -13.2% ($815.31 to $708.07) after FICO held its mortgage score at $10 for lenders in its Preferred Program (no 2027 increase; bundled with 10T), Pulte promised “more action”, Wells Fargo downgraded and BMO ($897), JPMorgan ($900), UBS ($942) and Mizuho ($986) cut targets.
  • Direct-licence and price history: FICO roughly doubled score prices since the end of Trump's first term in Jan 2021 (Motley Fool) and, per press reports, lifted the mortgage score to ~$10 from $4.95; VantageScore is $0.99. Lenders, bureaus and the regulator now treat that gap as the lever. FICO is moving to “bespoke” pricing and will no longer disclose mortgage pricing terms.
  • Regulatory/legal: $65M accrual (Q4) for a proposed settlement of antitrust class actions over Scores pricing; reports of a Senate inquiry into 2026 pricing (single secondary source, not verified).
  • No FY2027 guidance (Nov 4); Scores growth already decelerating (+60% Q2, +41% Q3, +33% Q4); mortgage volumes fell high-single digits in Q4; any loss of share or price in mortgage hits the highest-margin revenue in the company. Scores is 65% of revenue and most of the profit, mortgage dependence cannot be quantified from FICO's releases.
  • Leverage on a debt-funded buyback: net debt $5.0B (3.9x EBITDA), negative equity -$3.87B, interest cost rising, buybacks paused; FY2026 repurchases ($3.05B) were made at prices far above today's ($1,100-$1,850 range during the year) and EPS-per-share support from buybacks is ending.
  • Unverified/caveats: insider holdings, post-Q4 analyst consensus and forward EPS could not be verified; reported single-day drops differ by source (e.g. -27% intraday vs -26.5% close on Sep 29).
