Nestlé is the weakest scorecard in the GRANOLAS batch so far, and honestly so — this isn't a case of a one-off accounting distortion masking a strong underlying business, the way GSK's Haleon gain or Roche's writedown year did. Revenue has declined for four straight years (CHF 94.8B in FY2022 to CHF 89.9B in FY2025), operating margin has sat in the 15-17% range the entire window without ever reaching this framework's 20% bar, and net debt/EBITDA at 3.10x is the highest leverage seen in this batch. Free cash flow is a genuine bright spot — up from CHF 6.8B in FY2022 to CHF 11.4B in FY2025, aided by the "Fuel for Growth" cost program — and the company remains, unambiguously, the world's largest food & beverage business by revenue. Gross margin has held roughly flat (45.41% to 45.83% over the past three years), neither expanding nor eroding. Ownership is genuinely dispersed: no founder, no controlling family, and Nestlé's own Articles of Association cap any single shareholder's voting power at 5%. H1 2026 results showed real, if uneven, progress: organic growth accelerated to 3.6% (from 2.9% a year earlier) on stronger volumes, even as reported net profit fell 31.4% on FX and one-off portfolio-reshaping costs.

Half-Year Development — Latest Results
Nestlé reports semi-annually, not quarterly, for detailed P&L figures. Latest reported period: H1 2026 (six months to June 30, 2026, reported July 23, 2026).
H1 2026 Headlines

Organic growth accelerated to 3.6% (from 2.9% in H1 2025), with real internal growth (volume) up to 1.5% from 0.2% — a genuine, if early, sign that the volume-led turnaround strategy is gaining traction. Reported sales were CHF 43.1B, down slightly from CHF 44.2B in H1 2025 as currency and portfolio effects offset the organic gain. Underlying trading operating profit (UTOP) margin was 16.4%, roughly flat year-over-year (16.5% in H1 2025) despite higher marketing investment. Net profit fell sharply to CHF 3.5B from CHF 5.1B (−31.4%), reflecting one-off portfolio-reshaping costs and the absence of prior-year gains, not a deterioration in the underlying operating trend. Free cash flow rose to CHF 3.4B. Emerging markets (ex-China) delivered organic growth of 7.1%; developed markets grew a more modest 2.3%. Nutrition was the one major category to decline (−1.2%), still weighed down by an ongoing infant-formula recall, though the drag eased through the second quarter.

Half-Year Group Sales (CHF B)
Reported sales dipped slightly year-over-year even as organic growth (the volume-and-price measure stripping out FX and portfolio changes) accelerated — a similar currency-and-portfolio story to Roche's, though for Nestlé the effect is compounded by the ongoing divestment of Waters, VMS, and ice cream businesses.

The full FY2021-FY2025 annual series on page 2 remains the primary growth reference.
Portfolio reshaping in progress: Nestlé announced a 50:50 joint venture for its Waters & Premium Beverages business (expected to generate ≈CHF 2.8B net cash proceeds in H1 2027), and has classified its mainstream Vitamins/Minerals/Supplements business and its ice cream division as held for sale — part of a broader effort to focus on four core areas (Food & Snacks, Coffee, Nutrition, Petcare) under new CEO Philipp Navratil. This is a real structural narrowing of the business, not a distortion, but it will keep near-term reported figures noisy.

Current, trailing & forward P/E: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. The trailing P/E series above is that same backward-looking ratio at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated Nestlé relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's price divided by consensus estimates of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and it captures expected growth that trailing P/E can't see. Nestlé's forward P/E of 16.91x is meaningfully cheaper than its 27.29x trailing P/E, implying the market expects a real earnings recovery from a currently depressed profit base — the H1 2026 volume-recovery story consensus is betting on. One more lens worth a glance: the PEG ratio, forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth. Analysts pencil in a modest ~5% EPS growth rate for Nestlé over the next three years, putting the forward-P/E-implied PEG around 3.4 — well above the traditional 1.0 fair-value line, a reminder that even at a discounted forward multiple, Nestlé isn't priced as a bargain once its slow underlying growth is accounted for.
Balance sheet: Nestlé held roughly CHF 57.9B in total debt against CHF 6.2B of cash & investments as of FY2025-end (Dec 31, 2025) — a net debt position of about CHF 51.6B, or 3.10x EBITDA (3.45x on a current/TTM basis). This is real, meaningfully elevated leverage for a consumer staples company — the highest in this batch so far — though it remains well covered by Nestlé's substantial and growing free cash flow.

What's Working

Nestlé remains the unambiguous global #1 in food & beverages, with genuinely broad, diversified exposure across coffee, nutrition, petcare, and confectionery. Free cash flow has grown meaningfully (+6.08% CAGR) and FCF conversion clears this framework's 80% bar, aided by real cost discipline through the "Fuel for Growth" program. The share count has declined a real ≈14% over the period, capex intensity is genuinely low, and H1 2026 showed the clearest sign yet of an operational turnaround — organic growth accelerating to 3.6% on improving volumes rather than just price increases.

What to Watch

This is a real, structural growth and margin problem, not a one-off distortion — revenue has declined for four straight years since FY2022, operating margin has never once reached 20% across this five-year window, and net debt/EBITDA at 3.10x is the highest leverage in this GRANOLAS batch so far. Ownership is genuinely dispersed with no founder or controlling family to provide long-term alignment. An ongoing infant-formula recall continues to weigh on the Nutrition segment, and the current 27.29x P/E sits meaningfully (≈18%) above Nestlé's own five-year average, a rich entry point for a business still mid-turnaround. Substantial portfolio restructuring is underway (Waters JV, VMS and ice cream held for sale), which is a real strategic narrowing but will keep reported figures noisy for several more quarters.
