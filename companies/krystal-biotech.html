Krystal Biotech, Inc. was founded by Krish Krishnan (Chairman and CEO) and Suma Krishnan (President, R&D), listed on Nasdaq in September 2017 and, until 2023, was a pre-revenue biotech burning $100M or more a year (FCF -$116M, -$154M and -$101M in FY2021-23). VYJUVEK changed that: net product revenue went from $50.7M in FY2023 to $290.5M in FY2024 and $389.1M in FY2025, with a gross margin of about 94%, a 41% GAAP operating margin in FY2025 and 48% over the last twelve months. The business now generates about $300M of annual free cash flow and sits on $1.2B of cash and investments with essentially no debt. Q3 2026 revenue was $127.3M (+30% YoY; $95.3M US, $32.0M international), with over 760 US reimbursement approvals and launches continuing in Germany, France and Japan, pricing completed in Germany and Italy (Italy launch early 2027) and a marketing application filed in Australia. Despite a beat on revenue and EPS ($2.05 vs about $1.84 consensus), the stock fell 10.95% on Nov 2 as several pipeline timelines slipped into 2027 (KB803 eye study, KB801 keratitis, KB407 cystic fibrosis interim data); it closed at $298.55 on Nov 17, about 22% below its 52-week high of $382.54. Read the numbers with care: this is a single-product company (all revenue is VYJUVEK, a rare-disease therapy for a small patient population), FY2025 net income included a tax benefit (a valuation-allowance release in Q3 2025), and the 5-year growth criteria cannot be scored because the company only began selling in late 2023.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Concentration — 100% of product revenue comes from VYJUVEK; pricing, reimbursement, patient-finding and competition in DEB (and any safety or supply event) hit the whole company, and the pipeline (about 8 programmes) has not yet produced a second revenue source. (2) Short history — only three years of revenue (two full years), so growth rates are launch-ramp rates and valuation history is thin. (3) Founder ownership — the Krishnans hold roughly 11-12% (third-party / 13G-derived), a meaningful but not controlling stake, and they have sold some shares under 10b5-1 plans.

What moved the stock

Q3 2026 was a modest beat: net product revenue $127.3M (+30% YoY; consensus about $126.8M), GAAP EPS $2.05 against about $1.84 expected, operating income $71.1M (55.9% margin) and cash and investments of $1.19B. The shares nevertheless fell 10.95% on Nov 2 to $295.90 (traded as low as $281.40), and several brokers trimmed targets in the following days. I did not verify the cause, but third-party coverage points to pipeline timing: the KB803 eye study's last patient visit moved to February 2027, the KB801 (neurotrophic keratitis) enrolment is slower than planned with top-line results now in 2027, and KB407 (cystic fibrosis) interim data moved to 1H 2027. The Q3 tax charge ($17.1M vs a $31.4M benefit a year earlier) also made net income fall 20.5% YoY. Offsetting positives: a $400M share-repurchase authorisation (over about two years, opportunistic), pricing completed in Germany and Italy, and positive KB113 proof-of-concept data in Gorlin syndrome (Oct 15). Earlier, the Q2 print (Aug 3) was a miss (revenue $119.2M vs about $121.0M, EPS $1.79 vs $1.91) and the stock fell about 8%.

What's Working

A rare example of a recently launched gene therapy that is already highly profitable: 94% gross margin, 41% GAAP operating margin in FY2025 (48% TTM), $189M of FY2025 FCF (117% of EBIT) and $1.19B of cash with no meaningful debt. Revenue grew 30% YoY in Q3 2026 with a re-acceleration through 2026, international sales reached $32M a quarter, and operating expenses have been flat while revenue scaled. The founders still lead the company, hold about 11-12% and have built a pipeline in dermatology, ophthalmology, respiratory and inhaled oncology funded entirely from VYJUVEK cash flow. A $400M buyback was authorised and the stock has pulled back about 22% from its 52-week high while earnings keep rising.

What to Watch

Single-product concentration: all revenue is VYJUVEK for DEB, a small patient population, so growth depends on finding new patients, international pricing and reimbursement (Germany pricing was a drag in 2026 coverage; France not before 2027) and defending against competing DEB therapies. The pipeline has no second approved product and several readouts slipped into 2027, which is what the stock reacted to on Nov 2. Dilution of about 33% since 2021 (although slowing); stock compensation of $54.5M a year; FY2025 net income was flattered by a tax benefit and the tax rate is now rising (21% in Q3 2026). Valuation is not low (about 40x trailing GAAP and about 36x forward earnings, about 20x sales) and criteria scoring rests on a short track record.
