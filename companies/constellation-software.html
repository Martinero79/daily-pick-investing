Constellation Software is a Toronto-based, TSX-listed company that acquires, builds and permanently holds "vertical market software" (VMS) businesses — mission-critical, niche B2B software for narrow industries that are rarely glamorous and almost never covered by Wall Street: library systems, funeral-home management, public-transit dispatch, utilities billing, hospitality point-of-sale, and hundreds of others. Founded by Mark Leonard, who ran it with a famously unusual, radically decentralized operating philosophy (hundreds of operating groups and business units, each run by its own GM, judged on return on invested capital, and left alone as long as it performs), CSU has compounded revenue at roughly 20%+ per year for two decades by buying small, entrenched software companies at disciplined prices and never selling them — the opposite of a typical roll-up. It later spun out two of its own operating groups, Topicus.com and Lumine Group, as separately listed companies using the same playbook, effectively franchising the model. The stock's defining 2025-26 event has nothing to do with the underlying business breaking: shares fell roughly 45% from a May 2025 high near CAD 5,060-5,180 to a January 2026 low of CAD 2,196, driven by two things happening at once — a market-wide "SaaSpocalypse" repricing of vertical-market and enterprise software stocks on fears that AI coding tools would erode moats and pricing power across the industry, and Mark Leonard's resignation as President in late 2025 for health reasons, which raised real (if largely unanswered) questions about succession at a company whose culture was built almost entirely around one person's discipline. Reported fundamentals did not confirm the panic: FY2025 revenue grew 15.5% and free cash flow grew over 25%, and Q2 2026 revenue (USD 3,335M vs USD 2,844M a year earlier) kept growing at a similar pace under new President Mark Miller, a 30-year CSU veteran. This is a name for people comfortable owning a real, proven, diversified compounder through a genuine valuation reset and a real leadership transition — not a distressed business, but not a risk-free one either.
The "SaaSpocalypse," in plain terms: through 2025, AI coding assistants and "vibe coding" tools sparked a genuine, industry-wide fear that vertical-market and enterprise software moats — long defended by switching costs and integration depth rather than raw code complexity — could erode as competitors, or even customers, started building bespoke software faster and cheaper. CSU, as the largest and most-followed pure-play VMS compounder, became a lightning rod for that fear even though its own AI commentary has stressed adoption as a development-productivity tool rather than a threat to its acquired businesses' entrenched customer relationships. The panic reset the stock's multiple hard; it did not show up in the reported numbers.
Leadership transition, current status: Mark Leonard resigned as President in September/October 2025 for health reasons; the board appointed Mark Miller — CSU's longtime COO and co-founder of Trapeze Group, the company CSU acquired in 1995 to start the whole strategy — as his successor. In March 2026, Leonard announced he will not stand for re-election to the board, with his term concluding at the May 15, 2026 annual meeting, but he will continue as an advisor focused on the "Permanent Engaged Minority Shareholder" (PEMS) strategy, a newer initiative around selective, long-term minority stakes alongside CSU's traditional full-control acquisitions. Read this as a real governance change to monitor, not a settled non-issue — but also not a founder cutting ties; Leonard is staying involved in an advisory capacity.

The 2025-26 Repricing & Leadership Transition
Two real, overlapping stories — a market-wide software valuation reset, and a genuine founder succession — landed on the stock at the same time. Neither shows up in the reported numbers below.
The stock move, in plain terms

CSU shares peaked around CAD 5,060-5,180 in May 2025, then fell roughly 45-57% to a January 2026 low of CAD 2,196 as AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" fears about software-moat erosion swept through vertical-market and enterprise software valuations broadly, compounding with uncertainty after Mark Leonard's health-related resignation as President. The stock has since partially recovered to CAD 2,785 (Sep 24, 2026), still down roughly 45% from the peak and about 34% over the trailing 12 months. Over the same window, FY2025 revenue grew 15.5%, FY2025 FCF grew over 25%, and the company kept deploying capital into acquisitions (reportedly ~USD 1.6 billion in Q1 2026 alone) — a large, visible gap between what the market priced and what the business actually did.

Succession detail: Mark Leonard resigned as President in Sep/Oct 2025 citing health reasons. The board appointed Mark Miller — COO and a 30+ year CSU veteran who co-founded Trapeze Group, the transit-software business CSU acquired in 1995 to launch the entire strategy — as his successor. Early public commentary (including from Miller himself) has emphasized continuity with Leonard's playbook rather than a strategic pivot.
What to actually watch here: CSU's culture and capital-allocation discipline were built almost entirely around one person over three decades — a real, non-trivial key-person risk even with a credible internal successor and a decentralized operating structure designed to survive exactly this kind of transition. This is genuinely different from a normal "CEO retires, business unaffected" story, and it's a fair part of why the stock derated as hard as it did. Watch capital-deployment discipline (acquisition pace and pricing) and organic growth commentary over the next several quarters as the clearest read on whether the culture survived the handoff intact.

Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership
Unlike many names on this site, CSU has always had positive, meaningful earnings — trailing and forward P/E are both directly usable, and both currently sit far below the company's own 5-year average.
Trailing P/E — FY-End vs. Current
Current trailing P/E (~44.2x) sits at roughly 47% of CSU's own FY2021-25 average (~94.8x) — a large discount to its own trading history, driven by the 2025-26 selloff rather than by any earnings shortfall (TTM net income is actually up sharply, to USD 961m).

Forward P/E & PEG: forward P/E (next-12-month consensus) is currently ~15.5x, versus a FY21-25 average of ~31.6x — an even sharper discount than the trailing multiple, reflecting analysts' expectation of continued strong earnings growth from here. PEG ratio is ~0.95x (forward P/E ÷ forecast EPS growth), below 1.0x — on a growth-adjusted basis this reads as inexpensive for a business of this quality, not just "cheap because it fell." The 12-analyst consensus price target of CAD 3,961 implies ~42% upside from the current price, though note only 12 analysts cover the name and CSU management does not issue formal earnings guidance, so estimates carry real dispersion.

Shares outstanding have sat at essentially 21.0-21.4m for years, currently 21.19m — no meaningful dilution, and no active large-scale buyback either. This is "flat by discipline," consistent with a company that funds growth via retained cash flow and debt rather than issuing equity.

All three are meaningfully smaller, as a percentage, than the 20-40%+ founder stakes seen elsewhere on this site (e.g. Oracle's Larry Ellison at ~40%) — but in absolute dollar terms it's still a large, real personal stake, and Leonard's continuing advisory role on the PEMS strategy keeps him economically and operationally engaged even as he steps back from day-to-day leadership and the board.

Note on criteria #5/#6 — scoring market share for a diversified portfolio: Constellation isn't a company that competes in "a market" — it's a permanent holding company for roughly 1,000+ independently-run software businesses across public sector, healthcare, hospitality, agriculture, real estate, and dozens of other verticals, none individually material to the group. Mark Leonard's stated acquisition discipline has long targeted businesses that already hold a dominant, entrenched position in their specific niche (deep integration, high switching costs, often only one or two credible vendors serving that exact niche) — so at the level of any individual acquired business, "#1 or #2 in a tight, concentrated market" is usually true and is a real, structural source of CSU's returns. But scoring the parent entity's overall "market concentration" on a single 1-5 scale, as this checklist does for a single-product or single-industry company, doesn't honestly capture that structure — CSU's real moat is breadth and diversification across hundreds of small monopolies/duopolies, not dominance of one large market. Criterion 5 is scored Met on that qualitative, portfolio-level basis; criterion 6 is scored conservatively as Not Met because forcing a single concentration number onto a portfolio this fragmented would overstate the case either way.
What's Working

A genuinely rare combination at this scale: revenue, EBIT and FCF all compounding above 20% annually for four straight years, an elite and stable ROIC-vs-WACC spread (~12pp), essentially no share dilution, structurally low capex, improving margins, and — after the 2025-26 selloff — a trailing and forward P/E both sitting at roughly half of the company's own 5-year average despite TTM earnings actually being up sharply. The reported fundamentals (15.5% FY2025 revenue growth, 25%+ FCF growth, continued ~17% revenue growth into Q2 2026) never confirmed the AI-fear narrative that drove the stock down 45% from its 2025 highs, and CSU kept deploying capital into acquisitions (~USD 1.6bn in Q1 2026 alone) straight through the drawdown — exactly what a disciplined, permanent-capital allocator is supposed to do when its own stock and its acquisition targets both get cheaper.

What to Watch

This is a genuine leadership transition, not a manufactured worry: Mark Leonard resigned as President for health reasons in late 2025 and is leaving the board entirely in May 2026, handing a culture built around one person's capital-allocation discipline to successor Mark Miller and a more conventional governance structure. Founder/insider ownership, while real in dollar terms, is modest as a percentage relative to other founder-led names on this site. Operating margin (16.6%) still sits below this site's 20% bar, gross leverage is rising as more M&A gets debt-funded (even though net leverage remains comfortable), and CSU's famous lack of formal earnings guidance means the market is pricing a continuation of the historical playbook with limited near-term visibility into whether the post-Leonard culture holds.
