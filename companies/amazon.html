Amazon's scorecard tells an honest, two-sided story. It's the clear #1 in US e-commerce by a wide margin, operating income has grown explosively off a depressed FY2021 base (that year's aggressive fulfillment-capacity overbuild crushed margins), and the balance sheet remains manageable despite a genuine net-debt position. But the current AI infrastructure buildout is a real, measurable drag right now: FY2025 capex hit $131B, pulling capex intensity well above this framework's bar and collapsing free cash flow 71% year-over-year to just $11.2B. ROIC has actually dipped below WACC on a trailing basis — a signal worth taking seriously, even though AWS itself remains highly profitable (39%+ segment operating margin). This is a business investing very heavily for a future payoff that hasn't fully shown up in the numbers yet.

What's Working

Amazon holds a commanding, durable lead in US e-commerce and continues extending it even as competitors like Walmart post strong online growth of their own. AWS remains the profit engine, growing 37% year-over-year with a 39%+ segment margin, and its AI/chips business more than doubled in the latest quarter. Operating income has recovered dramatically from the FY2022 low, the balance sheet remains manageable, the founder retains a meaningful stake, and today's multiple sits modestly below its own recent trading range.

What to Watch

The core tension right now is capital intensity: FY2025 capex hit $131B and FY2026 is tracking higher still, which has pushed free cash flow down 71% and pulled ROIC below WACC on a trailing basis. Thin overall operating margins (11.2%) reflect the low-margin retail business diluting AWS's much stronger economics. None of this signals distress — AWS's own numbers are excellent — but it does mean today's Amazon is spending very heavily on a bet that hasn't fully paid off in the reported numbers yet, and that's a real, ongoing risk to underwrite.
