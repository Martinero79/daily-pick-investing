Hewlett Packard Enterprise was split off from Hewlett-Packard in 2015 and is led by CEO Antonio Neri. It sells servers (including AI systems), storage and the GreenLake hybrid-cloud platform, finances customer purchases through HPE Financial Services, and, since Juniper, runs a large networking business (Aruba campus and Wi-Fi, Juniper routing, security and data-centre switching). Revenue grew only from $27.8B in FY2021 to $34.3B in FY2025 (+5% a year), EBIT slipped, and FY2025 was a messy year: a $1.6B impairment, Juniper deal costs, higher debt (net debt rose from $5.5B to $19.0B) and a thin $0.6B of free cash flow. FY2026 looks very different. Q3 FY26 revenue was $12.2B (+34%), gross margin 40.1% (up 10.9 points), non-GAAP EPS $1.11 and guidance was raised to $3.75–3.85 non-GAAP EPS and at least $3.75B of free cash flow, helped by AI server orders, Juniper's higher-margin networking and cost synergies now targeted at $800M. The shares have risen from ~$52 at the Sep 2 print to $72 and a record, so the market is already pricing the recovery: ~37x trailing GAAP and ~15-19x forward earnings.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Past growth is not like-for-like — FY2021-25 numbers mix a stagnant legacy business, divestitures (H3C, CTG) and ~4 months of Juniper; the FY2026-27 jump is largely acquired and AI-server volume. Networking's +75% YoY in Q3 FY26 still compares against a quarter with only one month of Juniper; Q4 FY26 guidance is +11-13%. (2) The finance arm distorts cash flow and debt — HPE Financial Services' lease equipment runs through capex and its borrowings through net debt, so headline FCF and leverage look weaker than an industrial peer with no financing arm. (3) Server margins and inventory — inventory was $11.8B at Jul 2026 (vs $6.4B at Oct 2025) as AI systems and component prices rise; the recovery depends on AI-server margins holding.

Eight fiscal quarters (FY ends Oct 31, so Q3'26 = May–Jul 2026). Juniper joined on Jul 2, 2025: Networking revenue stepped from ~$1.1B to ~$2.7B a quarter, and AI servers drove the second leg. Q4'26 is due in early December 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q3 FY26 (reported Sep 2, 2026): revenue $12,213M, up 33.7% (company: “34%”) and a record; Server $6.8B (+35%), Networking $2.9B (+74.9%; Routing +270%, Data Center Networking +112%, Campus & Branch +31%), Financial Services $0.9B (flat). GAAP gross margin 40.1% (+10.9pp YoY), GAAP operating margin 11.4% (non-GAAP 16.2%), GAAP EPS $1.06 (includes a $444M, ~$0.31 a share gain on selling the rest of the H3C stake) and non-GAAP EPS $1.11 versus $0.44 a year ago. Networking's segment margin was 22.0% (flat), while Cloud & AI reached 17.0% from 7.0%. Operating cash flow was $1.6B and company-defined FCF $958M. HPE raised FY26 to non-GAAP EPS $3.75–3.85 and FCF of at least $3.75B, and plans to return at least 75% of FCF to shareholders in Q4. The shares then climbed from ~$52 to a record ~$72, helped by a Sep 30 Networking Investor Day (cost-synergy target raised to $800M by 2028) and a $1.2B Vultr order for AMD Helios racks, per press coverage.

Share Count & Dividend

Basic shares were 1,309M (FY21), 1,324M (FY25) and 1,333M (Dec 2025): essentially flat, with small repurchases ($202M in FY25, $447M in the nine months to Jul 2026) offsetting stock compensation. A $1.5B 7.625% Series C mandatory convertible preferred (issued for Juniper) converts into ~76M shares, lifting the diluted count to ~1.45B. Dividend $0.57 a year (about 0.8% yield).

Ownership caveats: percentages come from the Feb 2026 proxy (holdings as of Dec 31, 2025, based on 1,332.6M shares); the three index-fund holdings come from 13G filings dated Jan–Feb 2024, so they may be stale. Insiders (22 people) hold 5.8M shares, ~0.4%; no founder or controlling holder.
Balance sheet & HPE Financial Services: at Oct 31, 2025 borrowings were $22.4B ($4.6B current, $17.8B long-term) against $5.8B cash, so net debt of $16.6B (S&P, including leases: $19.0B, up from $5.5B a year earlier as Juniper was paid for in cash). By Jul 31, 2026 debt was $20.2B and cash $6.2B (net $14.0B, ~2.0x trailing EBITDA). Finance arm: HPE Financial Services (FS) had $13.2B of net portfolio assets, 20.8% ROE and $889M of Q4 revenue; it is funded with debt at high leverage, which I estimate at roughly $11-12B of the total (HPE does not give one clean figure in the release; this assumes ~7:1 leverage on the portfolio). Ex-FS net debt is therefore roughly $5B, about 1x EBITDA, but the scorecard uses the reported total. The finance arm also flatters or depresses FCF: equipment bought for leases is in capex (a cash outflow) while the matching borrowing is in financing flows. Goodwill and intangibles are $30B (40% of assets) after Juniper.

What's Working

The business is growing fast again: revenue +34% and gross margin 40% in the July 2026 quarter, FY2026 EPS guidance raised, FCF guided to at least $3.75B and $5B in FY2027. Networking is now a scaled, ~22%-margin business with AI-driven networking software (Mist, Marvis) and a path to $800M of synergies; AI-server demand and a record backlog support the top line. Debt is already falling (net ~$14B from $19B), the capex burden is easing, shareholders get ~75% of Q4 FCF, and the share count is flat.

What to Watch

History: five years of ~2-5% organic growth, falling EBIT and a FY2025 GAAP loss; 13 of 14 criteria fail on reported FY2025 numbers. Valuation: ~37x trailing, ~15-19x forward and a price equal to the consensus target after a ~3.6x move off the low. Execution: Juniper integration, server margins, $11.8B of inventory, and AI-server demand tied to a few large orders. The finance arm and ~$20B of debt add leverage, and index funds, not insiders, own the stock. Several inputs are third-party or my estimates: ROIC/WACC, FY2021-23 operating income (S&P basis), finance-arm debt and HPE's market-share rank.
