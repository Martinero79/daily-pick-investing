Caterpillar (founded 1925, Irving, Texas) makes the yellow iron most people picture — excavators, dozers, mining trucks — but services (parts, rebuilds, digital) were about 39% of machinery-and-power revenue in 2024 (the latest figure I found), and its Power & Energy segment (engines, gensets, turbines, rail) is the biggest by sales at $28.6B in FY2025. Financial Products (Cat Financial) finances dealers and customers. Revenue rose from $51.0B (FY21) to $67.6B (FY25), a 7.3% CAGR; operating profit grew 12.8% a year off a depressed FY21 base, and ME&T free cash flow rose about 57% to roughly $9.5B. The stock is up ~4x since end-2021, driven by two things: margins that moved from ~13% to a ~20% peak, and, more recently, AI data-center power demand that has pushed the backlog to a record $72B (+92% YoY at Q2 2026).
Maarten's note

Caterpillar is the best example I have on the site of a cyclical that has quietly become less cyclical. Each cycle bottoms higher: operating margin went from ~13% to ~20% at the FY24 peak, and about 39% of machinery-and-power revenue (2024) is services on an installed base that has to be maintained whether or not anyone is buying new trucks. Add a share count that fell ~16% in five years and a 33-year dividend growth streak, and the compounding story is real — it just doesn't show up in the 15%-CAGR screens, which is why the growth criteria score only 2–3 out of 5.

The part that changes the story is power. Data-center gensets, gas recips and turbines are booked out into 2028–29, Q2 power-generation sales to users grew 72%, and the backlog nearly doubled to $72B. That turns a mining-and-construction stock into a power-infrastructure story with multi-year visibility. What I can't underwrite is how much of it is durable versus an AI capex cycle that could pause — large, lumpy orders are the exact thing that makes backlog look better at the peak.

The valuation is where I slow down. 35x trailing and 28.5x forward earnings against a five-year average near 19x means the market is already paying for the AI-power cycle, and the entry-multiple criterion scores a flat zero. The shares are ~23% off the June high, which helps, but what I'd want to see is margins holding at 20%+ after ~$2.2B of tariff costs this year and the second-half dealer destock. Great business, demanding price — my own reasoning only, not advice.

Eight quarters show a clean turn: revenue fell year-on-year through Q2 2025, then accelerated to +24% in Q2 2026 as power-generation demand, rental fleet loading and price kicked in.
Q2 2026 (reported Aug 4, 2026): first $20B+ quarter, backlog $72B

Sales and revenues were $20.5B (+24% YoY, +$4.0B on higher volume of $3.1B and price of $0.6B). Operating margin was 20.9% reported (21.9% adjusted vs 17.6% a year earlier); adjusted EPS was $8.17 (+73%). Record MP&E free cash flow of $5.1B (+$2.8B YoY), with $2.2B returned via dividends and buybacks in the quarter. Backlog reached $72B, +$9B in the quarter and +92% YoY, with all three segments contributing and orders extending into 2030. The stock rose 5.6% on results day but has since given back that move.

Any pause in hyperscaler capex, or a mining or construction downturn, would hit a stock priced for continuity.

Valuation, Balance Sheet & Ownership
Caterpillar earns real profits, so trailing and forward P/E are both usable here. Both have roughly doubled from their FY21-24 range of 14-19x; the stock is priced for the AI-power cycle to last.
Price / Earnings — FY-End vs. Current (trailing and forward)
Current trailing P/E 35.3x vs. a FY21-25 average of 19.4x (1.83x its own history); current forward P/E 28.5x vs. a 18.6x average (1.53x). PEG ratio ≈ 1.56. P/FCF is 41.9x now vs. a 23.8x five-year average.
Trailing P/E (FY-end) Forward P/E (FY-end) Current (trailing / forward)
How to read the three ratios. Trailing P/E (35.3x) divides today's price by the last four quarters of GAAP EPS ($23.21). Forward P/E (28.5x) divides by consensus next-twelve-month EPS, so it is lower because analysts expect earnings to keep growing (consensus 3-yr EPS growth ≈ 27%). The PEG of ~1.56 says the market is paying about 1.6x for each point of expected growth — not extreme for a quality industrial, but well above Caterpillar's own history. Every measure agrees: the multiple has expanded far faster than the earnings. The stock has already de-rated slightly from June (trailing P/E was ~45x at the Jun 30 peak).
Balance sheet: how I separate Cat Financial from ME&T. Consolidated debt is $45.1B against $5.9B of cash (Jun 2026), i.e. net debt of $39.2B or 2.4x TTM EBITDA ($16.3B). But most of that is Cat Financial funding customer and dealer receivables. At year-end 2025, Financial Products debt was ~$32.6B (short-term $5.5B, long-term $27.1B) while ME&T (MP&E) debt was only ~$10.7B, against $10.0B of enterprise cash. Netting that gives ME&T net debt of roughly $0.7B, or ~0.1x a MP&E EBITDA proxy of $12-13B. Because consolidated cash fell to $5.9B by mid-2026 (buybacks and working capital), I put ME&T leverage at ~0.1-0.4x. This is an estimate: enterprise cash is not split by segment in the sources I used, and MP&E depreciation isn't disclosed separately. Scored on the ME&T basis.

What's Working

A category-leading franchise with a record $72B backlog (+92% YoY), Q2 2026 revenue growth of 24% and a 20.9% reported margin, ME&T free cash flow near $9.5B a year (about 87% of MP&E operating profit), and a very light industrial balance sheet once Cat Financial is separated. Services are ~39% of machinery-and-power revenue, which cushions the cycle; AI data-center power demand (power generation sales to users +72%) is a genuine second growth engine with order books into 2029-30; and shareholders benefit from a ~16% lower share count and 33 years of dividend growth.

What to Watch

The price already discounts the good news: trailing P/E is 1.8x its own five-year average and the entry-multiple and insider-ownership criteria score zero. Five-year growth (7% revenue, 13% EBIT) is respectable, not exceptional, and depends on the last two quarters holding. Risks include ~$2.2B of FY2026 tariff cost, a Q4 dealer-inventory drawdown, capacity-expansion costs (depreciation, freight) and rising capex intensity, lumpy data-center orders that could pause if hyperscaler spending slows, and the usual mining and construction cyclicality. The stock is also volatile (beta 1.59) and already ~23% below its June high.
