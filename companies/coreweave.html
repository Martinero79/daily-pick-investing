CoreWeave is the poster child of the "neocloud" boom: a former crypto-mining operation that pivoted into renting out Nvidia GPU capacity and rode the generative-AI wave to a $40 IPO in March 2025, a run to $183.58 by June 2025, and revenue that's compounded from effectively zero to a $9.89B TTM run rate in about three years. Q3 2026 revenue was $3.66B (+168% YoY, above the top of the company's $3.45B-$3.6B guide), and the revenue backlog reached $132.5B (up from about $104B at June 30% YoY) — genuinely extraordinary demand signals. But the checklist used on this site is built for capital discipline and durable profitability, and CoreWeave fails almost all of it today: operating margin is still marginally negative on a TTM basis (-0.48%), free cash flow is deeply negative and getting more negative every year (-$20.42B TTM), capex alone ran over 200% of FY2025 revenue as the company races to buy and deploy GPUs, net debt/EBITDA sits around 12x (funded via GPU-collateralized loans and convertible notes), and shares outstanding have grown about 45.7% in the last year. Two-thirds or more of revenue has historically come from a single customer (Microsoft), though CoreWeave has been actively diversifying via large new OpenAI (~$6.5B through 2031) and Meta (up to ~$21B) agreements. The one genuinely strong signal is founder alignment: the three co-founders retain a combined ~73.6% of voting power (via 10-votes-per-share Class B stock) even though their aggregated economic/insider ownership is a still-meaningful 19.03%. The one scorecard change since Q2: after the share price fell roughly 38% since September, the EV/EBITDA entry multiple (17.59x) now sits below its only prior reference point (21.56x), so criterion 10 moves to Met. Net: 4 of 14 criteria met, Martinero Index 29/100 — a name defined by extraordinary growth and extraordinary financial risk in roughly equal measure. This isn't a call to buy or avoid CoreWeave — just what the checklist says today, on a company still writing its first few chapters as a public entity.

Q3 2026 Headlines

Revenue reached $3.66B, up 168% YoY from $1.37B and about $0.1B above consensus (~$3.55B). Revenue backlog (remaining performance obligations) rose to roughly $132.5B from about $104B at June 30. GAAP operating income was $207M (vs $52M a year earlier), but the net loss widened to $709M ($1.27 per share) from $110M, with debt near $47B and interest expense still surging. For the full year 2026 CoreWeave raised revenue guidance to $13.0B-$13.3B and adjusted operating income guidance to $1.275B-$1.375B (an adjusted, non-GAAP measure; this page otherwise uses GAAP), raised capex guidance to $37B-$39B, still expects more than 1.85 GW of active power by year-end, and guided Q4 interest expense to $1.05B-$1.15B. Nine-month revenue was $8.31B (+133.6%), but nine-month GAAP operating income is only $14M. Shares fell after the report and were around $53 on Nov 13, 2026, well below the ~$85 level of mid-September.

Gross Margin Trend (FY2022-FY2025)
Gross margin jumped from a startup-like 23.42% (FY2022, when the business was tiny and infrastructure was underutilized) to the low-to-mid 70s as CoreWeave scaled — a +48.3pp improvement over the window used for criterion #14, though a large part of that swing reflects moving off an unrepresentatively low, pre-scale base rather than steady-state margin durability. TTM gross margin is 66.75%, down slightly from the FY2024 peak of 74.26% as newer, larger, and somewhat lower-margin enterprise contracts have entered the mix.
Market position, honestly stated: CoreWeave is the clear #1 pure-play "neocloud" — a specialist GPU-cloud provider — with roughly 10x the deployed GPU fleet of its closest independent rival, Nebius, and a widely-cited "Platinum tier" ranking among GPU-as-a-service providers. But that's a narrow, self-selected category. Against the comparison set the user actually asked for — AWS, Azure and Google Cloud — CoreWeave is small: those three hyperscalers combine for roughly two-thirds of total worldwide cloud infrastructure spend, each generate tens of billions of dollars of cloud revenue per quarter (vs. CoreWeave's ~$2.6B in its best quarter so far), and each is investing heavily in proprietary AI accelerator silicon (Trainium, TPU, Maia) that reduces their own need for third-party GPU capacity over time. CoreWeave's edge is speed-to-deploy and Nvidia-partnership depth (it was an early, favored buyer of new GPU generations including the Vera Rubin NVL72), not scale versus the giants. Scored here as "#4-5" rather than "#1 or #2" given that broader, more honest comparison set.
Customer concentration, a known structural risk: Microsoft was ~62% of FY2024 revenue and roughly ~67% of FY2025 revenue — an extraordinary reliance on a single counterparty. The top two customers together were ~65% of Q1 2026 revenue, showing some diversification is underway (a ~$6.5B OpenAI agreement signed May 2025, running through May 2031, and an order from Meta worth up to ~$21B signed March 2026 are the main drivers), but CoreWeave remains dependent on a small number of very large AI-lab and hyperscaler relationships rather than a broad customer base. If any one of these relationships were to shrink, renegotiate, or move workloads in-house, the revenue impact would be immediate and severe.

Balance sheet: net debt of $61.99B (S&P Global basis, including leases; company-reported debt was nearly $47B at Sep 30) against TTM EBITDA of $5.20B, for a Net Debt/EBITDA of roughly 11.92x (current) to 10.94x (FY2025 year-end) — extreme leverage, far outside this framework's <2x bar, funded mostly by debt specifically collateralized against GPU purchases plus a series of convertible note offerings (including a $3.7B issue priced mid-September 2026). The Altman Z-Score sits at just 0.2 (a score under 3 flags elevated bankruptcy risk under that model, though the model wasn't built for capital-intensive, contract-backed infrastructure financing structures like this one) and the Piotroski F-Score is a weak 2 out of 9. None of this means CoreWeave is in imminent distress — its debt is largely asset- and contract-backed against a $132.5B revenue backlog — but the leverage is real, growing, and the single biggest financial risk on this dashboard.

What's Working

Revenue growth is genuinely extraordinary (+167.9% FY2025, +168% Q3 2026 YoY), backed by a $132.5B revenue backlog (up from ~$104B at June 30) that suggests years of visible demand ahead. Gross margins have scaled into the high-60s/low-70s range as the business matured past its startup phase. Founder alignment is real and unusually strong for a newly-public company — the three co-founders control ~73.6% of voting power. And CoreWeave has moved quickly to diversify beyond its historical over-reliance on Microsoft, signing large new agreements with OpenAI and Meta.

What to Watch

Leverage is the central risk: ~$51.6B of total debt and a ~12x net debt/EBITDA ratio, funded through GPU-collateralized loans and a growing stack of convertible notes, with interest coverage currently negative. Customer concentration remains high (Microsoft alone was ~67% of FY2025 revenue) even after recent diversification. Free cash flow is deeply and increasingly negative as capex (over 200% of FY2025 revenue) races ahead of cash generation. Shares outstanding are up about 45.7% in a year. And with under 18 months of public trading history, CoreWeave simply doesn't have the multi-year track record this checklist is built to evaluate — every historical read here is a short, early, and volatile one.
