Forward P/E (11.01x currently) has consistently run below trailing P/E — see explainer below.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed. For Adobe specifically: trailing P/E (16.34x) is already well below its own recent history, and forward P/E (11.01x) is cheaper still, implying the market expects continued EPS growth even as revenue growth has decelerated. One more lens worth a glance: the PEG ratio, forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether that "cheap" multiple is actually cheap once growth is accounted for. Adobe's PEG currently runs around 0.78 — comfortably under the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, and well down from 2.53 in FY2021, suggesting today's multiple is genuinely inexpensive relative to expected earnings growth, not just relative to Adobe's own inflated past multiples.
Balance sheet: Adobe ended FY2025 with a net debt position of just $59M against FY2025 EBITDA of roughly $9.52B, for a Net Debt/EBITDA of about 0.01x — effectively net cash and far inside this framework's <2x bar. The company has run net cash every year FY2021-FY2024 (net cash of $1,125M, $1,463M, $3,762M and $1,830M respectively) before the FY2025 close landed almost exactly at breakeven; the current quarter shows a modest net-debt tilt of roughly $1.45B after continued heavy buybacks. ROIC has run 36-61% every year in this window (FY2021 38.23% → FY2025 59.33%, current 60.74%), reflecting Adobe's very low capital intensity — it clears both the assumed 11.0% WACC and the framework's 15% absolute bar with enormous headroom. Buybacks have been net share-count-reducing every year: diluted shares outstanding fell from 480M (FY2021 year-end) to 417M (FY2025 year-end) to 403M (Q2 FY2026), a buyback yield that has actually accelerated, from 0.93% (FY2021) to 5.84% currently.

What's Working

Operating margin, FCF conversion, ROIC and gross-margin stability are all excellent and have been for years; the balance sheet is essentially net cash; capex is negligible; buybacks have cut share count every year since FY2021; and the entry multiple (16.34x trailing, 11.01x forward, PEG 0.78) is genuinely cheap versus Adobe's own recent history after the stock's decline.

What to Watch

Growth has decelerated across the board — revenue, EBIT and FCF CAGRs all miss this framework's 15% bar, and the market has priced in real concern that generative-AI tools erode the Creative Cloud moat, taking the stock down ~54% from its FY2023 high. The blocked Figma acquisition removed Adobe's cleanest path to neutralizing its most credible design-software competitor, and insider ownership at 0.19% offers little of the founder-alignment cushion seen elsewhere on this site. Whether Adobe's own AI features (Firefly, generative fill) defend the franchise or whether cheaper AI-native tools keep chipping at the edges is the central question from here.
