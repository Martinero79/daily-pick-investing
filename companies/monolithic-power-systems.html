Monolithic Power Systems was founded in 1997 by CEO Michael Hsing and designs power-management semiconductors: chips that convert and regulate electricity inside servers, AI accelerators' boards, cars, base stations, SSDs and consumer devices. It is fabless, relying on foundry partners such as TSMC for wafers, but has been spending more on its own packaging and test capacity (capex $172M in FY2025, $237M TTM; company says capacity now supports over $6B of annual revenue and targets $8B by H2 2027). The growth engine is Enterprise Data: from $116M of revenue in FY2021 to $702M in FY2025 and $507M in Q3 2026 alone, driven by power solutions for AI and server platforms. Revenue compounded 23% a year over FY2021-25 and accelerated to +63% YoY in Q3 2026, with Q4 2026 guided to $1,315-1,385M. The share price is roughly 29% below its 52-week high even so, which mostly reflects a multiple that was already stretched (about 66x trailing GAAP earnings, ~37x forward on consensus) and sector volatility in AI-linked chip names. Note that gross margin is flat at ~55%, so profit growth is coming from volume and operating leverage rather than pricing power.
Three things to read carefully: (1) GAAP vs non-GAAP — MPS reports non-GAAP EPS of $8.19 for Q3 2026 against GAAP $5.51 because stock-based compensation ($106M in Q3, doubling QoQ) and related items are excluded; this page uses GAAP throughout. (2) FY2024 net income includes a one-off tax benefit (Q4 2024 net income $1.26B vs operating income $163M), so the FY2024 trailing P/E of 18x is not a real valuation signal. (3) AI concentration — Enterprise Data is now 42% of revenue and drove most of the recent acceleration (+165% YoY), which makes the growth rate more cyclical and customer-concentrated than the 5-year CAGR suggests.

Q3 2026 was reported on Oct 29, 2026 (fiscal quarters = calendar quarters). Eight quarters shown so the chart covers the FY2023-24 lull, the 2025 recovery and the 2026 acceleration.
What the quarter actually showed

Q3 2026 revenue was $1,202.4M, up 22.6% QoQ and 63.1% YoY, with GAAP gross margin 55.4%, GAAP operating income $357.7M (29.7% margin) and GAAP EPS $5.51 (non-GAAP $8.19). Enterprise Data revenue reached $506.7M (+165% YoY, +33% QoQ) and Communications $187.4M (+135% YoY, optical modules and switches), while Storage & Computing fell 3.8% QoQ and Consumer was down 11.6% YoY. GAAP operating margin dipped 1.3pp QoQ because operating expenses jumped 30% (stock-based compensation was $106M, up from $54M in Q2). MPS bought back $453M of stock in the quarter (versus $4M in Q2). The stock closed $1,198.81 on the report day and $1,211.63 the next day, roughly flat despite the beat, and about 29% below its 52-week high.

What's Working

Revenue has compounded 23% a year with accelerating growth (+63% YoY in Q3 2026), operating income and FCF have grown faster than sales over the window, ROIC of ~32% is well above any reasonable cost of capital, and the balance sheet is net cash with $1.09B in the bank. FCF conversion is high (91% in FY2025) and the company is returning cash through a growing dividend and a new $500M buyback authorisation. Management has guided Q4 2026 revenue to $1.315-1.385B and is adding capacity ahead of demand, and the founder-CEO is still running the company after 29 years.

What to Watch

The multiple is the main risk: ~66x trailing GAAP earnings, 12% above its own five-year average, and the stock is already ~29% off its high after sharp swings (-9% on Oct 22, 2026). Growth is now concentrated in AI/data-center demand (Enterprise Data 42% of revenue), which is cyclical and customer-dependent. Gross margin has not grown in 2+ years and is 3.3pp below FY2022; capex intensity and stock-based compensation are both rising; the Oct 29 release flags a material weakness in internal controls and a CFO change, and law-firm ‘investigations’ of the board were announced in October 2026 (allegations only, no outcome). Market-share and insider-ownership scores rest on judgment or third-party data.
