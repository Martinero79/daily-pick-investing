Booking Holdings is one of the cleanest compounders on this whole site: high, steadily expanding margins, an asset-light platform model, aggressive buybacks and a valuation that looks cheap relative to its own recent history. Revenue grew 13.39% in FY2025 to $26.92B, operating margin expanded to 35.24% (from 24.02% in FY2021), and free cash flow conversion has stayed near or above 100% of EBIT most years. Q2 2026 Headlines

Revenue reached $7.35B, up 8% YoY (≈7% constant-currency), beating consensus by roughly 2%. Gross bookings grew 9% to $51.0B on 5% room-night growth (325 million room nights) plus higher constant-currency average daily rates. Adjusted EPS was $2.54, up 15% YoY, and adjusted EBITDA reached $2.6B, up 9%. Domestic room nights grew high-single-digits globally, while international room nights rose only slightly — long-haul travel has faced some indirect drag from Middle East conflict disruption. Growth has clearly moderated from the post-COVID snap-back years, but it's still healthy, margin-accretive growth paired with record capital returns to shareholders.

Gross margin has actually improved every year in this window, from 80.12% to 87.36% — a +1.78pp gain versus FY2022 alone (the window used for criterion #14), driven by mix shift toward the higher-margin Merchant (agency-to-merchant) revenue model and disciplined cost control, not margin erosion like some capex-heavy platforms elsewhere on this site.
Market position, honestly stated: Booking Holdings is usually described as the world's largest online travel company by gross bookings (≈$172B FY2025) and the most-visited travel website globally — ahead of Expedia (≈$108B) on that measure. But one widely-cited "global OTA market share" estimate actually puts China's Trip.com Group narrowly ahead (26.0% vs Booking's 24.8%), a result driven almost entirely by Trip.com's dominance of China's enormous domestic travel market rather than any weakness in Booking's international position. In its core Western/international OTA business, alongside Expedia, Booking is the clear #1 — but Airbnb keeps taking share in alternative accommodations, and Trip.com is a real and growing global competitor, not just a domestic Chinese player. Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers, and the PEG check Maarten actually wanted: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same ratio computed at each fiscal year-end. Forward P/E is today's price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — the more useful lens for judging whether a multiple is fair relative to where earnings are actually headed. For Booking specifically, both readings point the same direction: trailing P/E (18.94x) is roughly half its own FY21-25 average, and forward P/E (15.40x) is cheaper still. The number this dashboard set out to verify is the PEG ratio — forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth — to check whether it genuinely lands in the "reasonably priced growth" 1.0-2.0 range. It doesn't — it's cheaper than that. stockanalysis.com puts BKNG's PEG at 0.88 (forward P/E ÷ ~16.4% consensus 3-yr EPS growth forecast); Nasdaq's data shows 0.93; GuruFocus shows 0.43 using a different (10-year historical) growth methodology. Every source checked lands below 1.0, not just inside the 1.0-2.0 band — on this specific metric, Booking isn't merely "reasonably priced growth," it's priced as if the market is either underestimating the growth or applying an unusually large discount for travel-sector cyclicality and macro risk.

Book equity is negative (-$10.78B) purely because of years of buybacks exceeding retained earnings — not a sign of financial distress, since leverage against cash flow remains low and interest coverage is a healthy 8.92x. ROIC clears the assumed 9.0% WACC by a wide margin under every methodology checked. What's Working

Margins keep expanding (35.24% operating, 87.36% gross) even as growth normalizes off the COVID-recovery years; free cash flow conversion has stayed near or above 100% of EBIT; leverage is minimal (0.33x net debt/EBITDA) despite negative book equity from years of buybacks; the share count has fallen roughly 27% since FY2021; and the valuation is genuinely cheap versus its own history, with a PEG ratio around 0.88-0.93 — below the 1.0-2.0 "reasonably priced growth" range this dashboard set out to check, not just inside it.

What to Watch

Insider ownership is essentially zero (0.20%) — there's no founder or controlling shareholder with major personal skin in the game. The headline FY21-25 growth CAGRs are inflated by a COVID-depressed FY2021 base; the cleaner FY22-25 growth rates (mid-teens revenue, low-20s EBIT) are still good but noticeably less dramatic. And while Booking is a clear leader, the OTA market remains genuinely competitive — Expedia, Airbnb and China's fast-growing Trip.com Group are all real, well-funded rivals, not afterthoughts.
