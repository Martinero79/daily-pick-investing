Schneider Electric is a genuinely excellent, well-run business — ranked #1 on Gartner's Top 25 Supply Chain list for ten straight years, twice named World's Most Sustainable Company, and sitting at the center of two of the decade's strongest structural trends: AI-driven data-center buildout and grid electrification. But this framework's mechanical scorecard comes out low regardless. At €40B+ revenue, Schneider is simply too large to clear a 15% five-year growth bar; it competes in a genuinely fragmented market rather than holding a monopoly or duopoly position; and the 2025-26 AI-fueled re-rating has pushed the entry multiple well above its own five-year average. None of that makes it a bad company — it's a case study in this score measuring something narrower than overall quality.

H1 2026 Headlines

Record H1 revenue €21.2B, +14% organic (Q2 alone: €11.5B, also a quarterly record). Adjusted EBITA €4.09B, +22% organic, margin 19.3% (+120bps). Net income €2.49B, +30% reported. Half-Year Adjusted EBITA (€B) & Margin
Company-reported non-GAAP measure. Margin dipped in H1 2025 on FX headwinds and raw-material inflation, then expanded 120bps to a record 19.3% in H1 2026 on volume and productivity.
Active, debt-funded M&A: Schneider announced the $3.1B acquisition of Cognite (an industrial AI platform, to be integrated with AVEVA) in July 2026, and in July 2025 agreed to pay Temasek €5.5B for full ownership of its India joint venture. Both deals lean into the electrification/AI thesis, but they've pushed net debt from €8.1B (Dec 2024) toward €16B+, lifting net debt/EBITDA from ~1.0x to ~1.9x — still under this framework's 2x screen, but moving the wrong direction.

What's Working

A genuinely dominant, well-diversified franchise sitting at the center of two of the decade's strongest structural trends — AI/data-center power and cooling demand, and grid electrification — with ten straight years atop Gartner's global supply chain ranking and back-to-back recognition as the World's Most Sustainable Company. Leverage remains modest in absolute terms, share count has stayed essentially flat, and capex intensity is low for an industrial of this scale. 2026 guidance has already been raised twice on real momentum: H1 organic revenue growth accelerated to 14%.

What to Watch

This framework's growth and market-concentration bars are simply tough for a €40B+ diversified industrial to clear — Schneider competes against Siemens, ABB, Eaton, Legrand, Honeywell and Rockwell across genuinely open markets rather than holding a monopoly position. The entry multiple (35x trailing P/E) sits well above its own five-year average following the AI-driven re-rating, and net debt/EBITDA has nearly doubled since FY2024 funding the India JV buyout and Cognite acquisition. None of these misses signal operational trouble — H1 2026 was a record half — but they do mean paying a premium multiple for a business whose own scorecard doesn't yet show a clear margin of safety.
