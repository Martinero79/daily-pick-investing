Arista Networks, founded in 2004 and led by CEO Jayshree Ullal and co-founder Andy Bechtolsheim, makes high-speed Ethernet switches and routers and the EOS network operating system that runs them. Its customers are the “Cloud and AI Titans” (about 48% of FY2025 revenue) plus enterprises and service providers (about 32%). Revenue grew from $2.95B in FY2021 to $9.01B in FY2025 (+32% a year), with a GAAP operating margin that rose from 31% to 43% and free cash flow of $4.25B in FY2025. The AI build-out is the engine: networking is the plumbing between thousands of GPUs, and Arista is the main Ethernet alternative to NVIDIA's own switches. Q2 2026 delivered the first $3B quarter ($3.04B, +37.7% YoY), a GAAP operating margin of 45.4%, non-GAAP EPS of $1.02 (+40%) and a raised 2026 outlook of ~$12.6B revenue, and the shares jumped ~12% on the day to new highs. The flip side is price and concentration: the stock trades at ~67x trailing earnings versus a ~43x average since 2021, and Microsoft and Meta alone were about 42% of FY2025 revenue.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Two customers matter a lot — Microsoft (~26%) and Meta (~16%) were ~42% of FY2025 revenue per coverage of the 10-K (Arista's own filing is the primary source); a pause in either one's AI spending would show up quickly. (2) Gross margin is drifting down — from 64.1% in FY2025 to ~62% in Q1–Q2 2026 and guided at 62–64% for the year, because cloud/AI customers carry lower margins than enterprise and memory/silicon costs are rising. (3) The competition is not standing still — NVIDIA went from under 4% to 21.5% of data-centre Ethernet in two years, and Cisco, Broadcom-based white-box switches and HPE/Juniper all fight for the same budgets.

Quarterly Trend (Q3 2024 – Q2 2026)
Eight quarters. Revenue growth has stepped up from ~28% to ~38% YoY while gross margin has slipped about 2.3 points from its Q2 2025 peak. Q3 2026 is due Nov 3, 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was $3,036M (+37.7% YoY, +12% on Q1), the first $3B quarter. GAAP operating margin reached 45.4% (non-GAAP 49.9%) and non-GAAP EPS was $1.02 (+39.7%); both beat the company's own guide of ~$2.8B revenue, a 46–47% operating margin and ~$0.88 EPS. GAAP gross margin was 62.9%, down from 65.2% a year earlier but up from 61.9% in Q1; management cited customer mix and tariff refunds, and warned of higher memory and silicon costs. Full-year guidance was raised to ~$12.6B revenue (+40%), gross margin 62–64% and operating margin 48–49% (non-GAAP). The shares rose ~12% on the print.

What's Working

Few companies at this scale grow 30%+ with a 43% GAAP operating margin, $4.3B of free cash flow, net cash and almost no capex. Arista is the leading independent Ethernet vendor for AI back-end and front-end networks, share has held ~20% while NVIDIA entered, deferred revenue is $6.2B, and the 2026 guide was raised to ~$12.6B. The founders are still involved, and the business converts earnings to cash at over 100%.

What to Watch

The valuation: ~67x trailing and ~47x forward earnings, 56% above its own five-year average, with consensus only ~5% above the price. Concentration: Microsoft and Meta were ~42% of FY2025 revenue. Gross margin is drifting down (64.1% → ~62%) as hyperscale mix grows and component costs rise. NVIDIA (21.5% share and bundling GPUs with its own switches), Cisco and white-box switches compete directly, and AI capex cycles can reverse.
