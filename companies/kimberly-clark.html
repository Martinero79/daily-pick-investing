Q2 2026 Headlines

Net sales were $4.19B, up 0.6% YoY as reported, with organic sales growth roughly flat versus the prior year (including an estimated 50bp drag from a China social-media-driven disruption). Adjusted gross margin rose to 38.8%, up 190bp YoY, on one-time tariff refunds and productivity savings, partially offset by planned brand and supply-chain investments. Management now expects full-year 2026 organic sales growth to run roughly 100bp below the ~2% weighted-average growth of the categories and countries Kimberly-Clark competes in, largely due to the China disruption. None of this yet reflects the pending Kenvue acquisition, still awaiting final regulatory clearances (Australia's competition authority conditionally approved the deal on Sep 2, 2026, requiring divestiture of the Carefree and Stayfree brands there).

Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and normally the more useful of the two for judging whether growth is priced in. For Kimberly-Clark specifically: trailing P/E (20.78x) sits modestly below its own five-year average (21.45x), and forward P/E (14.75x) looks considerably cheaper still. But the PEG ratio — forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth — tells a more cautious story: KMB's PEG currently runs around 6.27, one of the highest on this site, versus the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line. That's because 3-year consensus EPS growth is forecast at only ~1.85% a year; the "cheap-looking" forward multiple is cheap mostly because the market expects very little growth to go with it, not because the stock is a bargain on a growth-adjusted basis. Any Kenvue-driven EPS step-up once the deal closes is not yet fully reflected in that consensus estimate.
Balance sheet: net debt of $5.58B against TTM EBITDA of $3.48B, for a Net Debt/EBITDA of roughly 1.60x — comfortably under this framework's <2x bar, and the lowest point in the five-year window shown here (approximate FY-level figures: ~2.50x FY2021, trending down to ~2.13x FY2025 before the further TTM improvement). ROIC is 25.35%, clearing both the 15% absolute bar and the assumed 7.5% WACC by a wide margin — though this is meaningfully inflated by a very thin book-equity base ($1.87B) after decades of buybacks (ROE is a startling 104.91%), so treat it as "efficient use of a small permanent capital base" rather than "exceptional returns on a large one." Share count has been essentially flat-to-slightly-declining (-0.40% YoY, 332.6M shares outstanding), consistent with the framework's flat/declining bar, though buyback activity has been modest rather than aggressive given an 87% dividend payout ratio. None of this reflects the pending Kenvue deal, which is expected to add substantial new debt and roughly proportional new shares once it closes.

What's Working

Huggies holds a durable global #2 diaper position, ROIC comfortably clears both the absolute bar and assumed WACC, net debt/EBITDA is comfortably under 2x, the current P/E sits modestly below Kimberly-Clark's own five-year average, share count is essentially flat, and gross margin has genuinely improved nearly 6.5 points over three years on cost transformation work plus the portfolio mix shift.

What to Watch

Reported revenue, EBIT and FCF all show negative 5-year CAGRs (heavily distorted by the Suzano tissue-JV reclassification, but still real on a reported basis), operating margin has never cleared the 20% bar in this window, FCF conversion dipped back below 80% in FY2025, insider ownership is negligible, and capex intensity is elevated versus typical CPG peers. Above all, the pending $48.7B Kenvue acquisition — approved by both companies' shareholders but still awaiting final regulatory clearances, expected to close in H2 2026 — is not reflected anywhere in these figures and will materially reshape KMB's revenue base, leverage and share count the moment it closes.
