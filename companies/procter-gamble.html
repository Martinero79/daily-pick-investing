Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY22-26 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed. For P&G specifically, trailing (22.11x) and forward (20.98x) P/E sit fairly close together, which makes sense: analysts aren't modeling a big earnings inflection either way, just the same low-single-digit growth P&G has delivered for years. That's exactly where the PEG ratio (forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth) earns its keep: P&G's PEG currently runs around 4.2x — well above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, and one of the higher PEG readings on this site. In other words, the modest discount to its own trailing-P/E history looks cheap only until you account for how little earnings growth is actually expected (consensus EPS growth of roughly 4.25% over three years); relative to that growth rate, the stock is not statistically cheap.
Balance sheet: net debt of $25.08B against EBITDA of roughly $24.8B gives a Net Debt/EBITDA of 1.01x — comfortably under this framework's <2x bar, and has stayed in a tight 1.0x-1.3x band across all five fiscal years (1.18x FY2022, 1.32x FY2023, 1.03x FY2024, 1.10x FY2025, 1.01x FY2026). ROIC has likewise held steady in a narrow 20.4%-22.4% range across the same window, clearing both the assumed 7.5% WACC and the framework's 15% absolute bar every year. Buybacks reduced share count every year in this window (shares outstanding down -1.30% over the past year alone), with a positive buyback yield in FY2022 (2.38%), FY2023 (2.17%), FY2024 (0.48%), FY2025 (0.71%) and FY2026 (1.30%) — no dilution years at all.

What's Working

Operating margin (24.81%) and gross margin (50.87%, +3.01pp over three years) are both expanding, not eroding. ROIC has held a tight 20-22% band for five straight years, comfortably clearing WACC and the framework's 15% bar. Net debt/EBITDA (1.01x) is conservative and stable, buybacks have reduced share count every single year with zero dilution, capex intensity is low, and the entry multiple sits modestly below the stock's own five-year average.

What to Watch

Growth is genuinely mature-market slow: five-year CAGRs of 2.07% (revenue), 4.02% (EBIT) and 2.79% (FCF) all badly miss this framework's 15% growth bars, and there's no realistic scenario where a company this size in these categories re-accelerates to double-digit growth. FCF conversion (70.15%) has been inconsistent, insider/founder ownership is effectively zero, and the household/personal-care category remains genuinely fragmented and competitive rather than a tight oligopoly — P&G's #1/#2 brand positions don't translate into pricing power over the whole industry. The PEG ratio (≈4.2x) is a useful reminder that "cheaper than its own history" doesn't mean cheap in absolute terms given how little growth is on offer.
