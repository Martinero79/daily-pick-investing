Hyundai Motor Company is South Korea's largest carmaker, founded in 1967 and headquartered in Seoul. It sold 4,138,389 vehicles in 2025 under the Hyundai and Genesis brands, and reports three segments: Vehicle (₩145.6T of FY2025 revenue), Finance (₩30.2T, the captive lending arm) and Other (₩10.4T, including rolling stock). Together with Kia it forms Hyundai Motor Group, usually ranked the world's third-largest automaker by units. Between FY2021 and FY2023 revenue grew from ₩117.6T to ₩162.7T and operating profit more than doubled to ₩15.1T on strong pricing, SUV and hybrid mix and a weak won. Since then the trend has turned: FY2025 operating profit fell 19.5% to ₩11.5T (6.2% margin) on U.S. import tariffs, higher incentives and raw-material costs, and TTM profit to June 2026 is ₩9.6T. The latest quarter (Q2 2026) delivered record revenue of ₩49.2T but operating profit of ₩2.85T (-20.8% YoY) as wholesale volume fell 6.9% on supplier-related production constraints in Korea; management kept its FY2026 guidance of 1-2% revenue growth and a 6.3-7.3% operating margin, which implies a second-half recovery after a 5.6% H1 margin. The share price roughly doubled from ₩296,500 at end-2025 to ₩677,000 on May 28, 2026 (S&P/Yahoo closes), then fell about 49% to ₩348,000 today with daily moves of 5-10% in July-August; I did not verify what drove either leg. On trailing earnings the stock now trades at ~12.9x versus a ~6x FY2021-25 average, so the multiple, not the business, has done most of the moving.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Captive finance — consolidated FCF is negative every year except FY2022 and net debt/EBITDA is 7-10x because customer-loan growth and its funding run through the consolidated cash-flow statement and balance sheet; the carmaker alone looks very different, but I could not verify an industrial series, so these criteria are scored on the consolidated numbers and are probably too harsh. (2) Preferred shares and share-class blending — Hyundai has common and several preferred share classes; S&P per-share figures and market cap blend them, so P/E and EPS are approximate. (3) S&P FY2025 balance-sheet cash shows ₩26.4T at Dec 2025 versus ₩78-88T in the neighbouring quarters, a classification break, so FY2025 net debt (₩150.1T) is not comparable with the quarterly path (Jun 2026: ₩102.6T).

Latest Quarter (Q2 2026) & Eight-Quarter Trend
Reported Jul 23, 2026; Q3 2026 results are expected around Oct 23, 2026. Revenue is growing slowly while operating profit has lagged the prior-year quarters for four quarters running.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 revenue was a record ₩49,215bn (+1.9% YoY) but operating profit fell 20.8% to ₩2,851bn (5.8% margin) and net profit incl. non-controlling interests fell 11.2% to ₩2,888bn. Wholesale volume was 991,885 units (-6.9%): Korea -16.4% on supplier-related production constraints, rest of world -4.9%, while U.S. wholesale grew 0.9% (about a 6% U.S. share for a fifth straight quarter). Hybrids set a record at 187,661 units (18.9% of sales), and electrified vehicles reached 26.9% of the mix. Management cited higher incentives, raw-material costs, tariff costs and supply disruption for the margin squeeze. Revenue growth is helped by a weaker won and by Finance-segment revenue (₩9.5T, +15.7% YoY), while Vehicle-segment revenue fell 2.1%. A union wage deal ending weeks of strikes was approved on Aug 31, 2026.

What's Working

Revenue is at a record and has compounded at 12% for four years, led by a hybrid-heavy mix (record 187,661 hybrids in Q2, 26.9% electrified) and a steady ~6% U.S. market share (fifth straight quarter). Operating profit is still 72% above FY2021, capex is moderate (4.5% of revenue), the share count is flat with a new buyback, and a controlling-family group provides long-term continuity. The Korea strike season ended with a wage deal on Aug 31, guidance has been held, and a large U.S. manufacturing commitment (Georgia plant, ~$26B group programme) reduces tariff exposure over time.

What to Watch

Profit is falling while the price has run: operating profit is down 24% from the FY2023 peak, Q2 2026 EBIT fell 20.8% on volume -6.9%, and the multiple (~12.9x trailing) is about double its own five-year average. Margins are thin (6.2%) and gross margin is down 1.5pp in three years under tariffs, incentives and raw-material costs. H1 2026 margin (5.6%) is below the 6.3-7.3% guidance range, so the second half has to deliver. Chinese makers are gaining share globally, the in-house driver-assist system has slipped to 2029 (Nvidia interim), and the stock moves 5-10% in a day. Consolidated cash flow and leverage figures look poor mainly because of the finance arm, but that also means credit-cycle exposure. Unverified: industrial FCF and net debt, the drivers of the 2026 price swing, and the latest ownership filings.
