Amplifon, founded in Milan in 1950 by Algero Vita, is the world's largest retail and distribution network for hearing solutions — more than 10,000 points of sale across 25 countries, roughly 12–13% of the global fitting/retail channel, about double its nearest rival. Crucially, it buys hearing aids from manufacturers (historically Sonova, Demant, WS Audiology, GN and others) and fits/sells/services them through its own stores and professionals — it has never made the devices itself. That changes, pending regulatory approval: on 16 March 2026 Amplifon agreed to acquire GN Store Nord's entire hearing-aid manufacturing business (GN Hearing, maker of the ReSound brand) for DKK 17.0bn (~€2.3–2.5bn) on a cash-free, debt-free basis — €1.69bn in cash (funded via a newly secured €1.35bn loan facility) plus 56 million new Amplifon shares, making GN Store Nord Amplifon's second-largest shareholder at roughly 16%. The deal is not yet closed as of this writing (expected by end-2026, subject to the carve-out of GN Hearing and regulatory clearance), so none of the financials below reflect it. If and when it closes, Amplifon becomes a vertically integrated retailer-and-manufacturer for the first time in its history — a genuinely business-model-changing shift, and the reason we cross-reference Demant A/S (also on this site): today Demant is a manufacturer with its own growing retail arm, while Amplifon is a retailer about to become a manufacturer too. The two businesses are converging from opposite directions. Standalone, the numbers tell a story of deceleration: revenue grew from €1.95bn (FY2021) to a FY2024 peak of €2.41bn, then contracted slightly in FY2025 (-0.6% reported) as a soft European market and tough comps bit; adjusted EBITDA margin has slipped from 24.8% (FY2021-22) to 22.6% (FY2025). Management's "Fit4Growth" restructuring program (clinic closures, back-office efficiency, divestiture of the loss-making UK business) is showing early results — organic growth reaccelerated to +2.2% in Q1 2026 and +4.7% in Q2 2026, the best quarter in two years. The stock, meanwhile, is down roughly 75% from its December 2021 all-time high of €47.56, reflecting both the growth slowdown and the market's digestion of the pending GN leverage and integration risk.
The GN Hearing deal, in one paragraph: Combined pro forma revenue of roughly €3.3bn, 20,000+ employees, operations in 100+ countries, management-targeted synergies of €60–80m by 2029. It fixes Amplifon's historical structural gap — no manufacturing scale, thin margins on a distribution-only model — but adds real integration risk and a step-change in leverage on top of a balance sheet that was already drifting toward this framework's 2x net-debt/EBITDA ceiling before the deal.

The GN Hearing Acquisition & Quarterly Momentum
Deal terms per GN Store Nord's 16 Mar 2026 disclosure and Amplifon's own M&A announcement; quarterly figures per Amplifon's Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 press releases (latest reported: Q2 2026, published 30 Jul 2026).
Deal terms: Amplifon to acquire GN Hearing (GN Store Nord's hearing-aid business)

Enterprise value DKK 17.0 billion (≈€2.3–2.5bn / ~$2.5bn), cash-free and debt-free basis. GN Store Nord receives €1.69bn in cash (funded via a newly arranged €1.35bn loan facility) plus 56 million new Amplifon shares — making GN Store Nord Amplifon's second-largest shareholder at roughly 16% of the combined company, behind founder-family vehicle Ampliter S.r.l. GN Hearing makes the ReSound brand of hearing aids and is one of the five major global hearing-aid manufacturers (alongside Sonova, Demant, WS Audiology and Starkey). Announced 16 March 2026; expected to close by end-2026 pending the carve-out of GN Hearing from GN Group and regulatory approvals. Not yet closed — none of the standalone financials in this dashboard reflect the transaction. Management targets €60–80m of annual cost synergies by 2029; pro forma combined revenue is guided at roughly €3.3bn with 20,000+ employees across 100+ countries.

Why this matters for the Demant comparison on this site: Demant's own dashboard (companies/demant.html) already flags that "GN just sold its hearing business to Amplifon" as a sign the manufacturing side of the industry is consolidating. The honest framing: as of today, Amplifon is a retailer/distributor (it buys devices from manufacturers and fits/sells them through its own stores) while Demant is a manufacturer with a growing retail arm of its own (HearingLife, KIND Group). Once the GN deal closes, Amplifon becomes both a retailer and a manufacturer for the first time — a genuine business-model change, not just a bolt-on acquisition. It does not make Amplifon and Demant direct manufacturing-scale peers overnight (GN Hearing's device volumes are smaller than Sonova's or Demant's), but it does end the clean "retailer vs. manufacturer" distinction between the two companies.
Organic Revenue Growth Is Reaccelerating
FY2025 organic growth was weak in H1 (management cited "global tensions" affecting patient confidence) before improving materially in H2. The two most recently reported quarters show a clear reacceleration, the best in two years.
Revenue by Region, FY2025
EMEA remains dominant but soft (market growth below historical levels); Americas overtook individual European markets in importance in 2024 on the back of Miracle-Ear direct-network expansion in the US.

WACC assumed at 7–8%, consistent with stockanalysis.com's own 7.0% estimate for Amplifon — a large-cap, investment-grade-adjacent European consumer-healthcare name.
Trailing & Forward P/E — FY21-FY25 & Current
FY2021's 68.9x reflects post-COVID growth-multiple euphoria (revenue +29.6% that year) and is an outlier; excluding it, the FY22-25 average is closer to 39.7x. Current trailing P/E (38.25x) sits just below the full 5-year average (45.5x) but forward P/E (15.20x) is dramatically cheaper — see explainer below. PEG ≈ 0.99, almost exactly the traditional "fair value" line.
Trailing P/E (FY-end / current) Forward P/E (current only)
Trailing vs. forward P/E: current P/E (TTM, ~38.25x) divides today's price by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings, which are depressed by FY2025's margin compression; forward P/E (~15.20x) divides today's price by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings, capturing the expected recovery as Fit4Growth cost savings land and organic growth reaccelerates. The wide gap between the two (38.25x vs 15.20x) is one of the largest on this site and signals the market is pricing in a meaningful earnings rebound — the PEG ratio (forward P/E ÷ expected EPS growth) sits at roughly 0.99, almost exactly the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, suggesting that rebound is priced fairly rather than excessively optimistically. It's also worth being skeptical here: consensus estimates baked into forward P/E don't yet reflect the leverage and integration risk of the pending GN Hearing deal.
Net Debt / EBITDA (Company-Reported Financial Leverage)
Leverage has drifted up from 1.50x (FY2023) to 1.92x (Dec 2025) as capex, M&A, dividends and buybacks together exceeded €350m in 2025 alone — still under this framework's 2x bar, but only just, and before the GN Hearing deal (funded partly via a new €1.35bn loan facility) adds meaningfully more debt once it closes.

What's Working

Amplifon is the clear #1 in a genuinely large, fragmented global retail/distribution niche — roughly double the #2 player's share, with a founder-family (Ampliter/Holland-Vita) controlling 42% of capital and 68% of votes, among the more aligned ownership structures on this site. FCF conversion is very strong (127% of EBIT), leverage remains just under the 2x bar, the entry multiple is below its own 5-year average, and organic growth is visibly reaccelerating — +2.2% in Q1 2026, +4.7% in Q2 2026, the best quarter in two years, as the Fit4Growth restructuring program starts to bite.

What to Watch

Growth has stalled (FY2025 revenue actually contracted), EBIT margin is far below the framework's 20% bar even accounting for the structural IFRS16 lease-amortization gap versus EBITDA margin, and ROIC (~5.9%) doesn't clear even a conservative WACC estimate — a genuine value-creation concern, not just a scorecard technicality. The pending GN Hearing acquisition (~€2.3-2.5bn, not yet closed) is the single biggest swing factor: done well, it fixes Amplifon's manufacturing-scale gap and adds real synergies; done poorly, it adds meaningful leverage and integration risk on top of a balance sheet already drifting toward this framework's ceiling, and dilutes existing shareholders by issuing GN Store Nord a ~16% stake.
