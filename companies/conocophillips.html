ConocoPhillips is the largest independent oil and gas producer: a pure exploration-and-production business with no refining or retail (those went to Phillips 66 in 2012), so its earnings move almost one-for-one with oil and gas prices. In November 2024 it closed the all-stock acquisition of Marathon Oil, adding Lower 48 acreage and volumes; that lifted FY2025 revenue 6.8% even as operating income fell to $12.6B, because realised prices were lower than in FY2022-23. Five years on from FY2021, revenue is up about 6.6% a year, operating income is slightly lower and free cash flow is down by more than a third, while capex has more than doubled to about 21% of revenue. The balance sheet is sound (net debt roughly 0.6x EBITDA) and the share count has fallen despite the Marathon issuance, but the Martinero Index of 37 reflects a business whose five-year record is mostly a price cycle, not compounding. Q2 2026 was a sharp rebound (revenue +35.5% YoY, operating income more than doubled) and the shares are up about 72% in 52 weeks with Brent near $114, so the current trailing P/E of 19.6x sits well above its own FY21-25 average of 12.2x, an average itself depressed by the FY2022 earnings peak.

Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of reported earnings (TTM EPS $7.53, which includes the strong Q2 2026). Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same ratio at each fiscal year-end. Forward P/E is today's price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings; here 12.3x versus 19.6x trailing, implying consensus expects EPS to rise by roughly 60% on higher oil prices. That is a commodity-price call, not a growth trend. stockanalysis.com reports a PEG ratio of 0.79 (3-year consensus EPS growth forecast 18.7%), but that growth is a rebound from a trough and should not be read as steady compounding. Because earnings are cyclical, a low P/E can signal peak earnings and a high P/E trough earnings; today's elevated trailing multiple partly reflects TTM earnings still catching up with the price move.

What's Working

A strong balance sheet (net debt about 0.6x EBITDA) and a ROIC of ~11.6% that clears its estimated cost of capital. The company is the largest independent E&P, the Marathon Oil deal added scale, and the share count is down roughly 8-10% since FY2021 even after issuing stock for it. Q2 2026 showed how much operating leverage the business has to higher prices: operating income more than doubled year on year, FCF reached $4.4B, and the dividend was raised 7.7%.

What to Watch

Five-year growth is flat: operating income is slightly below FY2021 and FCF is down about a third, while capex has risen to ~21% of revenue and operating margin has slid from 33.9% to 20.85%. Earnings are a function of oil and gas prices, so the Brent ~$114 backdrop (Iran/Houthi-Saudi tensions) flatters the latest quarter and the forward P/E; if prices fall back, earnings and the multiple could move in opposite directions. The trailing P/E of 19.6x is well above the FY21-25 average, there is no insider anchor, and Q3 2026 results (Nov 5) are still to come.
