Palo Alto Networks is a real contrast to CrowdStrike: this is a company with genuine, sustained GAAP profitability (operating income turned positive in FY2023 and has grown every year since), a comfortable net-cash balance sheet, and a clear #1 or #2 platform position across network, cloud, and now identity security. What's Working

This is a genuinely, durably GAAP-profitable cybersecurity platform — operating margins have expanded every year since FY2023, revenue growth accelerated to +34% YoY in Q4 FY2026 on AI-security demand, and Next-Gen Security ARR is compounding at 63% YoY to $9.10B. The balance sheet remains net cash even after a $25bn acquisition, and CEO Nikesh Arora has recently been buying shares in the open market rather than selling. CyberArk adds a genuine #1-or-#2 identity security position on top of the existing network/cloud security leadership.

What to Watch

Valuation is the clear risk here: the current ≈62.3x P/FCF (estimated) is roughly 2x the company's own five-year average, even after the stock pulled back about 15% from its Aug 28 close. Share count is up about 40% over five years, and GAAP operating margin is only 5.0% in Q4 FY2026 (6.05% for the year) as CyberArk integration costs and intangible amortization keep weighing on reported profit. Q1 FY2027 results on Nov 18, 2026 will show how quickly margins normalize.
