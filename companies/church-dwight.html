Market position: Church & Dwight (CHD) is a mid-sized US consumer-staples company: ARM & HAMMER baking soda, laundry and cat litter, OxiClean, Batiste dry shampoo, Waterpik, TheraBreath, Hero and Touchland. Seven “power brands” make up roughly 70% of sales and profit. It leads several niches (baking soda, condoms, dry shampoo, acne patches, alcohol-free mouthwash, per third-party and company statements) but sits well behind P&G, Unilever and Colgate in the big categories (laundry, oral care).
Definition note: every figure is in US dollars. “EBIT” is GAAP income from operations; it includes large non-cash impairments in FY2022 ($411M FLAWLESS) and FY2024 ($357M vitamin-business trade name), which is why EBIT is flat over five years. Company “adjusted” figures (e.g. FY2025 operating income $1,171M, 18.9% margin; adjusted EPS $3.53) are shown separately and labelled. “Free cash flow” is operating cash flow minus capex. Annual and quarterly figures come from Church & Dwight's own earnings releases (SEC 8-Ks); the Q3’24 and Q1/Q3’25 and Q1’26 quarters are derived by subtracting reported half-year and nine-month totals.
Sleep exposure — a small slice, now gone. Church & Dwight's only sleep-related products were the sleep and melatonin gummies inside Vitafusion, its gummy vitamin brand (vitamins, minerals & supplements, “VMS”). The company says the whole VMS business was less than 5% of 2025 net sales (so under ~$310M); it does not disclose the sleep products separately. My own rough estimate (not company data): sleep gummies were well under 1% of FY2025 sales — likely a fifth or less of VMS. Vitafusion and L'il Critters were sold to Piping Rock Health Products on Dec 31, 2025, so from 2026 the sleep share of revenue is effectively 0%. Direct competitors in sleep/melatonin gummies: Olly (Unilever), Nature Made (Pharmavite) and Natrol (Jamieson Wellness). Compared with the other household-staples dashboards: P&G (dashboard) has the Vicks ZzzQuil sleep aid in its brand portfolio, inside a far bigger health-care business (sleep revenue not disclosed), and Colgate-Palmolive (dashboard) has no meaningful sleep product. Church & Dwight, founded in 1846 and led by CEO Rick Dierker, sells everyday household and personal-care products in the US (about 77% of sales), internationally (about 18%) and bulk sodium bicarbonate and animal-nutrition products (about 5%). Revenue grew from $5.19B in FY2021 to $6.20B in FY2025 (+4.6% a year) and free cash flow from $875M to $1.09B (+5.7% a year), but GAAP operating income was flat at ~$1.08B because of two big non-cash impairments. In 2025 it reshaped the portfolio: it bought Touchland (hand sanitiser and skincare; 2025 acquisition spend was $656M), then exited the vitamin business (Vitafusion/L'il Critters, sold Dec 31, 2025), FLAWLESS, SPINBRUSH and the WATERPIK showerhead line. Q2 2026 showed the benefit: organic sales +5.8% (volume +4.3%), adjusted gross margin 45.4% and a raised 2026 outlook (organic +4% to 5%, adjusted EPS +6% to 8%). The stock, up ~20% in a year, trades at ~34x trailing GAAP EPS and ~30x adjusted EPS, around its own five-year average on the adjusted basis, with consensus targets about equal to the price.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Growth is mid-single-digit at best — five-year revenue CAGR is +4.6% and reported FY2025 growth slowed to +1.6% as exited businesses (about $400M of 2025 sales) roll off, so reported 2026 sales are guided flat to +1%. (2) The sleep angle is historical — the sleep gummies left with Vitafusion on Dec 31, 2025; anyone looking for sleep exposure in CHD today will not find it (my estimate of the sleep share was well under 1% even before the sale). (3) Reported vs adjusted — GAAP EBIT, EPS and the entry-multiple test are distorted by impairments and deal-related charges; this page scores GAAP for growth and margin and flags where the adjusted view differs.

Quarterly Trend (Q3 2024 – Q2 2026)
Eight quarters. Reported growth is muted by the 2025 exits (VMS, FLAWLESS, SPINBRUSH, Waterpik showerheads) while organic growth has re-accelerated to ~5-6%. Q3 2026 is due Oct 30, 2026 — it has not been reported yet, so Q2 2026 is the latest quarter.
What the quarter actually showed

Q2 2026 net sales were $1,530.0M (+1.6% reported, ahead of the company's own guide of a 1% decline), with organic sales +5.8% (volume +4.3%, price/mix +1.5%): Domestic +5.1%, International +9.1%, Specialty Products +2.8%. Gross margin was 45.4% (up 240bps GAAP, 40bps adjusted); adjusted EPS was $0.89 versus $0.94 a year ago (-5.3%) because marketing (+40bps of sales) and Touchland amortisation and SG&A rose; reported EPS was $0.85 (+9%). Global e-commerce grew 22.7% and is 25.5% of consumer sales. The company raised its 2026 outlook and bought the MISS MOUTH'S stain-remover brand (~$300M cash paid in H1). The shares are up ~20% over 12 months.

What's Working

Cash conversion is excellent (FCF ~101% of EBIT, 17.6% of sales), the balance sheet is conservative (1.36x net debt/EBITDA), capex is ~2% of sales, gross margin has rebuilt by ~2.9pp since FY2022, and the share count is shrinking. After the 2025 portfolio clean-up, organic growth re-accelerated to +5.8% in Q2 2026 on volume, e-commerce is 25% of consumer sales, the 2026 outlook was raised, and the dividend has risen 30 years in a row.

What to Watch

Growth is low-to-mid single digit and reported sales are guided flat in 2026; GAAP EBIT is no higher than in FY2021; the market is crowded with larger, better-funded rivals; insiders own almost nothing; and the stock is at the top of its 52-week range with consensus targets near the price. Acquisitions (Touchland, MISS MOUTH'S) add integration and amortisation costs. Sleep is not a driver: the sleep gummies went with Vitafusion on Dec 31, 2025.
