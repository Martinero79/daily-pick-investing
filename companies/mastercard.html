Mastercard runs the same toll-booth business as Visa, just from the #2 seat: it carries no credit risk and holds no loan book, taking a small cut of every transaction that flows across its network. Gross dollar volume reached roughly $9.2 trillion in 2025 against Visa's ~$14.5 trillion — Mastercard is a genuine, strong #2 globally, not the #1, and that gap has been fairly stable even as Mastercard has quietly out-grown Visa on the income statement: revenue CAGR of 14.79% (FY21–25) sits just under this model's 15% bar, but EBIT (+17.24%) and FCF (+17.33%) both clear it comfortably — a cleaner growth profile than Visa's, where all three growth CAGRs now miss. Operating margin is essentially in the same elite tier as Visa's (59.17% FY25, up every year since FY21), ROIC is absurdly high on any measure (asset-light network economics plus a decade of buybacks that have shrunk book equity to almost nothing), leverage is trivial (0.43x EBITDA), and the entry multiple is actually more attractive than its own recent history: current trailing P/E of 31.85x sits meaningfully below the FY21–25 average of ~36.4x, versus Visa trading right at its own average. Mastercard and Visa form a real duopoly outside China (UnionPay) and away from Amex's closed-loop niche — a genuine moat, but the same slow-burn threat applies to both: real-time account-to-account rails (Pix in Brazil, UPI in India, FedNow in the US) are explicitly built to move money without touching either network, and Mastercard, like Visa, is racing to embed itself into that infrastructure (stablecoin settlement, the BVNK and Borderless.xyz deals) rather than fight it. No founder or controlling family sits behind Mastercard either — though unusually, the Mastercard Foundation, an independent global-development charity created at the 2006 IPO, still holds roughly 8.8% of shares, a legacy structure with no bearing on management control.

What's Working

A cleaner growth profile than Visa's: EBIT and FCF CAGRs both clear this model's 15% bar, and revenue CAGR (14.79%) missed by only a hair. Margin has expanded every year since FY21 with no litigation-driven dips. Leverage is trivial (0.43x EBITDA), capex is even lighter than Visa's, buybacks have shrunk the share count every year, and the entry multiple is a genuine discount to Mastercard's own recent history — unlike Visa, which trades right at its average. Strong Buy analyst consensus with ~15.8% upside to the average price target.

What to Watch

Mastercard is genuinely smaller than Visa — a strong #2, not the #1, in both global and US volume, and that gap hasn't meaningfully closed. Revenue growth (14.79%) is right at the edge of this model's bar and could easily fall below it in a slower year. No founder or controlling holder means governance rests entirely on the professional board (the Mastercard Foundation's 8.8% stake is philanthropic, not managerial). Most importantly, the same structural threat that hangs over Visa applies here with equal force: real-time account-to-account rails (Pix, UPI, FedNow) are explicitly designed to move money without touching either card network, and Mastercard's bets on stablecoin infrastructure (BVNK, Borderless.xyz) are a hedge against that future, not a guarantee it plays out in Mastercard's favor.
