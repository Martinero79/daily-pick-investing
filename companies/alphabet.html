What's Working

A genuine, durable near-monopoly in the single most valuable advertising surface on the internet, now paired with a Google Cloud business growing 82% year-over-year on AI infrastructure demand. Margins have kept expanding even through the current capex surge, the balance sheet is comfortably net cash, buybacks keep reducing share count, and the founders retain firm voting control despite owning a modest economic slice. The current multiple is historically cheap relative to Alphabet's own five-year trading range — a real, quantifiable margin of safety that's unusual for a mega-cap AI beneficiary.

What to Watch

The AI capex ramp is the dominant story here: capex jumped 74% in FY2025 and is guided to $175-185B for FY2026, which is pulling both capex intensity and FCF conversion below this framework's bars — a real cash-flow cost even if it doesn't show up in the income statement yet. Growth has also cooled from Alphabet's earlier hyper-growth years to a still-solid but sub-15% pace. Antitrust and regulatory pressure in the US and EU is an ongoing, non-trivial risk for a company this dominant in search — a $3.5B EU fine already hit Q3 2025 results directly.
