KLA Corporation, based in Milpitas, California, makes the inspection and measurement tools that chipmakers use to find defects and control variation while wafers are processed. It was formed by the 1997 merger of KLA Instruments and Tencor and has been the leader in this niche ever since; the idea is simple and economically sticky: as chips get smaller, stacked and more complex (leading-edge logic, high-bandwidth memory, advanced packaging), the share of capital spending that goes on finding yield-killing defects rises, and KLA sells the tools plus a growing, recurring services stream (service was $851m, about 21%, of Q1 FY2027 revenue). Segments for FY2026: Semiconductor Process Control $12.2B, Specialty Semiconductor Process $0.58B, and PCB, Display and Component Inspection $0.75B. The financial profile is unusual for hardware: gross margin has held at 60-62% for five years, GAAP operating margin reached 41.7% in FY2026 and 45.0% in Q1 FY2027, capex is below 3.5% of revenue and the business returned $3.79B to shareholders over the last twelve months. Growth is the debatable part: the 4-year revenue CAGR is a solid but not spectacular 10.2% because FY2024 fell 6.5% in the equipment downturn, but the last four quarters ramped from +7% to +29% YoY. The share price has moved far more than the business: after a 10-for-1 split in June 2026 the stock peaked at $307 on June 30 and then fell about 49% to $157.91 as chip-equipment stocks sold off in October on worries about AI-related capex, component supply constraints and valuation, even as KLA beat its September-quarter guidance.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Stock split — KLA split 10-for-1 on June 11, 2026; all prices, EPS and dividends here are split-adjusted, but some third-party pages still quote older unadjusted values. (2) Cycle — semiconductor equipment is cyclical (FY2024 revenue -6.5%, and Q2 FY2026 growth was only +7%), so a 4-year CAGR from FY2022 reflects one down year and a rebound, not a steady trend. (3) Price vs. financials — the FY2026 year-end P/E of 81.6x was computed at the June 30, 2026 peak price ($301.37) on trailing EPS of $3.66; that single point lifts KLA’s 5-year average P/E and makes today’s ~39.5x look closer to its history than the forward multiple (~29x) suggests. Latest Quarter & Momentum (Q1 FY2027, Sep 2026)
KLA reported Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended Sep 30, 2026) on Oct 28, 2026 after the close. Fiscal quarters: Q2 FY25 = Dec 2024 … Q1 FY27 = Sep 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Revenue was $4,136m (+28.9% YoY, +13.1% on the June quarter), above the $4.0B ± $0.2B guidance midpoint, with GAAP diluted EPS of $1.18 (non-GAAP $1.22) versus $0.85 a year earlier. GAAP operating income of $1,862m gives a 45.0% operating margin (gross margin 61.85%); a third-party summary cites a record 46.1% non-GAAP operating margin. Product revenue rose to $3,285m (+33% YoY) and service to $851m (+14%). Cash generation was strong in the quarter: operating cash flow $1.53B, free cash flow $1.34B (KLA’s figure) and $1.24B returned to shareholders ($936m of buybacks and $305m of dividends). The quarter also showed the build behind the growth: inventory rose to $4.09B from $3.65B in June and capex was $195m (4.7% of revenue, more than double the year-ago $96m). Management said it expects accelerated growth through the second half of calendar 2026 and into 2027.

Share Count & Buybacks

Basic shares fell from 1,513M (Dec 2021) to 1,305M (Sep 2026), -13.7%, and the YoY reduction was still -1.0% in Q1 FY2027 (S&P Global; all split-adjusted). Buybacks were $936m in Q1 FY2027 and total capital returns were $3.79B over the last twelve months. The quarterly dividend is $0.23 ($0.92 annualised, ~0.6% yield) after 16 consecutive years of increases (S&P Global); the FY2026 dividend per share was $0.80. Note that net share count reduction is smaller than gross buybacks because of stock compensation (tax-withholding payments were $239m in Q1 FY2027).

Founders, insiders & honest caveats: KLA has no founder or controlling shareholder; it is run by professional management (CEO Rick Wallace) and owned ~93% by institutions. S&P Global shows insider ownership of ~0.10%, which scores 0 on the Martinero band (below 1% and no verified employee ownership plan of ~5% or more). Equity awards and an employee stock purchase plan exist but I did not verify employee ownership as a share of the company, so no credit is given. The largest holders are, as far as I know, the big index managers; I did not verify a current top-holder list for this page.
Balance sheet & leverage: at Sep 30, 2026 KLA held $1.53B cash and $3.21B marketable securities against $5.72B long-term debt (plus a small current portion), total assets of $18.4B and stockholders’ equity of $6.5B; at Jun 30, 2026 net debt was $1.25B or 0.2x EBITDA. Interest cover is ~20x. Book equity is small because KLA has repurchased far more stock than it has issued, which is also why ROE (87.5%) and ROIC (66.8%) look extreme; both are accurate arithmetic but reflect leverage and buybacks, not just operating strength.

What's Working

Best-in-class economics: gross margin 60-62% for five years, GAAP operating margin 41.7% (45.0% in the latest quarter), ROIC far above WACC, net debt only 0.2x EBITDA and capex under 3.5% of revenue. KLA is a clear #1 in a concentrated niche with a recurring services stream, the share count is down ~14% since 2021 and the dividend has grown for 16 years. Momentum is improving (YoY growth +28.9% in Q1 FY2027; December-quarter guide +43% at the midpoint), the September quarter beat the guidance midpoint on every key metric, and the shares are about 49% below their June peak, with forward P/E of ~29x versus ~32x for its own FY-end average.

What to Watch

The business is cyclical and the stock is volatile (beta 1.45, -9.6% in a day on Oct 22): FY2024 revenue fell 6.5%, 4-year revenue and EBIT CAGRs are only 10-12%, and FCF CAGR is just 5.8% with FY2026 FCF flat despite higher earnings, because inventory ($4.09B) and capex are rising. Valuation still depends on the growth ramp: trailing GAAP P/E of ~39.5x is above the 5-year average of 37.0x and forward ~29x assumes consensus EPS growth. Demand is tied to AI-related capex, supply-chain bottlenecks and export controls on China, and customer concentration among a handful of chipmakers.
