Signet Jewelers is the world's largest retailer of diamond jewellery, a Bermuda-domiciled group with its operating base in the US that sells mainly through mall and off-mall stores under Kay, Zales and Jared (with Banter, Diamonds Direct, Peoples, H.Samuel and Ernest Jones), plus online through Blue Nile. FY2026 sales of $6.81B split roughly 42% bridal, 39% fashion, 5% watches and 12% services such as repairs and extended service plans (Fiscal.ai data via stockanalysis.com). It is a business that has been shrinking since the FY2022 peak (revenue −13% since) while earnings and free cash flow have been rebuilt through cost cuts, higher average unit retail (about +6-7%) and aggressive buybacks: the share count is down about a third since FY22. The Grow Brand Love strategy under CEO J.K. Symancyk is concentrating on Kay, Zales and Jared, closing stores (2,534 now vs 2,642 a year before), folding James Allen into Blue Nile and absorbing impairments on the digital brands. Q2 FY2027 (Jul 2026 quarter) delivered same-store sales +2.2%, adjusted operating income of $107.2M (+25% YoY, 7.0% margin) and adjusted EPS of $2.19 (+36%); full-year adjusted EPS guidance was lifted to $10.45-12.15 and the stock jumped 24% on the day. A new consumer-credit agreement with Bread Financial runs to calendar 2035 and adds an estimated $30-40M of non-comp profit in FY2027. The shares trade at ~13x GAAP and ~11x adjusted trailing earnings: cheap in absolute terms but above Signet's own historically very low GAAP multiples.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Post-COVID peak base — FY2022 (sales $7.83B, EBIT $903M, FCF $1.13B) was a boom year for engagements and discretionary jewellery, so every 4-year CAGR on this page is measured from an unusually high base. (2) Impairments — GAAP operating income carries $369M (FY25) and $91M (FY26) of mostly goodwill and brand impairments on Blue Nile and the digital brands, plus a $19M impairment in Q2 FY27 and a $19M equity-method write-off; both GAAP and adjusted figures are shown. (3) FY2024 tax benefit — GAAP EPS of $15.01 included a $4.94-per-share one-off deferred-tax gain from Bermuda's new corporate tax law, which flatters the FY24 P/E used in the own-history average.

Quarterly Trend & Latest Quarter (Q2 FY2027)
Signet's business is highly seasonal: Q4 (Nov-Jan, holiday) produces roughly a third of sales and most of the year's profit, so compare each quarter with the same quarter a year earlier. Q2 FY2027 (13 weeks to Aug 1, 2026) was reported Sep 9, 2026; Q3 FY2027 is expected around Dec 1, 2026.
What actually moved the stock

Sales were flat ($1,528.1M vs $1,535.1M, −0.5%) but profit was not: adjusted operating income rose 25% to $107.2M (7.0% margin vs 5.6%), adjusted EPS jumped 36% to $2.19 (vs $1.74 consensus per Google Finance) and GAAP operating income went from $2.8M to $87.5M as last year's $80M impairment lapped. Same-store sales were +2.2% with positive comps in every month and average unit retail +~6%; gross margin rose 80bp to 39.4%, helped by ~$15M of tariff refunds ($13M more than expected), and SG&A fell to 32.3% of sales on restructuring savings. Management lifted FY2027 adjusted operating income guidance to $535-605M (from $480-560M) and adjusted EPS to $10.45-12.15 (from $9.20-11.00), added a $125M accelerated share repurchase and enlarged the buyback authorisation to $700M. The shares rose 24% on Sep 9 (from $82.67 to $102.48), the biggest one-day gain in over a year. Operating cash flow was negative year to date (−$73.5M, seasonal) and Q2 free cash flow fell to $30.8M from $62.3M.

What's Working

Profit is recovering faster than sales: adjusted operating income grew 25% in Q2 FY27 on +2.2% comps, gross margin is at 39.4-39.6% and FY27 adjusted operating income is guided to $535-605M (versus $515M). The balance sheet has no financial debt, FCF was $525M in FY26 (134% of GAAP EBIT; ~12% of the current market cap), and the share count is down about a third since FY22 with another $125M ASR under way. The Bread Financial credit agreement is renewed to 2035 with a new profit-share, AUR is up 6-7%, higher-price-point unit growth is strong, the dividend has risen five years running, and the stock still trades at ~10-11x adjusted and ~9.7x forward earnings.

What to Watch

Top-line growth is absent: revenue is 13% below FY22, Q2 FY27 sales were −0.5%, the store base is shrinking (2,534 vs 2,642) and the Q3 guide is SSS −1% to +2% with adjusted operating income of only $31-48M. GAAP earnings carry recurring impairments (FY25 $369M, FY26 $91M, Q2 FY27 $19M plus a $19M investment write-off) from the Blue Nile / James Allen digital acquisition. Structural threats are lab-grown diamonds and record-low natural-diamond prices (deflation can cut average prices), higher gold costs, tariffs (refunds of ~$30M are a one-off), consumer-credit quality and mall traffic. Holiday quarter Q4 drives most profit, so a weak December can undo the year. High short interest (~13%) adds volatility, and the P/E is above its own GAAP history, so a lot of the recovery is already priced. Several inputs could not be verified: market-share percentages, insider ownership, individual shareholders and a clean Q3 FY25 revenue figure.
