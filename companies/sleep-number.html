Sleep Number

LISTING STATUS WARNING — CHAPTER 11 BANKRUPTCY, DELISTED FROM NASDAQ. Sleep Number filed for Chapter 11 on June 12, 2026 and agreed to sell substantially all assets and the ongoing business to a subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada (initial offer $415M; the court approved an improved bid on July 20, 2026, reported by Reuters at about $700M; closing was expected by July 31, 2026 and I could not confirm it). Nasdaq suspended the stock on June 23 and delisted it effective July 23, 2026. The shares now trade over the counter as SNBRQ (the “Q” suffix means bankruptcy) at about 2 cents. The company itself says that, at the sale price, the common shares are out of the money and would have no recovery. This page is an analysis of the business as it was, not a suggestion that the shares have value.

Sleep Number, founded in 1987 and led since April 2025 by CEO Linda Findley, sells its beds almost entirely through its own stores and website, which gave it high gross margins (~59%) but also a heavy fixed-cost base of leased stores, advertising ($197M in FY2025, down from $323M in FY2021) and a revolving credit facility. When the housing market and big-ticket furniture demand weakened, sales fell from $2.19B in FY2021 to $1.41B in FY2025 and operating income went from $194M to a $47M loss. Share buybacks funded with the revolver ($382M in FY2021 alone) left the company with a shareholders’ deficit and $588M of revolver debt due within a year. A new product line launched in 2026 (the ComfortMode bed was reported to be selling 3.5x plan) and $185M of annualized cost cuts were not enough: the 10-K carried going-concern doubt, lenders granted covenant relief and a $25M term loan in April, and on June 12, 2026 the company filed Chapter 11 to sell itself to Sleep Country Canada. Equity holders are not expected to receive anything.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Equity is the last claim in line — in a sale where debtor-in-possession and revolver lenders are paid first, the company says common shares have no recovery, so the 2-cent price reflects option value only. (2) The business is not the stock — the beds, stores and brand continue under new ownership; this scorecard rates the old public company’s numbers. (3) The score is honest, not harsh — a mechanical application of the site’s bands to negative growth, margins and equity gives 21/100 even with two criteria set to N/A.

What actually moved the stock

The stock collapsed from about $8 at the end of 2025 to cents as the lender and bankruptcy news arrived. Q1 2026 revenue was $319M (−18.9% YoY; the company had guided to the high teens), gross margin 57.9% (vs 61.2%) on discounting of legacy inventory, a net loss of $50M and adjusted EBITDA of just $6M (1.8% margin). Restructuring and non-recurring charges were $22M. Free cash flow was −$13M in the quarter. On April 28 lenders granted covenant relief and added $55M of liquidity (including a $25M term loan); the 10-K and 10-Q cited substantial doubt about the company’s ability to continue as a going concern. On June 11–12 the company announced a $415M stalking-horse sale to Sleep Country Canada and filed for Chapter 11 with up to $260M of debtor-in-possession financing; Nasdaq delisted the stock on July 23. Shares trade at ~$0.02 on the OTC market.

What's Working

The brand and product are intact: #1 in J.D. Power customer satisfaction for mattresses bought in-store, ~59% gross margin, 1,000+ patents and a new product line whose ComfortMode bed was reported selling 3.5x plan. Costs were cut by $185M annualized and capex fell to $14M. A strategic buyer (Sleep Country Canada) is taking over the business as a going concern, so customers, warranties and most store jobs continue.

What to Watch

For equity holders, everything: Chapter 11 with the sale price below the debt, delisting from Nasdaq, going-concern doubt, a −$578M shareholders’ deficit and a stated expectation of no recovery. Operationally, four years of sales declines (−35% in total), store traffic and comparable-store sales down 17% in FY2025, leverage that outran earnings, and heavy lease commitments. Several inputs are third-party or estimated: FY2021-24 financials, market share, ROIC/WACC, insider ownership, the reported ~$700M sale value, and whether the sale has closed.
