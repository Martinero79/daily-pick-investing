Sonova is the global #1 in hearing care, built around the Phonak (wholesale hearing instruments) and Advanced Bionics (cochlear implants) brands plus a fast-growing owned retail/audiological-care network. At roughly 31% global value share it narrowly leads #2 Demant A/S (≈30%) — the Danish Oticon maker already covered on this site and the closest sector comparable — in what both companies operate as a genuine five-player oligopoly (Sonova, Demant, WS Audiology, GN/ReSound, Starkey) controlling an estimated 99%+ of the prescription market. Unlike Demant, Sonova has no controlling family or foundation shareholder, but its pre-IPO founding shareholders (Beda Diethelm at 11.3%, the Rihs family at 6.2%) still hold a meaningful, disclosed 17.7% combined stake. The headline growth numbers look weak in reported Swiss francs — revenue CAGR of just 1.75% and a negative EBIT CAGR over FY2022–FY2026 — but that's overwhelmingly a currency story: local-currency sales growth was +5.9% in FY2025/26 alone, and the strong franc has been a persistent headwind against a USD/EUR-heavy revenue base for years. Where Sonova clearly separates itself from Demant is balance-sheet and capital-efficiency quality: net debt/EBITDA of just 1.1x (versus Demant's 3.0x post-KIND-acquisition), a company-disclosed ROCE of 19.0% comfortably above a ~7.5% WACC assumption, asset-light capex (~2.3% of sales), and gross margin that has actually expanded, not compressed, over the past three years.
Reported EBITA vs. GAAP EBIT — read carefully: Sonova's own headline profitability metric is EBITA (earnings before interest, tax and acquisition-related amortization), not GAAP EBIT. FY2025/26 reported EBITA was CHF 724.2m (20.1% margin); normalized/adjusted EBITA (excluding non-recurring legal, restructuring and impairment items) was CHF 811.2m (22.5% margin, comfortably above this framework's 20% screen). GAAP EBIT (after acquisition-related amortization) was lower at CHF 675.8m (18.7% margin). This dashboard's 5-year chart and CAGR figures (page 2) use a consistent GAAP-style "operating income" series sourced from S&P Global Market Intelligence via stockanalysis.com throughout, for year-over-year comparability — which is more conservative than the company's own adjusted-EBITA framing and is why criterion #3 (operating margin) scores as a near-miss rather than a clean pass.

FY2025/26 Headlines (reported 18 May 2026)

Group sales reached CHF 3,605.9m, up 5.9% in local currencies but down 0.2% in Swiss francs as FX cut CHF 221.0m off reported growth. Wholesale (Phonak) accelerated to double-digit local-currency growth in the second half on the Infinio Ultra and Virto R Infinio launches; Retail (audiological care) grew 5.1% LC; Cochlear Implants declined 11.1% LC on China volume-based-procurement headwinds and a competitor product launch. Forward P/E (21.69x) implies further earnings recovery is priced in; dividing it by Sonova's guided 7-10% constant-FX core EBIT growth puts the PEG around 2.2-2.5x — comfortably above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, so despite trading near its own historical multiple, the stock isn't cheap once growth is accounted for.
Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings. Trailing P/E (the FY22-26 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and better able to capture expected growth Sonova's FY2026/27 guidance (5-8% sales, 7-10% core EBIT at constant FX) implies. For Sonova, forward P/E (21.69x) sits meaningfully below trailing (25.75x), consistent with the market expecting earnings to recover as FY2025/26's one-off legal, restructuring and impairment charges roll off. The PEG ratio (forward P/E ÷ expected earnings growth) sanity-checks whether that gap represents genuine value: at roughly 2.2-2.5x, Sonova's PEG sits well above the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line — the stock is not demonstrably cheap once its own growth outlook is factored in, even though it trades close to (and slightly below) its own 5-year average multiple.

What's Working

Sonova is the global #1 in a genuine five-player oligopoly, with a materially stronger balance sheet and capital-efficiency profile than sector comparable Demant — 1.1x net debt/EBITDA versus Demant's 3.0x, a 19.0% ROCE comfortably above WACC, asset-light capex, and gross margin that has expanded rather than compressed. Local-currency demand has stayed genuinely healthy throughout (+5.9% FY2025/26), the trailing multiple sits modestly below its own 5-year average, and pre-IPO founding shareholders still hold a meaningful, disclosed 17.4% stake with no controlling block diluting alignment.

What to Watch

The reported-CHF growth and margin numbers are genuinely weak on this framework's terms — a sub-2% four-year revenue CAGR, a negative EBIT CAGR, and an operating margin that's a hair below the 20% screen — and while FX is the dominant driver, a persistently strong Swiss franc is a real, ongoing structural headwind that shows no sign of reversing. Cochlear Implants remains a genuine soft spot (China volume-based-procurement pressure, competitive product launches). The Consumer Hearing divestment adds a one-off complexity to year-over-year comparisons. And despite trading near its own historical multiple, the stock isn't demonstrably cheap on a growth-adjusted (PEG) basis.
