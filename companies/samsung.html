Samsung Electronics is really two very different businesses wearing one ticker. The Device Solutions division — memory chips (DRAM, NAND) plus a much smaller foundry unit — is in the middle of an extraordinary AI-driven upswing: Samsung reclaimed the #1 spot in global DRAM market share (39%, up 5.4 points YoY) and holds #1 in NAND (29%), and quarterly operating margin has rocketed from 6.27% (Q2 2025) to 52.18% (Q2 2026) as HBM and general memory pricing surged. The catch, and the reason this framework's five-year growth criteria mostly miss, is that FY2021-25 spans the last full memory down-cycle: FY2023's operating income cratered to just ₩6.57T (2.54% margin) from FY2021's ₩51.63T, dragging the trailing revenue and EBIT CAGRs to roughly flat or negative even though the business has since more than recovered and is now compounding at a pace unmatched anywhere else in this batch. Meanwhile Samsung's other bet — catching TSMC in leading-edge foundry — is not going well by the numbers: TSMC's foundry share actually widened its lead over Samsung's 6.5-7% share this past year, even as Samsung began 2nm mass production. This dashboard tries to hold both truths at once: a genuinely dominant, currently red-hot memory franchise, and a foundry ambition that remains a distant second place.

Governance note — a classic chaebol cross-shareholding pyramid: the narrow "insiders" ownership metric most trackers report for Samsung Electronics shows just 3.94%, which badly understates real control. The Lee family controls the entire Samsung Group not through direct Samsung Electronics shares, but through a chain: Lee Jae-yong and family hold roughly 31% of Samsung C&T (the group's de facto holding company), Samsung C&T is in turn the largest shareholder of Samsung Life Insurance (19.34%), and Samsung Life Insurance directly holds 8.51% of Samsung Electronics. This dashboard scores the ownership criterion on that more complete 8.51% figure rather than the narrow 3.94% headline stat, since it's the more accurate reflection of how control actually flows — but the structure itself, common across Korean chaebols, means minority shareholders are one more layer removed from direct founder accountability than at, say, Alphabet or Meta.

What's Working

Samsung is riding a genuine, dominant #1 position in memory straight into the strongest pricing environment the industry has seen in years, with margins expanding at an almost unprecedented pace and a net cash pile that dwarfs nearly everything else on this site. The current valuation, remarkably, has not kept pace with the earnings surge — the P/E has actually compressed even as the stock has nearly quadrupled.

What to Watch

Memory is a famously cyclical business, and FY2023 is a real, recent reminder of how quickly this exact profile can invert. The foundry bet against TSMC continues to lose ground rather than gain it (6.5-7% share, gap widening), capex intensity stays high, and this framework's trailing growth metrics will look very different a year from now depending on whether the current supercycle proves durable or is itself a peak.
