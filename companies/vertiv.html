Vertiv Holdings Co is a US-based supplier of critical digital infrastructure — the power and thermal-management equipment, software and services that keep data centres, telecom sites and industrial facilities running. It was formed from Emerson's former Network Power business, sold to Platinum Equity in 2016, and listed on the NYSE in 2020 via a SPAC merger. For most of 2021-22 it was a low-margin, heavily indebted company (operating margin 4-5%, net debt about $2.7-3.1B). Since then the AI build-out has transformed it: revenue went from $5.0B (FY2021) to $10.2B (FY2025) and, on Q4 2025 plus the first three quarters of 2026, to a $13.1B TTM; GAAP operating income went from $0.27B to $1.9B (and $2.8B TTM); free cash flow rose from $0.14B to $1.9B ($3.1B TTM); and net debt turned into net cash. Q3 2026 (reported Oct 28) was the strongest yet: sales of $4,313m (+61%), adjusted operating profit of $1,087m (+83%, 25.2% margin), organic orders more than double the prior year, and FY2026 guidance raised on every key metric (sales $14.45-14.85B, adjusted EPS about $7.15 at the midpoint). The share price rose 16.5% on the day, but it had fallen roughly 21% in the month before on concerns about debt-funded AI spending (press reports), and at $232.50 it is still about 39% below its 52-week high of $379.94. This is a high-beta stock whose fortunes depend on hyperscaler and AI-data-centre capital spending.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) Low base — FY2021-22 earnings were depressed (operating margin 4-5%), so the +62% EBIT CAGR and +93% FCF CAGR overstate the steady-state growth rate. (2) One-theme demand — the growth is tied to AI data-centre capital spending; I could not verify a customer-concentration figure. (3) GAAP vs adjusted — this page scores GAAP figures and the company's P/E history is flattered by low early-period earnings (see valuation).

What's Working

Revenue doubled and operating income grew roughly sevenfold over FY2021-25, with gross margin up 7.9 points since FY2022 and FCF now essentially equal to operating income (FY2025 conversion 99.7%). ROIC is 37.6% against a 14.9% estimated WACC, capex is ~2% of sales and the balance sheet moved from $2.7B net debt to $355m net cash. Q3 2026 sales grew 61% with organic orders more than doubling, and FY2026 guidance was raised on every key metric. The company is a top-two supplier in a structurally growing niche (data-centre power and cooling), and the trailing P/E is below its own history.

What to Watch

The business is tied to AI data-centre capital spending; the stock has a beta of 2.05, fell about 21% in the month before Q3 on debt-funded-AI worries (press reports), and is still ~39% below its 52-week high. The growth CAGRs start from a very low FY2021-22 base and Q4 guidance (organic +49-51%) leaves little room for a delivery miss. The trailing P/E 'discount' to history is a low-earnings artefact; the forward P/E (~25.8x) is slightly above its own average. Vertiv has no insider or founder block (0.19%), market concentration is moderate, and ~$1.45B of October acquisitions will take it back to net debt. Backlog and customer concentration were not verified; ROIC/WACC and market-position inputs are third-party or estimated.
