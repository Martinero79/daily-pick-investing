Chow Tai Fook Jewellery Group is a Hong Kong-listed jewellery retailer and brand founded in 1929, controlled by the Cheng family (via Chow Tai Fook Enterprises). It sells gold, gem-set, platinum and fixed-price jewellery and watches through company-operated stores and franchisees, with Chinese Mainland generating about 80% of FY2026 revenue (HK$76.0B of HK$94.4B) and Hong Kong, Macao and other markets the rest. The business has two very different engines: weight-based gold jewellery (about 64% of FY2026 product revenue), which is priced off the gold price and earns a thin margin, and fixed-price jewellery (about 32%), which carries a higher margin and is the focus of the multi-year “brand transformation” (Mainland fixed-price share of RSV 35.4% in FY2026, targeted at 45–50% by FY2030). FY2026 (year to 31 Mar 2026) was a record: revenue HK$94.4B (+5.3%), operating profit HK$18.85B (+27.8%), profit attributable HK$9.0B (+52.2%), and a dividend of HK$0.67 per share. The latest operating data (quarter to Sep 30, 2026) was mixed: group RSV was -0.5% year-on-year, with Mainland RSV -5.3% (store closures plus a high base) but same-store sales still up (+2.2% at Mainland self-operated stores, +29.6% in Hong Kong and Macao), and the company warned that net profit for H1 FY2027 should rise 90–100%, helped partly by a reversal in the fair value of gold-loan contracts. Read the profit growth with care: it rides on a gold price that rose 47.9% in FY2026 alone.
Three distortions to keep in mind: (1) Gold price — gold went from US$3,115 to US$4,608/oz during FY2026. That lifts revenue per gram and creates inventory gains inside gross profit (10.3% of FY2026 revenue), while volumes fall (Mainland same-store volumes -17.8%) and the company's gold-loan hedges lose money below the operating line (HK$6.3B). A falling gold price would reverse part of the margin gain. (2) Definitions — the company changed how it presents operating profit between FY2023 and FY2024, so the 5-year EBIT CAGR (17.2%) mixes two definitions. (3) Cash flow — FY2026 operating cash flow collapsed to HK$1.3B (from 10.3B) because inventory rose HK$8.3B and gold loans were net repaid, so FY2026 FCF of HK$0.7B says little about earnings power but is real cash absorbed.

What actually moved the stock

Group RSV in Q2 FY2027 was -0.5% year-on-year: Mainland RSV fell 5.3% (about 83% of group RSV) on a net closure of 240 Chow Tai Fook Jewellery stores in the quarter (263 closed, 23 opened) and a tough base (Q2 FY2026 same-store sales were +7.6%), while Hong Kong, Macao and other markets grew 33.2%. Same-store sales were better than RSV: +2.2% at Mainland self-operated stores, -2.9% at franchised stores, +29.6% in Hong Kong and Macao (Morgan Stanley called both Mainland and HK/Macao figures ahead of expectations). The new DAWN collection (launched April) generated nearly HK$2B of RSV in the quarter. The shares fell 3.4% on Oct 22, traded as low as HK$10.27 on Oct 23 (the 52-week low) and have since recovered to HK$11.81. Morgan Stanley noted same-store sales declined in October on a high base and Mid-Autumn timing, with the base effect easing from November.

Founders, insiders & caveats: S&P Global shows only 0.31% “insider” ownership because it counts individual directors, not the controlling family vehicles. High control means strong alignment but also a thin free float (~21%) and related-party considerations for minority holders.
Balance sheet & the gold-loan hedge (Mar-26): cash HK$8.3B; bank borrowings HK$4.8B; gold loans HK$13.7B (down from 15.9B); convertible bonds HK$7.2B (new); equity HK$32.0B; company-defined net gearing 54.4% (44.2% a year earlier). Inventory rose from HK$55.4B to HK$63.7B and inventory days from 320 to 364. Gold loans are borrowed gold used as stock: when gold rises, the loans lose value (fair-value loss HK$6.3B in FY2026, 6.6% of revenue) while inventory and gross profit gain. The hedge ratio by weight fell to 39.0% from 55.4%, so the group is now less hedged against a gold-price fall than a year ago. Profit before tax (HK$11.9B, 12.7% margin) is the figure after that cost.

What's Working

Profitability and returns have stepped up sharply: operating margin from ~10% to ~20% in four years, ROE 28.4% and ROIC far above WACC, with capex under 1% of revenue. FY2026 was a record on every profit line, brand-transformation products (fixed-price jewellery, DAWN, Palace Museum, Joie, Rouge collections) are gaining share of sales, Hong Kong, Macao and overseas are growing fast, and the controlling family owns ~73%. The shares trade at ~13x trailing and ~11x forward earnings versus a ~19x five-year average, with a ~5.7% dividend yield. The Mainland network is being pruned toward stronger stores: same-store sales are still rising and Mainland closures are expected to slow in FY2027.

What to Watch

Revenue is no larger than it was in FY2022 and Q2 FY2027 RSV was -0.5% with Mainland RSV -5.3% and Mainland franchised same-store sales negative. Margins and profit lean on a gold price that rose 48% in FY2026: a reversal would hit inventory gains while the hedge ratio is only 39%. FY2026 free cash flow was HK$0.7B on an HK$8.3B inventory build, net gearing rose to 54%, and the H1 FY2027 profit jump (+90-100%) partly reflects a non-operating fair-value reversal. Competition from newer gold-led Chinese brands, a thin free float (~21%) and the definitional changes in operating profit make year-on-year comparisons harder.
