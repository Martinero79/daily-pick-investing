Samsara Inc. (San Francisco; founded 2015 by Sanjit Biswas, CEO, and John Bicket; NYSE IPO Dec 15, 2021; reincorporated from Delaware to Nevada in 2026) sells the Connected Operations Platform to organisations that run physical operations: fleet telematics, AI dashcams and video-based safety, equipment and asset monitoring, site visibility and IoT sensors, with workflow apps and AI agents on top. Customers span transportation, construction, field services, wholesale and retail, utilities, public sector and others. The model is subscription: hardware (gateways, cameras, sensors) is installed and customers pay a recurring fee, so ARR reached $2.125B at Aug 1, 2026, up 30% for the third straight quarter, and subscription is about 98% of revenue. Revenue grew from $428M in FY2022 to $1,619M in FY2026 (+39% a year), gross margin has risen from 70.9% to 76.7%, and the GAAP operating loss shrank from 82% of revenue to 3%. The company posted its first GAAP net profit in Q3 FY2026 and four straight profitable quarters through Q2 FY2027, but the margin is thin ($4.9M of GAAP operating income in Q2 against $12.7M of interest income), stock-based compensation was $315M in FY2026 (19.5% of revenue, more than FCF of $207M), and the stock sits at about 12.5x trailing sales with a market cap of $23.1B. Q3 FY2027 results are due around Dec 3, 2026 and are not in this page.
The pitch, in one paragraph: an “undisputed market leader” with “churn virtually zero”, a “trillion data point dataset” and four straight GAAP profitable quarters. Checked against the data: What holds: four straight quarters of positive GAAP net income and EPS (Q3 FY26 to Q2 FY27) is correct, and the data asset is real and larger than the pitch says (30+ trillion data points collected per year, company-reported). What does not (or cannot be verified): Samsara does not publish churn or gross retention, only a ~115% dollar-based net retention target for core customers, which is not “virtually zero churn”; “undisputed leader” is contested (Geotab reports 6M+ connected vehicle subscriptions, and no independent market-share figure was found); and the four profitable quarters are net income, not operating income (three quarters, and Q1 FY27 includes a one-off $30.3M arbitration gain). A tenbagger from $23.1B would need about $231B of market cap. Not a recommendation. See Sections 4 and 6.

Latest Quarter (Q2 FY2027) & the GAAP-Profit Test
Quarter ended Aug 1, 2026, reported Sep 3, 2026. Q3 FY2027 (quarter ended Oct 31, 2026) is not yet reported; aggregators list it for about Dec 3, 2026 after the close (estimated). Nothing on this page includes it.
What the quarter actually showed

Revenue was $508.4M (+29.9%; +29% constant currency), ending ARR $2,124.7M (+30%) and net new ARR $134.1M (+28%; $485M over the last 12 months, +27% in constant currency). The big-customer engine is the story: 3,605 customers above $100K ARR (record +242 in the quarter) now represent $1.3B, or 63% of ARR (59% a year earlier), growing 38%; 210 customers above $1M ARR (+20, also a record) hold over $500M of ARR. Non-GAAP operating margin was 21% (+6pp YoY), GAAP operating income $4.9M (1.0%, from -$26.6M), GAAP EPS $0.03 and FCF $64.7M (13%). Management says “emerging products” (AI multicam, asset maintenance, site visibility and others) supplied more than 20% of net new ACV for a third quarter, and that multi-product use is rising (96% of $100K+ customers use two or more products; 72% use three or more). The stock opened at $44.90 on Sep 4, hit $45.12 intraday and closed at $40.20 (+3.7%); the 52-week high is $47.47 (date not checked) and the Nov 25 close of $39.43 is about 17% below it.

The “four straight GAAP profitable quarters” claim — HOLDS on net income, with caveats. GAAP net income: Q3 FY26 +$7.8M (the first positive quarter), Q4 FY26 +$22.0M, Q1 FY27 +$44.5M, Q2 FY27 +$16.2M; GAAP EPS was positive in each (Samsara's own release calls Q2 “the fourth consecutive quarter of GAAP profitability”). Caveats: (1) GAAP operating income was positive in only three of those quarters (Q3 FY26 was -$1.7M); (2) interest and other income ($11-13M a quarter, TTM about $83.7M) exceeds operating income ($19.4M TTM), so profit is partly interest on $1.3B of cash; (3) Q1 FY27 includes the one-off $30.3M gain; (4) FY2026 as a whole was still a loss (-$9.1M), so “profitable” means the last four quarters, not a full year yet. Management guides to GAAP profit for Q3 and for FY2027.
Guidance (given Sep 3, 2026): Q3 FY27 revenue $514-516M (+24%; +23-24% constant currency), non-GAAP operating margin 21%, non-GAAP EPS $0.18-0.19, GAAP profitable. FY2027 revenue $2.043-2.047B (+26%), non-GAAP operating margin 21%, non-GAAP EPS $0.76-0.78, GAAP profitable for the year. In March the FY27 guide was $1.965-1.975B (+21-22%) with 19% margin, so it has been raised. The Q3 guide implies only about +1.3% sequential growth from $508.4M; management says guidance is “de-risked” and the Q2 revenue beat was about 5% versus consensus.
Net retention & churn: Samsara says it achieved its “target dollar-based net retention rate of approximately 115% for core customers” (Q2 call). It does not report gross retention or a churn rate in the documents reviewed here; when an analyst asked about churn on the call, the CFO answered with the ARR mix of $100K+ customers (63%) rather than a churn figure. Net retention of ~115% nets expansion against churn and downgrades, so it cannot show churn is near zero.

Claim 1: “undisputed market leader” — PARTLY HOLDS, “undisputed” does not. Samsara has the largest disclosed recurring revenue of the telematics and video-safety names researched: ARR $2.125B against Motive's company-stated >$600M (about 3.5x) and Powerfleet's $444M of annual revenue, and it is growing at 30% at that scale. But (a) Geotab, which is private, reports 6M+ connected vehicle subscriptions, and Samsara's own filings reviewed here give no vehicle count, so a lead by vehicles is not shown; (b) no independent market-share table was found; (c) Samsara's ARR is about 1.2% of the $175B total addressable market cited by one broker (Jefferies, third-party estimate), so it is a leader in a market where most of the opportunity is not yet digitised, not a dominant incumbent; (d) Motive is growing at a similar pace and its $100K+ cohort reportedly grew nearly 60%. Scored as a contested #1/#2 (4/5, judgment).
Claim 2: “churn virtually zero” — DOES NOT HOLD AS STATED / UNVERIFIABLE. No churn rate, gross retention or logo-churn figure was found in the Q2 FY27 release, transcript or Q4 FY26 release. What exists is a dollar-based net retention target of about 115% for core customers. Net retention above 100% means expansion outweighs losses, which is good, but it is a net figure and says nothing about the gross churn that sits underneath it. The fastest-growing part of ARR is $100K+ customers (63% of ARR, +38%) and the sub-$100K base grows more slowly, which is consistent with strong expansion but not with “zero”. A cohort-level gross retention figure is the data that would settle this.
Claim 3: “trillion data point dataset” — HOLDS, and understates it, but data scale is not unique. Samsara says it now collects more than 30 trillion data points a year (Q2 FY27, up more than 40%), up from more than 25 trillion in the March release, plus 105 billion miles driven and 340 million workflows digitised over the last year. These are company-stated, unaudited operating statistics; “data points” are not defined. Geotab says it processes more than 100 billion data points a day, which is about 36.5 trillion a year (100B × 365, site arithmetic), so the scale is comparable at the leaders. The data matters for AI features, but dataset size alone does not prove a moat.
Claim 4: “four straight GAAP profitable quarters” — HOLDS on net income and EPS (Q3 FY26 to Q2 FY27); three quarters on operating income. See Section 2 for the quarter-by-quarter figures and caveats (interest income, one-off $30.3M gain, FY2026 full-year still a loss of $9.1M).

Tenbagger check
A six-point checklist for stocks that might multiply tenfold, tested against Samsara's numbers. Result: 2 pass (profitability flip; the P/S warning is not triggered), 1 partial (dilution), 3 fail (starting size, revenue acceleration, ROIC). These tests are separate from, and do not change, the 14-criterion scorecard.
1. Small starting cap (under ~$5–10B) — FAIL. Market cap is about $23.1B ($39.43 × 585.6M shares, Nov 25, 2026 close; a third-party page shows $22.7B), about two to four times the checklist ceiling. For context it was about $8.4B at the end of FY2022, $7.1B at FY2023, $17.6B at FY2024, $28.9B at FY2025 and $16.2B at FY2026 (S&P Global, fiscal year-end); the stock is about 17% below its 52-week high of $47.47. A tenfold from here implies a cap near $231B (arithmetic below, not a forecast).
2. Revenue acceleration toward 40%+ — FAIL. Year-on-year growth: FY2023 +52.3%, FY2024 +43.7%, FY2025 +33.3%, FY2026 +29.6%; by quarter Q4 FY25 +25%, Q1 FY26 +31%, Q2 FY26 +30%, Q3 FY26 +29%, Q4 FY26 +28%, Q1 FY27 +30.5%, Q2 FY27 +29.9%. ARR growth is steady at 30%. Growth is high and durable but flat, not accelerating, and below 40%; guidance is +24% for Q3 and +26% for FY2027. Positives that are not the same as acceleration: net new ARR growth of +28% and $100K+ ARR growth accelerating (38%).
3. Profitability flip (operating margin moving from negative toward positive, and when) — PASS (already happening, still thin). GAAP operating margin was -82.2% (FY22), -39.8% (FY23), -34.5% (FY24), -15.2% (FY25), -3.2% (FY26), +1.1% TTM and +1.0% in Q2 FY27; GAAP operating income has been positive for three quarters (first in Q4 FY26) and GAAP net income for four.
