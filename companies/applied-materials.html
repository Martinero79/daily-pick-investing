Applied Materials, based in Santa Clara, California and founded in 1967, makes the equipment that deposits, etches, planarises and modifies the materials inside a chip. Its pitch is “materials engineering”: as transistors move to gate-all-around (GAA) structures, memory moves to stacked DRAM and high-bandwidth memory (HBM), and chips are joined in advanced packages, more of the value sits in new materials and thin-film steps, which is where Applied is strongest. FY2026 was a record: revenue $34.45B (+21.4%), GAAP operating margin 32.2%, free cash flow $6.2B and $4.75B returned to shareholders ($3.18B of buybacks and $1.57B of dividends). The growth is lopsided toward AI-related demand: Semiconductor Systems revenue was $26.1B (63% foundry and logic, 30% DRAM, 7% flash), advanced-packaging revenue is expected to rise about 90% in calendar 2026, and management expects wafer-fab-equipment (WFE) demand to grow more than 30% again in 2027. China, which Reuters reports was nearly 40% of revenue in recent years, fell to 27% of FY2026 revenue ($9.3B) as US export rules narrowed what Applied can sell there, while a recovery in “ICAPS” (mature-node chips for internet-of-things, communications, automotive, power and sensors) is expected in 2027 and, per management, mainly outside China. The share price has moved far more than the business: from a $723.00 close on June 30, 2026 it fell to $370.50 as chip-equipment stocks de-rated on worries about the durability of AI capex, even though FY2026 and the Q4 results were records.
Three things to keep in mind: (1) China and export controls — China was 27% of FY2026 revenue and 25% of Q4; Applied paid a $253m settlement to the US Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) over an export-controls compliance matter in Q1 FY2026, and the company said in Nov 2025 that it can no longer serve parts of China’s memory and mature-node market. Future rule changes can move revenue. (2) GAAP vs non-GAAP — GAAP net income includes large swings in the value of strategic investments (a $653m loss in Q4, gains of $949m in the first three quarters), so GAAP EPS ($12.62) and non-GAAP EPS ($12.89) are close for the year but far apart in Q4 ($3.40 vs $4.16). (3) Price vs. financials — results are at record highs while the price is about 49% below its June close; the share-price chart and the P/E history reflect sentiment, not just earnings. Market share, ROIC/WACC and analyst targets are third-party or judgment and are flagged as such.

Applied reported Q4 FY2026 (quarter ended Oct 25, 2026) and the full fiscal year on Nov 12, 2026 after the close. Fiscal quarters: Q1 FY25 = Jan 2025 … Q4 FY26 = Oct 2026.
What the quarter actually showed

Revenue was a record $10,409m (+53.1% YoY, +14% on the July quarter), above the $10.25B ± $0.5B guidance midpoint. GAAP operating income was $3,658m (35.1% margin; gross margin 50.4%), GAAP EPS $3.40 and non-GAAP EPS $4.16 (+92% YoY) against guidance of $4.02 ± $0.20; the gap between the two is a $653m unrealised loss on strategic investments. Semiconductor Systems revenue rose 64% to $7,996m (58% foundry/logic, 33% DRAM, 9% flash) and AGS rose 26% to $1,896m. Operating cash flow was a record $3.30B and free cash flow $2.62B; Applied returned $2.42B, including $2.00B of buybacks, its largest quarterly repurchase in more than eight years. The balance sheet shows the build: receivables $9.0B (from $5.2B a year ago), inventory $6.9B, and Q4 capex of $684m. Management expects Semiconductor Systems revenue to grow more than 40% in calendar 2026 and WFE demand to grow more than 30% in 2027, but also says supply chain and clean-room capacity gate how fast the industry can grow.

CapEx ($m) & CapEx Intensity (% of Revenue)

Capex fell sequentially to $684m in Q4 FY26 from $707m in Q3. That is moderate for an equipment maker but no longer asset-light.

Buybacks & Dividend

FY2026 shareholder returns were $4.75B: $3.18B of buybacks and $1.57B of dividends, about 77% of FCF ($6.20B); management targets 80-100% of FCF over time. Q4 alone had $2.00B of buybacks, the largest in more than eight years, with $10.8B left on the authorization. Basic shares fell from 871M (FY2022) to 793M (FY2026), -9.0%, and the YoY reduction was -1.1% in the latest quarter; stock compensation and tax-withholding payments ($570m in FY2026) offset part of the gross buyback. The quarterly dividend is $0.53 (raised 15% from $0.46 in May 2026, the ninth straight annual increase), $2.12 annualised, a yield of ~0.57% and a payout of ~16% of earnings.

Founders, insiders & honest caveats: Applied has no founder or controlling shareholder; it is run by professional management (CEO Gary Dickerson) and owned ~83% by institutions. S&P Global shows insider ownership of ~0.26%, which scores 0 on the Martinero band (below 1% and no verified employee ownership plan of ~5% or more). Applied also holds minority strategic investments, notably in BE Semiconductor Industries (Besi), where an accelerated share purchase agreement is meant to lift its stake to about 20%; these are marked to market through GAAP earnings. I did not verify a current top-holder list for this page.
Balance sheet & net cash / debt: at Oct 25, 2026 Applied held $7.24B cash, $2.10B short-term and $4.76B long-term investments (total $14.1B) against $6.53B of debt ($1.30B short-term debt, $5.23B long-term), total assets of $45.4B and equity of $26.1B. Net cash is $2.8B on cash and short-term investments (S&P basis) or $7.6B including long-term investments, up from $2.0B at Oct 2025 on the first basis. Working capital rose sharply: receivables $9.0B (from $5.2B), inventories $6.9B (from $5.9B), contract liabilities $3.6B; the build is why FY2026 FCF ($6.2B) lagged EBIT ($11.1B). Debt has been roughly flat at $6.5B for five years.

What's Working

Record FY2026 with revenue +21.4%, GAAP operating margin 32.2% (35.1% in Q4) and gross margin up 3.3pp in three years to 50.0%. Applied is positioned in the AI-driven parts of WFE (leading-edge logic, DRAM/HBM, advanced packaging, e-beam inspection) and guides Q1 FY2027 revenue +58% YoY at the midpoint, with management seeing WFE up more than 30% again in 2027 and customers giving rolling eight-quarter forecasts. Services are 20% of revenue at a 35% gross margin and growing double digits. Net cash is $2.8B ($7.6B including long-term investments), ROIC is far above WACC, $4.75B went back to shareholders in FY2026 and the dividend has grown nine years in a row. China fell to 27% of revenue while the total grew, and forward P/E (19.3x) is about in line with its own FY-end average.

What to Watch

The 4-year record is modest: revenue CAGR +7.5%, EBIT CAGR +9.2% and FCF CAGR +7.7%, with three flat years before FY2026, and equipment demand is cyclical and concentrated in a handful of foundry and memory customers. FCF conversion fell to 56% as receivables, inventory and capex (7.8% of revenue) rose; trailing GAAP P/E of 29.4x is 40% above its own FY-end average. China is 27% of revenue and exposed to export-control changes (a $253m BIS settlement in FY2026; management said in Nov 2025 that it cannot serve parts of China’s memory and mature-node market). The stock is volatile (beta 1.67; -13.6% in a day on Nov 5, +11.4% on Nov 9) and about 49% below its June close; the +53% Q4 growth compares with a depressed year-ago quarter; supply and clean-room capacity may limit shipments; and GAAP EPS swings with the value of strategic investments.
