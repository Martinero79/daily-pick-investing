Citigroup is the third-largest U.S. bank by assets (~$2.9tn) and the most global of the big American lenders, with a leading cross-border Services franchise (Treasury & Trade Solutions, Securities Services) and a top-three Markets business. It is also mid-restructuring: the multi-year transformation to simplify the firm has exited Asia consumer banking, and Citi has agreed to sell a 25% stake in Banamex ahead of a planned deconsolidation expected in 2027. The honest case for including it on a growth/AI-tilted scorecard is a self-help turnaround rather than growth: revenue compounded at only ~4.4% over FY2021–FY2025 and net income to common fell from $20.8bn to $13.0bn (FY21 was inflated by large loan-loss-reserve releases), RoTCE was just 7.7% in FY25, but it has climbed to 11.5% in Q3 2026 (12.6% year to date) and management guides above 11% for the year with a path to 14–15%. The efficiency ratio fell from 71.8% (FY23) to 64.7% (FY25) and 58.5% in Q3 2026, and Citi returned $17.6bn in FY25 (133% of earnings). The catch is the price: at ~11.8x trailing earnings and ~1.19x tangible book value the stock trades well above its own FY2021–FY2025 range (~10.7x P/E average, ~0.78x P/TBV), so much of the turnaround is already reflected in the share price.
Framing note — why this scorecard looks different for a bank: Citigroup is the second large U.S. bank on this site after JPMorgan, so the same bank substitutions apply, all flagged inline: Total revenues, net of interest expense stand in for revenue (criterion 1); Net income attributable to common substitutes for both EBIT (criterion 2) and Free Cash Flow (criterion 4) — a bank's operating cash flow is dominated by deposit and trading-book swings, so the two criteria are correlated by construction; RoTCE vs. an assumed ~10–11% cost of equity substitutes for ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7); CET1 ratio vs. regulatory requirement substitutes for net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8); (dividends + buybacks) ÷ net income substitutes for FCF conversion (criterion 9); and the efficiency ratio replaces gross margin for margin stability (criterion 14, lower ratio = better, bands inverted). Entry multiple (criterion 10) uses P/E and P/TBV. Everything is in USD; nothing is converted.

Market position, honestly stated: Citigroup is the third-largest U.S. bank by assets (~$2.9tn at Sep 30, 2026) behind JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America, so criterion #5 (#1 or #2) is Not Met on the standard assets measure — even though Citi is a top-three global markets franchise, a leader in cross-border payments and trade finance, and has the largest U.S. general-purpose card portfolio of its peers outside JPMorgan/Amex-type issuers. U.S. banking is a competitive top tier (JPM, BofA, Wells Fargo, Citi, plus Goldman/Morgan Stanley in markets) over thousands of smaller banks and fintechs, so concentration is scored 3/5 as for JPMorgan. Citi's G-SIB status also means a higher capital surcharge than most peers, which is why its CET1 cushion above requirement is comparatively thin even at a 12.8% ratio.

Reading the valuation: At ~11.8x trailing earnings and ~1.19x tangible book value, Citigroup trades above its own FY2021–FY2025 year-end averages on both measures (P/E ~10.7x, range 6.0x–16.7x; P/TBV ~0.78x, range 0.55x–1.20x). The ratio to own history is ~1.10 on P/E and ~1.52 on P/TBV; the scorecard uses their average (~1.31), which lands just over the 1.30 line for a score of 0. Context matters: FY21–FY23 multiples were depressed by weak returns (RoTCE 5–13%) and an unresolved restructuring, so a re-rating is partly earned; also, FY25 year-end (16.7x P/E) was inflated by an earnings trough. Governance & Capital Notes

No founder-owner or controlling shareholder — Citigroup is the product of the 1998 Citicorp–Travelers merger and decades of restructuring, and its directors and executives together own well under 1% (Chair and CEO Jane Fraser holds a small stake relative to the ~$200bn market cap). Under a 2020 pair of regulatory consent orders, the bank has been rebuilding risk-management and data infrastructure (a multi-year transformation with associated expense). On the restructuring: the Asia consumer businesses have been sold or wound down; Citi sold a 25% stake in Banamex (Mexico) and expects to deconsolidate the rest, with the Mexico consumer/SBMM business shown in All Other (Legacy Franchises). What's Working

A genuine self-help turnaround is showing up in the numbers: efficiency ratio 71.8% (FY23) → 64.7% (FY25) → 58.5% (Q3 2026), RoTCE 4.9% → 11.5%, and record-for-a-decade revenue with positive operating leverage across segments. Capital return is large ($17.6bn in FY25, 133% of earnings) and the share count is down ~9% since FY21, with the dividend raised 12% in Q3 2026. Services and Wealth are growing mid-teens and the exits (Asia consumer, Banamex) shrink complexity.

What to Watch

Returns are still only just reaching the cost of equity: FY25 RoTCE 7.7% is below the ~10–11% hurdle used here, and growth (~4.4% revenue CAGR) misses the 15% bar. The stock has re-rated to ~1.2x tangible book vs. a ~0.78x five-year average, so execution is largely priced in; it fell 5.6% on a revenue beat in Q3 2026. CET1 headroom (~120bp) is thin for a G-SIB paying out >100% of earnings, capital rules remain uncertain, and Banamex deconsolidation, consumer-credit losses ($2.2bn quarterly net credit losses) and the transformation/consent-order work remain live risks. Insider ownership is negligible.
