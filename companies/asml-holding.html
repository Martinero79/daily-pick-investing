ASML is the sole supplier of EUV lithography systems — 100% of EUV, ~83–94% of lithography overall — making it the irreplaceable chokepoint behind every leading-edge chip, including the ones powering the AI buildout. The model's own numbers say margins, ROIC and balance-sheet strength all clear the bar comfortably, and revenue and EBIT growth still clear the 15% bar too, though by a slimmer margin once the 5-year window is correctly measured from FY2021 rather than the artificially-strong FY2020 base. Gross margin has also expanded (50.5% FY22 to 52.8% FY25), the new margin-stability criterion. Two things now miss: valuation, and — on a strict 5-year reading — free cash flow CAGR, which comes in at just 5.4% because FY2021 was itself inflated by one-off customer prepayments (the same dynamic that produced the FY23 FCF dip visible in the chart); FCF has actually grown solidly on a smoothed basis, but the CAGR math is distorted by an unusually strong starting year, not a deteriorating cash-generation trend. The stock is up over the past year and trades well above its own trading history on both earnings and free cash flow — you'd be paying a materially higher multiple than ASML has commanded for most of the last five years. Insider ownership is also negligible (0.01%), though this is a professionally-governed Dutch public company rather than a founder- or parent-controlled one, so it reads differently than Arm's or Nvidia's version of the same miss.

Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot. Trailing P/E (the quarterly series above) is that same backward-looking ratio recalculated at each quarter-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of (non-GAAP) earnings — forward-looking, and generally the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where earnings are headed. For ASML specifically, the consensus FY2026 forward P/E (≈36.6x) sits well below the current trailing P/E (≈55x) — the market is pricing in a large step-up in earnings (EPS growth guided at ~55% for FY2026 on the High-NA EUV ramp), and that expected growth, not multiple expansion, is what's meant to close the gap. If that growth doesn't show up, the trailing multiple has much further to re-rate down than the forward multiple suggests. The PEG ratio (forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth) is normally a handy gut-check here, but ASML's near-term earnings are lumpy around the High-NA EUV ramp rather than growing at a smooth annual clip, so data providers generally don't publish a reliable PEG for it right now — the honest takeaway is to lean on the trailing-vs-forward P/E gap above instead of a single growth-adjusted number.

What's Working

Revenue and EBIT growth, profitability, ROIC, balance-sheet strength, market position and FCF conversion (FY25 basis) all clear the bar; the closest thing to a genuine monopoly in this whole screen (score 1/5); net cash and share buybacks both healthy; margins have stayed remarkably stable through a volatile revenue cycle.

What to Watch

Valuation is stretched and getting more so — P/E and P/FCF are both roughly 55–70% above their own 5-year averages after a 140% 52-week run; the 5-year FCF CAGR (5.4%) is a base-effect artifact of FY2021's prepayment-swollen cash flow, not a demand problem, but it's a genuine model miss as measured; quarterly FCF and net cash are genuinely volatile (prepayment timing), don't over-read a single quarter; Canon nanoimprint and China's SMEE are real but currently distant threats, worth monitoring rather than acting on; concentration risk if you already hold Nvidia or other AI-infrastructure names — same cycle, adjacent layer of the stack.
