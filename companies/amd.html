Market position, honestly stated: AMD is a strong, improving #2 nearly everywhere it competes — never the outright #1. In GPUs and AI accelerators the gap is far wider: Nvidia holds an estimated 73-80%+ of AI-accelerator revenue, with AMD's MI300X/MI350X/MI400 (“Helios”) roadmap capturing only roughly 5-7%, mostly in inference workloads where Nvidia's software-stack advantage (CUDA) matters less than in training. AMD clears this framework's “#1 or #2” bar in both categories, but the honest read is a fast-improving #2 in CPUs and a distant, credible-but-small #2 in AI accelerators.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY21-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are actually headed, since it accounts for expected growth (or decline) that trailing P/E can't see. For AMD specifically: the trailing P/E series is dominated by one outlier — FY2023's 278.85x reflects a temporary earnings trough, not genuine over-valuation — which makes the "FY21-25 average" a noisy yardstick. Forward P/E (43.00x) is a cleaner read and sits meaningfully above its own FY21-25 average of roughly 35.55x, suggesting the market is paying up for the AI-accelerator growth story rather than finding a bargain. One more lens worth a glance: the PEG ratio, forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether that premium multiple is justified by growth. AMD's PEG currently runs around 0.65 — comfortably under the traditional 1.0 "fair value" line, which is the main reason the entry-multiple criterion still scores as borderline-acceptable despite the high absolute P/E: the market's growth expectations, if met, would make today's price look reasonable in hindsight.
Balance sheet: AMD has held a net cash position — not net debt — in every one of the last five fiscal years: $2.95B (FY2021), $2.99B (FY2022), $2.77B (FY2023), $2.92B (FY2024), and $6.55B (FY2025), rising further to $8.84B on a current basis. Gross debt/EBITDA has stayed low throughout (0.16x-0.75x), so net debt/EBITDA is negative (net cash) every year — comfortably clearing this framework's <2x bar. Set against that clean balance sheet, ROIC has been weak since the Xilinx/Pensando deals closed in FY2022 — 4.49% (FY2022), 0.77% (FY2023), 3.14% (FY2024), 6.73% (FY2025), 9.77% on a current TTM basis — well under both the assumed 11.5% WACC and the framework's 15% absolute bar; the pre-deal FY2021 figure (72.76%) is not a fair comparison since the capital base ballooned with the acquisitions. Share count rose sharply from roughly 1.23B (FY2021) to about 1.57B (FY2022) as Xilinx was an all-stock deal, drifted up further to around 1.64B (FY2024), then edged down slightly to about 1.64B (FY2025) as buybacks modestly outpaced new issuance — net dilutive over the full window, though close to flat in the most recent two years.

What's Working

Revenue growth is real and re-accelerating (+34.34% FY2025, +50% YoY in the latest quarter), free cash flow is growing even faster (20.3% five-year CAGR, 180% FCF/EBIT conversion), the balance sheet carries net cash every year with no leverage risk, capex intensity is low thanks to the fabless model, gross margin has actually improved slightly over the window, and AMD holds a genuine, growing #2 position in both x86 CPUs and AI accelerators.

What to Watch

Profitability has not kept pace with revenue: operating margin (10.80%) remains under half this framework's bar and well below AMD's own pre-acquisition FY2021 level, and ROIC (6.73%-9.77%) clears neither the assumed WACC nor the 15% bar — a lingering effect of the goodwill-heavy Xilinx/Pensando deals. Share count is up roughly a third since FY2021 from that same M&A, insider ownership is thin (Lisa Su holds only ~0.5%), and in the fastest-growing part of the business — AI accelerators — AMD remains a distant #2 to Nvidia's 73-80%+ share. Whether margin recovery and AI-accelerator share gains continue at the current pace is the central question from here.
