COSCO SHIPPING Holdings is the world's fourth-largest container liner by capacity (≈10.7% share, per Alphaliner) and the container-shipping arm of a Chinese state-owned conglomerate, dual-listed on the Hong Kong (1919.HK) and Shanghai (601919.SS) exchanges. Its five-year window (FY2021-25) captures almost the entire pandemic-era freight-rate supercycle in one framework: revenue peaked at ¥390.8B in FY2022 on record spot rates, then collapsed -55% in FY2023 as rates normalized, before a partial, uneven recovery to ¥219.5B in FY2025 — still 44% below the FY2022 peak. Every growth-rate criterion in this scorecard (#1, #2, #4) and the latest-FY operating margin (#3) are consequently deeply negative or below-bar, not because the underlying business deteriorated structurally, but because FY2021-22 set an extraordinary, temporary high-water mark that FY2025 was never going to match. What holds up is the balance sheet and capital-return story: COSCO has stayed net-cash through the entire cycle, cut its share count by ≈6.5% via buybacks, and pays one of the highest dividend yields in this shipping batch (≈7.6-8.8%, though a payout ratio above 100% in the trough year means the dividend isn't currently earnings-covered). Ownership is unusual for this site: COSCO SHIPPING Group, itself wholly state-owned and SASAC-supervised, holds ≈44% — comfortably meeting this framework's "meaningful ownership" bar, but that is state industrial-policy alignment, not a founder's personal capital at risk. On valuation, the current trailing P/E (≈9.6x) sits roughly 2.1x the company's own FY2021-25 average (4.54x) — but that average was itself compressed by the supercycle's enormous, temporary earnings, so a "cheap-looking history, expensive-looking present" read here says more about how extraordinary FY2021-22 was than about today's price being unusually rich in absolute terms.

What's Working

The balance sheet and capital-return story is genuinely solid: a net cash position maintained throughout the entire down-cycle, an active buyback program that has cut the share count by ≈6.5% since FY2021, and one of the highest dividend yields in this shipping batch. The industry itself is a reasonably concentrated, alliance-structured oligopoly (top 4 carriers ≈58% of capacity) with high barriers to entry, and COSCO's 44% state-backed ownership provides strategic continuity and access (e.g., its pursuit of a stake in Panama Canal port assets) that a purely private competitor might lack.

What to Watch

Every growth and margin metric in this scorecard is still working through the aftermath of the 2021-22 freight-rate supercycle and 2023 crash — revenue, EBIT and FCF are all deeply negative on a 5-year CAGR basis, and the latest-FY operating margin sits well under this framework's 20% bar. FCF conversion and capex intensity both miss, reflecting a genuinely capital-intensive fleet-renewal cycle. Valuation looks expensive relative to the company's own (supercycle-depressed) history, though less so in absolute terms. As a Chinese SOE dual-listed in Hong Kong and Shanghai, COSCO also carries governance, transparency and geopolitical/sanctions-scrutiny considerations that don't apply to most other names on this site — worth weighing independently of the numeric scorecard.
