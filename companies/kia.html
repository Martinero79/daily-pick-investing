Kia is the smaller half of the Hyundai Motor Group's car business: about 3.1 million vehicles a year, record Q2 2026 sales and revenue, a global market share of roughly 4% (a company-stated all-time high), and a product mix that is moving toward hybrids and EVs (electrified vehicles were 35.3% of Q2 2026 sales). From FY2021 to FY2025 revenue compounded at about 13.1% a year (₩69.9T → ₩114.1T) and operating income at about 15.7% (₩5.1T → ₩9.1T), but the path was not smooth: operating margin rose from 7.3% to a peak of 11.8% in FY2024, then fell to 8.0% in FY2025 and 6.8% on a trailing basis as US tariffs on Korean-built cars and higher sales incentives hit profit even while revenue kept growing. The balance sheet is the strongest part of the case: Kia holds ₩21.3T of net cash (June 2026) against a ₩43.8T market cap, pays a ₩6,800 dividend (about 6% yield, 37% payout) and has trimmed its share count by about 3% since FY2021. The stock trades at roughly 6x trailing and 5x forward earnings, below the market, but that is a cyclical, low-margin industry where low multiples are the norm, and the current multiple is about 19% above Kia's own FY2021-25 average of 5.2x. Free cash flow has not kept up with profit: FY2025 FCF of ₩5.3T was 58% of operating income and 41.7% lower than FY2024 as capex rose to ₩3.8T. 6 of 14 criteria met, Martinero Index 50/100.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E (current only)
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings; the trailing-P/E series above is that same ratio at each fiscal year-end. Forward P/E divides today's price by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and the more useful lens for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where earnings are headed. For Kia: trailing P/E has been in a 4.0–6.9x band for five years, so a rise from 4.0x (FY2024) to about 6x partly reflects falling earnings rather than a rising price (the stock is roughly flat versus end-FY2025 at ₩116,678). The vendor's PEG ratio is 0.57 (Sep 29), based on a 3-year EPS growth forecast of 9.5%. Korean automakers have historically traded at a persistent discount to global peers (the so-called Korea discount, along with cyclicality, tariff exposure and a controlling-shareholder structure), so a low absolute multiple is not by itself a signal of cheapness.
Balance sheet: at Jun 30, 2026 Kia held ₩23.55T of cash & investments against ₩2.25T of debt — net cash of ₩21.29T, or about 49% of the current market cap, and total debt has fallen from ₩9.6T at end-FY2021 to ₩2.7T at end-FY2025. Net debt/EBITDA is negative (net cash), so criterion #8 is a clear Met. The dividend was raised to ₩6,800 per share for FY2025 (+4.6%, 37% payout on TTM earnings, about 5.9% trailing yield) and the vendor reports a 1.0% buyback yield. Price/book is about 0.70x. Keep in mind that a large net cash balance on a cyclical automaker is partly a buffer for tariffs, EV investment and Korean labour-cost risk (a tentative wage deal averted partial strikes in August 2026).

What's Working

Revenue is at a record (Q2 2026 +12.6% YoY) and growing at a rate above the industry, with market share at a company-stated high near 4%; the electrified mix has reached about a third of sales. The balance sheet holds ₩21.3T of net cash, about half the market cap, with debt down from ₩9.6T to ₩2.3T in five years. Operating margin has recovered for three straight quarters from its Q3 2025 low, the dividend has risen four years in a row, the share count has drifted down, and the stock trades at roughly 6x trailing and 5x forward earnings. A controlling parent provides a stable anchor.

What to Watch

Profit is shrinking even as revenue grows: FY2025 operating income fell 28% and the FY2025 operating margin (7.95%) is far below the 20% screen. Tariffs and incentives are the main drivers and could persist; FCF fell 42% in FY2025 as capex rose, and FCF conversion has dropped from above 100% to 58%. Gross margin is down 1.1pp over three years, ROIC is falling, and a low P/E is the norm for cyclical automakers (the multiple is about 19% above Kia's own five-year average). Kia is not a top-five global automaker, competes with fast-moving Chinese brands, faces periodic Korean labour disruption, and its controlling-parent structure means minority holders have limited influence. Q3 2026 results (Oct 26) will show whether the margin recovery continues.
