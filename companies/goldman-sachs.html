Goldman Sachs is one of the world's leading investment banks. Its earnings come mainly from Global Banking & Markets (advisory, underwriting, FICC and equities trading; $41.5bn of $58.3bn FY25 net revenue) and Asset & Wealth Management ($16.7bn). That is a very different model from a spread bank such as JPMorgan or Bank of America: revenue is dominated by fees and trading, so it swings with capital-markets activity. Net revenue fell from $59.3bn (FY21, a record capital-markets year) to $46.3bn (FY23) before recovering to $58.3bn in FY25 — which is why the 4-year revenue CAGR is slightly negative (−0.4%) and the net-income-to-common proxy CAGR is −6.3% off a strong base year. The recent run is much stronger: Q3 2026 net revenue of $18.7bn was up 23% YoY, the company-reported 9M'26 ROE is 20.7%, and the 9M'26 efficiency ratio improved to 59.4% from 62.1%. Capital return is heavy (FY25 buybacks of ~$14.5bn plus an estimated ~$4.4bn of common dividends, ~116% of net income to common), and period-end shares are down ~12% since FY21, but CET1 has moved down to a preliminary 12.4% in Q3'26 and the stock trades at ~2.35x tangible book against a FY21–FY25 average of ~1.68x.
Framing note — why this scorecard looks different for a bank: the standard revenue/EBIT/FCF/net-debt framework does not map cleanly onto a bank, so this page uses the same substitutions as the JPMorgan Chase dashboard, all flagged inline: Total Net Revenue stands in for revenue (criterion 1); Net Income to common substitutes for both EBIT (criterion 2) and Free Cash Flow (criterion 4) — a bank's cash flow statement is dominated by deposit and trading-book swings, so criteria 2 and 4 are necessarily correlated here, a genuine limitation of adapting the framework; ROTCE vs. an assumed ~10.5% cost of equity (mid-point of 10–11%) substitutes for ROIC vs. WACC (criterion 7); CET1 ratio vs. the regulatory requirement substitutes for net debt/EBITDA (criterion 8) — points are banded on the CET1 buffer over an estimated requirement (≥3.0pp = 5, 2.0–3.0 = 4, 1.0–2.0 = 3, 0.5–1.0 = 2, 0–0.5 = 1, below = 0; Met = buffer ≥1.0pp), and the requirement itself (4.5% + stress capital buffer + G-SIB surcharge) is the author's estimate, not a quoted figure; (common dividends + buybacks) ÷ net income to common substitutes for FCF conversion (criterion 9); and entry multiple (criterion 10) is the average of two ratios — current P/E and current P/TBV, each divided by its own FY21–FY25 average of year-end values. Criterion 14 uses the change in the efficiency ratio FY22→FY25 (a falling ratio counts as a rising margin) because banks report no gross margin. Goldman-specific: GS is an investment bank, not a deposit-led spread lender — trading and advisory fees make revenue and earnings far more cyclical than at JPMorgan, Bank of America or Wells Fargo, so the growth criteria (1, 2, 4) and the efficiency ratio read very differently here. Net revenue is the reported segment total (S&P's 'revenue after provisions' of $59.4bn for FY25 is not used).

Reading the valuation: At 12.6x trailing earnings Goldman Sachs trades slightly below its own FY21–FY25 year-end average (13.2x; range 6.4x–17.1x), but at 2.35x tangible book value it sits well above its average (1.68x; range 1.23x–2.63x). What's Working

Strong momentum into 2026: Q3'26 net revenue +23% YoY with equities +72% and investment-banking fees +21%; the 9M'26 efficiency ratio improved to 59.4% (from 62.1%) and company-reported 9M'26 ROE is 20.7%, comfortably above a ~10.5% cost-of-equity hurdle. Capital return is large — shares down ~12% since FY21 while tangible book value per share rose from ~$270 to ~$372 (Q3'26). Capex is modest and the franchise is a clear leader in advisory and equities.

What to Watch

Revenue and earnings are cyclical and trading/advisory-driven: both the revenue CAGR and the net-income proxy CAGR are negative from the record FY21 base. CET1 has moved down to a preliminary 12.4% while buybacks continue, and the shares trade at ~2.35x tangible book against a ~1.68x five-year average. The consumer Platform Solutions business is being reduced (Apple Card portfolio held for sale; segment revenue −72% in Q3'26), Q3'26 expenses included a ~$450M charitable contribution, and insider ownership is below 1%.
