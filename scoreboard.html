Scoreboard — Ranked by Martinero Index

This is not a buy list. The Martinero Index is a fundamentals scorecard only — it measures growth, margins, returns on capital, balance sheet health, moat, valuation-vs-own-history and ownership at a fixed point in time. It takes no account of whether a stock is currently trending up or down, whether today's price is attractive, or whether a company will actually go on to succeed. The Growth Trend (Annual) and Latest Quarter YoY columns (click any header to re-sort) are separate, non-scored signals — and despite sounding like "momentum," neither has anything to do with share-price momentum. Growth Trend (Annual) is purely about the trend in a company's own annual revenue growth: it takes the most recently reported full fiscal year's revenue growth rate and compares it to the fiscal year before that (two full years only — a strong quarter doesn't move this number, and it can lag real-time developments by up to a year). Each row's label and the "FY X%→Y%" in parentheses are the two fiscal-year growth rates being compared; "accelerating" means growth sped up year over year, "decelerating" means it slowed. Latest Quarter YoY is a different, timelier snapshot: just the most recently reported quarter's revenue versus the same quarter a year earlier — a single number, not a trend comparison, and it is deliberately kept separate rather than blended with the annual figure, since the two measure different things (stable multi-year trend vs. current-period reading) and mixing them would muddy both. So a company accelerating from 10%→15% ranks above one decelerating from 20%→18%, even though the latter's absolute growth is higher. It is not part of the Martinero Index and does not affect it. A high rank on either column is not a signal to buy, and a low rank is not a signal to avoid — verify everything yourself before acting on anything on this site.
