About

Hi, I'm Maarten

MSc Business Economics CPA 13 years investing

ASML's one of my better calls, GoPro one of my worse ones. I don't know what's coming next — AI, market volatility, inflation, US jobs numbers, factory indices, or how to time a buy or a sell. Nobody reliably does. What I do believe in is consistency, and having a fixed set of indicators I trust enough to check every company against. This site is that checklist, made public.

Why

I like turning complicated things into something simple — summaries and dashboards, not investor calls or 200-page annual reports. What I actually want to see is what the business is doing, not just what management says about it. I can't predict the future, and past results are no guarantee of future ones — but if a company holds up well against a fixed, simple set of KPIs, that's at least something solid to base a decision on. Every write-up ends in one simple score, not just a wall of numbers. My hope is that these write-ups are simple enough to help you make up your own mind too.

How I invest

No technical analysis, no day trading, no chasing hype — fundamentals only. I started out almost entirely in blue chips; over time that's shifted toward a heavier weighting in growth stocks, but the process hasn't changed. Every company gets the same checklist: growth, margins, return on capital versus its cost, balance sheet health, ownership, and valuation against its own history — including whatever doesn't look good. Some figures are third-party estimates, flagged where it matters. Don't expect a lot of trading from me — I buy or sell about one company a year, and most of what happens in between is fine-tuning the existing portfolio, not drastic moves.

One honest caveat: the score isn't perfect. A company with a lower score can still outperform one with a higher score, and vice versa — treat it as a structured starting point, not a verdict.

I'm in this for the long run, with two goals in mind:

Baseline goal
10%+ CAGR
Stretch goal
2x / 5 yrs

The stretch goal — doubling my portfolio every five years — is something I've fortunately managed to do so far. My take on why that's achievable: in a ten-stock portfolio, you don't need every pick to work out. Two or three real stars are enough to carry double-digit annual growth for the whole portfolio — the rest just need to not blow up. I hope this site helps you find a few of your own.
