CBRE is the genuine, durable #1 in global commercial real estate services — 25% share of global investment sales in 2024, an 800bps lead over the nearest competitor, and its 15th consecutive year on top. The growth headline is real too: revenue was up 13.4% in FY2025 and accelerating further on a trailing basis (+14.9% TTM, +16% in Q2 2026), with Core EBITDA up 34% and Core EPS up 30% in the latest quarter as data-center and infrastructure services demand runs hot. But this framework is built to look past the growth headline, and what it finds underneath is less comfortable: operating margin has drifted down every year for five years straight, from 5.64% (FY2021) to 3.19% (FY2025) — one of the thinnest margins on this entire site — as the lower-margin, contractual Building Operations & Experience segment (facilities/property management, now the majority of revenue) scales faster than the higher-margin Advisory Services brokerage business. Return on invested capital (5.17%) doesn't clear stockanalysis.com's own auto-computed 8.86% cost of capital, let alone this framework's 15% bar, and it has been sliding for years (11.37% in FY2021). Net debt/EBITDA has climbed from a low 0.90x in FY2021 to roughly 3.76x-4.21x today. What's genuinely working: capex is minimal (0.90% of revenue, classically asset-light), FCF conversion is solid (92.2% of EBIT in FY2025), share count is quietly shrinking (buybacks funded by strong cash generation), and the current trailing P/E (32.17x) sits almost exactly at its own FY22-25 average (32.23x) — not a bargain, but not obviously rich either, with a forward P/E (17.44x) that implies a meaningful expected earnings recovery and a PEG ratio of roughly 1.0 against consensus 3-year EPS growth. Net: 5 of 14 criteria met, Martinero Index 36/100 — a real #1 market position and real growth that this framework's profitability and capital-efficiency criteria are specifically designed to stress-test, and they do. This isn't a call to buy or avoid CBRE — just what the checklist says today.
Maarten's note

Built this one as part of a "Big 5" commercial real estate batch (alongside Colliers, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield and Savills) — wanted to see how the actual #1 in the space stacks up against the others once you strip away the "world's largest" headline. The honest answer: being #1 by revenue doesn't mean being #1 on this checklist. CBRE's margin has gone the wrong direction for five straight years, and it's not an accident — it's the deliberate consequence of leaning harder into lower-margin, high-volume facilities management and infrastructure services (which is also, to be fair, where a lot of the AI-data-center-driven demand is showing up right now). I don't think thin margins are automatically bad here; asset-light services businesses are supposed to look like this. What actually gives me pause is the trend combined with rising leverage — net debt/EBITDA more than quadrupling from FY2021 to now while ROIC keeps sliding further below the firm's own cost of capital. That's the kind of thing I'd want a clean explanation for before getting excited about the stock, "biggest in the industry" or not.

Trailing P/E Forward P/E (current only)
Reading the three P/E numbers: current P/E is today's share price divided by the last 12 months of actual reported earnings — a backward-looking snapshot at today's price. Trailing P/E (the FY22-25 series above) is that same backward-looking ratio calculated at each fiscal year-end, showing how the market has re-rated the stock over time relative to what it had already earned. Forward P/E is today's share price divided by analysts' consensus estimate of the next 12 months of earnings — forward-looking, and generally the more useful of the two for judging whether a stock is priced fairly relative to where its earnings are headed. For CBRE specifically: trailing P/E (32.17x) sits almost exactly at its own FY22-25 average (32.23x), a neutral read rather than cheap or rich. Forward P/E (17.44x) is meaningfully lower, implying the market expects real earnings growth to keep coming through. One more lens worth a glance: the PEG ratio, forward P/E divided by expected annual EPS growth, which sanity-checks whether a multiple is cheap once growth is accounted for. Using forward P/E (17.44x) against the 3-year consensus EPS growth forecast (17.38%, per stockanalysis.com), CBRE's PEG works out to roughly 1.00 — right on the traditional "fair value" line, suggesting the stock is priced about in line with its expected growth, neither a bargain nor obviously expensive.
Balance sheet: net debt of $8,363M against FY2025 EBITDA gives a Net Debt/EBITDA of ≈3.76x — above this framework's <2x bar, and up sharply from just ≈0.90x as recently as FY2021. On a trailing-twelve-month basis (through Jun 2026), net debt/EBITDA has widened further to ≈4.21x. This is a meaningful, multi-year re-leveraging, not a one-off spike — debt/equity has climbed from 0.46x (FY2021) to 1.18x (current). ROIC of 5.17% doesn't clear stockanalysis.com's own auto-computed 8.86% WACC, let alone this framework's 15% bar, and has been sliding for years (11.37% in FY2021). Share count is down 2.26% YoY — genuine, real buybacks, a rare bright spot funded by consistently positive free cash flow.

What's Working

CBRE is the genuine, durable #1 in global commercial real estate services, revenue growth is real and accelerating on a trailing basis, FCF conversion is strong (92.2% of EBIT), capex is genuinely minimal (0.90% of revenue), share count is quietly shrinking via real buybacks, and the current entry multiple sits almost exactly at CBRE's own five-year average — a fair, not stretched, starting point.

What to Watch

Operating margin has declined every single year for five years straight, from 5.64% to 3.19%, as the lower-margin Building Operations & Experience segment (now the majority of revenue) outgrows the higher-margin Advisory Services brokerage business — and EBIT itself is lower today than in FY2021 despite revenue growing nearly 46%. ROIC sits below CBRE's own cost of capital and has been sliding for years, and net debt/EBITDA has climbed from 0.90x to roughly 3.8x-4.2x. Whether the infrastructure/data-center growth engine eventually converts into real operating leverage — or keeps diluting margin as it scales — is the central question for CBRE from here.
