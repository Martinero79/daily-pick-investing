🤖 AI Corrections: Bubble Bursting or Boom Being Tested?

On 5 November the S&P 500 fell 4.4% and the Nasdaq 5.5% in a day, the chip index fell about 12%, and investors blamed Democratic midterm wins and fears for data-centre building. It was the sixth sharp AI-related selloff in under two years. Each time the boom was declared over, and each time the numbers kept growing. Is this one different? Here is the evidence on both sides. 👇

🎯 The short answer

📈 Is demand real?
Yes, so far

Capex, chip orders and AI-lab revenue are still growing at rates few industries have ever seen. Q3 sales on our scoreboard: NVIDIA, Micron, SK Hynix, Vertiv, Arista and Amphenol are up 50% to 300%+.

🫧 Is it a bubble?
In parts

Not in the profitable leaders. But the debt-funded layer (neoclouds, leveraged builders) shows bubble-like stress, and spending of about $800B a year still needs revenue to catch up.

🗳️ Politics?
New risk

A Democratic Congress cannot ban data centres, but power costs, tax breaks and permits are now in play. A real but slower-burning risk than the 5 November panic implied.

🚀 The case that the boom continues

Spending is still rising. Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft and SpaceX are expected to spend $803B to $828B on capex in 2026. Q3 alone came to about $227B, up roughly 20% on Q2. Amazon expects to spend "meaningfully more" in 2027, and Alphabet expects capex to rise "significantly".

The labs' revenue is compounding. Anthropic reported an annualised run rate near $65B in July and OpenAI between $40B and $50B by late summer, roughly double where they stood at the start of the year. These are run rates, not recognised revenue, and the two companies count sales differently, so treat them with care.

The leaders make real money. This is not 2000. NVIDIA scores 12 of 14 on our checklist, Arista runs a 45% operating margin with net cash, and Palantir's revenue grew 96% last quarter.

🫧 The case that parts of it are a bubble

The revenue gap. One Goldman-based estimate puts break-even for AI capex at about $308B a year in AI revenue, against roughly $800B of 2026 spending by the biggest buyers. Goldman's chief economist has been quoted saying "FOMO, not ROI" is driving hyperscaler spending. That estimate comes from secondary coverage, so check the original before relying on it.

The debt. The stress in October was in credit, not in earnings. CoreWeave's bonds yielded close to 15% and credit-default swaps implied a nearly 63% five-year default probability. Oracle carries about $160B of debt rated just above junk. When the builders borrow to buy chips from companies that invest in them, a funding squeeze can travel quickly.

Concentration. A handful of customers drive much of the revenue at chip, network and power suppliers. If two or three of them slow orders, the effect is large and fast, as the memory companies know well from past cycles.

🗳️ The midterm effect: what Democrats can and cannot do

Democrats won control of both chambers on 4 November. Trump said he would accept the result and work with them, and the Dow had its best post-midterm day since 1982 (S&P 500 +1.6%). One day later the market reversed on AI-specific fears. Seat counts were still being finalised and one source said the majorities are not large enough to override a presidential veto.

The realistic channels are narrower than the panic suggested:

ChannelWhat is on the tableMy read on the near term
⚡ Power costsA House bill making data centres cover their electricity costs passed 417-3; the Senate has not actedThe most likely, cross-party channel. It raises operating costs for builders but does not stop building.
💸 Tax breaksAbout half a dozen bills to roll back data-centre tax breaks or tax AI usage and excess profitsUnlikely to become law with a veto in play, but they shape the 2027-28 debate and the capex return math.
⛔ MoratoriumThe Sanders/Ocasio-Cortez bill (March 2026) would pause large facilities (reportedly above 20 MW). New York enacted a state moratorium in JulyFederal passage looks very unlikely; Democrats themselves are split (Fetterman called it "China First"). State action matters more.
🔍 OversightHearings and investigations into AI firms, energy costs and "reasonable guardrails" on AIHeadline risk and delays rather than direct earnings hits.
🏛️ Federal preemptionThe administration wants to override burdensome state AI laws and speed permittingA new Congress makes this harder to pass.

Winners and losers differ. A slower build hurts the volume of chips, servers and power equipment (Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider, Amphenol), while higher power costs squeeze the margins of the leveraged builders first. Hyperscalers with cash flow adapt more easily. A mild offset: strategists noted in the past that the 12 months after midterms have tended to be strong for stocks, but that is a pattern, not a promise.

🧠 So: bust or boom? The evidence fits a middle path better than either extreme. The demand and the profits at the suppliers are real and still growing. The financing, the valuations of some names and now the politics of power are where the risk sits. Corrections of 10% to 30% have come about every few months without breaking the spending trend; a true bust would need the spending itself to turn, which no hyperscaler has signalled yet. 🎯
