🛡️ Cyber Security After the Outage Week: Does It Matter for Investors?

In five days the Microsoft 365 cloud, US air traffic control, United Airlines, several big US banks and Apple services all failed. Officials say none of it was a cyberattack. So why does it matter for security stocks? Because it shows how much depends on a few systems, and it separates two ideas investors often mix up: security (keeping attackers out) and resilience (staying up when something breaks). 👇

🔑 Security versus resilience

🛡️ Security
Keeps attackers out

Firewalls, endpoint protection, identity and zero-trust access. This is what Fortinet, Palo Alto, Zscaler and CrowdStrike sell.

🔧 Resilience
Keeps things running

Redundancy, careful upgrades, monitoring and backups. Mostly the job of the operator, with observability tools such as Datadog helping to spot problems.

📉 Link to earnings
Indirect

None of these outages was an attack, so they do not prove more security spending. They raise board-level attention to concentration risk, which may matter over time.

A reminder that cuts the other way: in July 2024 a faulty update from a security vendor, CrowdStrike, itself crashed millions of Windows machines worldwide. Security software sits deep in systems, so a vendor mistake can look just like an attack from the outside.

🧭 How to read this table

Quality is not price. Fortinet has the best scorecard in the group, yet its shares reportedly fell about 26% after hours following its Q3 report on 28 October. A great business can be a poor entry, and a bruised share price can be a good one. Our scores measure the business against the checklist, not today's valuation.

Growth is slowing at the leaders. CrowdStrike and Zscaler both show slower growth ahead, and Zscaler guided FY2027 growth down to about 17%. Palo Alto's 34.5% includes acquisitions such as CyberArk, so organic growth is lower.

AI cuts both ways. AI-enabled attacks and new "AI agent" risks are a demand driver, and press reports show security shares rallying on that story. But the same stories have reversed sharply when worries about AI spending hit the market. Treat the AI-security trade as a theme, not a sure thing.

🧠 So: does it matter? The outage week is a reminder of concentration risk, not evidence that attacks are rising. For investors the useful questions are narrower: which vendors grow fastest, which balance sheets and margins hold up, and how much of the good news is already in the price.
