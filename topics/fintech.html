FinTech: The Industry and Its Stars

FinTech is not one business. It is a stack of very different ones, from toll collectors that never take credit risk to lenders whose results swing with the credit cycle. Scoring nine of its biggest names on the same 14-point checklist shows which layer deserves the attention.

The stack: four ways to make money from payments

Networks. Visa and Mastercard sit between banks and merchants and take a small cut of every card transaction. They lend nothing, so they carry no credit risk.

Processors and merchant platforms. Adyen, Shopify and Toast sell the software merchants run on and earn on the payments flowing through it. Switching costs are high.

Wallets and payment apps. PayPal and Block (Square and Cash App) own consumer relationships on one side and merchants on the other.

Lenders. Nu Holdings (digital bank) and Affirm (buy now, pay later) earn on loans they fund. Growth is faster, but so is the exposure to defaults and funding costs.

What the scores say

The toll collectors score highest on quality. Mastercard and Visa combine duopoly positions with high margins and returns on capital. Their weakness is not the business but the growth rate: about 14% is good, not explosive.

Platforms grow faster but earn less of it. Adyen posts a 47% EBIT margin with growth near 20%, yet the shares are down about 35% in a year on cooler guidance and rising capex. Toast has swung from loss to profit and holds net cash, but its GAAP operating margin is only about 5%.

Lenders show the fastest growth and the most caveats. Nu's revenue is up 52% and its return on equity is 33%, but it lends unsecured to emerging-market consumers. Affirm's growth is strong, yet returns sit far below the cost of capital and a funded loan book carries debt.

The incumbents are cheap for a reason. PayPal trades at roughly 10x earnings with 4 to 5% growth, and Block's reported revenue is distorted by bitcoin pass-through. Both could re-rate, but the checklist rewards what is already visible in the numbers, not a turnaround that might come.

The pattern is consistent: the further a FinTech sits from lending, the cleaner its scorecard. Networks and software-like platforms earn their margins on every transaction. Lenders earn theirs only if the credit cycle cooperates.

What could go wrong

Regulation of card fees and bank-like licences. Credit cycles for Nu and Affirm. Competition from the big platforms, banks and stablecoin or account-to-account rails that bypass card networks. Valuation: several of these names have already fallen sharply from their highs, which can mean opportunity or a thesis that has genuinely weakened.

A personal note

I think FinTech is a sector worth investing in, but the choice is which layer and which company. Visa is the only name from this list in my own portfolio, and it is the toll-collector end of the stack. In this case I picked it for its near-duopoly position and as a safe choice: close to linear, low-volatility growth, but not so exciting. The scores above say that end is where the quality is. The fast-growing lenders are the more exciting story and the harder one to own through a downturn.
