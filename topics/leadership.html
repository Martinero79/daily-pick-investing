👔 Do Leaders Move Share Prices? The Key-Person Premium

On 12 November Nvidia announced that founder Jensen Huang had resigned as CEO. On 23 November the board reinstated him, and the shares rose about 31% in a day. Few events show so clearly how much a share price can hang on one person. But it does not hold for every company: some businesses barely notice a new CEO. This page looks at when a leader moves a stock, and when the system does the work. 👇

What it shows, and what it doesn't. The shares fell about a third in two weeks, from $186.64 on Fri 6 Nov to $125.53 on Fri 20 Nov, then won back about two-thirds of the drop in a single session (to $165.05), while the business numbers did not change. That points to a large leader premium. But the slide began before the first reports, in a chip-sector selloff (the stock was near $200 on 4 Nov), and Monday was also a market-wide rally: the Nasdaq rose about 6.5% and Micron about 19%, so not all of the move was Huang. Treat the 31% as an upper bound for the leader effect, not a measurement of it.

The pattern: reactions are biggest when the news is a surprise and the person is hard to replace (Niccol, Musk, Buffett, Huang). They are small when the succession was planned or the system carries the business (Costco, Microsoft 2014, Apple 2011).

🎓 What the research says

Johnson, Magee, Nagarajan & Newman (1985)
Mixed

Studied 53 sudden executive deaths. On average little systematic effect on stock returns, but wide differences between firms, linked to founder status, the executive's role and how hard they were to replace.

Salas (2010)
Sign can flip

After sudden executive deaths, reactions were strongly positive (about +6.8%) when the executive had more than 10 years of tenure and the firm had been underperforming. The author reads this as entrenchment: sometimes a leader's exit is good news.

Sources: Journal of Accounting and Economics 7 (1985) 151-174; Journal of Banking & Finance 34(3) (2010) 656-666. Summaries are from abstracts and secondary descriptions, not full-text reading.

🧠 What this means for an investor. A leader premium is a hidden risk as well as a strength. A stock priced on one person's vision can fall further on bad news about that person than on bad news about the business. A stock carried by a system will usually shrug at a change of CEO. Knowing which kind you hold tells you which risks to watch: succession and governance in one case, competition and execution in the other.
